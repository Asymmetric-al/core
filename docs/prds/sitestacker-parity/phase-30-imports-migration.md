# Phase 30 — Imports & Migration

Status: **Ready — complete ratified specification; implementation and activation pending.** Publication issue: [AL-2543](https://github.com/Asymmetric-al/core/issues/2543). Baseline inspected: `develop` at `629cb71dc`. The founder selected `/to-spec` and confirmed the testing seams on 4 November 2026. This specification preserves the 21 founder-ratified decisions [Q1]–[Q21], with every recorded amendment, and reopens none of them. It authorizes no implementation, tracker backlog, live policy, provider mutation or deployment. `Ready` applies to the finished specification only: no import kind is built, qualified or switched on by this document, and every owner requirement it names is identified, not agreed. Implementation tickets require the separate, explicitly authorized `/to-ticket` stage.

## Problem Statement

A ministry that chooses Asym arrives with years of supporter and giving records in another system. The first ministries mostly come from SiteStacker; others use Bloomerang, Salesforce, Raiser's Edge, DonorPerfect, CiviCRM or a spreadsheet. Their staff need to bring people, households, giving history, recurring gifts, consent and opt-outs, notes, files, custom data and the web addresses donors still use into Asym, while the ministry keeps receiving gifts and doing its work. They are not data engineers, they cannot freeze the old system, and many will have only coaching from Asym.

A move can go wrong in ways a ministry cannot easily put right. History can be lost or rolled up into yearly totals, so first-gift and lapsed conclusions become false. Imports can duplicate supporters, attach a shared email to the wrong spouse, split or merge couples by guesswork, or record the wrong legal donor permanently. An import can set off welcome emails, appeals to people who opted out, or thank-you messages for gifts made years ago. Printed QR codes, text-to-give replies and old giving links can break the day the domain moves. In the switch year donors can receive two tax receipts for one gift, or none. Recurring donors can be charged twice or silently stopped.

A raw export also names missionaries who serve where being identified puts them in danger. Showing those names, or the fund names that point to them, to helpers, Asym staff, a download or the wrong page is not a cosmetic error. The same applies to care, counselling and personnel records that a careless import would turn into ordinary notes.

After go-live, staff still need ordinary imports: a list of people from an event, a donor-advised fund or workplace-giving payout, a corrected address list. These files should fill gaps without overwriting good data, should never send anything or grant consent, should be easy to check before anything changes, and should be reversible when the file turns out to be wrong. Staff also need to see, at any time, what an import did, what is still waiting and who can act.

Today Core has none of this. There is no Data Tools route, navigation entry or import capability; no import, plan, staging or run records; no CSV or XLSX reader; the only upload path accepts 5 MB images; there is no private import storage or malware scan; the people and money owners have no import commands; the only bulk-job pattern depends on the browser staying open; and the staff notification bell is a demonstration [decision log §8], [D77], [D78]. Several present-code exposures, such as public storage buckets and care notes readable across tenants, would make imported notes, files or history unsafe until their owners fix them [D38], [D40]–[D44], [D65], [D66], [D24].

## Solution

A ministry leaving its previous system makes one planned **Switch** [Q1], [Q3]. SiteStacker has Direct support, with presets, an export checklist and an omissions sheet; any other system moves through per-tenant Import templates under the same rules (Reviewed transformation). The ministry completes a readiness census, runs Practice runs that write nothing to live records, and can commit a Practice run into a short-lived Practice copy in the separate Practice environment to time every step and click through the result [Q6]. At go/no-go it records the Switch Boundary, the End date, its Email list dispositions and Legacy Surface Dispositions, and pause limits measured in rehearsal. After the boundary it takes one final export, commits each family through its owner's import command with no outward effect, reconciles counts and per-currency totals at two levels, signs off and declares the Switch live.

Recurring gifts are never moved on switch day. They keep running in the old system during a bounded Legacy Recurring Tail; their later facts arrive through reviewed Legacy Tail Backfills that are silent to donors and honest to staff [Q7]. Donors move with one Continue link, unmoved gifts end with notice only after a real offer, and the Switch closes only when every legacy executor is proved stopped, formally cut over or ended and the Legacy Recurring Tail has closed: after the last old charge plus its reversal window, extended until every open dispute resolves [Q1], [Q8].

Everything the source can export comes across into its owner's protected home, or is a Visible exclusion with its reason [Q14]. People come first and households only from evidence [Q10]. Imported restrictions commit before any Adopted email permission, and Adopted email permissions carry over only from evidence [Q9]. Giving history comes across gift by gift, complete or waiting, and is Recorded outside Asym, so the ministry keeps documenting those gifts outside Asym [Q4], [Q13]. Custom fields are created by a person from the mapping step [Q16]. Old giving links are kept in place, and every other old link has one owner outcome [Q17]. The ministry decides every step; Asym helps hands-on only through ministry-approved, time-limited Asym help access [Q5].

After go-live, staff use two kinds of Everyday import [Q12]. A People import adds new people and, by default, fills only empty fields; Replace and Clear are explicit, counted choices; contact details are added, never replaced; newer work in Asym always wins. A Gift file becomes draft Phase 15 gift batches with an expected count and total; nothing is posted and no receipts are sent until someone posts those batches in Contributions.

All of this lives in **Data Tools**: one Mission Control entry with Imports, Switch and Remembered mappings tabs [Q19]. "Import people" on the People list and "Upload a Gift file" in Contributions open the same flow. Each import has one Import page, saved after every change: File, Columns, Values when needed, then Review and import, with counts that add up to the file's rows and a final button that names the result. Imports run in the background; the person can leave; progress uses real counts; results, downloads and next steps stay on the page; notices come through Phase 17.

For 30 days, a person with the reversal permission can **Prepare reversal** on a People import, a Gift file or, after go-live, a Switch run [Q18]. One page shows what can be reversed now, what needs its owner's correction and what stays as it is. A value goes back only if nothing has written that field since the import; people the import created are archived only if Untouched; money, restrictions and opt-outs are never changed by an import reversal.

Access is one Data Tools row with View, Manage and Admin levels on Phase 12 [Q20]. Only the Owner group starts with it, and Data Tools stays off until Phase 12 can grant it. Rows that match someone the preparer cannot see, a staff member or someone marked deceased are Held for review by someone who can see them, never created as new people. Throughout, Asym never uses AI or an outside importer service for mapping [Q11], never merges records automatically, never sends, welcomes or triggers automations because of an import or an import reversal, never issues tax documents for imported history, never moves a recurring executor or handles card or bank numbers, never decides for the ministry, and never sends a raw export back [Q15].

## Implementation Decisions

These decisions are binding. They name modules conceptually; exact file layout, table and column names, and route paths are set during implementation within these rules. Where a decision cites a ratified clause, the clause governs any wording difference.

### Ownership and boundaries

1. **Phase 30 owns mechanics; owners keep meaning.** Phase 30 owns import transport, staging, mappings, plans, import-run evidence and the reversal-workbench mechanics [C1], [C5]. Each target owner keeps validation, business acceptance and every command that writes its records: Phase 4 identity, matching and merge; Phase 9 people, households, contact points, notes and interactions; Phase 10 Record tiers and aliases; Phase 11 custom fields, options and collections; Phases 13, 14, 15 and 16 every money rule; Phase 7 receipting; Phase 23 website content; Phase 24 giving addresses and domains; Phase 29 exact bytes, custody and disposal; Phases 38 and 41 sealed care and personnel records. Phase 30 never defines a target domain's schema, never loads target tables directly, never bypasses an owner invariant, and never resolves uncertainty in historic finance, media rights or identity itself.

2. **Predecessor constraints carry unchanged.** The 23 accepted constraints bind every decision below. In particular: no automatic donor merge, ever [C2]; a consumer phase cannot acquire a producer's authority by shorthand [C3]; history adopts as settled facts with no imported status, refunds append-only and noncash described, never valued [C6]; imports never mint receipts for pre-platform gifts [C7]; a Phase 30 completed state never activates or proves a balance or a Field Account [C8]; a different executor is a Phase 16 formal cutover [C9]; imported flags never grant consent by themselves [C10]; Phase 29 custody is verified independently of row acceptance [C11]; Twenty is retired and is never a source of authority [C12]; child sponsorship is a Visible exclusion [C13]; a source or provider ID is a link, never an identity [C14]; templates come from the Phase 11 field catalog [C15]; holding a Data Tools permission never widens a target owner's authority, and deferred work re-proves it [C16]; restricted identities carry an alias and restricted export is hard-blocked [C17]; recurring operational source intake belongs to its source domain [C18]; Legal Entity and currency are never inferred from Site, history never falls back to the Default Site, and locale maps exactly [C19]; imports never issue or release a giving address [C20]; Entry Method is an attribution axis, not a status [C21]; missing history blocks first-gift and lapse conclusions [C22]; and zero unintended send, enrollment, payment or public effect is a release gate [C23].

3. **No family without its owner command and its correction path.** A record family becomes importable, by Everyday import or Switch, only when its owner's typed import command exists and its row in the import reversal table is published with a working link or command [Q18], [C23]. Each family commits only into its owner's protected home [Q14]. A family whose home does not exist yet is a Visible exclusion, or waits in the retained final manifest under the same Switch Boundary [Q1], [Q14], [Q15]. A Phase 30 import never writes the prototype `donations` table [Q4], [Q13].

### Server module, commands and queries

4. **One Phase 30 domain module in the shared API package.** All Phase 30 business logic lives in one imports domain module in the shared API package, which exposes server commands and permission-filtered queries. Mission Control route handlers and server actions stay thin: they authenticate, call exactly one command or query with the request's intent, and map its result to a response. Pages read only through the module's projections. No app holds import business logic, and no browser code reads or writes import, plan, staging, outcome or import reversal records directly.

5. **Commands.** The module exposes these server commands, each named for the user's intent:
   - **Import commands:** create import draft (the kind, People import or Gift file, is chosen up front); record upload (completes a resumable upload, runs the File safety check and reads the file); confirm header row (with the sheet, where the file has several); confirm columns; confirm values; build plan (including each column's Write mode, and any Clear as its own confirmed, counted decision); decide match; fix cell; leave rows out; confirm import; run batch step (invoked only by the durable job, never by a browser); stop; cancel; resume; review held rows; prepare reversal; confirm import reversal; and stream downloads. Supporting commands in the same families: rename an import, discard an import draft, rename or delete a Remembered mapping, and stop or resume an import reversal.
   - **Switch commands:** create Switch (with its Source profile and source declaration); record census answers (including Source mapping statements, Email list dispositions and Old link list rows); narrowing; practice run; Practice copy request (build, extend once, destroy); sign-off (two-level reconciliation, History spot check and the Verify your copy confirmation); set Switch Boundary (at go/no-go, with the End date, pause limits and tail closure date); attest editing pause; commit family; declare Switch live; tail backfill (prepare, review and commit one Legacy Tail Backfill); close (tail closure and the decommission checklist); legacy surface dispositions; and domain readiness (each Domain readiness check and each "Pointed to Asym" state). Supporting commands: reassign the Switch lead, place or release a Legal hold, record the end of the SiteStacker licence, and abort before go-live.

6. **Queries.** The module exposes the Imports list, the import page, the Switch task list, the results list and Remembered mappings, plus the held-row review list. Every query is scoped by the server-derived tenant, applies the viewer's Phase 12 permissions and record visibility before counting, paging or serializing, and pages, filters and searches on the server; nothing filters only a loaded page.

7. **Command contract.** Every consequential command:
   - derives tenant, actor, confirmer and reviewer from server context and the stored import record, never from the request body [Q20];
   - checks the single Phase 12 decision point, as every page, action, projection and batch does;
   - carries the expected plan revision when it changes a plan, and refuses a stale revision;
   - carries an operation identity so a retry finds the original result instead of doing the work twice;
   - records the step on the import record with actor, time, counts and outcome codes, never row content; and
   - returns a safe outcome code with a plain reason and one next step, never a raw error message.

   Import status, held-row state, import reversal state and Switch stage change only through these commands. No client, route or job sets a status directly.

### Durable execution

8. **Durable jobs on the existing durable-function infrastructure.** Reading, plan building, import commits, held-row commits, import reversals, Switch stages and Legacy Tail Backfills run as durable functions on the existing durable-function infrastructure, with job payloads carrying identifiers only. Each step claims a server-side chunk, writes through owner commands with idempotency keys, records a per-row outcome and recovers stale claims, following the contribution-operation batch processor's server chunk, claim and stale-recovery pattern. Continuation is always server-driven; the browser-driven continuation of today's bulk contribution operations is never a precedent [decision log §8]. Run visibility follows the `/workflows` run model: a dispatch ledger, product-owned summaries and safe error codes, with a Phase 8 catalog signal for stuck or failed imports [O11]. A job acts for its recorded confirmer, reviewer or resumer, whose current authority and clearance are re-proved through Phase 12 before each batch; a job never gains authority that person lacks and never uses service-level access to bypass an owner's rules [C16], [Q20]. Today's background jobs cannot yet carry a staff actor's authority [decision log §8]; that carrier is built under Phase 12's rules before any import kind activates.

9. **One heavy job per ministry.** Each ministry has one heavy-job lock, keyed by tenant [Q15], [Q18], [Q19]. Import commits, import reversals and Switch practice-run, commit and backfill stages take it. A later Confirm shows "Waiting to start, after {name}'s {import} (started {time})", starts on its own when the lock frees, and re-checks permissions, plan freshness and the Switch pause at start, refusing with a reason if any check fails. Another ministry's job is never named or counted. The missionary self-import refuses a second import instead of queueing [Q21].

10. **Stop, cancel, resume and failure.**
    - Before any write, "Cancel import" changes nothing. After writes, "Stop after this batch" stops at the current batch boundary, explains that saved rows stay, and points to Prepare reversal [Q19].
    - A crashed worker resumes automatically from per-row outcomes. A write whose outcome is unknown is verified before its outcome is recorded; if it cannot be verified, the import ends Needs attention.
    - Repeated failure stops after the batch as Needs attention when rows were written, or as Failed when nothing was written, with a safe code, and raises the Phase 8 signal.
    - Lost authority stops after the batch as Needs attention, "Stopped: {name} no longer has permission"; anyone holding the needed permissions can resume the rest under their own name [Q20].
    - If the permission service is unavailable, pages fail closed to the no-access page and jobs pause.
    - Resume continues the same import record, plan revision and idempotency records; it never starts a competing import.

### State machines

11. **Import statuses.** The one status vocabulary is: Draft · Checking file · Ready to review · Waiting to start · Importing · Stopping · Done · Needs attention · Stopped · Failed · Discarded [Q19]. The forward path is Draft → Checking file → Ready to review → (Waiting to start) → Importing → (Stopping) → one of Done, Needs attention, Stopped or Failed; an Import draft, not yet confirmed, can become Discarded. Rules:
    - "Done" only when nothing is left out, waiting or needing review; otherwise "Needs attention".
    - "Stopped" and "Failed" mean nothing changed; anything partly written is "Needs attention".
    - "Stopping" is transient.
    - Done and Discarded are immutable. The only path back to Importing is resume of an import that stopped after a batch with unprocessed rows remaining, on the same record. An import reversal never rewrites the import's status; it adds a second label: Reversal prepared · Reversing · Reversed · Partly reversed.
    - Status is shown as colour plus an icon plus words.

    The exact transition table, including how resume and held-row review interact with the terminal set, is fixed during implementation against Phase 23 D29's terminal model, without weakening these rules.

12. **Held rows.** Held → Reviewed (committed by the reviewer, or left out) or Held → Expired ("Left out: not reviewed in time"). Forward-only; each review write is idempotent per row and plan revision; two reviewers are separated by the plan revision check [Q20].

13. **Import reversal record.** Preparing → Prepared; Prepared → Expired (after 24 hours or at the window's end, whichever is first); Prepared → Running (on confirm); Running → Stopping → Stopped (resumable), Finished, Finished with items left as they are, or Failed (resumable) [Q18]. Each import is reversed at most once: at most one import reversal per import is ever confirmed, and a stopped or failed one resumes for its remaining items on the same record and is never replaced by a new one. At most one prepared page exists for an import at a time. No record returns to Prepared: an Expired page is never revived, but while the Import reversal window is open and no import reversal of that import has been confirmed, preparing again builds a new page. No confirm is accepted after the window.

14. **Switch stages.** A Switch moves forward only through recorded stages [Q1]: preparing (readiness census, Practice runs, Practice copies) → scheduled (go/no-go passed; Switch Boundary, End date, pause limits and tail closure date recorded) → committing (boundary passed; Editing pause attested; final Source Snapshot Manifest sealed; readiness census retaken at the final export; families committed and reconciled in owner order) → **Switch live** (go-live declared) → **Switch closed** (tail closed) → decommissioned (decommission checklist signed off). Abort is possible only before Switch live: plans are discarded, any Practice copy is destroyed, committed rows stay invisible and are never deleted unless the aborted Switch is later purged as abandoned (decision 19), and a later attempt converges [Q1], [Q6], [Q15]. After Switch live, system-of-record authority never returns to the previous system without a new founder decision. An abandoned Switch is purged under decision 19 [Q15]. A new Practice run replaces the previous one [Q6]. Internal stage names are set during implementation; the Switch task list is derived from these recorded facts, never stored separately [Q19].

15. **Plan revisions and stale plans.** Every plan change carries the plan revision, and a stale revision is refused, so two people never overwrite each other [Q19]. Confirm is idempotent per plan revision; a second press shows the running import. A plan older than 24 hours, or whose file changed, is rebuilt before Confirm, showing what changed. Changing an earlier step rebuilds Review and says so.

### Data model

16. **Entities and invariants.** Every Phase 30 record carries its tenant and uses tenant-aware composite keys to its parent. At the level of entities:
    - **Import record:** kind (People import, Gift file or Switch run; Practice runs are recorded with their Switch and listed on the Switch tab), name (default: kind plus date and time, such as "People import, 4 Nov 2026, 10:42"), status, who started it, the confirmer, UTC timestamps, outcome counts, reversal deadline, mapping version and plan revision, with an attributed step history (upload, header row, mapping confirmed, decisions, cell fixes, Confirm, Stop, Cancel, resume) [Q19], [Q20]. It is the single count source for the list, the page and handover.
    - **Plan with revision:** mapping version, per-column Write mode, decisions, any Clear, expected count and total for a Gift file, the file's digest and the target revisions it was built against.
    - **Staging rows:** each source row's parsed cells, row number, source ID and planned outcome; server-only.
    - **Plan edits:** each fixed cell as row, column, old value, new value, who and when. The held file never changes [Q19]. Old and new values use the plan-edit cell-value retention class (decision 19). Erasure removes those values and leaves row, column, who and when.
    - **Row outcomes:** exactly one append-only outcome per row with its code and reason; outcome counts sum to the file's rows.
    - **Remembered mapping:** one tenant and one source; the exact header set; column and value maps that store each option's stable ID, never its label; a version; last used by and when [Q11], [Q16].
    - **Import reversal record and Reversal prior values:** described in decision 33.
    - **Narrowing record:** who narrowed, when, and each column decision or the file-level declaration that the file names no restricted person [Q5], [Q20].
    - **Switch record:** Source profile and source declaration (system and instance), Switch lead, Switch Boundary (a half-open UTC instant shown in the tenant's giving time zone), census answers, dispositions, attestations, sign-offs bound to report versions, End date, tail closure date and each history family's Coverage start.

17. **Source Record Bindings and the Source Snapshot Manifest.** A Source Record Binding is an append-only link from (tenant, environment, source system, installation or provider account, mode, record family, source ID, role) to an Asym target; one source row may bind several targets by role, and a source household binds as its own record family [Q10]. Bindings follow Phase 4 survivors, block re-creation after erasure through tombstones, and carry convergence and Exact repeat import; identity never rests on a file digest or manifest [Q1], [Q15]. Phase 30 writes bindings only through the owner's typed, audited command on the ratified binding carrier, after its owner generalizes the carrier and revokes direct staff writes [D9], [O7]. Money references use the source-qualified import identity [O16]. Each Source Snapshot Manifest records, per file and per file inside a ZIP, its SHA-256 digest, filter or partition, exporter role, source version, export times and on-screen control counts; the final manifest is sealed after the Switch Boundary [Q1], [Q15].

18. **Run evidence, history coverage and source basis.** Run evidence (counts, totals, digests, mapping versions, sign-offs and actors) is kept for the life of the account and holds no donor or supporter row content; an erased staff member shows as "former staff member" [Q15]. Phase 30 supplies the history-coverage record (each history family's Coverage start and import provenance) that Phases 28, 33 and 35 consume [O3], and run-level terminal states and visibility [O11]. **Source basis** means the one declared origin of a gift population for a period (for example a source system's transaction export, or the ministry's processor report for an executor the source does not record), against which counts and totals reconcile; each population and period has exactly one, with an overlap proof, and Historic Giving ledger lines are never gifts [Q1], [Q3], [D16]. Its catalogue is aligned with Phase 33's semantic layer during implementation.

19. **Retention classes.** Every stored Phase 30 artifact has a Retention class; an artifact without a class cannot be stored [Q15], [Q18], [Q19]:

    | Retention class                                                                           | Deleted                                                                                        |
    | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
    | Final export (final Source Snapshot Manifest)                                             | The earlier of 90 days after Switch closed or 24 months after go-live                          |
    | Practice and superseded exports, backfill inputs, switch-off export and kept-list returns | 30 days after being superseded, or after that backfill or return is signed off                 |
    | Per-run staging                                                                           | 30 days after that run is committed or discarded; the final run's 30 days after go-live        |
    | Unfinished uploads                                                                        | 24 hours                                                                                       |
    | Everyday import drafts (People import or Gift file, with their file)                      | 14 days after their last change, with the date shown 7 days ahead; no email is sent            |
    | Everyday import files                                                                     | 30 days after commit                                                                           |
    | Reversal prior values                                                                     | When the Import reversal window ends, after any import reversal confirmed in time has finished |
    | Plan-edit cell values                                                                     | When that import's reversal window ends; row, column, who and when remain                     |

    Run evidence is kept for the life of the account (decision 18). Practice copies keep Q6's own lifetime: 14 days from ready, one 14-day extension with a reason, and earlier destruction on any Q6 trigger [Q6], [Q15]. A daily database-driven Retention sweep deletes through the storage service, verifies each deletion and logs it with no personal data; it runs report-only first, pausing it is audited, and Asym publishes the schedule [Q15]. A Legal hold is placed by a holder of Run the Switch or by Asym's named legal role, which never reads content, and records a typed reason, scope and review date; it stops every deletion path, never auto-releases (a named person releases it with a reason) and does not extend the Import reversal window [Q15], [Q18], [O74]. Erasure suppresses the person in every held file at once through binding tombstones and rewrites each affected held file within 14 days as a successor file, keeping the original digest; refused identifier columns are stripped by the same rewrite within 14 days of sign-off [Q15], [Q16]. The same erasure removes that person's plan-edit cell values together with their Reversal prior values. Switch files and staging are deleted within 30 days of tenant closure unless held. An abandoned Switch's files and staging are purged after 180 days without owner activity (uploading, mapping, planning, committing or signing off; viewing does not count), with warnings at 150 and 173 days; a Switch aborted before go-live and then purged has its never-visible committed rows disposed by the owners. Raw files and staging stay in the tenant's hosting region.

20. **Tenant scoping and grants.** There are no client grants on import, plan, staging, plan-edit, outcome, Remembered mapping, narrowing, import reversal or prior-value tables; row-level security denies anonymous and authenticated roles; Reversal prior values are never readable through any view or remote procedure exposed to clients [Q18], [Q19]. Tenant, actor and timestamps come from server context. An import from another ministry, or an unknown identifier, is not found for Data Tools holders and shows the no-access page to everyone else, identically whether or not it exists [Q20]. Per-tenant status is streamed, never cached. Logs, traces and error reports carry import identifiers only, never file names, import names or values; job payloads carry identifiers only [Q15], [Q19].

### Files

21. **Custody and the File safety check.** Exact source bytes live in Phase 29 custody under a staff-migration custody profile defined with the Phase 29 owner [O19], [C11]; Phase 30 holds the logical records (imports, plans, staging rows, outcomes, run evidence). Where staged derivatives of non-self-import profiles are held is settled with the Phase 29 owner through that profile [D11]. Uploads are resumable and direct to storage, with determinate progress and resume after a dropped connection [O99]. Every imported file, including self-import text, XLSX and ZIP files, passes the File safety check before it is stored: quarantine; a malware scan Asym runs itself, never a third-party upload service; a type allowlist and a size cap (import files follow their qualified profile's limits, such as the Size profile in decision 22, and an imported attachment or photo is capped at 25 MB per file by default, with Phase 29 setting the profile); image re-encoding that removes location data; an opaque storage name; download-only access [Q14], [Q21]. Imported files never use today's public buckets [D44].

22. **Readers and templates.** CSV, TSV, XLSX and ZIP are read inside Asym by open-source parsing libraries chosen and qualified as an engineering choice under Phase 29; no hosted or embedded importer service and no AI is used [Q11]. Asym also writes XLSX for templates and downloads [D78]. The Size profile's starting limits apply: CSV or TSV up to 2 GB per file; XLSX up to 50 MB and 1 GB uncompressed at no more than 100:1; ZIP with per-entry limits; up to 10 GB and 20 million rows per Switch; raising a limit is a recorded profile change [Q15]. Limits are set per qualified profile behind one shared reader seam, never as shared engine constants [Q21]. The File step shows rows, columns, sheet and encoding; a person picks the sheet and confirms the header row [Q11], [Q19]. IDs, dates, postal codes and phones are read as text; IDs in scientific notation or with detectably lost leading zeros are rejected; ambiguous dates are flagged, never guessed [Q3], [Q11]. Blank tokens are declared per column, and partial dates are never padded [Q16]. Identifier refusal applies to every import with no override on headers [Q16]. Import templates are generated per tenant from the field catalog and owner template descriptors, preferring XLSX with text-typed ID, date, postal-code and phone columns [Q3], [O21]. For the Reviewed transformation path, the template families are Parties and households; contact points with explicit consent and adverse-evidence columns, held as evidence; relationships; custom fields and collections, with collections only into those the ministry created first; one-time giving history with reversals, and soft credits and tributes where their owners allow; and recurring evidence for the Phase 16 classifier [Q3], [Q9], [Q16].

23. **Downloads.** There are two kinds [Q20]:
    - **Your file's rows** (problem, left-out and held rows) need Data Tools Manage, or the Prepare permission (`imports.prepare`) granted on its own (decision 24), and contain only the file's own cells and reasons, never Asym-side values; held rows are included with the same reason, so counts never change.
    - **Downloads with Asym data** (results lists, Switch Waiting rows) need the ministry's existing export permission plus on-screen visibility, and never include protected people.

    Every download is streamed within the request and never stored, is text-typed and formula-safe through the single CSV-safe encoder [ADR-0212] with no private encoder, is audited by identifiers only, and is limited to 1,000,000 rows; larger sets are filtered first [Q15], [Q20].

### Permissions and visibility

24. **A Data Tools module on Phase 12.** Phase 12 adds a Data Tools permission module with View, Manage and Admin levels [Q20], [O102]. Phase 30 mints dotted permission atoms under Phase 12's rules, matching the code-authoritative capability registry's dotted-key pattern [D86]. The working atoms are `imports.view` (View); `imports.prepare` and `imports.run` (Manage); and `imports.replace_values`, `imports.clear_values`, `imports.reverse` and `imports.switch` (Admin). Q20's "Replace and Clear" is the pair `imports.replace_values`, which offers Replace as a column's Write mode, and `imports.clear_values`, which offers the separate Clear action; a person holding only one of them is offered only that action. Final names are confirmed during implementation under Phase 12's rules without changing this placement. The four Admin atoms are Phase 12 `sensitive`: never on the View or Manage level, granted through Phase 12's sensitive-grant flow, and audited. Each atom stays grantable on its own, for example Prepare without Run for a helper. `imports.prepare` covers uploading, choosing columns and values, making decisions, fixing cells and downloading your file's rows, and `imports.run` covers confirming [Q20]; a person granted `imports.prepare` on its own therefore sees and fixes the file's own cells (decision 26) and downloads their file's rows (decision 23), but cannot confirm. The Owner group holds Data Tools Admin; no other seed holds any Data Tools permission; existing users receive nothing new at launch; access is denied by default. Clearance is never part of any level. There is no role-name gate, #2281's platform approval is not used, and Data Tools stays switched off until Phase 12 can grant these permissions [O102].

25. **Run the Switch and money.** `imports.switch` (Run the Switch) is Q5's Switch decision capability. It covers starting a Switch; census answers, dispositions and attestations; plan and match decisions; reconciliation controls and sign-off; commit, tail closure and decommission; reassigning the Switch lead; Practice copies; and Legal hold [Q20]. A Legal hold may also be placed and released by Asym's named legal role through its own Phase 12 legal-hold capability, without reading any content and without any Data Tools permission [Q15], [O74]. Money commits also need the money owner's permission [C16]; history correction keeps its own capability [Q13]; creating fields needs Phase 11's. Narrowing needs Run the Switch and clearance. The Start action appears only once Asym's preconditions are met and the Switch kind is switched on for the ministry (decision 42) [Q2], [Q6], [Q19], [Q20]; scheduling a started Switch needs the further gates in decisions 34 and 43. A Gift file can be prepared with Manage; its Confirm also needs `finance:record_contribution`. Amounts in the Imports list and on pages show only to people who can see contributions; others see counts.

26. **The visibility rule.** Asym-side facts for a row (a match, which record, current values, outcome detail) show only to people who can see that record. The file's own cells show to Manage holders and to anyone granted Prepare on its own (decision 24). Counts are whole-file and the same for everyone [Q20]. The Switch real-export floor is unchanged: a real Switch export is restricted until a cleared member narrows it; until then uncleared viewers, helpers and Asym help access see headers, counts and validation summaries only [Q5]. In a Switch real export, source fund names are shown only to cleared members, before and after narrowing; others see each fund's counts without its name [Q11], [Q20]. Everyday imports are the ministry's own files and are not restricted until narrowed. The Funds step shows the file's own labels and the designations the person can see, with Phase 10 aliases. The Columns step offers only fields the person may edit; if someone else confirms, Review lists "Won't be written: you can't edit {field}" and those columns are left out; the write path re-checks. The file name shows on the import page to Manage holders only.

27. **Held for review.** Any row matching a record the preparer cannot see, a staff member, or someone marked deceased is held with one wording for every reason: "Held for review: someone who can see the matching records will review these rows. Nothing has changed for them." [Q12], [Q20]. A held row is never created as a new person, never shows the match and never changes the counts; the rest of the import goes ahead. After commit, any Manage holder who can see the matched record, including the preparer if they can, reviews it and commits it under their own permissions on the same import record. A Tasks Hub item reaches eligible reviewers without naming anyone [O97]. Rows not reviewed by the file's deletion date become "Left out: not reviewed in time", with the date shown. Data Tools Admins see one setup notice when nobody with Manage has clearance. Held matches to protected people count as restricted-access attempts in Phase 12's audit and budget, without plaintext [O104].

28. **People without access, helpers, Asym help access and Hide values.** People without Data Tools access see no menu entry and no shortcuts; any Data Tools address shows "You don't have access to Data Tools." with **Request access**, which goes to Phase 12's Access requests and names no one [O103]. View holders see one line on an import page, "You can follow this import. Preparing and confirming need Data Tools Manage access.", plus Request access; actions they cannot take are absent. View holders see every import's status, counts and starter; Manage holders can continue any draft, with every change attributed. Helpers get ordinary Data Tools grants with an end date, by default the day the Switch closes [O105]. Asym help access is the Phase 12 Operator Tenant Grant for `switch_preparation` [O26]: it sees only the Switch tab and its preparation actions, never the everyday Imports list, import names, values before narrowing or downloads, and never decides, commits, attests, uploads or grants; no Asym identity holds a Data Tools permission [Q5], [Q20]. A **Hide values** switch on Data Tools pages hides every value for the current session and never reveals more.

29. **Authority while running.** Each batch re-checks the confirmer's permissions and clearance; on loss, the import stops after that batch as Needs attention (decision 10). If the confirmer's access is time-limited, Confirm shows when it ends [Q20].

### Everyday imports

30. **People import rules** [Q12], [Q10]. A People import carries names and contact details, tags and lists, existing custom fields only, household columns under the evidence rules, and restriction columns as restrict-only. Each column has a Write mode: Fill empty only (default), Replace (opt-in, showing "N existing values will change") or Ignore. A blank cell never changes anything; Clear is a separate, confirmed action with a count. Contact details are added, never replaced, and become primary only if the import says so; old addresses are kept with an end date; tags and lists are only added; an address change never spreads to other household members; change-of-address addresses always go to review; a new email already held by another person goes to review. A field changed in Asym after the plan was made is a conflict and is kept ("Changed in Asym since: kept"). Do-not-contact people are updated but flagged; imports cannot lift restrictions. Matching uses the source ID remembered per source, then email plus an agreeing first name; a shared-mailbox marker always sends the match to review [O47]. Nothing merges; possible duplicates go to Phase 4's queue after commit carrying the import's ID [O95]. Everyday imports grant no consent; a dated opt-in in the file is kept as evidence only. Everyday imports never create fields or options [Q11], [Q16].

31. **Gift file handoff** [Q12], [Q19]. Phase 30 reads and maps the file; Phase 15 owns the draft batches, review, posting, receipts and thank-yous, treating the rows as ordinary current gifts first recorded in Asym, not as the held import kind [O50]. The File and totals step requires an expected count and total. Each payout and row carries the platform's key, so the same payout cannot be imported twice. Each row names the payer of record and the recognised person; employer matches stay separate gifts. During a Legacy Recurring Tail, rows belonging to a legacy executor are refused. A Gift file never sets Recorded outside Asym. Confirm reads, for example, "Create 3 draft gift batches: 214 gifts, $18,440.00", followed by "Nothing is posted and no receipts are sent until someone posts these batches in Contributions." Batches created from a Gift file link back to it [O94]. Phase 30 never posts money.

32. **Switch pause and effects suppression.** Between a Switch Boundary and go-live, everyday import start actions say "Paused during the Switch, from {Boundary} until go-live.", and an import waiting to start is held with that reason [Q12], [Q19]. Writes made by any import and by any import reversal fire no automations, field-change triggers, sends, welcomes or goodbyes [O78], [O89]. Switch history and Legacy Tail Backfill money is written by owner commands with Recorded outside Asym and the event flag `historical=true`, so each owner derives its existing gate at write time and no gift-triggered effect follows [Q7], [O1], [O33]. A Gift file is different: its rows are ordinary current gifts first recorded in Asym, never import-origin and never carrying Recorded outside Asym or `historical=true`, so once a person posts its draft gift batches in Contributions, Phase 15 issues their receipts and thank-yous under Phase 7/14 rules [Q4], [Q12], [O50]. Import-origin creates never apply field defaults [O76]. Within an import reversal, only Phase 15's reversal of a posted gift batch has donor-visible effects, under Phase 15's rules [Q18].

### Import reversal

33. **Import reversal mechanics** [Q18]. "Prepare reversal" appears on each committed People import, each Gift file, each website content import (Phase 23's plan) and, after go-live, each Switch run; never on the missionary self-import, Practice copies or Switch runs before go-live. Pressing it changes nothing: a background job builds one page with three groups, Can be reversed now, Needs its owner's correction (each item linking to the owner's tool) and Stays as it is (each with a plain reason); owners' finer groupings, such as Phase 23's seven, appear as reasons [O91].
    - **Who does what:** People results are reversed through Phase 9's reversal command [O87]; Gift files hand every resulting batch to Phase 15, to discard a draft batch or reverse a posted one [O90]; Switch giving history goes to Phase 13's void-and-re-adopt correction; recurring gifts and tail evidence to Phase 16; notes, interactions, files, applications and care to their owners' correction runs; fields and options a Switch created to Phase 11's Created-by-Switch list; Retired designations, restrictions, opt-outs and suppression evidence stay as they are.
    - **Checks, applied again at the moment each item changes:** a value, including a custom field value, goes back only if nobody and nothing has written that field since the import, including a later import; putting it back also restores which write it came from. Each change is one owner mutation conditioned on "the latest write to this field is still the import's write", never on value equality. Tags count as one field. Contact details, list memberships and household links the import added are removed as Entered in error only if unchanged and nothing was sent to them; an address the import ended is reopened; removing a list membership is never an unsubscribe and never records or lifts a block. People the import created are archived only if Untouched [O88]. Values on created people are corrected by importing a corrected file. Anything failing a check stays as it is, with its reason.
    - **Confirming:** the page is the confirmation, with no typed phrase and no second person; only a ministry member holding `imports.reverse` confirms; the final button names the result in plain, non-money words, for example "Restore 1,204 values and archive 37 people". A prepared page lasts 24 hours or until the window closes, whichever is first.
    - **Running:** in the background; "Stop" stops after the current batch and says items already reversed stay reversed; one People import or import reversal per ministry at a time; each import is reversed once; a stopped or failed import reversal resumes for its remaining items. There is no redo: importing the file again re-applies it, and people the import reversal archived are restored only through one explicit grouped decision in that import's plan.
    - **The window:** 30 days from commit for Everyday imports and from go-live for a Switch run, shown as an absolute date and time in the ministry's named time zone, with "in 30 days" as a hint. An import reversal confirmed before the deadline finishes, and deleting prior values waits for it. A Legal hold keeps prior values but does not extend the window. An erased person's prior values are removed, and the import reversal skips them ("Erased since") [O92].
    - **Records:** each change is a new attributed change, for example "Restored by reversal of import 12, confirmed by J. Smith"; the import's own history is never erased. The import reversal record keeps who prepared and who confirmed, when, counts and each item's outcome code, with append-only outcomes and no row content. Reversal prior values hold tenant, import, record, field, prior value and prior write, server-only. The results list is generated on demand under the viewer's permissions and never stored.
    - **Wrong file:** a file that should never have been in Asym is reversed to take it out of use, then the ministry's privacy role erases those people through the erasure path, which reaches the held file and the prior values; the import and import reversal records give the ministry its breach-log facts; if Asym staff caused it under an operator grant, Asym tells the ministry without undue delay.
    - **Words:** the action is "Prepare reversal"; the term is always "import reversal", never a bare "reversal", "Undo import", "rollback", "revert" or "restore snapshot"; buttons for people results name the result, never "reverse" or "undo"; money verbs (reverse, void, refund) stay with the money owners.
    - **Invariants:** an import reversal never deletes a person, gift, note, file or history entry; never changes money; never removes or lifts a restriction; never overwrites a later write; changes each item at most once; has no outward effects; is confirmed only by a capability holder; and reverses nothing after the window. Prior-value capture ships in the same release as the People import, and the People import never launches without import reversal [Q12], [C23].

### The Switch

34. **Switch lifecycle, authority and rehearsal** [Q1]–[Q3], [Q5], [Q6]. One tenant is fed by exactly one source installation per Switch; a second source is a separate Switch with its own source declaration, and overlaps go to match review. A Switch can be scheduled only when every Minimum Switch Kit family is committable through its owner's typed import command, every other family is committable or an accepted Visible exclusion, every legacy recurring cohort has a Phase 16 external read-only target, the readiness census is complete, and any declared residual system is accepted in writing. From the Switch Boundary Asym is the system of record and only writer for supporter identity, contact data, consent and suppression (except lists kept in the ministry's email tool), relationships, custom data, imported giving history and all staff work. The Switch Boundary governs Phase 30 classification only and never sets a Phase 16, 20 or 21 boundary. Practice runs stage the whole Switch from one set of exports, check every row through each kit owner's no-write check using its commit rules [O28], and write nothing to live records. A Practice copy is built only in the Approved Practice environment, through the same owner commands, holding only the tenant's allowlisted configuration, the existing records the plan references (keeping production IDs) and the practice run's rows; it is never promoted, nothing flows back except content-free status, and its results are rehearsal evidence only [O29], [O30]. Go/no-go needs the latest copy on the current mapping version and production release with no unresolved mismatches, and sets pause limits at measured × 1.5. Every decision is made by a ministry member holding Run the Switch, except that Asym's named legal role may also place and release a Legal hold (decision 25); the Switch lead receives notices and holds nothing by being lead. Real exports enter only through a ministry member's upload, only under Q2's LATER gates, and the Source profile status ladder (Unavailable → Pilot-qualified → Supported) applies to every profile, including the template profile. Only a Representative real export can qualify a profile; Synthetic evidence and Vendor-format samples never qualify one or meet the release's representative-migration gate. No Switch is scheduled on an Unavailable profile, and on a Pilot-qualified profile only its Design partner's Switch is scheduled until the profile is Supported. Each Switch records the share of non-empty columns matched by key or preset, Remembered mapping, the Common-header list, an accepted suggestion, by hand or left out, and the time spent mapping [Q11].

35. **Switch record families** [Q9], [Q10], [Q13], [Q14], [Q16], [Q17]. Commit order puts restrictions first: every Imported restriction commits before any Adopted email permission, scoped by what its source signal means; parties, contacts and suppression evidence commit and reconcile before Asym giving and exact continuity open; Asym checkout opens only after a verified Phase 13 Settlement Account Binding exists, and anyone who gives in Asym before the final import completes is resolved in match review; remaining families then commit with no outward effect before Switch live [Q1], [Q9]. Adopted email permissions start on Adoption hold and are created only through a published Consent adoption disposition [O42]. Households come only from explicit evidence; Combined-name records are flagged, never split; one email has one holder [Q10]. Giving history commits gift by gift through Phase 13's one adoption command only when every Fixed gift fact is known, otherwise it is a Waiting row; no default fund, date, currency, Site or entity; Legal Entity and Site come from the signed Source mapping statement; Retired designations are created by a person through the owner's command; anonymity fails closed; history never moves Field Account, payout, accounting or receipt facts [Q13], [O53], [O56], [O58], [O59]. Notes, interactions, files, applications and care go to their owners' protected homes at the Record tier the ministry chose per source category, never by reading content; care and personnel files whose sealed home does not exist stay with the ministry as a Ministry-held sealed export with their digest recorded [Q14]. Field drafts are keyed by draft ID and created all-or-none through Phase 11's batch command at the real commit [Q16], [O75]. The Old link list is staged and each row is handed to its owner; Phase 30 never holds or activates routes; old giving links are kept in place as equivalent addresses, and everything unmapped gets the Site not-found page [Q17], [O80], [O82].

36. **Tail, tax documents and closure** [Q4], [Q7], [Q8]. Gifts reaching Asym through a Switch's history import or a Legacy Tail Backfill carry Recorded outside Asym, written by the owner command in the same transaction as the money fact and never removed [O22]; Asym issues no tax document for them, and go/no-go is blocked while any population lacks a named issuer. Legacy Tail Backfills are separately reviewed, bounded plans on a fixed cadence (weekly until the last old charge, then every four weeks through the reversal window); they bring append-only money facts, schedule-state and stop evidence, and restrict-only adverse contact facts applied first; they never write supporter details or override an Asym edit; covered-through dates only move forward. No recurring gift moves on switch day; each Move is a Phase 16 move-start that charges nothing until the old gift's stop evidence exists, with no catch-up [O36]. Phase 30 never coordinates processors, transfers vaults, handles card or bank numbers or credentials, or proves an executor stopped. Opt-outs, do-not-contact and erasure requests recorded in Asym after the boundary are listed "pending in legacy systems" until a named ministry user marks each executed. The tail closes after the last old charge plus its reversal window, extended until every open dispute resolves; Switch closed then ends helper grants by default [O105]. Recording the end of the SiteStacker licence is refused, naming each outstanding item, until SiteStacker's last charge has run, the switch-off export taken after that charge is in custody, and its tax documents for gifts recorded outside Asym are issued. The switch-off export is its own Source Snapshot Manifest of the gifts, schedules and receipt evidence created after the Switch Boundary; the manifest sealed at the boundary does not meet this gate. The licence end never waits for tail closure. Once the licence has ended, processor reports are the only source for reversals of its old charges, and the old merchant, gateway and PayPal accounts are listed as staying open until the reversal window closes [Q1], [Q8]. Tail closure and decommission sign-off are both blocked while any "pending in legacy systems" item is open; decommission sign-off is a separate, later ministry-attested checklist covering SiteStacker access reduction, credential rotation, the SiteStacker data deletion or retention decision and confirmation that no Practice copy is live; it also requires the two tax-document attestations, which never block tail closure [Q1], [Q4], [Q6].

37. **Reconciliation, repeat imports and corrections** [Q1], [Q13], [Q15]. Reconciliation has two levels: every final-manifest row has exactly one disposition and accepted totals tie to export totals; and export totals tie to independent controls captured outside the files, with unavailable periods declared. Counts and native-currency totals are reported by Legal Entity × original currency × source basis, never summed across currencies, with a per-year breakdown and a History spot check of 20 random gifts and the 5 largest. Sign-off needs zero Waiting rows and no unexplained variance, binds to a report version, and records the Verify your copy confirmation; Asym never sends the raw export back. An Exact repeat import writes nothing; any other snapshot converges on Source Record Bindings, shows differences as source-changed conflicts for reviewed correction, never applies them source-wins, and reports a row missing from a later snapshot without treating it as a deletion. Before go-live, a mistake is fixed by discarding and re-running; after go-live, history is corrected only by an append-only void and re-adopt under the same binding, with its own capability.

## Testing Decisions

The founder confirmed these seams on 4 November 2026. No new test runner is introduced. Substantive behaviour is built red-green-refactor at the nearest stable seam.

1. **Primary seam: the Phase 30 server commands and queries.** The commands and queries of the Phase 30 domain module in the shared API package are tested as Vitest acceptance suites, following the repository's per-issue acceptance-suite pattern (a per-issue folder with a harness, static checks and acceptance tests). Owner modules sit behind their published interfaces; where an owner command does not yet exist, the suite uses a controlled double that honours the published contract, and the dependent kind stays off until the real owner command passes the same suite. This seam carries the deterministic matrices:
   - **States:** every import status, held-row, import reversal record and Switch-stage transition, allowed and forbidden, including terminal immutability.
   - **Permissions:** each atom and level × record visibility × clearance × narrowing; Prepare without Run; Gift-file Confirm with and without `finance:record_contribution`; View-only and no-access pages; helpers with end dates; Asym help access limits; authority lost mid-run; permission service unavailable.
   - **Counts:** every row has exactly one outcome; Review counts add up to the file's rows; counts are identical for every viewer; held rows never change counts.
   - **Idempotency:** Confirm per plan revision; row writes per source ID and plan; payout keys; Exact repeat import writes nothing; retries return the original result.
   - **Import reversal checks:** not written since (including by a later import); restoring the prior write; tags as one field; Entered in error; Untouched; created-people values; Erased since; the window, deadline and Legal hold.
   - **Retention:** each Retention class's date; Legal hold stops deletion; prior-value deletion waits for a running import reversal; erasure suppression is immediate.
   - **File reading:** CSV, TSV and XLSX through this seam with Asym-authored synthetic fixture files covering encodings, a header row below the first line, several sheets, quoted line breaks and embedded quotes, 4-byte characters, IDs in scientific notation and lost leading zeros, ambiguous dates, Blank tokens, formula-like cells, Identifier refusal, Size profile limits and the XLSX expansion ratio.

2. **Real database seam: the same commands against migrated local Supabase with real roles.** Following the existing realstack acceptance pattern, these suites prove: effective grants (no client grants on any Phase 30 table; anonymous and authenticated denied; prior values unreachable through views or remote procedures); row-level security and tenant scoping (another ministry's import is not found for a Data Tools holder); constraints (at most one confirmed import reversal per import and at most one prepared page per import at a time, one outcome per row, tenant-aware composite keys, constrained states); forward-only states that a direct update cannot bypass; the per-ministry heavy-job lock; idempotency under retries; and two-session races: two editors on one draft, two Confirms, two held-row reviewers, a plan rebuilt during Confirm, the Switch Boundary reached while an import is Waiting to start, and an import reversal racing a later edit.

3. **Journey seam: Mission Control Data Tools in Playwright.** Under the admin Playwright configuration, with axe at 1280, 768 and 320 CSS px and keyboard-only runs: a People import from the People list shortcut through results and Prepare reversal; a Gift file from Contributions to draft gift batches; the Imports list as the way back; the Switch task list; held-row review; the no-access and follow-only pages; Hide values; the segment error boundary keeping the import's identity; polite announcements and the lasting banner. This seam depends on the authenticated admin accessibility harness [O101]. Browser tests cover journeys, not backend permutations.

**What a good test is.** A good test asserts external behaviour only: the user-visible copy, statuses, counts and available actions, and the domain outcomes in owner records. It never asserts private helpers, internal table shapes beyond grants and constraints, or component internals. Fixtures are Asym-authored synthetic files with generic labels; no received file or real export is ever committed or used in a test [Q2].

**Prior art.**

- The repository's per-issue acceptance suites (for example the suites for AL-2402 and AL-2364: harness, static, route and realstack tests), run by the acceptance test command.
- The realstack tenant-guard and RLS-posture acceptance suites (AL-2250, AL-2251), which run against migrated local Supabase with real roles.
- The shared data-table accessibility end-to-end spec, and the general accessibility spec, which today runs on the donor base URL only [D81].
- The admin Playwright configuration used by the admin authentication and table-page smoke tests.
- The contribution batch processing unit tests (server chunk, claim and stale recovery). The browser-continuation tests are not a precedent for Phase 30.

**Required proofs.** Implementation is not complete until tests prove, at the stated seam:

1. Counts add up to the file's rows, every row has one outcome, and every viewer sees the same counts.
2. No import and no import reversal fires a send, automation, field-change trigger, welcome, goodbye, receipt or public effect (production-shaped test with a live automation); a Gift file's gifts are receipted only after a person posts its batches in Phase 15 [C23].
3. Nothing is written without the needed permission; each batch re-checks authority; lost authority stops after the batch; no Asym identity decides or holds a Data Tools permission, and Asym's named legal role can only place or release a Legal hold, without reading content.
4. An import reversal never overwrites a later write, never deletes, never changes money, never lifts a restriction, never writes to an erased person and reverses nothing after the window.
5. Held rows are never created as new people, never reveal the match and never change counts.
6. Downloads never include protected people; own-file downloads never include Asym-side values; every download is streamed, never stored, text-typed and formula-safe.
7. Logs, traces and job payloads carry identifiers only.
8. An Exact repeat import writes nothing; a repeated payout is refused; Confirm is idempotent; a stale plan revision is refused.
9. A Gift file never posts money, never sets Recorded outside Asym, and refuses legacy-executor rows during a Legacy Recurring Tail.
10. Every Switch history and backfill gift carries Recorded outside Asym, and no tax document is offered for it.
11. A blank cell never changes anything; Fill empty only is the default; Replace states its count.
12. Another ministry's import, or an unknown identifier, is not found for Data Tools holders; everyone else sees the no-access page, identical whether or not the import exists.
13. Every artifact is deleted on its Retention class date unless under a Legal hold.
14. The Data Tools journeys pass axe at 1280, 768 and 320 CSS px and complete keyboard-only.
15. The performance targets in Further Notes are met in a production-shaped test.
