## Purpose and research standard

D47 decides whether an independently validated Tenant attention cadence can
ever become legitimate Phase 12 business time even though a D43 holder-initiated
direct-grant review has no due date, expiry, risk transition, service level, or
no-response consequence. The founder selected Option 1. This research tests
that class of future policy against Core's governing ADRs and OpenSpec, current
repository behavior, current first-party IAM/CMS/CRM/nonprofit/work-management
documentation, accessible settings and notification guidance, PostgreSQL
temporal/concurrency semantics, and Inngest's documented execution limits.

This is an admission decision, not an implementation decision. It deliberately
does not choose a cadence value, calendar unit, send time, channel, content,
recipient override, table, field, API, event, job, or executor. It does not
weaken D46's affirmative no-reminder/no-placeholder contract. A future feature
may proceed only after representative evidence proves the root problem, the
exact bounded options and clock model receive separate approval, and the full
D46 admission pack passes.

Evidence labels are strict:

- **Repository fact:** verified in the current Core repository.
- **Verified external fact:** verified in current official first-party material.
- **Requirement inference:** necessary to preserve an already-governing Core
  boundary.
- **Product judgment:** a deliberate Core choice where evidence does not dictate
  one answer.
- **Assumption:** plausible but not established by representative ministry
  evidence.
- **Unresolved unknown:** requires research or another founder decision.

Vendor behavior is comparative evidence, never a transplanted requirement.
No vendor default establishes ministry demand, an interval, a calendar, or an
acceptable interruption rate for Core.

## Executive finding

**Disposition: Accept with required amendments.** Option 1 is a modern,
proportionate future boundary only when “validated cadence” is a narrowly
defined source policy—not a free-form reminder setting or a synonym for record
age. Two strong evidence families coexist:

- Microsoft Entra, Contentful, HubSpot, Okta, and Blackbaud usually derive
  reminders from a review window, due date, expiry, or other source lifecycle
  fact. This is the strongest alternative and establishes a high semantic bar.
- SailPoint lets the access-request source configure reminder start, count,
  frequency, time, timezone, and schedule preview; GitHub lets organizations
  schedule reminders for still-pending review requests without requiring a
  pull-request due date. These prove that a source-owned courtesy-attention
  rhythm can be a legitimate modern pattern even when it is not a deadline.

Neither family justifies building anything now. Core has no representative
evidence that D43 requests are missed, no accepted cadence, and no clock model.
The permanent path is therefore an evidence-gated policy class:

- default **Off**;
- one bounded code-owned choice only after separate approval;
- at most one reminder per exact still-current source episode;
- no **Due**, **Overdue**, SLA, escalation, recurrence, priority, access change,
  or no-response action;
- Phase 12 owns the eventual immutable policy input and occurrence;
- Phase 17 and Phase 6 separately own presentation and delivery;
- Tasks Hub remains the durable source-work projection, not the clock;
- Inngest may later execute an identifier-only wake but never owns business
  time, cancellation, permanent uniqueness, authorization, or outcome;
- D47 does not pre-decide current-request inclusion, enable/Off/change impact,
  rescheduling, clock/calendar, or recipient binding; each remains an explicit
  downstream decision, and already committed history can never be rewritten.

The UX stays absent today. If a later feature passes every gate, the control
belongs beside the Phase 12 Access-request source policy, not in generic
Notifications, Tasks, System Messages, or Workflows. One compact Base Maia
summary and one explicit editor can explain the single nudge without implying a
deadline. This creates a clean future seam while avoiding dormant schema,
calendar, scheduler, or settings debt.

## Exact corrected D47 decision

1. D47 admits only a **possible future policy class**: after independent,
   representative validation, Phase 12 may own one bounded Tenant attention
   cadence for D43 access-review requests that otherwise have no deadline or
   lifecycle consequence.
2. D47 creates no reminder now. D46's no-runtime/no-placeholder contract remains
   in force until a later founder/source decision ratifies the exact policy and
   every admission proof.
3. The future Tenant default is **Off**. Absence, unknown version, failed proof,
   corrupt state, migration ambiguity, or unsupported value also resolves to
   Off and creates no occurrence.
4. Any future non-Off values are a small, code-owned finite set supported by
   the approved evidence pack. D47 does not choose `3`, `5`, `7`, `14`, working
   days, calendar days, elapsed hours, send time, timezone behavior, or any
   other value.
5. Free-form duration, cron, RRULE, calendar builder, per-request date, per-user
   cadence, conditional rule, arbitrary offset, “custom,” recurrence, and
   Tenant-authored workflow remain prohibited.
6. An admitted policy may create **at most one** stable reminder occurrence for
   each exact Tenant and D43 request episode. Policy revision, recipient
   resolution, channel, executor, retry, and presentation are inputs/evidence,
   never identity dimensions that can mint a second reminder for that episode.
7. The reminder's meaning is only: **this exact access review is still waiting
   at the Tenant's admitted courtesy-attention threshold**. It is not a deadline,
   overdue finding, service promise, escalation, performance judgment, or risk
   classification.
8. Crossing the cadence changes no request status, grant, capability,
   EffectiveAccess, priority, assignment, coordinator authority, holder access,
   decision default, authorization epoch, or no-response consequence.
9. Phase 12 is the only owner of cadence eligibility, immutable policy heads,
   source clock interpretation, occurrence identity, cancellation,
   supersession, and source actionability.
10. Phase 17 owns only the reviewed message contract, presentation, and finite
    Delivery Steps. It cannot invent a cadence, extend it, repeat it, turn it on,
    or infer it from a channel/recipient preference.
11. Phase 6 owns communication intent, preparation, provider handoff, delivery
    evidence, ambiguity reconciliation, and transport repair. It cannot create
    source reminder eligibility or report delivery as source completion.
12. ADR-0183 Tasks Hub retains one source-backed task for the D43 work. Task age,
    due/reminder fields, engagement, manual edits, sorting, priority, comments,
    or completion cannot create, move, suppress, or satisfy the reminder.
13. ADR-0027 Notification Center may present a separately admitted reminder
    occurrence as a new attention child, but unread/read/archive behavior cannot
    create another occurrence or alter the request/task/grant.
14. D45 initial email remains a different immediate occurrence. Its Tenant plan,
    personal email preference, provider retry, replay, bounce, or delay cannot
    authorize or become a reminder.
15. Reminder, digest, escalation, reassignment, manual nudge, snooze, recurrence,
    timeout, expiry, and no-response action remain distinct source meanings and
    require separate decisions.
16. A future cadence choice can publish only through the established D44
    boundary: a trusted expected-head Phase 12 command, a current same-Tenant
    Active Tenant Assignment with `permissions.manage_grants`, and the
    registered policy-management purpose. D47 creates no new capability. Role
    labels, coordinator assignment, Tasks ownership, recipient state, System
    Messages administration, or provider administration alone grant nothing.
17. The source derives Tenant, actor, policy revision, effective instant, and
    audit attribution from trusted server context. Caller-supplied Tenant,
    actor, recipient, clock, or occurrence identity is rejected.
18. D47 does not decide whether later activation applies only to new request
    episodes, may deliberately include selected current pending episodes from a
    new baseline, or automatically includes current requests from original age.
    D48 must decide that application boundary before any value or runtime exists.
19. D47 does not decide how Off, non-Off changes, removal, current-request
    inclusion, rescheduling, cancellation, or policy replacement affects a
    not-yet-created reminder. Those exact transitions and impact-preview copy
    require the D48 decision and later lifecycle proof.
20. Automatic backfill, age-in, or rescheduling is not authorized by D47. It is
    an unresolved hazard, not a default implementation inference.
21. Whatever later application rule is chosen, a committed reminder records the
    exact source-policy revision and temporal inputs as immutable evidence.
    Policy revision is never part of reminder uniqueness and a policy change can
    never create a second reminder for the same request episode.
22. The exact temporal model—elapsed duration, Tenant-local calendar threshold,
    or working-day calendar—remains a later decision after D48. No
    implementation may assume one or name presets first.
23. Recipient binding is also later: D47 does not decide creation-time versus
    fire-time generation, zero-member behavior, reassignment impact, or whether
    one occurrence fans out to the then-current bounded cohort. Every eventual
    choice must preserve at most one reminder per request episode and fresh
    same-Tenant authorization.
24. At occurrence claim and again before every presentation or provider
    submission, Core freshly proves the source episode is still pending, the
    exact later-ratified policy/application rules permit the occurrence, and
    every resolved recipient remains current and same-Tenant authorized for the
    exact action.
25. Withdrawal, keep/remove, no-longer-applicable, Off publication, assignment
    end, recipient ineligibility, authorization loss, and every other source
    change require explicit later fence semantics before activation. Regardless
    of the selected fence, no worker may bypass the then-current source and
    authorization proof at its applicable irreversible boundary.
26. Product-database uniqueness and claims—not event IDs, task IDs,
    notification IDs, Inngest runs, or provider keys—own the permanent at-most-
    once business effect.
27. The source transaction atomically records the admitted temporal intent or
    occurrence and its identifier-only product dispatch/projection handoff.
    A crash may leave recoverable product work, never an unrecorded message.
28. If later used, Inngest may sleep, wake, scan, or reconcile only identifiers
    after product commit. Every resumed execution claims product work and
    re-reads current truth; cancellation events are optimization, not authority.
29. Missed or late execution may present only while the source says the one
    reminder remains current and useful. It never bursts catch-up reminders,
    recreates terminal work, or converts an outage into urgency.
30. No future cadence can activate until independent research validates a real
    attention problem against the no-reminder baseline, demonstrates nudge-not-
    deadline comprehension, covers current materially distinct Tenant/user
    contexts, and passes Access Product, UX Research, IAM/Security, Privacy, Accessibility, and
    Architecture review.

## Evidence synthesis

### Verified conclusions

- Cadence-only attention for pending review work is a current, real pattern in
  SailPoint and GitHub; therefore Option 1 is not inherently obsolete or an
  anti-pattern.
- Due/expiry/review-window anchoring is more common and semantically clearer in
  IAM, CMS, CRM, and nonprofit examples; therefore cadence-only admission must
  meet a higher evidence and copy-comprehension bar.
- Modern products separate initial assignment, reminder, digest, escalation,
  terminal outcome, durable work view, and transport preferences.
- Exact timezone, schedule, bounds, preview, removal, eligibility, and history
  semantics are essential when a cadence is implemented.
- One notification can be useful; repeated notifications for an unchanged item
  create fatigue and opt-out risk.
- Persistent source work is required because every notification transport may
  be delayed, disabled, suppressed, missed, or unavailable.
- PostgreSQL can enforce permanent business identity and safe claims; Inngest
  can wake execution but cannot own durable source truth or permanent dedupe.
- Core currently lacks representative D43 cadence evidence and an exact clock;
  building now would be speculative technical debt.

### Product judgments

- A truthful courtesy nudge need not imply a due date when copy and behavior
  explicitly deny deadline, overdue, escalation, and access consequences.
- Tenant-default Off is preferable to a platform-wide default because current
  evidence does not prove universal benefit and the subject is sensitive access.
- At most one reminder provides the smallest useful policy class and avoids a
  recurrence/scheduler domain.
- Bounded code-owned presets are preferable to numeric/custom/cron entry because
  they keep UX testable, supportable, localizable, and semantically reviewed.
- The source-policy editor belongs in Phase 12's People & access information
  architecture; notification settings only narrow channels after an occurrence
  exists.
- Current-request inclusion, enable/Off/change impact, and rescheduling are
  consequential lifecycle choices; leaving them explicit for D48 prevents an
  accidental backfill or silent-reschedule contract.
- No dormant artifact is the best future-ready implementation today.

### Assumptions and unresolved unknowns

- **Assumption:** some missions ministries may use part-time, volunteer, weekly,
  or distributed review rhythms. This is a sampling hypothesis, not a verified
  workflow fact.
- **Assumption:** one nudge may improve attention without unacceptable fatigue.
  This requires observed outcome evidence against the no-reminder baseline.
- **Unresolved:** which current Tenant/user contexts are materially distinct;
  the research sampling frame must derive from actual Tenant evidence.
- **Unresolved:** whether activation applies only to new request episodes,
  permits a deliberate previewed current cohort, or automatically ages in all
  current pending work; D48 decides this first.
- **Unresolved:** whether the right clock is elapsed duration, Tenant-local
  calendar time, or a working-day calendar; a later question decides the class.
- **Unresolved:** which interval choices, if any, evidence supports.
- **Unresolved:** which presentation or delivery steps should carry a future
  occurrence. D47 authorizes none.
- **Unresolved:** what useful-lateness ceiling should suppress an outage-delayed
  reminder. It must be decided with the exact cadence.
- **Unresolved:** whether a later personal preference may suppress external
  delivery while required in-product remains. Phase 17 must decide it.
- **Unresolved:** how recipient generations bind, what happens with zero
  recipients, and how later reassignment interacts with the one source
  occurrence; a later source/recipient decision must resolve it.

## Independent validation gate

“Independently validated” is a falsifiable admission gate, not an adjective.
Before a future cadence value or implementation is proposed, the evidence pack
must include all of the following:

1. **Preregistered problem statement.** Define the observed attention failure,
   affected source outcome, strongest no-build baseline, research questions,
   success measure, guardrails, exclusion rules, and decision rule before
   collecting evidence.
2. **Actual-user sampling frame.** Derive materially distinct Tenant, staffing,
   locale/timezone, device/connectivity, coordinator-count, and access-needs
   cohorts from current authorized product/customer evidence; do not invent
   ministry personas and call them facts.
3. **Inclusive iterative research.** Conduct multiple rounds with actual or
   likely source users, include disabled/assistive-technology and support-
   needing users, and continue until the predeclared questions have stable
   answers. Participant count follows the chosen method rather than a magic
   universal number.
4. **Baseline comparison.** Test D46's complete persistent Access requests lane,
   source-backed task, required in-product item, and optional initial D45 email
   before attributing a problem to missing reminders.
5. **Behavior over preference.** Establish that one nudge improves the declared
   attention/recovery outcome; “I would like reminders” or a vendor feature list
   is insufficient.
6. **Meaning comprehension.** In final-round task-based testing, participants
   must understand that the setting creates one courtesy nudge and does not
   create a deadline, overdue state, SLA, escalation, access change, or automatic
   decision. Any critical contrary interpretation blocks activation and forces
   copy/interaction redesign and retest.
7. **Fatigue and fairness guardrails.** Evaluate perceived pressure, blame,
   duplicate attention, accessibility, mobile/low-bandwidth use, localization,
   timezones, and effects on part-time/volunteer staff without using individual
   staff behavior for performance scoring.
8. **Preset evidence.** Every proposed non-Off option must be supported by the
   research and operational proof. Unsupported values are absent; they are not
   hidden, disabled, or marked experimental in production settings.
9. **Independent review.** Access Product, UX Research, IAM/Security, Privacy,
   Accessibility, and Architecture reviewers who are not solely the feature's
   proposer must approve the evidence and limits. Founder/source approval then
   chooses the exact policy.
10. **No automatic activation.** Passing research creates a decision proposal,
    not code, schema, configuration, telemetry, a beta flag, or a Tenant default.

## Source ownership and unresolved application/timing contract

### Source and immutable policy heads

- Phase 12 owns a closed policy family with explicit Off and separately ratified
  non-Off values. No other domain can add values through data or plugins.
- Each published head is immutable, Tenant-scoped, versioned, audited, and
  activated at a trusted server commit instant through expected-head concurrency.
- If a later rule evaluates or commits reminder eligibility, it records the
  exact policy revision and temporal inputs as immutable evidence. D47 does not
  decide whether that binding occurs at request creation, policy application,
  threshold evaluation, or occurrence commit.
- Policy revision is an input, not reminder identity. No policy publication,
  replacement, or reevaluation can mint a second reminder for the same request
  episode.
- A committed occurrence retains its original policy/source evidence; policy
  history is never rewritten to make a past reminder appear newly valid or
  invalid.

### Application decisions D47 deliberately leaves open

| Unresolved decision                                                  | What can go wrong if inferred                                                                   | Required next proof                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| First activation: new episodes only versus current pending inclusion | Backlog storms, surprising silence, or automatic attention for work created under Off           | D48 chooses one explicit model, exact baseline/effective instant, preview, authorization, zero-member, rollback, and no-duplicate behavior.                       |
| Current-cohort selection if allowed                                  | Partial/biased cohort, stale preview, cross-Tenant inclusion, or one request admitted twice     | Source-derived complete cohort, expected-head command, immutable baseline, count/privacy treatment, all-or-nothing application, and at-most-one episode identity. |
| Off publication                                                      | Stale reminders may fire, or historical occurrences may be rewritten                            | Later decision names which uncommitted/committed/presented/submitted boundaries Off fences and exact admin copy.                                                  |
| Non-Off value change or removal                                      | Silent reschedule, immediate age-in, skipped request, or second occurrence                      | Later decision defines current-versus-new scope, successor/cancellation evidence, and proves the same request episode can never mint another reminder.            |
| Policy-version binding time                                          | Current policy may reinterpret history or an old value may survive unexpectedly                 | Later decision selects the binding point and immutable inputs; policy version remains evidence, never uniqueness.                                                 |
| Recipient binding and zero-member handling                           | Reminder may target stale/recreated assignees, disappear, broadcast, or re-fire on reassignment | Later decision defines generation/fanout timing and current auth proof without changing the one-per-request-episode invariant.                                    |

Until these are resolved, no source calculation, preview copy, schema, schedule,
or worker may assume an answer. D48 addresses the first activation/current-
pending boundary next; recipient binding and clock semantics follow later.

### Temporal admission still required after application scope

A future feature decision must define:

- the threshold type and unit;
- whether “day” means elapsed 24-hour blocks, civil calendar days, or working
  days;
- source anchor instant and precision;
- named IANA zone, local send/eligibility time, DST gap/overlap rule, tzdb
  revision, weekends, holidays, and calendar owner when applicable;
- earliest and latest useful execution instants;
- deterministic computation and immutable evidence;
- policy/source replacement and terminal cancellation fences;
- behavior when creation occurs exactly on a boundary;
- late wake, outage, deploy, clock skew, tzdb update, restore, and replay rules;
- display in Tenant and viewer local time without changing authoritative meaning.

D47 chooses none of these. This deliberate deferral prevents an attractive UI
phrase such as “after 5 working days” from silently requiring a holiday-calendar
engine or creating inconsistent international behavior.

## Source of truth and domain invariants

1. There is zero D47 runtime/persistence/UI artifact until a later feature
   decision passes every gate.
2. One Tenant has exactly one current cadence-policy head for this source
   family; absent/unknown/corrupt resolves Off.
3. A policy value comes only from the reviewed closed code-owned registry.
4. Any eventual eligibility/occurrence records the exact evaluated policy
   version as immutable input; D47 leaves its binding time unresolved.
5. Off/change/current-request effects are unresolved until their exact lifecycle
   decision; no runtime may infer them from ordinary settings semantics.
6. One exact source episode can create zero or one reminder occurrence, never
   more.
7. Only current `pending_review` plus every later-ratified application, policy,
   recipient, authorization, and usefulness predicate can create, present, or
   deliver the occurrence.
8. The reminder creates no source status, access, permission, priority, SLA,
   decision, task, or escalation effect.
9. Reminder uniqueness is the exact Tenant plus source request episode and
   reminder meaning; policy version, recipient generation, presentation,
   channel, executor, and retry are evidence or fanout inputs and cannot mint a
   second reminder.
10. Product uniqueness survives retries, restores, replays, provider windows,
    worker replacement, and executor migration.
11. Source facts and occurrence history are authoritative; Tasks, Notification
    Center, delivery records, providers, logs, analytics, and Inngest are
    projections/evidence only.
12. Every Tenant relationship is same-Tenant composite and every tenant row is
    `tenant_id NOT NULL` with RLS; a bare identity or request ID authorizes
    nothing.
13. Actor, Tenant, source head, policy input, any later-ratified recipient
    binding, and audit attribution are server-derived.
14. Terminal state or a later-ratified suppressive fence observed before its
    defined boundary yields no new effect; provider-accepted external delivery
    is not retractable.
15. A failure in task, in-product, email, push, Slack, Teams, Google Chat, or any
    later projection cannot mutate the source or trigger a fallback effect.
16. Channel enablement/preference cannot create a reminder; source occurrence
    cannot create a channel without a separately admitted Delivery Step.
17. A digest cannot consume or replace the one reminder identity; it has its
    own source-bounded grouping meaning if later admitted.
18. Historical timestamps and policy revisions are retained according to
    governing audit/retention rules, not recalculated from current settings.

## UX/UI contract

### Current journey — intentionally no UI

- Tenant administrators see D44 coordinator routing and D45 initial-email
  delivery only. No **Reminder**, **Reminders off**, **Coming soon**, disabled
  control, cadence value, timer, or beta affordance appears.
- Coordinators keep one complete Access requests lane, one source-backed task,
  and required in-product attention. Request age does not create red/yellow
  treatment, countdown, urgency, due/overdue copy, or another task.
- Holders see the source-owned request status and authorized dates without a
  response promise or implied staff deadline.
- Operations use current source/projection/delivery repair. No D47 dashboard,
  job, metric, or alert is created.

### Future Tenant administrator journey after separate activation

The setting belongs in the existing Phase 12 **People & access → Access
requests** settings area, beside source policy and coordinator routing. It does
not belong in **System Messages**, personal **Notifications**, **Tasks**, CMS,
or **Workflows**.

The collapsed Base Maia card is quiet:

> **Review follow-up**  
> Off  
> Pending access reviews remain available in Access requests and Tasks.  
> **Manage follow-up**

One route-addressable responsive Base Maia Sheet (or the established equivalent
at implementation time) contains a single vertical form, not a wizard, modal
stack, workflow canvas, or scheduler:

> **Courtesy reminder for waiting access reviews**  
> Core may create at most one courtesy reminder while a review is still waiting.
> This does not set a due date, mark a request overdue, or change access. How it
> is delivered is managed separately.
>
> ○ **Off — do not create a courtesy reminder**  
> ○ **After [reviewed choice A]**  
> ○ **After [reviewed choice B]**

The mutually exclusive values use one labelled Base UI RadioGroup/fieldset.
The exact control—small radio list or Select—depends on the later number of
evidence-backed values; it never accepts arbitrary text, a date, cron, or
recurrence or makes the user enable a switch before choosing the cadence.
Off is explicit and remains the default for new Tenants, but the editor displays
the stored current value when revisiting rather than covertly preselecting a new
answer.

Below the field group, one impact summary states the exact application and
change behavior only after D48 and later lifecycle decisions ratify it. If D48
chooses new-episodes-only, the summary can stay quiet and state that existing
requests are unaffected. If deliberate current-cohort application is later
allowed, it requires a fresh source-derived baseline, stale-safe preview, and a
separate explicit choice rather than silently following the cadence radio. An
automatic original-age model would need to expose immediate-effect risk before
save and remains unapproved through D47. No variant may leak a private recipient
cohort, individual behavior, guessed send guarantee, or provider status.
**Cancel** and **Save changes** are explicit; save is disabled while
unchanged or pending. There is no autosave and no confirmation modal for this
reversible configuration alone; a later deliberate apply-current operation may
require a distinct reviewed confirmation because its fanout is not equivalent
to editing future policy. An inline receipt-backed status persists after save;
toast-only success is insufficient.

Only a current same-Tenant Active Tenant Assignment whose
`permissions.manage_grants` authority passes the registered policy-management
purpose sees the edit action. D47 adds no capability. Other authorized viewers
may see a truthful read-only effective summary when useful; coordinators, task
assignees, recipients, email/System Messages administrators, and broad role
labels do not inherit edit authority.

### Future coordinator journey

- The original Access requests row and source-backed task remain the primary
  work. The reminder does not duplicate or reprioritize the task.
- A separately admitted in-product occurrence, if required by Phase 17, uses
  neutral copy such as **Access review still waiting** and one source link. It
  never says **late**, **overdue**, **urgent**, or **due**.
- Opening, reading, dismissing, archiving, or following the reminder changes no
  source/task/access fact. The destination freshly authorizes and shows current
  status; stale or ended work resolves to safe current-state recovery.
- The reminder reveals no holder/requester identity, capability, protected
  provenance, reason, group, grantor, peer recipient, raw ID, or inline decision
  outside the authenticated source surface.
- A recipient manages transport-specific preferences only in personal
  Notification settings after those channels are separately admitted; the
  recipient cannot alter the Tenant source cadence from a notification or task.

### Holder and non-recipient journeys

- The holder receives no D47 reminder unless a later source decision explicitly
  defines a separate holder-facing meaning; coordinator attention is not copied
  to them.
- Holder status never displays the Tenant cadence, coordinator behavior, or a
  predicted response date.
- Donor, missionary, public CMS, and unrelated staff surfaces receive no D47
  data or badge.

### Accessibility, internationalization, mobile, and low bandwidth

- Reuse shared `@asym/ui`, Base UI behavior, Base Maia/Zinc semantic tokens,
  existing spacing/type/radius, and server-owned privileged commands.
- Use visible labels, fieldset/legend relationships, concise helper text,
  programmatic descriptions, text errors, visible focus, keyboard operation,
  forced-colors support, reduced motion, and no color/icon-only meaning.
- Meet Core's 44-CSS-pixel important-target contract, WCAG 2.2 AA, 320-CSS-
  pixel/400-percent reflow, zoom/text spacing, screen-reader reading order, and
  persistent `role=status`-appropriate save/recovery feedback.
- Localize complete sentences, not concatenated fragments; permit RTL and CJK
  wrapping and international names without truncating authoritative context.
- Display times only after a later clock decision defines their meaning. When shown, state the
  relevant date, time, and zone without changing the source instant for the
  viewer's locale.
- A low-bandwidth submit has one durable command identity, preserves the draft
  through ambiguous response, and can recover authoritative head/receipt without
  duplicate publication.

## D48 — Which pending requests may a newly enabled cadence govern?

### Why this is the next decision

Suppose Hope Mission has 12 current D43 requests created while the Tenant policy
was Off. After independent evidence and later preset approval, an authorized
administrator enables one courtesy reminder. Core still must decide whether
those 12 requests are outside the policy, can be deliberately enrolled from a
new source baseline, or are automatically evaluated from their original ages.

That decision precedes clock and recipient details. Otherwise a harmless-looking
save can create a catch-up burst, silently exclude work the administrator
expected to include, or make original `created_at` an accidental reminder
authority. Every option still preserves default Off, at most one reminder per
exact request episode, no deadline/access consequence, and the entire D46/D47
admission contract. D48 chooses application scope only; D49 will address
recipient binding and D50 will address clock/calendar semantics.

### Option 1 — new request episodes only — recommended

Enabling a non-Off cadence applies only to D43 request episodes created after
the new Phase 12 policy head commits. Every current pending request remains under
its original no-reminder admission. It cannot age in later. The editor's impact
summary states **Applies to new access reviews only. 0 existing requests will be
reminded.**

**UX/engineering impact:** one save has one predictable effect, no backlog
preview or bulk source mutation, no immediate burst, and the simplest rollback,
audit, and mixed-version behavior. Staff still have the complete Access requests
lane, Tasks Hub, required in-product attention, and any admitted D45 initial
email for current work. The cost is that an administrator cannot use the new
policy to nudge already-waiting requests.

### Option 2 — new requests by default plus deliberate Apply to current requests

The ordinary cadence save remains new-request-only. A separate secondary
**Apply to current requests** action may deliberately establish one immutable
source baseline for a complete freshly eligible current cohort. Before commit,
Core recomputes a permission-filtered preview, explains how many request
episodes would be admitted and whether any could become immediately eligible
under the later clock, requires explicit confirmation, and applies all or none
through a distinct idempotent source command. Stale preview, zero recipients,
recipient binding, clock, Off/change, rollback, and partial-fanout rules still
need later decisions.

**UX/engineering impact:** gives ministries controlled recovery for existing
work without making it an automatic side effect of the setting. It also creates
a bulk source operation, baseline identity, preview privacy, concurrency,
confirmation, migration, and repair surface that must be justified by evidence.

### Option 3 — automatically include current requests from original age

Enabling the cadence immediately evaluates every current pending request from
its original source creation time. Any request already beyond the eventual
threshold can become eligible without a separate apply-current decision.

**UX/engineering impact:** superficially simple and maximally inclusive, but it
can create an immediate cross-Tenant load/delivery burst, surprise recipients,
turn historical age into newly active policy, complicate rollback, and obscure
what the administrator's one save will do. Even a count preview cannot make the
implicit bulk mutation or original-age semantics low risk.

### Recommendation and exact question

**Recommend Option 1 — new request episodes only.** It is the clearest modern
safe default: configuration widening is prospective, current work keeps its
original source contract, and the Tenant sees an exact zero-backfill impact
statement. It avoids building D48's bulk-baseline/application machinery before
evidence proves ministries need it. If representative research later proves a
strong current-work use case, Option 2 can be considered as a separately
governed source command; Option 3 should be rejected because automatic original-
age inclusion is the least predictable and most brittle path.

**Which D48 application policy should Core record: Option 1 — new request
episodes only, Option 2 — new requests by default plus a deliberate previewed
Apply to current requests action, or Option 3 — automatically include current
requests from original age?** You may amend any option.

## Evidence limits

- SailPoint and GitHub prove cadence-only review reminders can be modern; they
  do not prove Core's ministries need one, prefer one, or share an interval.
- Microsoft Entra, Okta, Contentful, HubSpot, Jira, and Blackbaud prove stronger
  due/expiry/window patterns; they do not make a consequence-bearing deadline
  necessary for every truthful courtesy nudge.
- Vendor feature breadth is not a Core architecture recommendation. Cron,
  recurrence, arbitrary recipients, repeated reminders, escalation, timeout,
  and custom channels remain rejected.
- GOV.UK/USWDS/WCAG guidance supports research and interaction quality; it does
  not validate product demand or a cadence value.
- PostgreSQL and Inngest prove technical properties and limits only. They do not
  choose a business clock, useful lateness, recipient, content, or channel.
- The repository docs are intended design, not shipped D43–D47 behavior. Current
  finance/tasks code is explicitly nonprecedent.
- Assumptions about volunteer, part-time, weekly, international, or low-bandwidth
  ministry rhythms remain hypotheses until actual representative evidence
  verifies them.

## Final research disposition

Record D47 as **Accept with required amendments**: a bounded, independently
validated, Tenant-default-Off cadence may later become Phase 12 source policy
for at most one truthful courtesy reminder, even without a due/expiry/risk fact.
It creates no present artifact, deadline, status, access effect, repeat,
escalation, channel, or executor authority. Independent evidence, D48
application scope, D49 recipient binding, D50 clock semantics, later exact
presets, and the entire D46 admission contract are mandatory before
implementation. This is the modern, low-noise, reliable path that preserves
future capability without speculative technical debt.
