# Question 15 — Restarting giving with helpful previous values

7 September 2026. **Disposition: Accept with required amendments.** Conrad selected A. The corrected A1–A4, mapped J01–J12, C01–C22 and presentation defaults below await explicit ratification. This is completed grooming research and a decision review, not a formal specification, implementation authorization or release certificate. Q01–Q14 remain ratified; Q14 G01 remains unresolved.

## Corrected decision

When a donor deliberately opens **Restart giving** on a fully canceled recurring gift, enter the existing new-recurring-giving journey. Use its last effective agreed gift amount and frequency as editable suggestions when the source can certify them and current rules permit them. Preserve the exact eligible ministry and authorized donor context. Make the new financial terms easy to inspect and change. Final acceptance creates fresh authorization and a linked successor; the canceled gift remains canceled.

The donor should feel recognized, not rushed or persuaded. The smallest sound product is a contextual entry into the shared checkout, with a short explanation and one readable financial review. It is not another checkout, billing service, draft product or retention funnel.

### A1 — Precisely define the remembered values

Propose the source-owned accepted line terms effective at the cancellation boundary: **supported gift amount per occurrence, its exact currency, and the typed frequency**. Do not use the first-ever donation, last charged total, last successful payment, monthly equivalent, suggested fundraising amount or a future amendment that never became effective. Canceling while paused or before a first successful payment can still leave valid accepted terms; no successful payment is required merely to identify those terms.

The source must certify the relevant term identity/revision. Missing or ambiguous term evidence produces a brief explanation and current choices only after independent restart eligibility/control is proved, not reconstruction from emails, provider metadata or legacy totals. Unknown old executor/stop evidence remains fenced; a blank new form cannot bypass it. A currently ineligible value may appear as a safe historical reference, but the donor must choose a permitted current value; never silently raise the amount, round it, change frequency or convert currency. Preserve unaffected valid inputs. Switching currency deliberately enters the current owner-qualified new-gift path and requires a fresh amount choice; an old number must never retain an ambiguous dollar symbol.

This is a material clarification of “previous,” proposed for ratification. It requires no portal-owned copy of historical financial truth.

### A2 — Preserve the exact selected gift scope

The current owning action identifies the canceled line or explicit line set being restarted. Preserve every selected line's amount, frequency and visible destination separately; a group total is not a per-line amount and a shared old charge does not select its siblings. Multiple lines remain one understandable review where the owner permits that scope, with separate charges clearly explained when required.

If part of the selected set is unavailable, do not silently remove it and continue. Explain the safe, relevant limitation and let the donor deliberately revise the selected set through the same owner journey. Never infer a substitute ministry, legal donor, issuer, payer or merchant from the old gift. A donor who can read history may still lack authority to create new giving. The common personal donor needs no extra role-selection step.

### A3 — Show the new terms without carrying old instructions forward

Only amount/frequency are historical financial suggestions. The new schedule uses the canonical Phase 16 current giving timezone and today/future rules, including its twice-monthly exception. Do not copy the old date, end, pause, fee election, method default, mandate, source attribution or recovery budget. Current qualified saved methods may be offered normally; fresh authorization does not automatically require typing the card again.

The ordinary optional-end default remains visible as **Until you cancel**. If the former gift had an end date, add a concise contextual explanation that its old end date has not been carried over, with **Set an end date** nearby. Do not hide an open-ended new agreement behind “same as before.” Fee cover uses the current owning choice/policy, with its exact effect separate from supported giving.

**The inherited Phase 16 initial-gift rule is unchanged:** final authorization attempts one immediate initial contribution per disclosed compatible billing cohort, including when the continuing series starts later. Show **Today / Next gift / Then**, next three governed dates, optional end, exact currency, fees and charge count. Entry, field changes and review do not charge. Final accepted submission initiates the disclosed attempt; success is not guaranteed. For delayed bank payment, show initiation/processing rather than claiming money has arrived. This is a new-gift authorization, not Q09's ordinary non-charging save.

### A4 — Repeated entry recovers work; it does not multiply gifts

Resolve restart preparation and acceptance through the existing owner command/line locks and durable operation identity. Repeated entry, two tabs and transport retries for the same unresolved restart recover its authorized current operation/result. A different browser request key must not create a second initial charge for that same unresolved action. If an overlapping restart has materially different proposed terms, return the existing pending result/conflict for review; do not merge, overwrite or launch competing work.

After an accepted restart, the historical entry directs the donor to its linked current result/gift. A genuinely separate additional gift remains possible through a deliberate new-gift action and fresh authorization. Do not deduplicate different gifts solely by amount, ministry, method or frequency, and do not impose a permanent UNIQUE(predecessor) prohibition. The source-owned typed successor links are authoritative; each accepted command's exact predecessor-to-successor mapping is unique and immutable. A scalar convenience pointer cannot silently overwrite or conceal legitimate history. Reconcile this precise meaning in Phase 16/#813 before implementation.

A4 is a material owner-contract refinement. A1–A3 resolve proposed defaults/scope rather than silently changing accepted ownership. Existing authorization, no-debt, cancellation, current validation and honest status rules are safeguards preserved throughout.

## Adversarial check

### What could go wrong with this answer?

The wrong historical amount could be reused; a future date could conceal an initial charge today; old permissions could be revived; siblings could restart accidentally; or two tabs could create two gifts. An unavailable ministry or changed representative authority could produce a leak or unauthorized payment. These are concrete design risks, not evidence that a hosted incident occurred.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

We have no observed Asym donor study proving that remembered values improve completion. A is a product judgment supported by contextual renewal precedents. “Previous amount” requires a precise source term, and “canceled” requires proved financial-control meaning. An attached payment method is neither donor identity nor current permission. The target recurring owner is not implemented merely because its documents are detailed.

### How does this affect the whole product?

Phase 16 owns new intent, schedule, control, successor and recovery; Phase 13 owns posted money. CRM/identity owners establish who may act. Phases 7/18/19 own documents; Phases 3/6/17 own appropriate communications. Staff use the same operation result and safe repair, while missionary support views receive only authorized source projections. A restarted gift creates no newsletter enrollment, invented impact, private ministry access or fixed-pledge debt.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

The common donor sees a familiar ministry, editable amount/frequency, current payment choices and one financial review. More explanation appears only when something materially differs. Errors preserve valid work. Pending outcomes remain recoverable after refresh. There is no forced call, repeated form, retention pitch or ceremonial confirmation after every field change.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

Yes as a contextual new transaction with explicit review and recoverable outcomes. Fundraise Up documents renewal from a canceled plan; GOV.UK documents preserved-input review and change links. WCAG supports financial-error prevention and accessible status/focus. None proves the exact Asym implementation or mandates reusing historical values across sessions.

### Does this fit Asym’s existing repo and product direction?

Yes through the Phase 13/16 owning path, current identity/projection gates and exact base-maia. It does not fit a direct route-level Stripe call, mutable legacy pledge clone or hosted-portal-only target. Current issue #813 allows safe presentation prefill but leaves repeated-entry/successor behavior insufficiently precise; A4 explicitly closes that contract gap.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

Keep A with A1–A4. B—same ministry plus ordinary current checkout defaults—is credible when reconsidering the donor's budget matters more than remembered values. It adds repeated choice for a donor intentionally returning through a particular gift. A's editable values and honest new-terms review retain the convenience without pretending the old agreement is current.

## Reviewed presentation defaults

- **Layout:** reuse the canonical checkout's page/surface. The common flow is a calm single-column form with readable grouping, one primary action per stage and no nested modal checkout. Do not freeze pixel widths or add a new full-screen stepper merely for restart.
- **Hierarchy:** title, currently authorized ministry, short provenance note, amount/frequency, current schedule/payment, review. Keep explanatory copy secondary; financial effect, validation and pending state remain readable primary content. No guilt, urgency, upsell or decorative success before confirmation.
- **Fields:** compose existing shared Field, Input/InputGroup, eligible cadence controls, calendar, Card and Button components. Use actual existing Base UI APIs; do not blindly copy an upstream component demo or add local wrappers for aesthetics. Labels remain visible; errors connect to controls. Provide typed, locale-explicit date entry alongside the shared calendar, labelled **Recurring schedule starts**, with an explicit format/example and linked invalid/past-date errors. Use the current locale parser/formatter and exact source minor units rather than JavaScript floating-point arithmetic. [Date-input precedent](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/dates/).
- **Actual component semantics:** shared `CardTitle` currently renders a div, so supply real heading semantics; its name is not accessibility proof. A Button's default h-9 does not by itself establish Core's 44px touch hit area. Use the shared approved size/layout treatment. Existing FieldError announcements must not become an alert on every keystroke. These are composition requirements, not global token changes.
- **Review:** the nearest charge and new agreement remain visible, not hidden behind a tooltip. Next three dates and the named giving timezone are compact scan-friendly text. Optional explanatory detail may expand, but collapsing cannot erase edits or hide changed financial terms. Keep a clear Change action by the relevant section.
- **Feedback:** stable Skeleton for loading; inline error plus linked summary; programmatic status for pending/results. A toast is not the only durable confirmation. Re-rendered source data cannot overwrite dirty fields without an explicit conflict resolution.
- **Mobile/accessibility:** no horizontal form scrolling, obscured focus or hover-only instructions; keyboard and screen-reader completion; paste/autofill where safe; reflow at 320 CSS px, 200% text/400% zoom, supported locale/RTL and reduced-motion checks. Use Core's comfortable controls; WCAG 2.5.8's 24 CSS px rule has exceptions and is a minimum, not a recommendation to shrink payment controls. Sticky actions are optional only if they cannot obscure focused content or the software keyboard. [Target size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html), [focus](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html), [status](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html).
- **Slow networks:** keep already verified text and safe edits stable; do not reload ministry imagery to complete the task. A source outage is not “no previous gift.” Provide a bounded retry/status action and an authorized help path without guessing success.
- **Scope:** no campaign/newsletter/impact feature, automatic donor-profile change or unrelated required name/address form. Collect only fields the current owner/provider actually needs; support international names/addresses through the shared checkout rather than assuming U.S. personal donors.

Cross-row topology and authority invalidation require a transaction/finalizer/lock boundary, not a CHECK expression that queries mutable neighboring rows. [PostgreSQL constraints](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/ddl-constraints.html).

Browser roles cannot insert/update authoritative financial tables or journals. Approved read projections and commands must enforce current scope. Inspect grants, views, RPCs, Storage and Realtime independently. USING governs row visibility/selection and WITH CHECK the allowed resulting row; when omitted, PostgreSQL may inherit USING, so absence of literal WITH CHECK is not by itself proof of a vulnerability. Table owners/BYPASSRLS and privileged functions require explicit checks; FORCE RLS is not a substitute for protecting service-role paths. Immutable scope/actor fields cannot be changed by an otherwise allowed update. SECURITY DEFINER functions need restricted execution, controlled search_path and trusted actor/context, with no caller-provided ownership/approval/audit facts. [PostgreSQL row security](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/ddl-rowsecurity.html).

Tenant/Party/entity/provider scope must qualify query keys, command reads, workers, logs and caches. Public URLs contain no financial draft, email or provider secret; an opaque locator is not permission. Minimize sensitive data in logs and append only the source-required durable business history. Retention remains the owning policy; do not invent a second restart retention regime or erase legal records on a profile deletion request.

## Category review — C01–C22

Severity describes plausible impact if the control is absent; likelihood is qualitative exposure under that condition, not a measured incident rate. Each category is independently evaluated. The quoted requirement is the exact proposed language to carry forward if ratified.

### C01 — Problem validity, necessity and alternatives

**Material concern: Yes. Severity: Moderate. Likelihood: ordinary donor variation.** Remembered inputs may save effort but may also anchor a donor to an outdated budget; an entirely new restart product would solve the problem at the wrong level. Current #813 permits prefill, and external renewal journeys support contextual entry, not measured superiority. This **narrows A** to helpful editable suggestions and preserves strong B.

**Permanent prevention / exact requirement:** “Restart giving enters the canonical new-recurring-gift journey with A1's eligible previous amount/frequency as editable proposals; it does not create a separate checkout. Donors can readily change either, leave, or use ordinary current choices. No conversion/retention claim is made without evidence.”

### C02 — Brittleness

**Material concern: Yes. Severity: High for incorrect money; Moderate for friction. Likelihood: ordinary policy/history changes.** A recent payment may contain fees or refunds; a future term may never have taken effect; older frequency/currency rules may no longer qualify. P16 term history, versioned cadence and current timezone expose these distinctions. This **requires A1/A3** rather than a generic old-object clone.

**Permanent prevention / exact requirement:** “Read the certified effective accepted term at cancellation and current policy independently. Missing, stale or ineligible evidence never silently defaults to USD, Monthly, another amount or another destination. Preserve valid inputs and require a current deliberate choice for affected fields.”

### C03 — Technical debt

**Material concern: Yes. Severity: High. Likelihood: likely if the current UI/helper is reused without qualification.** Current pledges/billing and one-time checkout helpers lack the intended recurring owner; copying them entrenches another writer and different money semantics. Existing source tests show this behavior rather than cure it. This **changes the implementation basis**, not A's product choice.

**Permanent prevention / exact requirement:** “Implement restart context in the Phase 13/16 owner flow and shared UI; retire or fence incompatible legacy entry/writer paths. Do not add app-local Stripe mutations, a second command journal, a portal-owned term snapshot or a second schedule calculator.”

### C04 — Edge cases

**Material concern: Yes. Severity: High. Likelihood: recurring operational variation.** Canceled while paused, never-funded but accepted terms, expired ends, twice-monthly slots, split lines, restricted destinations, changed authority and late old payments all defeat a simple Reactivate toggle. P16 distinguishes those states and terms. This **narrows eligibility and expands the mapped journey**.

**Permanent prevention / exact requirement:** “Apply J01–J12 to single and source-qualified multi-line subjects. Keep paused Resume, cancellation-pending, ended-as-scheduled, fixed-pledge completion and initial-activation repair distinct. No successful historical payment is required to identify a valid effective agreement, but unaccepted drafts confer no restart authority.”
