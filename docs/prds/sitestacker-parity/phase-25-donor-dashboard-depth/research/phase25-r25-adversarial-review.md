## Why B survives the adversarial review

The strongest alternative, confirmation before handoff, reduces wrong-address requests but adds a mailbox switch and delivery dependency. Conrad explicitly chose the lower-friction request pattern. That is a defensible product choice because Core is forwarding an expressed request to an authorized missionary, not automatically enrolling the submitted address or emailing it. A clear proof label, minimal payload, trusted recipient and existing abuse controls preserve that distinction. They reduce consequences; they do not prove every visitor's claim true.

Mailchimp documents both single and double opt-in; its signup settings do not automatically govern integrations. GOV.UK advises minimizing disruptive confirmation loops, allowing paste/autofill and not requiring repeat email without evidence. These support B's simplicity. Their subscription or government-account semantics do not become Core authority. Kit's confirmation recommendation remains useful counterevidence about the reliability tradeoff, not a mandate to reverse B. No Asym completion, spam, retention or satisfaction uplift is claimed. [Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/help/set-signup-preferences/), [GOV.UK email addresses](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/email-addresses/), [Kit](https://help.kit.com/en/articles/2971364-the-all-important-double-opt-in).

The remaining cost is honest: typos or forged addresses can reach missionaries, and an already delivered email cannot be recalled by editing a form. The product does not hide that consequence with Subscribed or Verified labels. Missionaries handle external enrollment/removal through their actual newsletter process and rules; Core does not infer or certify that outcome.

## Resend-specific review and conflicts resolved

1. **Suppression:** current Resend documentation says it suppresses hard-bounce/complaint addresses. The old Core comment that transactional suppression does not exist is stale. Keep Core P3/P6 authority and provider suppression as separate axes; do not remove either gate or unsuppress/change regions to force delivery. These checks apply to the actual missionary receiving endpoint, not an inferred subscriber contact. [Resend suppression](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/why-are-my-emails-landing-on-the-suppression-list).
2. **Idempotency:** Resend's key window is twenty-four hours and keys are bounded to 256 characters. Core's semantic request/slot identity outlives that window. The same qualified sealed envelope can be retried only while the source/provider proof permits; possible submission plus expired key is not a safe fresh send. New publication or locale selection belongs before preparation, never an uncertainty retry. [Idempotency](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/idempotency-keys).
3. **Provider evidence:** `email.sent` is request acceptance, `email.delivered` means recipient-server delivery, and suppression/bounce/complaint are separate facts.
