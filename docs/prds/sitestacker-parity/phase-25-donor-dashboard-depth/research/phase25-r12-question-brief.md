## Concrete situation

Maria returns to her donor dashboard. She has an unfinished, still-valid email-change step. Separately, the giving owner identifies a current payment-method problem with an available donor review action. A statement is ready and a ministry has published a new permitted Update.

Only the first two are current action needs in this illustration. The statement and Update remain easy to find in their normal areas; they do not become errors, debt or required chores. This is an illustrative product scenario, not measured evidence that donors commonly have exactly two issues. A payment status or failed attempt alone does not establish that a donor action is appropriate.

## Why A is the recommendation

The broader founder direction is effortless self-service with low noise. A small current-action section supports that by answering “Do I need to do anything?” in one predictable place. Its simplicity comes from excluding informational and resolved activity, using concise explanations and retaining source-owned grouping. It does not come from hiding known unfinished work or escalating every pending state.

A also avoids needing a cross-domain “best next action” policy merely to make Home useful. Real source-proved urgency and deadlines remain meaningful in either option; the portal does not invent them. A concise section is preferable to requiring the donor to scan across several contextual regions to establish the same overview. This is a product judgment, not a vendor-proven conversion or retention claim.

The strongest alternative is B's contextual presentation, supported by Church Center's documented Overview. An earlier research candidate highlighted one action and placed the others behind an explicit link. That is a possible progressive-disclosure treatment, but if the other actions remain visible it is only emphasis within A; if hidden, it adds a discovery step and requires a lead-selection policy. It is not the strongest separate founder choice. This brief therefore presents the clearer grouped-versus-contextual fork. Domain detail pages retain their appropriate status under either option.

## Safeguards shared by the options

These preserve existing decisions; they are not additional founder questions.

1. Show only a currently authorized, source-admitted need and its permitted next step. Re-check at destination; opening Home never performs a payment, email change or retry.
2. A known pending state can require waiting, verification, review or help. Use the owner's exact next action. Do not tell a donor to repeat work while a previous result is still indeterminate.
3. Required safety presentation overrides ordinary grouped-versus-contextual layout. Use genuine source urgency/deadlines; no guilt copy, invented countdown, fundraising upsell or AI urgency score.
4. Reuse current owner projections and any applicable Phase6/17 item/group. Add no competing completion/read ledger, generic workflow or reminder sender. Only source resolution ends the need.
5. Keep original context and source-required privacy, including safe labels for restricted ministries. One cause may yield one source-defined action; similar strings never establish that grouping.
6. With no current needs, the Home attention area can be absent. A failed/unavailable source is not proof of no needs and must receive truthful scoped status without blocking the rest of Home.
7. Ministry Updates remains prominent, directly addressable and easy to reach. Receipts & statements and ordinary navigation remain available; attention does not take over the entire dashboard.
8. Use exact shared shadcn base-maia/Base UI and semantic tokens. Short text labels, accessible links, calm statuses and stable focus beat carousels, icons alone or repeated toasts. Preserve keyboard, screen-reader, mobile and slow-network behavior.
