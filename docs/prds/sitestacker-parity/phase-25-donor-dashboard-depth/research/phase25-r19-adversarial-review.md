# Question 19 — A useful welcome and ministry connection

**Disposition: Accept with required amendments.** Conrad selected A: welcome and ministry connection first when there is no giving to manage. Preserve that direction. The corrected execution, presentation defaults and bounded approach to future flexibility below await ratification.

Research date: 8 September 2026. This is a grooming review, not a PRD, formal specification or implementation ticket. Questions 01–18 remain ratified. No source implementation, canonical ADR/OpenSpec edit, GitHub mutation or live-provider action is authorized. Repository intent, inspected code, official external documentation, product judgments and unrun target proof are distinguished throughout.

## Corrected decision

> Home welcomes an already authorized account holder and makes actual permitted Ministry Updates useful before financial records are available. Use one compact welcome, familiar account navigation, a clear ordinary Make a gift action when its current capability permits it, and up to three source-qualified update previews leading to the existing reader. Omit empty financial scoreboards, invented impact and setup chores. Welcome and independently authorized content can render while financial sections are loading; absence claims require sufficient current source evidence. The same Home adds truthful giving, document and current-need sections as their owners provide them. Future flexibility comes from ordinary product-owned components consuming existing domain contracts, tenant brand and published content—not a new dashboard builder or onboarding system.

The choice concerns neutral Home in an established authorized personal context. It does not create accounts, confer personal access on a representative-only user, resolve an unmatched claim or establish that a person has never donated. A direct link keeps its already chosen authorized destination.

## Proposed adjustments — A1–A4

### A1 — Welcome is immediate utility; financial absence requires evidence

Render safe account chrome and a neutral welcome from the current authorized context. Use a qualified preferred/display name as supplied by its owner, or simply Welcome; do not split the first word from an international name, expose an email as a greeting fallback or infer a family relationship. Use one complete current Tenant Donor Account Brand revision and approved localization, not the entry Site's brand or an arbitrary provider profile. One rendered document cannot mix brand revisions; later navigation/refresh may adopt the next complete revision under P24.

Welcome does not assert that all other services loaded. Each section retains its owner's present, pending, empty, incomplete, unavailable or denied meaning. Only sufficiently current and scoped successful reads can establish no available records in that context. A zero amount, missing array, disabled query, offline request, incomplete import, expired session or unclaimed donor is not proof of never giving. Do not wait for one cross-domain atomic snapshot merely to render independent safe content.

The financial absence condition is derived presentation, not a stored `new_donor`, welcome-completed flag or cohort/marketing fact. Historical coverage may remain incomplete without blocking the neutral welcome; its exact coverage/help remains available in the owning surface. Current pending payments, accepted operations and required work must never disappear behind a first-gift invitation.

### A2 — One useful content region, with a finite preview

Use a small welcome/header, the ordinary visible Make a gift action where qualified, and one primary Ministry Updates region. Propose **up to three** readable previews followed by View all. Three is a reviewed product default, not a claimed industry optimum or server page size. Show fewer when fewer are admitted. The full reader retains its own continuation/filter behavior; Home adds no infinite feed, virtualized table or nested scroll area.

Preview each update with its safe source/ministry label, readable title, source-qualified date and short admitted excerpt. An authorized thumbnail is optional; absence of imagery is a valid layout. Titles and essential names wrap. Avoid a large decorative hero, carousel, autoplay, opaque engagement counts, giving-based rankings and duplicate placements. All filtering, audience/history eligibility, current safety and Hide apply before limiting. Use the same P22/Q02/Q07 reader and ordering, not a new personalized query over raw posts.

The exact story link opens that current permitted story only where its destination is qualified. Otherwise provide the clearly labeled general Ministry Updates destination rather than a false Read full story promise. Reading the preview creates no follow, consent, financial instruction or notification-read claim. No personal impact claim can be inferred from viewing or from unrelated sample content.

For no admitted updates, show one short neutral absence in that region. Explain a hidden-by-you cause only if the owner can safely establish it. A neutral Manage Updates link may remain useful whenever that existing preference route is authorized; its presence does not claim hidden posts exist or caused the empty result. Unavailable content gets a local unavailable/retry state, not No updates. Never restore hidden sources or widen permissions to fill the page. Familiar account navigation and the ordinary giving action remain useful without repeating several empty cards.

### A3 — Evolve one Home without onboarding or false success

Keep the same page, landmarks and navigation as actual activity appears. Current needs retain Q12 priority; actual gifts, arrangements and documents populate their normal sections. Preserve Q16/Q18's distinction between accepted submission, processing, activation and received money. A pending bank gift belongs to its truthful existing status/result, never an invitation to donate again because a receipt or settled total is absent.

Refresh the affected source sections without replacing the whole page, stealing focus, resetting the Updates reading position or reopening a welcome flow. New ordinary posts do not reorder a person mid-read; use the existing reader's refresh behavior. Restrictive changes fence further disclosure through the owner's adverse-first rules; clients immediately retire affected fields and obsolete responses when revocation is known. This is not a promise to recall information already seen or downloaded. Offline reuse must satisfy the existing owner's explicit authorization/freshness rules. Return/Back, another tab and a fresh session re-resolve current scope; a remembered page position is not permission.

No welcome dismissal, progress checklist, welcome-email/bell event, auto-follow, newsletter handoff, automatic staff outreach or special Home financial mutation is added. Existing qualified workflows keep their own side effects. Make a gift navigates to the already authorized giving journey with its current context; it does not pick the most popular campaign, assume an amount, change currency, save a method or submit money.

### A4 — Keep future flexibility small and inside current ownership

Compose ordinary product-owned sections with narrow inputs from existing owners, stable meaning, local loading/error/empty treatment and qualified destinations. New future content can be added or an existing presentation replaced without copying the page or redesigning its data owners. This calls for normal component composition and localized product copy, not a runtime plugin catalog, generic widget schema, drag-and-drop builder, stored layout JSON or customization engine.

Use existing Phase 24 account-brand fields and Phase 22 released Updates now. Core retains private portal layout, navigation, forms and security. Phase 23 public Page/Block publishing does not become authority to publish inside the private donor Home. A future tenant-editable message/layout, new announcement type or campaign section requires its own justified source/authorization/admission decision before it becomes a product capability; do not build placeholders or a configuration database in anticipation.

Concrete later possibilities include improving the Updates preview, exposing a qualified Phase 26 contextual-help entry, or composing a Phase 36 My Campaigns summary once that product and its current access contract exist. They do not become Phase 25 requirements or visible Coming soon slots. This is future-compatible execution of the present scope, not a commitment to future customization. It preserves the founder's option to amend private-layout ownership later with an explicit tradeoff. No existing owner amendment is needed merely to adopt this component composition.

## Content and state examples

The following are illustrative journeys, not observed donor studies or fabricated ministry content.

**Jamie has no available giving and two permitted updates.** Home shows Welcome, Jamie, a brief organization-purpose sentence, the normal Make a gift action and exactly those two updates. There are no USD 0 charts, General Fund impact claim or prompts to finish an account.

**Jamie has hidden every previously visible ministry.** Home honors the preference. If the source can safely establish that personal choice, the Updates region offers Manage Updates. It does not unhide anything, display hidden titles or turn on email.

**Jamie has no available updates either.** A quiet welcome and one neutral Updates absence are enough. Normal navigation and qualified giving remain. We do not manufacture a ministry directory, announcement, story or setup task merely to fill the screen.

**The financial service is unavailable, but Updates is healthy.** Jamie can read permitted Updates. The financial area gives the appropriate local status/retry; it does not say no gifts, show zero or prevent unrelated account navigation.

**Jamie submits a first visible ACH gift and returns.** The accepted operation/payment has its independently evidenced processing state. The Home adds its existing status rather than asking Jamie to make the same gift again. Settlement or activation is not inferred from elapsed time, wallet success or an empty receipt list.

**A document is available without a recent gift.** The document owner supplies that fact. Home must not suppress it because a donation array, receiptCount estimate or current-year total is empty.

**Jamie manages an organization's giving but has no personal giving context.** R04 routing remains authoritative. This design cannot create an empty personal donor, displace represented records or invite personal giving as a substitute for the represented task.

## Adversarial check

### What could go wrong with this answer?

Warm copy could mask missing money, show unauthorized content or turn into a marketing dashboard. Source-qualified section states, protected content and finite scope are required; an attractive empty screen is not sufficient.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

We do not know the distribution of neutral Home visit intentions. No records does not mean first-time donor, no available updates does not mean no posts exist, and login alone is not a personal/audience grant.

### How does this affect the whole product?

The Home composes existing identity, brand, giving, document, attention and content owners. Staff and missionary products retain their responsibilities. Public Web Studio cannot publish arbitrary private Home modules under this decision.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

The ordinary visit becomes useful with minimal reading and no chores. Healthy sections stay usable during unrelated failures; actual activity appears without changing the account's identity or forcing a new journey.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

Yes: context-sensitive useful content, stable navigation, honest absence and proportional feedback. The exact composition is product judgment; competitor upsells, broad page builders and automatic account matching are not imported.

### Does this fit Asym’s existing repo and product direction?

It fits the governing direction and requires replacement of weak legacy readiness/summary/feed assumptions. Existing code is evidence to audit, not the owner model. A4 preserves P23/P24 rather than silently amending them.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

Keep A. Ratify A1–A4, J01–J14, V1–V10 and the following safeguards. No new business owner, onboarding state or CMS private-layout authority is necessary.

## Individual category review — C01–C22

Severity describes the possible consequence of an uncorrected implementation; likelihood is qualitative exposure, not an observed production incident rate. Each category was checked independently. Exact required language follows each finding. Risks shared with predecessor work remain requirements for that owner rather than new duplicate features.

### C01 — Problem validity, necessity and alternatives

**Material concern: yes. Severity: moderate. Likelihood: plausible.** A financially empty console offers little utility; assuming donation intent instead can make a legitimate reader feel unfinished. R01 leaves composition open, and primary account/empty-state guidance supports useful next steps, not one universal CTA. **Effect: keep A, narrow scope. Required language:** “Use the welcome-first composition only as presentation in an existing authorized personal context; preserve giving and account access without requiring a financial action. Do not create onboarding or a new donor segment.” Compare both plausible intentions in comprehension testing.

### C02 — Brittleness

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: observed source assumption; target failure plausible.** The existing fixture bootstrap delay and fallback arrays can make readiness appear successful while the real owner is pending/disabled/offline. This can hide activity or encourage another donation. **Effect: change readiness contract. Required language:** “Financial absence requires current qualified owner results; timers, HTTP success with malformed data, undefined arrays and zero totals do not establish it. Welcome and content readiness remain independent.” Test delayed, paused, disabled and invalid-envelope responses.

### C03 — Technical debt

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: likely if current monolithic snapshot is extended.** Capped legacy donations/pledges and derived document counts are not complete target authority. A second Home snapshot of business truth or onboarding flag would become difficult to reconcile. **Effect: replace the assumption through shared predecessors. Required language:** “Use existing source-owned projection/service paths and shared components; do not duplicate financial summaries, document existence or content admission in Home. Retire reached legacy reads at qualified cutover.” No general orchestration framework is required.

### C04 — Edge cases

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: plausible.** Hidden Updates, a historical statement without a current gift, pending ACH, representative-only access, a refunded zero total or partial imports defeat a simple empty boolean. **Effect: require the mapped variants. Required language:** “Distinguish available records, pending work, safely known absence, incomplete coverage, unavailable and denied states at their owning scope. No records in one section implies nothing about another.” J01–J14 and the concrete case matrix must be proved without made-up data or permissions.

### C05 — Footguns

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: plausible.** A welcome CTA could preselect an unrelated ministry, restore hidden content, mark notifications read or create setup writes on page load. **Effect: restrict Home to composition/navigation. Required language:** “Home render, read, refresh and navigation create no financial instruction, follow, consent, notification engagement, audience grant or welcome-completion fact. Make a gift uses only its existing qualified journey and does not submit money.” Existing actions reached from Home keep their own explicit authorization.

### C06 — Tenant safety

**Material concern: yes. Severity: critical. Likelihood: plausible given context-insensitive cache keys.** Reused responses across principals, tenants or represented contexts can show another person's content or misdirect giving. Clearing on user ID alone misses same-user authority changes. **Effect: current-context isolation is mandatory. Required language:** “Bind each read, cache identity, result and destination to its actual Tenant/environment/principal/subject/authority context. Reject obsolete responses after a switch or revision change; no shared method/email or empty result creates a context.” Test same-user cross-context and two-user races.

### C07 — Database, RLS and authorization safety

**Material concern: yes. Severity: critical. Likelihood: target proof gap; plausible exposure.** A service-role feed or broad view can bypass audience/field/Hide admission; filtering after fetch already exposes protected rows. **Effect: require source read protection, no new schema. Required language:** “Prove tenant-aware joins, least-privilege grants, RLS, views/RPC/storage/privileged-path parity and field visibility before rows/counts/excerpts/media leave the owner. Q19 adds no authoritative table, write grant, FK/default or mutation exception.” Existing preference mutations retain their own USING/WITH CHECK/immutable-scope proof; Home cannot invent an update path.

### C08 — Overengineering

## Data and architecture: the permanent path

There is no new Home business table, onboarding lifecycle, authoritative empty boolean or new write endpoint. The browser composes typed owner responses through the existing approved hooks/collections and server read-model paths. Privileged business reads remain in `packages/api`; route handlers stay thin. A convenient composed response may carry independently qualified sections, but cannot collapse denied/pending/unavailable into empty or become a second authority for its source facts.

Cache identity and delivery must reflect the actual request context, including relevant Tenant/environment, principal, personal or represented subject, locale, source revision and authority generation.
