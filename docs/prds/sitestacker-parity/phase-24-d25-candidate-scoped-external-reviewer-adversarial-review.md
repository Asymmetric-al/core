## Final disposition

**Accept with required amendments.**

Candidate-scoped external review is the right permanent escape hatch when D23
and D24 require a different human and ordinary internal review cannot provide
one. It preserves the independence promise without forcing a small ministry to
hire or permanently onboard somebody for one decision.

The unqualified choice is unsafe. An email invitation must not become Tenant
membership, a reusable guest account, standing review authority, or a broad CMS
view. “External reviewer” must mean one verified, independent human with a
short-lived **Candidate Review Authorization Context** for one exact immutable
candidate, only when every consequence-owning source proves that external
review and a complete minimized projection are safe.

The founder's flexibility requirement is binding. Staff must be able to reuse a
private contact reference, send again, cancel, revoke, replace, or invite the
same person to a successor candidate without support intervention. Those
conveniences never reuse authority. There is one active external invitation or
accepted grant for an exact candidate—not D22's three-recipient route—and a
replacement revokes the old path before releasing the new one.

The decision is acceptable only with D25-R1 through D25-R18 below.

## Plain-language outcome

Hope Ministries' only qualified staff member, Maria, prepares a protected
French-default change. D24 says Maria cannot independently review work she
helped make. D25 lets Maria invite Eli, a trusted bilingual board member, for
that version only.

Eli sees a safe before-and-after comparison. He does not join Hope Ministries,
cannot browse its Website workspace, cannot edit, download, export, invite
somebody else, or see Giving, donors, missionaries, private notes, or finance.
Maria can resend the same invitation, cancel it immediately, replace Eli with
Ana, or invite Eli again after a new version is created. Each new candidate gets
fresh authority. Hope's current English website stays Live until the exact
source command lawfully succeeds.

## Exact corrected D25 decision

### D25-R1 — source-authorized admission only

D25 applies only when D23 proves an applicable, still-unsatisfied
different-human review obligation and D24 proves the proposed external reviewer
is absent from every substantive-participant set for the exact candidate.

Every consequence-owning source included in that candidate must, through a
versioned closed adapter:

1. explicitly admit candidate-scoped external review for the exact action;
2. identify any required qualification, jurisdiction, confidentiality, or
   internal-officer restriction;
3. produce a complete minimized synthetic projection sufficient for the exact
   decision;
4. publish compatible evaluator, projection, policy, and identity generations;
   and
5. prove that the exact final effect remains owned by its ordinary source
   command.

One unsupported, incompatible, incomplete, stale, or indeterminate source makes
external review unavailable for the whole atomic candidate. Core never omits a
protected source, substitutes a partial comparison, or silently falls back to a
broader projection.

### D25-R2 — a new Phase 12 Candidate Review Authorization Context

Phase 12 must add a server-derived discriminated **Candidate Review
Authorization Context**. It is distinct from an Active Tenant Assignment,
Public Projection Context, Service Tenant Context, Operator Tenant Grant
Context, membership, role, group, Named grant, or ordinary guest account.

The context authorizes only the exact candidate-review surface and exact review
actions admitted by the sources. It carries no ambient Tenant navigation or
capability. The Phase 12 PDP and every policy-enforcement point re-resolve it on
every protected read and action. Possessing an invitation URL, email address,
session, contact card, provider account, or prior successful review is never
the context.

### D25-R3 — Phase 4 owns invitation and identity handoff

Phase 4 owns the authority-free Candidate Review Invitation lifecycle,
intended-human binding, deliberate scanner-resistant redemption, verified
identity correlation, single-use acceptance, expiry, revocation, replacement,
and reconciliation. Acceptance atomically consumes the invitation, activates
one exact source-authorized **Candidate Review Grant**, and makes the matching
Phase 12 Candidate Review Authorization Context derivable; it does not create
Tenant membership.

Phase 17 owns only immutable invitation presentation and safe variables. Phase
6 owns communication eligibility, dispatch, provider outcome, and history.
Neither message preparation nor delivery can create, extend, accept, revoke, or
complete review authority.

### D25-R4 — exact immutable grant and context binding

The Candidate Review Grant is the immutable exact-candidate authority basis;
the Phase 12 context is its short-lived, request-verifiable authorization
projection. Each invitation revision, grant, and derived context binds at least:

- Tenant, environment, Site, locale, and consequence-owning source set;
- exact candidate, review epoch, source heads, dependency digest, participant
  digest, and safe projection digest;
- exact review action and possible public effect;
- source-admission, evaluator, policy, identity, and authorization generations;
- authorized inviter and current delegation-capability epoch;
- intended canonical stable human, or an authority-free intended-contact claim
  that must resolve to that human at deliberate acceptance;
- selected closed assurance profile, issuance instant, absolute expiry instant,
  and revocation epoch; and
- immutable invitation revision, semantic command identity, and predecessor or
  replacement reference where applicable.

No client field is authoritative for Tenant, Site, candidate, actor, invitee,
identity, independence, source admission, scope, assurance, expiry bounds,
projection, capability, state, or effect.

### D25-R5 — exactly one active external path per candidate

At most one external path may be active for an exact candidate and review
epoch. “Active” means either an issued Phase 4 invitation awaiting acceptance
or an accepted Candidate Review Grant awaiting a terminal review outcome. A
short-lived Candidate Review Authorization Context is derived from that sole
grant; it is not a second grant or cardinality escape.

This is not D22's co-responsible-recipient set. Do not import its maximum of
three. Internal review routing and one external recovery grant are different
domains. A sole authoritative command and database-enforced active-head
invariant prevent a second active external human, including under retries,
aliases, replacement races, or mixed invitation/context states.

### D25-R6 — saved reviewer contacts are convenience, never authority

After a person completes trusted identity verification, authorized staff may
deliberately save a private Tenant-scoped reviewer contact containing only the
minimum reusable identity/contact reference and safe display label. A
staff-entered address alone never creates the saved contact. Selecting a saved
contact pre-fills a new invitation; it never creates membership, eligibility,
delivery consent, current identity assurance, review authority, or access to any
candidate.

The contact remains separately deletable or correctable. Deleting a contact
does not rewrite history or silently revoke an active invitation; staff use the
explicit cancel/revoke action. Revoking an invitation does not require deleting
the contact. Contacts never copy across Tenant, environment, Site clone,
template, import, export, transfer, or backup restore into active authority.

### D25-R7 — only one proved independent human

The intended invitee and accepted reviewer resolve to Phase 12's canonical,
stable, non-reassignable human principal. Shared mailboxes, groups, bots,
services, support identities, impersonation, **View as**, anonymous links, and
email possession alone cannot satisfy D24.

Invitation issue may begin from a contact address, but favorable acceptance and
every protected request require compatible identity proof. D24 independence is
re-proved at acceptance, each projection read, and the final action. Alias or
principal merges union history; identity uncertainty, split, mismatch, or a
new participant conflict ends favorable access immediately.

### D25-R8 — source-owned minimized synthetic projection

Each source owns a versioned external-review projection adapter. The composed
projection contains only what a qualified human needs to understand the exact
candidate: safe current/proposed/unchanged meaning, protected actions and
warnings, affected Site and locale, dependency consequences, validations, and
the exact possible effect.

The bundle may include exact tenant-authored candidate content intended to
become public when the source explicitly classifies it as safe for this review;
“synthetic” never means replacing the actual proposed public wording with a
fake approximation. It contains no raw production record, CMS document,
unrestricted diff, staff or
participant roster, donor, missionary, member-care, precise protected location,
private note, credential, payment, Giving, Legal Entity, Stripe, settlement,
bank, ledger, accounting, unrelated Site/locale, or unrelated draft. A source
that cannot safely express the complete decision externally does not admit
D25. The D25 layer references source evidence and digests; it does not copy
source truth or become a write authority.

### D25-R9 — small fixed reviewer action surface

The Candidate Review Authorization Context may permit only:

- identity verification and secure entry;
- the exact read-only projection;
- one typed, non-public feedback envelope admitted by the source; for
  `changes_requested`, D30 fixes this to one required plain-text explanation
  plus zero or one source-certified anchor and creates no taxonomy or thread;
- **Request changes**;
- **Complete review**, or one consequence-led equivalent admitted by the
  source; and
- **Decline**.

It never permits editing, direct source reads, source search, unrelated
navigation, re-delegation, inviting, membership management, role selection,
download, print-optimized bulk disclosure, export, API token issuance, offline
completion, or selection of another candidate/effect. If a future source admits
tracked suggestions, D24's proposer/accepter attribution and handoff rule still
apply; D25 does not create an editing loophole.

### D25-R10 — invitation, delivery, acceptance, review, and effect stay separate

Core must present and persist five different facts:

1. the invitation was authoritatively issued;
2. a Phase 6 communication was requested and has its own truthful outcome;
3. the intended human deliberately accepted after identity proof;
4. an authorized review result was committed; and
5. the consequence-owning source performed or did not perform the public
   effect.

Provider acceptance is not inbox delivery. Delivery is not acceptance.
Acceptance is not review. Review is not public activation unless the named
source contract deliberately combines the review and exact effect in one
atomic command. Lost responses reconcile from authoritative local state.

### D25-R11 — closed, separate lifecycles and terminal behavior

Do not force contact, invitation, grant, authorization context, delivery,
review, and public effect into one mutable status enum. Their closed dimensions
are separate:

| Dimension              | Nonterminal/current facts                                 | Terminal or unfavorable facts                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Saved contact          | active, needs correction                                  | inactive, removed; always non-authoritative                                                                                                              |
| Phase 4 invitation     | issued                                                    | accepted/consumed, expired, cancelled, revoked, replaced, candidate-superseded, indeterminate                                                            |
| Candidate Review Grant | prepared but unreleased, active after verified acceptance | changes-requested, completed/consumed, declined, expired, revoked, replaced, candidate-superseded, identity-ineligible, source-ineligible, indeterminate |
| Phase 12 context       | current for one active grant/request                      | stale, expired, revoked, absent, indeterminate                                                                                                           |
| Phase 6 delivery       | not requested, queued, provider accepted                  | failed, suppressed, indeterminate; never authority                                                                                                       |
| Source review/effect   | available, confirming authoritative outcome               | changes requested, declined, completed, stale/concurrent loser; public effect remains separately source-owned                                            |

Terminal invitation and grant states never reopen. A new attempt appends a
successor invitation/grant with a new revision and fresh proof. Candidate or
dependency mutation ends the old path; nothing silently rolls forward to a
successor candidate.

### D25-R12 — bounded, flexible assurance and expiry

D25 does not freeze one universal seven-day lifetime, one universal step-up
method, or one arbitrary Tenant-authored policy language. Phase 12 and each
source contribute compatible closed assurance and expiry constraints. The
strictest applicable requirement wins.

A versioned implementation manifest must define code-owned assurance profiles,
absolute minimum/maximum lifetimes, and allowed default or narrower presets per
source/action class. A Tenant posture may select only an admitted profile or a
shorter/stronger setting; it cannot choose infinite access, weaken a source, or
author executable authorization rules. The staff UI uses a safe recommended
default and reveals another admitted choice only when it has a real use.

Exact server time controls expiry on every request. Opening, reading, resending,
requesting changes, or staff engagement never extends authority. After expiry,
staff issue a fresh invitation. D26—not D25—now closes the bounded three-state
Tenant/Site posture governing when staff may choose external review.

### D25-R13 — flexible self-service recovery without widening authority

Authorized staff can act immediately while the path is nonterminal:

- **Send again** requests another Phase 6 communication occurrence for the same
  invitation, identity, and expiry; it creates and extends no authority.
- **Cancel invitation** ends an unaccepted path immediately.
- **Revoke access** ends an accepted context immediately and invalidates an
  already-open session on its next protected operation.
- **Replace reviewer** atomically ends the old invitation/context before
  releasing one successor for the newly selected human.
- **Invite for the new version** may preselect the previous private contact but
  creates a fresh invitation, projection, expiry, assurance proof, and review
  context.

These actions require current source visibility, the exact delegation
capability, Phase 12 step-up where required, compare-and-swap on the active
head, semantic idempotency, and body-free audit. No database repair or support
impersonation is the normal recovery path.

### D25-R14 — final action is one atomic re-proof

The final source command re-proves in one short deterministic transaction:

- trusted Tenant, environment, Site, locale, candidate, review epoch, action,
  and expected head;
- every current source head, dependency, candidate, participant, projection,
  policy, evaluator, identity, and authorization generation/digest;
- current invitation/grant/context state, absolute expiry, revocation, replacement,
  intended stable human, D24 independence, assurance, step-up, and exact
  capability;
- the source's external-review admission and projection-completeness proof; and
- semantic idempotency meaning and every ordinary source-effect precondition.

One compare-and-swap winner writes one immutable review receipt and ends the
Candidate Review Authorization Context. Same-key/same-meaning retry returns the
same effect. Same key with different candidate, reviewer, digest, action, or
meaning rejects. A failed proof creates no favorable receipt and no public
effect. No provider or other network call occurs inside the transaction.

### D25-R15 — scanner-safe, leak-resistant web handoff

The emailed URL is an opaque authority-free handoff reference. GET, HEAD,
prefetch, link scanning, preview generation, refresh, browser back/forward, and
provider callbacks cannot accept an invitation or review a candidate. A
deliberate same-origin action starts Phase 4 identity proof.

Use HTTPS, allow-listed origins and redirect targets, high-entropy single-use
redemption material stored safely, strict referrer policy, no-store response
and CDN behavior, no third-party trackers or session replay, bounded rate
limits, safe host handling, and uniform missing/expired/revoked/mismatched
responses. Protected content and bearer material never enter URLs, analytics,
logs, support exports, notification previews, or email bodies.

### D25-R16 — no automatic nagging or surveillance

Issuing an invitation deliberately requests its initial communication. D25
creates no recurring reminder, cadence, due date, SLA, escalation, automated
replacement, email-open tracking, presence indicator, or “unread by Eli”
surveillance. Staff may deliberately use **Send again**.

Any in-product attention for authorized staff is a privacy-safe projection over
the source state under ADR-0027. Reading it never completes, extends, revokes,
or reopens the invitation, and unresolved actionable attention cannot be hidden
through archive or dismissal. External reviewers receive no generic
notification center or standing dashboard.

### D25-R17 — privacy, retention, and audit remain purpose-limited

Ordinary staff see the intended reviewer's safe display identity, invitation
state, exact expiry, delivery status where Phase 6 can truthfully prove it, and
lawful recovery actions. External reviewers see the inviting Tenant/site label,
exact candidate purpose, permitted actions, expiry, and minimized projection.

Durable business history retains opaque stable actor/source/candidate keys,
closed reason/state codes, digests, timestamps, and causal references. It does
not retain projection bodies, unrestricted email content, names, mailbox
engagement, donor/missionary facts, or credentials. Contact data follows a
bounded Tenant policy; security/audit evidence follows its separately governed
policy. Offboarding or erasure pseudonymizes display data while preserving the
minimum non-reassignable conflict and audit evidence required for integrity.

### D25-R18 — no effect expansion and no generic guest product

D25 grants no Tenant membership, standing source authority, Page or
Communications permission, publication power beyond the exact admitted source
command, or future-review eligibility. It creates no external-user directory,
marketplace, workflow builder, reviewer pool, calendar, chat, generic guest
workspace, custom ACL language, or automatic routing engine.

Invitation, acceptance, feedback, decline, expiry, replacement, or review alone
changes nothing public unless the exact source command atomically owns that
effect. D25 never enables Giving; changes a Giving URL; redirects Giving intent;
selects or changes Legal Entity, Stripe, settlement, bank, currency, accounting,
ledger, contribution, receipt, or payout identity; or exposes donor or finance
data.

## Problem validity and strongest alternatives

The root problem is real. A protected different-human rule without a lawful
external path can indefinitely block a solo or small Tenant. The external
reviewer pattern is established in Core's governing Phase 12/17 direction and
in comparable collaboration and approval products.

The strongest simpler alternative is to require full internal onboarding. It
has less special-purpose code but imposes standing access, administrative work,
and potentially a paid/licensed user merely to decide one candidate. The
strongest more-flexible alternative is a standing external reviewer or reusable
guest account. It reduces repeated invitations but creates durable privilege,
stale-access cleanup, accidental disclosure, and hidden routing. A reusable
private contact gives the helpful convenience without reusing authority.

| Choice                                      | Best argument                                      | Core consequence                                                               | Disposition                     |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| Internal staff onboarding only              | Smallest special-purpose authorization surface     | Hard block or broad standing access for solo ministries                        | Keep as fallback                |
| Candidate-scoped external reviewer          | Exact least privilege and a real small-Tenant path | Safe with source admission, bounded context, and excellent self-service UX     | **Accept with amendments**      |
| Standing external reviewer/guest membership | Fast recurring review and familiar portal behavior | Stale privilege, membership sprawl, cross-candidate disclosure, cleanup burden | Reject and replace with contact |
| Same-human/admin exception                  | Fastest immediate recovery                         | Destroys D23/D24 independent-human guarantee                                   | Reject                          |

Google Drive's visitor sharing proves that verified, expiring, revocable
external access can be understandable, but its file-wide seven-day session is
broader than Core needs. Microsoft Entra separates external identity,
assignment, expiry, and removal but its access-package machinery is excessive
for one review. GitHub outside collaborators and Salesforce Experience Cloud
show the operational burden and standing-access risk D25 avoids. Blackbaud
Grantmaking's external reviewer categories establish a nonprofit approval use
case, not a safe Core authorization design. These are comparisons; Core's
repository contracts remain authoritative.

## Complete staff UX/UI contract

### Journey 1 — Core explains the safe dead end

When internal review cannot currently complete and D25 is source-admissible:

> **Independent reviewer needed**
>
> Everyone currently able to review helped prepare this version. Invite one
> trusted person to review only this change. They will not join your
> organization.
>
> Your current English website remains live. Giving is unchanged.

Actions:

- **Invite an external reviewer**
- **Keep current version**
- **Return to a reviewed version**, only when the source proves a compatible
  safe target.

Do not use a disabled **Complete review** button as the only explanation. Do
not call this an error, emergency, conflict of interest, or staffing failure.

If any source cannot safely support D25:

> **External review is not available for this version**
>
> One or more protected sources cannot safely provide a complete comparison
> outside your organization. Your current website remains live.

Show only lawful internal or safe-baseline actions. Never recommend broadening
permissions merely to make a button appear.

### Journey 2 — choose or reuse one person

Use one Base Maia sheet or dialog with progressive disclosure:

> **Invite an external reviewer**
>
> **Reviewing**
>
> hope.org · French (Canada)
>
> Make French (Canada) the website default
>
> They will see only a protected comparison for this version. They will not
> join Hope Ministries or see other Sites, donor data, Giving, or finance.
>
> **Reviewer**
>
> [Search saved reviewers or enter an email address]
>
> Eli Ramos — used previously by Hope Ministries

Only one external reviewer can be active. There is no **Add coverage reviewer**
control. Search is scoped to private contacts visible to the current authorized
Tenant staff. Entering a new address can offer an unchecked **Save after they
verify their identity** convenience, with a short privacy explanation. An
unverified or failed invitation never pollutes the saved-contact picker.

Do not require a job title, organization chart, role, permission bundle,
priority, due date, justification essay, typed confirmation, custom message, or
review workflow. If sources admit more than one assurance or expiry preset,
show the recommended choice first and place alternatives under **Review access
settings** with plain consequences. Never expose raw capability or policy names.

### Journey 3 — review before sending

The confirmation shows:

- the exact intended person and address;
- Site, locale, candidate summary, and possible effect;
- what the reviewer can and cannot see or do;
- the exact localized expiry date, time, and zone;
- any extra identity step in plain language;
- the current public behavior that stays unchanged; and
- whether the contact will be saved separately.

Primary action: **Send invitation**. Secondary action: **Back**. Submission is
idempotent and shows an in-progress state without allowing repeated competing
issuance.

### Journey 4 — truthful invitation management

After local issuance:

> **Review requested**
>
> Eli Ramos
>
> Can review only this version · Access ends 4 September at 17:00 ICT
>
> Your website is unchanged.

Actions shown according to current authority and state:

- **Send again**
- **Replace reviewer**
- **Cancel invitation**, before acceptance
- **Revoke access**, after acceptance

Do not show “opened,” “online,” presence, unread state, or guessed inbox
delivery. A truthful delivery failure says:

> **Invitation created, but the email could not be sent**
>
> Eli has not been told about this review yet. Your website is unchanged.
>
> **Send again** · **Replace reviewer**

### Journey 5 — replacement is fast and explicit

**Replace reviewer** opens the same selector, prefilled with the current person
and a warning:

> Ana will replace Eli. Eli's invitation and review access will end before
> Ana's invitation is created.

One confirmation atomically revokes Eli and issues Ana's successor. If issuing
Ana fails, Eli remains revoked and the UI truthfully offers retry or another
person; it never leaves two active reviewers or silently resurrects Eli.

### Journey 6 — decline, expiry, revocation, and new version

Decline:

> **Eli cannot review this version**
>
> No reason is required. Choose another reviewer when you are ready.
>
> **Choose another reviewer**

Expiry:

> **This invitation expired**
>
> Eli no longer has access. Send a fresh invitation when you are ready.
>
> **Invite Eli again** · **Choose another reviewer**

Candidate change:

> **This invitation ended because the version changed**
>
> Eli cannot see or review the new version.
>
> **Invite Eli to the new version** · **Choose another reviewer**

The previous private contact may be preselected, but staff see the new impact
summary and deliberately send a fresh invitation.

### Journey 7 — completion is consequence-led

After a valid review:

> **Review completed by Eli Ramos**
>
> The reviewed version is exact and current.

If the source combines review with a public action, say exactly what happened:

> **French (Canada) is now the website default**
>
> hope.org now opens French (Canada). Existing language-specific and Giving
> URLs are unchanged.

If review and activation are separate, show **Ready for authorized staff to
make default**, not **Published**. D25 never guesses the source outcome.

## External reviewer UX/UI contract

### Step 1 — minimal invitation

The message contains the Tenant's proved presentation identity, inviting human,
safe purpose, exact expiry, support/recovery route, and opaque handoff. It
contains no protected candidate body, participant names, donor or missionary
facts, or generic Tenant navigation.

### Step 2 — scanner-safe deliberate entry

Opening the link is inert. The page says:

> **Review Hope Ministries' French website change**
>
> Maria invited you to independently review one protected version. You will see
> only this comparison. You are not joining Hope Ministries.
>
> Access ends 4 September at 17:00 ICT, or sooner if the version changes.
>
> **Continue securely**

Only a deliberate action starts the compatible Phase 4 identity proof. Email
possession alone is not displayed as full identity or independence proof.

### Step 3 — purpose-bound review

The review surface uses text-first **Current**, **Proposed**, and **Unchanged**
sections, followed by source-owned warnings, validations, and exact possible
effect. Diff meaning cannot rely on red/green, strike-through, animation, or
side-by-side layout alone. The candidate locale remains exact; interface
language may follow the reviewer's preference. Core never machine-translates
protected content and calls it authoritative.

Actions:

- **Request changes**
- **Complete review**, or the exact consequence-led label
- **Decline**

There is no editor, Tenant sidebar, Site browser, download, export,
re-delegation, or generic account dashboard. A final confirmation restates the
exact effect and unchanged boundaries. The effectful action requires current
assurance/step-up and an online server round trip.

### Step 4 — safe state change while open

If candidate, dependency, identity, participation, source admission,
authorization, revocation, replacement, or expiry changes, remove the effectful
action and replace protected content with a safe terminal explanation. Never
leave a stale projection visible merely because the browser tab was already
open.

### Accessibility, mobile, language, and resilience

- Use `@asym/ui`, Base Maia/Base UI, Zinc semantic tokens, and existing Core
  field, sheet, dialog, status, and confirmation patterns.
- Prove WCAG 2.2 AA, 320 CSS-pixel and 400% reflow, 44 CSS-pixel targets, visible
  unobscured focus, keyboard-only use, screen readers, forced colors, high
  contrast, reduced motion, RTL/bidi isolation, CJK, combining characters,
  long names, and translated expansion.
- Keep identity, expiry, error, and status messages programmatic and polite;
  never steal focus or rely on toast alone.
- Preserve staff form input across network failure. Do not optimistically issue,
  replace, revoke, accept, or complete.
- The normal external surface is the low-bandwidth text-first surface. No media
  download is required to decide. Final review cannot queue offline.
- GET/HEAD, browser preload, scanner, and assistive-technology traversal are
  inert; accessibility does not require unsafe activation.

## Domain invariants

1. D25 exists only for an exact applicable D23/D24 independent-human
   obligation.
2. Every included consequence-owning source affirmatively and compatibly admits
   external review.
3. One unsupported or indeterminate source blocks the entire external path.
4. One exact candidate/review epoch has at most one active external invitation
   or accepted Candidate Review Grant; contexts derive only from that grant.
5. Invitation issue and acceptance do not create Tenant membership.
6. A saved contact is private convenience and grants no authority.
7. Every favorable reviewer resolves to one canonical stable human absent from
   the complete D24 participant union.
8. Shared, service, support, impersonated, **View as**, and anonymous identities
   never satisfy independent review.
9. The projection is complete for the decision and minimized against every
   unrelated fact.
10. Every protected projection read re-proves the current authorization
    context.
11. Resend never extends authority or expiry.
12. Replacement revokes the predecessor before the successor is released.
13. Candidate, source-head, or protected-dependency change ends old access.
14. Revocation and expiry deny at request time without waiting for cleanup.
15. Terminal invitation and grant states never reopen; a stale/revoked context
    cannot be refreshed without a current grant.
16. One valid final source command creates at most one business effect.
17. Invitation, delivery, acceptance, review, and public effect remain separate
    facts.
18. Every actor, scope, identity, time, state, assurance, and digest is derived
    from trusted server context.
19. Unknown identity, admission, projection, state, generation, or dependency
    never yields favorable access.
20. Site clone, locale copy, import, export, transfer, restore, or template use
    copies no active invitation or authority.
21. Durable audit is append-only and privacy-minimized; corrections roll
    forward.
22. D25 never widens Page, Communications, Site, Giving, or financial authority.
23. Current public behavior remains unchanged through pending, failed,
    declined, expired, revoked, replaced, superseded, or indeterminate states.

## Conceptual database, RLS, authorization, and concurrency contract

Exact table and RPC names remain a design decision, but the model must
conceptually retain:

- an immutable Phase 4 Candidate Review Invitation revision and closed state
  event lineage;
- one authoritative candidate external-review active head/slot;
- intended contact claim and accepted canonical stable-human reference;
- exact Tenant/environment/Site/locale/source/candidate/review-epoch keys;
- source, dependency, participant, projection, evaluator, policy, identity,
  assurance, authorization, and revocation digests/epochs;
- an immutable exact Candidate Review Grant revision/head and Phase 12
  Candidate Review Authorization Context derivation evidence;
- a separate minimal Tenant-private saved contact reference;
- separate Phase 17 prepared-content and Phase 6 communication references;
- one immutable source-owned review receipt; and
- identifiers-only business history, security audit, and technical correlation.

Do not store one mutable JSON permission bundle, candidate body, participant
list, source document, or email-delivery status as review authority.

### Structural constraints and indexes

- Every relationship preserves the same Tenant, environment, Site, source,
  candidate, and review epoch through composite keys or an equivalent
  database-enforced invariant.
- A unique active-head constraint plus the sole command enforces zero or one
  active external path per exact candidate across issued and accepted states.
- Unique invitation revision, intended canonical human, semantic command,
  acceptance, replacement edge, context, and review-receipt constraints make
  retry deterministic.
- Closed-state and temporal checks forbid active rows with terminal causes,
  expiration before issuance, accepted context without accepted identity, or
  successor release before predecessor revocation.
- Restrictive deletion protects audit and causal evidence. Contact erasure and
  pseudonymization cannot delete integrity keys needed to prevent identity
  reuse or replay.
- Index every foreign key and candidate-head, active-state, intended-contact,
  canonical-human, expiry, revocation, replacement, source-admission,
  projection-digest, participant-conflict, communication-correlation,
  reconciliation, and semantic-idempotency lookup.

### Grants, RLS, and privileged paths

- Browser roles cannot insert/update/delete invitation authority, identity
  binding, authorization contexts, source admission, projection proof, or
  review receipts directly.
- Internal staff reads require current Tenant/Site/source visibility plus the
  exact delegate-review capability; saved contacts have a still-narrower
  management/read policy.
- External reads occur only through a purpose-bound policy-enforcement point or
  security-barrier projection for the current Candidate Review Authorization
  Context. The reviewer cannot enumerate rows or infer another Tenant,
  candidate, contact, participant, or invitation.
- RLS defines both `USING` and `WITH CHECK`; an allowed row cannot be moved to a
  different Tenant, Site, candidate, intended human, state, or expiry.
- Explicit grants are least privilege. Table ownership and migration roles are
  not application roles. Security-definer functions use a fixed safe
  `search_path`, fully qualified objects, and trusted server-derived actor/scope.
- RPCs, views, workers, service-role paths, Payload hooks, imports, support,
  repairs, migrations, caches, and test utilities preserve the identical
  boundary. No “trusted backend” bypass may mint or widen a context.

### Transactions, concurrency, and idempotency

Issue performs one local authoritative transaction and appends an outbox intent;
the external delivery provider is never inside that transaction. Acceptance
atomically consumes the one-time handoff, resolves the canonical human,
re-proves D24, changes the Phase 4 head, activates the exact Candidate Review
Grant, and makes the matching Phase 12 context derivable. Replacement locks the
active slot, revokes invitation/grant/context and open sessions, then releases
one successor; if successor release fails, the old path stays revoked and no
second path exists.

Use a documented lock order: candidate/source head, external-review slot,
invitation head, identity/participant proof, authorization context, then review
receipt. Final review performs the D25-R14 compare-and-swap. No provider call or
unbounded projection compilation occurs while locks are held.

Same semantic key and meaning returns the existing business result. Same key
with different candidate, contact, reviewer, assurance, expiry, projection,
action, or effect rejects. Lost issue, accept, cancel, revoke, replace, decline,
request-changes, or complete responses reconcile by semantic command identity.

### Scale and execution bounds

One active external path prevents fan-out but does not justify unbounded
directory scans, projection graphs, or identity resolution. Use keyset/bounded
contact search, batched source adapters, digest-addressed projection artifacts,
and indexed request-time reproof. Every admitted source must publish a complete
bounded manifest and production-shaped performance budget. Timeout, missing
page, `limit + 1`, partial adapter response, or budget breach is indeterminate;
it never returns a favorable partial projection.

## Required future OpenSpec amendment

The active outbound-communications delta's one-sentence delegated-review
allowance is directionally correct but not implementation-safe. Before any D25
implementation or key activation, active normative specifications must include
the equivalent of:

> **Requirement: external review uses one source-admitted candidate-scoped
> authorization context**
>
> When an exact candidate has an applicable, still-unsatisfied different-human
> obligation, the system MAY offer external review only if every included
> consequence-owning source explicitly admits external review and supplies a
> complete compatible minimized projection. An authorized, stepped-up human
> MAY issue one authority-free Phase 4 invitation for one intended stable human
> and exact candidate. Deliberate verified acceptance SHALL create one Phase 12
> Candidate Review Authorization Context and SHALL NOT create Tenant membership,
> standing capability, source edit access, export, re-delegation, or access to
> another candidate. At most one external invitation or accepted Candidate
> Review Grant SHALL be active per candidate/review epoch; any authorization
> context SHALL derive from that sole grant. The reviewer SHALL remain absent from
> the complete D24 participant union. Candidate, dependency, identity,
> participant, source-admission, authorization, revocation, replacement, or
> expiry changes SHALL end favorable access. Invitation issue, Phase 6
> delivery, acceptance, review, and source effect SHALL remain separate facts.
> The final source command SHALL re-prove every bound fact atomically. A failed
> or indeterminate proof SHALL leave the current public state unchanged.

> **Requirement: staff convenience never reuses authority**
>
> Authorized staff MAY retain a private minimal reviewer contact and SHALL be
> able to resend, cancel, revoke, replace, or issue a fresh successor invitation
> without support intervention. A saved contact and resend SHALL grant and
> extend no authority. Replacement SHALL revoke the predecessor before
> successor release. Assurance and expiry SHALL use compatible versioned
> code-owned/source-owned bounds rather than one universal duration or
> Tenant-authored authorization language. D25 SHALL create no recurring
> reminder, deadline, SLA, external-reviewer pool, generic guest workspace,
> Giving, or financial effect.

Required normative scenarios:

1. **Source admission succeeds:** every included source admits D25 and provides
   a complete compatible projection; authorized staff can issue one invitation.
2. **One source blocks:** one included source requires an internal officer or
   cannot safely project; D25 is unavailable for the whole candidate.
3. **One active path:** an issued invitation prevents a second active external
   invite for the same candidate.
4. **Accepted grant occupies the same slot:** acceptance cannot coexist with
   another invite for the candidate; its context derives from that sole grant.
5. **Saved contact grants nothing:** saving or selecting Eli creates no read,
   membership, capability, invitation, or context.
6. **Resend extends nothing:** another communication is requested but invitation
   identity and expiry remain unchanged.
7. **Replacement is revoke-first:** concurrent access by predecessor and
   successor is impossible.
8. **Reinvite is fresh:** a successor candidate may prefill Eli but requires a
   new invitation, projection, expiry, assurance, and acceptance.
9. **Canonical-human independence:** an alias resolving to a D24 participant is
   rejected despite a different email.
10. **Shared identity rejected:** group mailbox, service, support, impersonation,
    or anonymous link cannot accept.
11. **No membership:** acceptance yields Candidate Review Authorization Context
    only and no Tenant Assignment or ordinary navigation.
12. **Projection minimized:** reviewer can decide the exact candidate and cannot
    enumerate or reveal unrelated protected data.
13. **GET is inert:** scanners, previews, prefetch, refresh, and back/forward
    never accept or decide.
14. **Delivery is independent:** local issue plus failed email remains truthful,
    active, and recoverable without claiming delivery.
15. **Decline is terminal:** no reason is required and staff can issue a fresh
    successor invitation.
16. **Expiry is request-time:** exact server-time expiry denies an open session
    without waiting for cleanup.
17. **Revocation is immediate:** the next protected operation in an already-open
    session denies.
18. **Candidate mutation supersedes:** old projection and action disappear; no
    invitation rolls forward.
19. **Final race loses safely:** any bound digest/epoch change defeats the final
    command and creates no public effect.
20. **One winner:** concurrent duplicate completion yields one receipt/effect.
21. **Effect separation:** completed review does not claim activation unless the
    exact source command owns both atomically.
22. **No reminders:** only deliberate initial send and deliberate resend create
    communication occurrences.
23. **Cross-scope poison denied:** browser, RPC, view, worker, service, Payload,
    support, repair, import, and migration paths deny cross-Tenant/Site/candidate
    access.
24. **Giving/finance unchanged:** invitation, review, and every failure state
    leave Giving and financial identity untouched.
25. **Accessible resilient UX:** staff and external journeys pass Core's
    accessibility, responsive, localization, weak-network, and low-bandwidth
    requirements.

The implementation-bearing change must also replace D24's weaker
latest-material-editor wording as required by the D24 review. D25 cannot be
activated on top of a favorable but incomplete participant rule.

## ADR-0181 disposition and rationale

**ADR-0181 is accepted:**
[Source-authorized candidate-scoped external review](../../../docs/adr/0181-source-authorized-candidate-scoped-external-review.md).

Unlike D24, D25 introduces a new cross-platform authorization context and a
hard-to-reverse security boundary spanning Phase 4 invitation/identity handoff,
Phase 12 PDP/PEPs, source-owned synthetic projections and final commands, Phase
17 content, Phase 6 delivery, CMS/operational reference boundaries, and public
effect fencing. It is surprising without context that a human outside a Tenant
can perform one protected action without membership, while a reusable contact
grants nothing. Alternatives—Active Tenant Assignment, standing guest
membership, signed bearer-only link, or per-source bespoke ACL—carry materially
different security and operational consequences.

ADR-0181 records, and implementation must preserve:

1. Candidate Review Authorization Context as a Phase 12 discriminated context,
   not membership or a Named grant;
2. Phase 4 invitation ownership and atomic accepted-context creation;
3. source-authorized, reference-not-copy synthetic projection boundaries;
4. one active external path per candidate;
5. saved-contact and resend non-authority;
6. request-time expiry/revocation and revoke-first replacement;
7. invitation/delivery/acceptance/review/effect separation;
8. the final source-owned CAS and no provider call in transaction;
9. deny-by-default source admission, rollout, kill switch, and migration; and
10. the precommitted re-evaluation trigger if a future product needs standing
    external collaboration or more than one external reviewer.

The ADR does not authorize implementation by itself; the future active
OpenSpec, design, source manifests, tickets, tests, and release evidence remain
required.

## Ruthless adversarial review — all 22 categories

### 1. Problem validity, necessity, and alternatives

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Core could build a guest-review product for cases
  ordinary internal review already solves, or make external review so narrow
  that solo Tenants still need support.
- **Why it matters:** The first adds exposure and maintenance; the second leaves
  the root small-Tenant dead end unsolved.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Likelihood:** Medium.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** Phase 12 and Phase 17 already identify one-person
  Tenant external review. Google, Entra, Salesforce, GitHub, and Blackbaud show
  real external-collaboration demand but also the standing-account burden.
- **Decision effect:** Narrows Option 1 to a source-admitted exact-candidate
  recovery kernel with self-service reuse and replacement; D26 now closes when
  it is offered beyond recovery.
- **Permanent fix:** Implement D25-R1 through R18; retain internal onboarding and
  safe-baseline restoration as lawful alternatives; reject same-human bypass.
- **Exact language to add:** “D25 SHALL be available only for an exact D23/D24
  obligation and source-admitted candidate; it SHALL NOT create a generic guest
  collaboration product.”

### 2. Brittleness

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Email delivery, identity correlation, source adapter,
  projection, expiry, candidate freshness, or final effect can fail
  independently; conflating them creates stuck or falsely completed states.
- **Why it matters:** Staff cannot diagnose or safely recover, and reviewers may
  see stale content.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Likelihood:** High.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** ADR-0027 separates presentation from source state;
  Phase 6 separates delivery; provider and network outcomes are inherently
  non-atomic.
- **Decision effect:** Amends the choice with closed lifecycle, five separate
  facts, resend/replace/revoke, and source-owned final CAS.
- **Permanent fix:** D25-R10 through R14 plus reconciliation by semantic command
  identity and safe terminal pages.
- **Exact language to add:** “Invitation, communication, acceptance, review,
  and public effect SHALL remain independently recoverable facts.”

### 3. Technical debt

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** A per-source guest ACL, CMS invitation table, bespoke
  auth link, duplicate delivery state, or mutable permission JSON would create
  parallel identity and authorization systems.
- **Why it matters:** Future sources would drift and security fixes would need
  repetition across seams.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Likelihood:** Medium.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** Core already assigns identity/invitation to Phase 4,
  PDP to Phase 12, presentation to Phase 17, transport to Phase 6, and source
  effects to their owners.
- **Decision effect:** Changes implementation shape, not product outcome.
- **Permanent fix:** One deep Candidate Review Authorization Context kernel,
  typed source adapters, reference-not-copy evidence, normalized records, and
  ADR-0181.
- **Exact language to add:** “Sources SHALL integrate through versioned
  admission/projection/final-action ports and SHALL NOT implement local guest
  roles or copy invitation authority.”

### 4. Edge cases

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Wrong address, alias collision, shared mailbox,
  decline, bounce, expiry, candidate edit, identity merge, source-policy change,
  replacement race, contact deletion, Tenant transfer, locale mismatch, or
  reviewer death/unavailability can strand or misauthorize.
- **Why it matters:** These are realistic small-ministry cases, not exotic
  attacks.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Likelihood:** High.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** External identity and asynchronous delivery introduce
  independent temporal/state axes; D24 identity is monotonic.
- **Decision effect:** Requires exact terminal causes and fresh-successor UX.
- **Permanent fix:** D25-R7, R11, R13; preserve current Live state and private
  contact prefill without authority reuse.
- **Exact language to add:** “Every decline, expiry, revoke, replacement,
  supersession, identity conflict, and source ineligibility SHALL have one
  terminal state and one lawful fresh-successor path.”

### 5. Footguns

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Staff may mistake saved contact, email delivery,
  resend, or prior success for current access; a developer may extend expiry on
  resend or release a replacement before revoking the predecessor.
- **Why it matters:** Ordinary convenient actions could silently grant standing
  or overlapping authority.
- **Severity:** Critical.
- **Likelihood:** Medium.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** Comparable products commonly model external people as
  reusable accounts; OWASP treats emailed tokens as sensitive single-use,
  expiring handoffs.
- **Decision effect:** Materially amends the flexible UX.
- **Permanent fix:** Explicit non-authority contact copy, same-invite resend,
  revoke-first replacement, one active slot, exact confirmation.
- **Exact language to add:** “Saved contact and resend SHALL grant and extend
  nothing; replacement SHALL revoke the predecessor before successor release.”

### 6. Tenant safety

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Contact search, projection cache, invitation token,
  source reference, or restored data can leak across Tenant, environment, Site,
  locale, or candidate.
- **Why it matters:** External review exposes unpublished protected meaning and
  could reveal sensitive ministry context.
- **Severity:** Critical.
- **Likelihood:** Medium.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** Core's platform boundaries require Tenant isolation;
  ADR-0029 warns against copied operational/CMS truth.
- **Decision effect:** Does not invalidate Option 1; requires structural scope
  and hostile tests.
- **Permanent fix:** Composite same-scope relationships, non-enumerating PEP,
  cache keys containing every scope/generation, and no clone/import authority.
- **Exact language to add:** “Every invitation, context, projection, contact,
  cache, and receipt relationship SHALL preserve Tenant/environment/Site/
  candidate scope structurally.”

### 7. Database, RLS, and authorization safety

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Mutable states, missing `WITH CHECK`, broad `anon`
  reads, service-role bypass, caller-selected identity, or separate invitation
  and context active rows can move or duplicate authority.
- **Why it matters:** UI checks cannot protect direct database/RPC/worker paths.
- **Severity:** Critical.
- **Likelihood:** Medium.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** Supabase RLS does not replace grants; PostgreSQL
  uniqueness/constraints and short transactions are required; current runtime
  has no D25 substrate.
- **Decision effect:** Requires conceptual schema and sole mutations before any
  surface ships.
- **Permanent fix:** D25 database contract above, explicit grants, `USING` plus
  `WITH CHECK`, fixed `search_path`, privileged-path parity, final CAS.
- **Exact language to add:** “Only trusted server commands MAY mutate invitation
  or authorization truth; every path SHALL enforce the same source admission,
  identity, independence, scope, expiry, and revocation checks.”

### 8. Overengineering

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Core could build reviewer pools, multiple external
  coverage reviewers, custom workflows, arbitrary security policy, guest chat,
  calendars, reminders, or a generic portal.
- **Why it matters:** Speculative machinery obscures the one needed recovery and
  adds privacy/operations burden.
- **Severity:** Medium.
- **Likelihood:** High.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** D22's three-person route is tempting but belongs to
  internal responsibility, not external authority. Entra's access packages are
  intentionally broader than D25.
- **Decision effect:** Narrows flexibility to convenience and bounded profiles,
  not more authority.
- **Permanent fix:** One active reviewer, fixed lifecycle/actions, saved
  contacts, typed source adapters, and no generic guest product.
- **Exact language to add:** “D25 SHALL NOT import D22's co-responsible set or
  create a reviewer pool, workflow language, recurring reminder, or standing
  workspace.”

### 9. UX/UI and user friction

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Re-entering trusted people, waiting for an expired
  link, unclear scope, dead disabled buttons, technical language, or support-
  only correction will make staff seek unsafe workarounds; reviewers may fear
  they joined the Tenant.
- **Why it matters:** Safe controls succeed only when the lawful path is clear
  and faster than broad onboarding.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Likelihood:** High.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** Google visitor sharing provides verify/remove/reuse

- **Decision effect:** The founder's flexibility clause becomes normative.
- **Permanent fix:** Complete journeys above; private contact reuse;
  resend/cancel/revoke/replace/reinvite; one primary action; clear expiry and
  no-membership copy; mobile/accessible text-first review.
- **Exact language to add:** “Every nonterminal staff state SHALL show the
  current public behavior, exact external scope, exact expiry, and one lawful
  self-service next action.”

### 10. Source of truth, ownership, and domain invariants

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Invitation or email status could become review truth;
  D25 could copy CMS content; contact preference could become identity or
  authority; review receipt could become public effect without source proof.
- **Why it matters:** Dual ownership creates drift and unsafe replay.
- **Severity:** Critical.
- **Likelihood:** High.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** ADR-0027 and ADR-0029 explicitly separate projections
  and reference-not-copy boundaries; Phase owners are already named.
- **Decision effect:** Requires the ownership table and invariants as binding.
- **Permanent fix:** Each owner writes only its fact; cross-domain records use
  immutable references/digests and current reproof.
- **Exact language to add:** “Invitation, identity, authorization, projection,
  communication, review, and public effect SHALL retain their named owners and
  SHALL NOT override one another.”

### 11. Hidden coupling

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Site locale, CMS source, Phase 4 auth, Phase 12 PDP,
  Phase 17 content, Phase 6 delivery, and D19 attention could become one large
  orchestrator or depend on provider-specific fields.
- **Why it matters:** A change to email, auth, or one source could break every
  review path.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Likelihood:** Medium.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** The repo's platform-boundary and deep-module rules
  require owner-domain commands and typed seams.
- **Decision effect:** Changes architecture, not the founder outcome.
- **Permanent fix:** Versioned ports, local authoritative transactions plus
  outbox, provider-neutral states, reference-not-copy projections.
- **Exact language to add:** “D25 SHALL orchestrate owner-domain commands by
  typed versioned references and SHALL NOT import provider or CMS internals into
  authorization.”

### 12. Failure modes

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Local issue may succeed while email fails; acceptance
  response may be lost; revocation may race a tab; replacement issuance may
  fail after revoke; review may race candidate change; source effect may fail
  after review.
- **Why it matters:** Ambiguous success can disclose stale work or falsely
  change public state.
- **Severity:** Critical.
- **Likelihood:** Medium.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** External transport is non-atomic; OWASP requires
  server-side validation; Core source commands already use CAS/idempotency.
- **Decision effect:** Requires safe intermediate and terminal states rather
  than a single success Boolean.
- **Permanent fix:** Outbox, semantic idempotency, reconcile endpoints,
  revoke-first replacement, request-time checks, source-owned atomic final
  action, current Live fallback.
- **Exact language to add:** “Every partial or ambiguous operation SHALL
  reconcile from local authority; no failed proof SHALL create review or public
  effect.”

### 13. Lifecycle, temporal correctness, concurrency, and idempotency

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Two invitations, two acceptances, replayed links,
  expiry worker lag, concurrent replacement/completion, clock boundaries, or
  same key with changed meaning could violate one-reviewer/one-effect
  invariants.
- **Why it matters:** Individually valid requests can jointly create overlapping
  access or duplicate effects.
- **Severity:** Critical.
- **Likelihood:** High.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** Invite and review are multi-step distributed flows;
  cleanup jobs cannot be authorization boundaries.
- **Decision effect:** Materially amends D25 with one active slot, absolute
  server time, closed transitions, lock order, semantic keys, and one CAS
  winner.
- **Permanent fix:** D25-R5, R11-R14 and concurrency tests for every competing
  transition.
- **Exact language to add:** “Authorization SHALL evaluate expiry/revocation at
  request time; one candidate slot and final CAS SHALL select one active human
  and one business effect.”

### 14. Data integrity risks

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Duplicate aliases, orphan contexts, stale projection
  digests, contact/invitation conflation, broken replacement links, or restored
  terminal rows can produce incorrect eligibility/history.
- **Why it matters:** Staff may invite the wrong person or Core may approve the
  wrong candidate.
- **Severity:** Critical.
- **Likelihood:** Medium.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** D24 requires canonical identity and immutable lineage;
  cross-domain retries require explicit causal keys.
- **Decision effect:** Requires normalized revisions/events and reconciliation;
  no favorable legacy inference.
- **Permanent fix:** Unique/composite/check constraints, restrictive deletion,
  immutable digests, explicit predecessor/successor, restore fencing.
- **Exact language to add:** “Restore, import, repair, or replay SHALL NOT
  resurrect or duplicate terminal invitation/context authority.”

### 15. Security and privacy risks

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Forwarded links, scanners, token/referrer/cache leaks,
  email takeover, enumeration, invite spray, unsafe projection, logs, exports,
  screenshots, or staff surveillance can expose protected work or grant access.
- **Why it matters:** External reviewers are outside ordinary Tenant trust and
  unpublished ministry information may be highly sensitive.
- **Severity:** Critical.
- **Likelihood:** Medium.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** OWASP requires random single-use expiring tokens,
  trusted origins, no-referrer, uniform responses, and rate limiting;
  Salesforce recommends private external defaults.
- **Decision effect:** Does not reject D25 but makes source minimization and
  scanner-safe verified entry release blockers.
- **Permanent fix:** D25-R7, R8, R15-R17; threat model; data minimization;
  content-free observability; incident kill switch. Core cannot prevent
  screenshots and must not claim DRM.
- **Exact language to add:** “Possession of a link or mailbox SHALL NOT alone
  authorize review; every protected read/action SHALL re-prove the intended
  stable human and current purpose-bound context.”

### 16. Scalability and performance risks

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Unbounded contact search, invite spray, synchronous
  projection fan-out, per-request N+1 identity resolution, or broad cache
  invalidation may degrade with large Tenants/multi-source candidates.
- **Why it matters:** Slow review encourages retries, duplicate sends, and
  abandonment; partial timeout cannot become favorable.
- **Severity:** Medium.
- **Likelihood:** Medium.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** One active external path bounds authority, not source
  graph or contact cardinality.
- **Decision effect:** Adds production-shaped budgets and bounded adapters
  without speculative sharding.
- **Permanent fix:** Keyset search, indexed lookups, digest-addressed projections,
  batched manifest, code-owned abuse limits, fail-closed execution bound.
- **Exact language to add:** “No timeout, pagination truncation, partial adapter
  result, or cache miss SHALL be interpreted as a complete favorable proof.”

### 17. Operational burden

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Staff may repeatedly type reviewers, wait for support
  to correct addresses, or require direct database repair; stale guest accounts
  may accumulate.
- **Why it matters:** Small ministries have the least administrative capacity.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Likelihood:** Medium.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** Entra documents lifecycle cleanup for standing guest
  users; D25 can avoid those accounts while retaining convenience.
- **Decision effect:** Strengthens founder's flexibility requirement while
  retaining one active reviewer.
- **Permanent fix:** Private saved contacts; self-service resend/cancel/revoke/
  replace/reinvite; truthful status; no standing account; reconciliation tools.
- **Exact language to add:** “Ordinary invitation correction and recovery SHALL
  be available to currently authorized staff without support impersonation or
  direct database mutation.”

### 18. Observability and auditability gaps

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** “Sent,” “accepted,” “reviewed,” and “live” may collapse
  into one status; investigators may lack identity/projection/candidate proof,
  or telemetry may leak content and email.
- **Why it matters:** Staff cannot trust the UI and incidents cannot be traced
  safely.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Likelihood:** High.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** ADR-0027 separates presentation/engagement/source;
  Phase 6 owns delivery evidence; NIST audit controls require attributable
  events.
- **Decision effect:** Adds separate timelines and identifiers-only evidence.
- **Permanent fix:** Closed reason codes, correlation IDs, immutable business
  events, security audit, technical traces, privacy scanning, staff-safe status.
- **Exact language to add:** “The product SHALL display and audit invitation,
  delivery, acceptance, review, and public-effect states separately without
  protected bodies or unrestricted personal data.”

### 19. Dependency and integration risks

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** Auth/email provider outage, token lifetime cap,
  webhook disorder, email client scanning, provider schema change, CMS adapter
  drift, or source-policy change can appear to grant/revoke/complete access.
- **Why it matters:** Providers do not own Core authorization or public truth.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Likelihood:** Medium.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** Phase 4/6/17 already separate provider mechanics from
  product truth; Google/GitHub/Salesforce models are vendor-specific and broader.
- **Decision effect:** Requires provider-neutral states and pinned adapters.
- **Permanent fix:** Core-owned invitation/grant/context/source state; versioned
  provider adapters; signed/replayed webhook handling; same-identity recovery;
  source fences.
- **Exact language to add:** “No provider response, callback, mailbox event, or
  CMS hook SHALL directly create Candidate Review Authorization Context or
  source review completion.”

### 20. Migration, rollout, and upgrade risks

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** New code may issue contexts before every source/PEP
  understands them; old code may ignore revoke; existing contacts may be
  inferred as reviewers; rollback may resurrect grants; new data may make schema
  rollback unsafe.
- **Why it matters:** Mixed versions can expose protected candidates across the
  rollout blast radius.
- **Severity:** Critical.
- **Likelihood:** Medium.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** No D25 runtime exists; this is a new cross-platform
  context requiring coordinated additive deployment.
- **Decision effect:** Requires source-by-source deny-default activation and an
  ADR before build.
- **Permanent fix:** Additive schema; inert context kind; compatible manifests;
  feature fence; no favorable backfill; kill switch; forward-only repair;
  revoke-capable rollback.
- **Exact language to add:** “A cohort SHALL issue no external invitation until
  Phase 4, Phase 12 PDP/PEPs, every source adapter, Phase 17, Phase 6, and
  observability declare one compatible generation.”

### 21. Testability, traceability, and proof

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** “Scoped,” “verified,” “safe,” “flexible,” and
  “immediate” may remain subjective; tests may assert rows rather than
  user-visible/source outcomes; artifacts may contradict one-active scope.
- **Why it matters:** A plausible UI can hide a broad authorization flaw.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Likelihood:** High.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** Current OpenSpec has only one sentence and no D25
  lifecycle or hostile scenarios.
- **Decision effect:** Requires the exact criteria below and traceability through
  glossary, ADR, OpenSpec, design, tickets, code, tests, and release evidence.
- **Permanent fix:** Positive, negative, boundary, RLS, concurrency, migration,
  accessibility, usability, privacy, and production-shaped tests at public
  seams.
- **Exact language to add:** “D25 SHALL NOT activate until every acceptance
  criterion is independently evidenced at the owning public seam and traced to
  one compatible release manifest.”

### 22. Other development hazards

- **Material concern:** Yes.
- **What could go wrong:** A reviewer may be independent but lack language,
  professional, legal, denominational, regional, safeguarding, or source-
  specific competence; external disclosure may violate a source restriction;
  staff may assume review prevents screenshots or collusion.
- **Why it matters:** Identity independence is not subject-matter qualification
  or confidentiality guarantee.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Likelihood:** Medium.
- **Evidence/reasoning:** Phase 17 distinguishes locale-capable review; sources
  own consequence and admissibility. No product can prevent out-of-band copying
  or collusion.
- **Decision effect:** Narrows claims and makes source qualification/admission
  explicit; it does not invalidate candidate-scoped review.
- **Permanent fix:** Closed source qualifications, clear staff copy, minimized
  projection, no DRM/collusion claims, internal/safe-baseline fallback.
- **Exact language to add:** “D25 proves current system-visible identity,
  independence, authorization, and exact-candidate scope; it SHALL NOT claim
  expertise, independent thought, confidentiality beyond product controls, or
  prevention of screenshots/collusion.”

## Acceptance criteria and proof suite

### Admission, scope, identity, and independence

1. **D25-AC001:** A D23/D24 different-human candidate with every source-admission
   proof offers external review to authorized staff.
2. **D25-AC002:** An ordinary candidate without a different-human obligation
   does not create a D25 requirement.
3. **D25-AC003:** One source that prohibits external review blocks the entire
   atomic candidate.
4. **D25-AC004:** One missing, stale, incompatible, or indeterminate source
   adapter blocks the entire candidate.
5. **D25-AC005:** A source requiring an internal officer does not accept a
   merely external independent human.
6. **D25-AC006:** A source-required language or professional qualification is
   re-proved without exposing restricted details.
7. **D25-AC007:** Authorized stepped-up staff with exact delegation capability
   can issue; ordinary editors, route recipients, and participants cannot.
8. **D25-AC008:** Invitation authority does not grant review authority, and
   review authority does not grant invitation authority.
9. **D25-AC009:** The invitee resolves to one canonical stable human before
   favorable access.
10. **D25-AC010:** A second email or account for a D24 participant remains
    ineligible.
11. **D25-AC011:** Identity merge that makes the reviewer a participant ends
    favorable access immediately.
12. **D25-AC012:** Identity uncertainty, split, mismatch, or correlation-version
    incompatibility blocks.
13. **D25-AC013:** Shared mailbox, group, service, support, impersonation,
    **View as**, bot, or anonymous identity cannot accept or review.
14. **D25-AC014:** A prior external reviewer has no authority over a later
    candidate without a new invitation/grant/context.
15. **D25-AC015:** Acceptance creates no Active Tenant Assignment, membership,
    group, role, Named grant, or ordinary Site access.

### One-active invariant, contact reuse, and lifecycle

16. **D25-AC016:** A candidate has zero or one active issued invitation/accepted
    Candidate Review Grant, never two; any context derives from that sole grant.
17. **D25-AC017:** A second concurrent issue loses the active-head CAS and
    creates no authority or duplicate email intent.
18. **D25-AC018:** Acceptance occupies the same one-active slot and cannot race a
    second invitation into existence.
19. **D25-AC019:** D22's maximum-three recipient behavior is absent from the D25
    external-review surface and contract.
20. **D25-AC020:** Saving a reviewer contact creates no invitation, delivery,
    identity assurance, membership, or authority.
21. **D25-AC021:** A saved contact is visible only to authorized staff in the
    exact Tenant and never to another Tenant or external reviewer.
22. **D25-AC022:** Selecting a saved contact starts a fresh server proof; prior
    success is not reused as eligibility.
23. **D25-AC023:** Deleting a saved contact does not rewrite immutable invitation
    history or silently revoke active access.
24. **D25-AC024:** Explicit revocation works whether or not the contact remains
    saved.
25. **D25-AC025:** Site clone, locale copy, import, export, template, Tenant
    transfer, or environment promotion copies no active invitation/grant/context.
26. **D25-AC026:** Decline is terminal, needs no reason, and exposes a fresh
    choose-another-reviewer action.
27. **D25-AC027:** Exact server-time expiry denies without a cleanup job.
28. **D25-AC028:** Opening, reading, feedback, and resend do not extend expiry.
29. **D25-AC029:** Expired, revoked, cancelled, replaced, superseded,
    identity-ineligible, and source-ineligible states never reopen.
30. **D25-AC030:** A successor invitation has a new revision, proof, projection,
    assurance, expiry, and semantic identity.

### Resend, cancel, revoke, replace, and reinvite

31. **D25-AC031:** **Send again** creates one new Phase 6 communication
    occurrence for the same invitation and no new authority.
32. **D25-AC032:** Duplicate resend with the same semantic key creates no
    duplicate communication intent.
33. **D25-AC033:** Resend preserves intended human, candidate, assurance, and
    absolute expiry.
34. **D25-AC034:** **Cancel invitation** makes an unaccepted link/context
    unusable on its next operation.
35. **D25-AC035:** **Revoke access** denies an already-open accepted session on
    its next protected read/action.
36. **D25-AC036:** **Replace reviewer** revokes predecessor invitation/grant/context
    before successor release.
37. **D25-AC037:** Concurrent predecessor access and successor issue never
    produce overlapping favorable contexts.
38. **D25-AC038:** Successor issue failure after revoke leaves zero active
    reviewers and a truthful retry state; it never resurrects the predecessor.
39. **D25-AC039:** Lost replacement response reconciles the predecessor and
    successor states by semantic command identity.
40. **D25-AC040:** A new candidate may prefill the prior contact but shows a new
    impact summary and requires deliberate fresh send.

### Projection, authorization, and privacy

41. **D25-AC041:** The projection contains every source-declared fact needed to
    decide the exact action.
42. **D25-AC042:** A missing protected dependency or projection fragment blocks
    instead of producing a partial review.
43. **D25-AC043:** The projection contains no donor, missionary, member-care,
    private-note, credential, Giving, payment, Legal Entity, Stripe, bank,
    settlement, ledger, accounting, unrelated Site/locale, or unrestricted
    participant data.
44. **D25-AC044:** External reviewer cannot enumerate Tenants, Sites, Pages,
    candidates, users, contacts, participants, invitations, or source records.
45. **D25-AC045:** Reviewer cannot edit, re-delegate, invite, change route,
    manage membership, download/export, issue API tokens, or navigate a generic
    Tenant workspace.
46. **D25-AC046:** Every projection read re-proves current context, intended
    stable human, D24 independence, expiry, revocation, candidate, and source
    admission.
47. **D25-AC047:** Projection digest or source-head mismatch removes protected
    content and effectful actions.
48. **D25-AC048:** Request changes and decline disclose no private participant
    roster or unavailable internal staffing.
49. **D25-AC049:** Logs, metrics, traces, analytics, errors, support views, and
    exports contain no projection body, bearer, unrestricted email, or protected
    personal data.
50. **D25-AC050:** Saved-contact retention/erasure and durable security/audit
    retention remain separate and independently enforceable.

### Scanner-resistant handoff and communication separation

51. **D25-AC051:** GET, HEAD, scanner, prefetch, link preview, refresh,
    back/forward, and provider callback cause no acceptance or review.
52. **D25-AC052:** Only deliberate same-origin action starts identity proof.
53. **D25-AC053:** Tokens are high entropy, safely stored, single-use for
    redemption, time bounded, and bound to intended human/candidate.
54. **D25-AC054:** Host-header injection, open redirect, wrong origin, wrong
    environment, and forwarded-host confusion are rejected uniformly.
55. **D25-AC055:** Referrer, browser cache, CDN cache, service worker, analytics,
    and third-party-resource tests leak no bearer or protected body.
56. **D25-AC056:** Known, unknown, expired, revoked, replaced, and mismatched
    invitation responses resist account/candidate enumeration.
57. **D25-AC057:** Per-contact, per-actor, per-Tenant, and global rate limits
    prevent invite and redemption spray without weakening lawful recovery.
58. **D25-AC058:** Local invitation issuance plus email failure remains an
    issued invitation with truthful failed/unknown communication state.
59. **D25-AC059:** Provider acceptance is not shown as inbox delivery or human
    acceptance.
60. **D25-AC060:** Email delivery, bounce, click/open, reply, or webhook never
    creates review completion or public effect.
61. **D25-AC061:** No recurring reminder, deadline, SLA, auto-replacement,
    presence, or unread surveillance is created.

### Final review, source effect, concurrency, and idempotency

62. **D25-AC062:** Final action re-proves every D25-R14 fact in one short source
    transaction.
63. **D25-AC063:** Candidate or dependency mutation concurrent with final action
    defeats or stales the review and creates no effect.
64. **D25-AC064:** Revocation, expiry, replacement, identity merge, participant
    addition, authority loss, source-policy change, or projection drift
    concurrent with final action fails safely.
65. **D25-AC065:** Two concurrent final submissions create one receipt and at
    most one source effect.
66. **D25-AC066:** Same semantic key/same meaning returns the same result.
67. **D25-AC067:** Same semantic key with different candidate, reviewer,
    projection, action, assurance, or effect rejects.
68. **D25-AC068:** Failed final proof creates no favorable receipt and leaves
    current public behavior unchanged.
69. **D25-AC069:** Review-only completion is presented as ready, not public,
    when source activation is separate.
70. **D25-AC070:** A source command that lawfully combines review/effect reports
    the exact consequence and unchanged boundaries.
71. **D25-AC071:** Request changes terminates the exact review episode and a
    changed successor requires fresh invitation/proof. D30 requires the same
    atomic result to contain one 1–1,000-code-point Request-changes explanation
    and permits zero or one exact-projection source anchor.
72. **D25-AC072:** No external provider call occurs inside invitation acceptance,
    replacement, or final source transactions.

### RLS, grants, privileged paths, and isolation

73. **D25-AC073:** Direct browser insert/update/delete of invitation/grant/context/

74. **D25-AC074:** RLS `USING` and `WITH CHECK` prevent scope, intended-human,

77. **D25-AC077:** Browser, RPC, view, worker, service role, Payload, import,

    environment/Site/candidate poison matrix.
78. **D25-AC078:** An allowed update cannot transform a permitted row into a
    forbidden Tenant, candidate, human, state, or authority context.
79. **D25-AC079:** Every foreign key and active-head/conflict/reconciliation path
    has a production-proved index.
80. **D25-AC080:** Delete/offboarding/anonymization cannot erase required
    non-reassignable identity conflict or review evidence.

### UX, accessibility, localization, and resilience

## Recommended next Grill decision — D26 external-review availability posture

> **Resolved by D26:** the founder chose bounded Tenant choice, amended after
> adversarial review to the three-state current-ceiling posture above. D27 now
> closes one source-owned responsibility lane with deliberate takeover; D28 is
> now the next founder decision.

### Why this decision is next

D25 now defines a safe, flexible mechanism. It does not decide when staff may
choose it. Locking the mechanism to emergencies minimizes disclosure but can be
needlessly controlling when a board member, translator, legal reviewer, or
regional specialist is the best independent human. Always showing it maximizes
choice but may normalize external disclosure where internal review is simpler.

### Hope Ministries example

Hope has an eligible internal reviewer, Ana, and a saved external reviewer,
Eli, who is bilingual. For one French candidate every source safely admits
external review. The current website stays English and Giving is unchanged
until review and the exact source action complete. Should Maria be allowed to
choose Eli even though Ana is available?

### Option 1 — bounded Tenant posture with recovery-only default — recommended

Core defaults each Tenant to **Use external review only when no qualified
internal reviewer is available**. An authorized, stepped-up Tenant Website-
policy manager may deliberately choose **Allow external review whenever the
source permits** or the stricter **Do not allow external review** after seeing
the disclosure and governance impact. A Site may inherit or narrow, never
widen. The current posture governs current compatible access; later widening
never resurrects an ended path. No posture weakens source admission, D24
independence, one-active scope, assurance, expiry, or final proof.

**Staff impact:** Small ministries are never trapped, while organizations that
regularly use board, language, legal, or regional reviewers can remove an
unnecessary product restriction. Ordinary staff still see internal review as
the quiet default.

**Trade-off:** Core must implement one bounded three-state current ceiling and
clear impact confirmation, but not a policy language.

### Option 2 — platform-wide recovery only

External review appears only after Core proves no eligible internal reviewer is
currently available.

**Staff impact:** Lowest routine external disclosure and least configuration.
It is more controlling: an organization cannot deliberately select a better
qualified external human while an internal reviewer exists, and “no eligible
internal reviewer” itself can be temporally brittle.

### Option 3 — always source-permitted for authorized staff

Whenever every source admits D25, authorized staff may choose internal or
external review for that candidate. There is no Tenant posture.

**Staff impact:** Fewest settings and maximum immediate choice. It makes
external disclosure a normal per-candidate option for every Tenant, including
those that would prefer a default governance guardrail.

### Recommendation and exact question

**Recommendation: Option 1 — a bounded Tenant posture with recovery-only as the
safe default.** It best satisfies the founder's “not inflexible” requirement
without making external disclosure automatic or weakening any D25 security
boundary. It gives a Tenant one understandable current authorization ceiling,
not a rules engine, and never resurrects a prior external path.

Which posture should D26 record: **Option 1 — bounded Tenant choice with
recovery-only default**, **Option 2 — platform-wide recovery only**, or
**Option 3 — always source-permitted for authorized staff**?
