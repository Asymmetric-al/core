## Final disposition

**Accept with required amendments.**

Option 2 is the best permanent direction. It preserves real independent-review
requirements without forcing every ordinary change through a second person. It
also matches Core's existing proportional standard/protected publication model.

The informal answer was not implementation-safe. It did not define:

- which exact source rule is applicable;
- how to avoid reviewing one source obligation twice;
- what happens when an evaluator is missing or contradictory;
- how Core proves two accounts are not the same human;
- what participation disqualifies a reviewer;
- how policy, content, identity, and authority changes stale review;
- how D21/D22 attention filtering interacts with independence; or
- how staff see the rule without permission jargon or a disabled dead end.

The corrected decision below is the decision that should be recorded.

## Exact corrected D23 decision

### D23-R1 — one closed result for one exact action

For each exact current D20/D17 action—zero-public **Review changes**,
zero-public **Review changed default**, or final **Review and make default**—the
D16/D17 source command owner derives one immutable, versioned
**Plan review independence floor** for the exact candidate and review epoch.

The semantic result is exactly one of:

1. **same human permitted**;
2. **different human required**; or
3. **indeterminate**.

The eventual API may mint different enum spellings, but it must preserve these
three meanings. **Indeterminate** always blocks. It is never interpreted as
ordinary, empty, zero qualified people, or permission to fall back.

### D23-R2 — the consequence owner classifies; D23 only composes

Each applicable Page, Navigation, publication, safety, locale, Site-default, or
Tenant-strengthening owner supplies a typed immutable fact containing:

- exact source family, scope, head, candidate, and effect;
- evaluator contract and version;
- closed result and closed reason codes;
- exact applicability to this D20/D17 action;
- its source-owned material-participation predicate or conflict proof;
- source authorization and governance epochs; and
- a permission-safe explanation projection.

The Plan composes these references. It never copies editable source truth,
reclassifies risk, invents an approver, or becomes the write authority for a
Page, Navigation, publication, or safety policy.

### D23-R3 — strictest applicable unsatisfied obligation wins

Any applicable **different human required** result makes the exact current
action require another human. Every required source must return compatible,
complete proof before **same human permitted** is favorable.

A source rule is applicable only when its authoritative typed contract
explicitly says that the rule governs this exact action/effect and identifies
the still-unsatisfied obligation. A protected Page, Navigation, or publication
that already received its own current independent review does not automatically
force a second Plan/default review. Conversely, Plan/default review never
satisfies, replaces, or weakens the source review.

Missing coverage, an unknown reason, an incompatible evaluator generation, a
changed head, an incomplete dependency graph, or contradictory results produce
**indeterminate**.

### D23-R4 — current authorization and human independence are different proofs

**Same human permitted** is not a grant. The actor must still be currently
authorized for the exact source command through Phase 12, the current Active
Tenant Assignment, the exact environment/Site scope, every required capability,
and any required step-up.

**Different human required** adds another proof: the actor's canonical stable
human identity must not match any human the applicable source predicate
disqualifies for the exact candidate and epoch.

Use the final Phase 12 stable human/Party identity—not profile ID, account ID,
email, display name, role, assignment, session, or browser state. Alternate
accounts or roles belonging to one person do not make a second person. Shared
or service identities, AI, automation, support tooling, impersonation, and
**View as** cannot satisfy a different-human requirement.

### D23-R5 — participation comes from accepted source mutations

Each source owner must classify its own substantive participation through a
closed, testable registry. Candidate participation is immutable provenance from
accepted source commands, not a caller-controlled `is_material` flag.

Potentially substantive actions include creating or changing the Plan target,
changing the acknowledged current-default basis, changing review-bound content,
or accepting a change to a material dependency when the source contract says
that action changes reviewed meaning.

Merely viewing, previewing, commenting, requesting changes, requesting review,
opening or reading an item, managing a route, holding a role, compiling unchanged
facts, or making a proved no-op/display-only change does not create substantive
participation. A human who accepts an AI-generated material change is the
attributable participant; AI cannot be the reviewer.

The repository currently contradicts itself on whether protected Phase 17 work
excludes only the latest material editor or every substantive participant. D23
requires an exact source predicate and fails Phase 17 evaluation closed unless
the closed D24 contract is present. D24 Option 2 now resolves the conflict by
excluding every source-defined substantive participant in the complete exact
protected-candidate lineage. D23 did not silently choose one through code.

### D23-R6 — Tenant policy may strengthen but never weaken

A Tenant may elevate an otherwise ordinary Site/default review only through a
bounded, code-defined, versioned setting owned by the Site/default policy
source. It may not author risk predicates, exception rules, thresholds,
approver graphs, weighted votes, quorums, timers, escalations, or a workflow
language.

Tenant policy cannot lower a platform/source floor, redefine a human, turn a
route into permission, or authorize a source action.

### D23-R7 — candidate and policy behavior is monotonic

An evaluation pins the candidate, review epoch, complete dependency digest,
source heads, evaluator versions, policy versions, safe reasons, participant
proof/digest, and required authorization/governance epochs.

- A material source, dependency, participant, candidate, or head change stales
  the review.
- A stricter current platform/source/Tenant policy stales or raises the pending
  candidate before favorable commit.
- A later relaxation never downgrades an already-open stronger candidate.
- Lawful relaxation applies only to a freshly compiled successor candidate.
- A role switch, route edit, label change, split change, import, shared
  dependency, or elapsed time cannot lower the floor.

### D23-R8 — responsibility routing remains attention-only

D21/D22 selects private attention recipients after current source authorization
and D23 conflict filtering. Route membership is neither necessary nor
sufficient to act.

- A selected person who cannot perform the exact action receives no actionable
  item for that episode and gains no access.
- That person remains selected for future episodes where qualified; staff are
  not forced to remove them from the route.
- If any member of the selected Site leg qualifies, only qualified members
  receive attention and D21 fallback does not run.
- D21 fallback may run only when the complete selected Site leg is proved zero.
- Unknown is not zero and never widens the audience.
- A currently authorized and independent person outside the route may still
  act from **Site → Languages**.

Opening, reading, marking read, prefetching, or losing an item never completes,
claims, approves, or locks review.

### D23-R9 — one authoritative command and one winner

The final source command re-proves inside one short, deterministic transaction:

- Tenant, environment, Site, Plan, candidate, and D20 episode;
- expected Plan/default/source heads and complete dependency digest;
- evaluation and policy generations;
- participant/conflict proof;
- stable human identity;
- Active Tenant Assignment, exact current capabilities, step-up, and authority
  epochs; and
- the command's semantic idempotency identity.

One expected-head/CAS winner appends one attributable receipt and performs only
the source action that the user explicitly confirmed. A stale, conflicted,
unauthorized, ambiguous, or concurrent loser changes nothing. Exact retries
return the committed receipt; reuse with different actor, candidate,
evaluation, or meaning rejects.

### D23-R10 — public and financial boundaries stay exact

D23 evaluation creates no mutation path of its own.

- **Review changes** and **Review changed default** remain zero-public re-review
  commands.
- D17's final **Review and make default** remains the sole deliberate source
  command that may consume a favorable D23 result and atomically perform D16
  activation after every current activation fence passes.
- D23 never independently activates the default, publishes a Page, changes
  Navigation, sends a communication, or creates a task/reminder/email.
- D23 never owns or affects Giving, a Giving URL, Legal Entity, Stripe,
  settlement, bank, currency, contribution, receipt, ledger, or accounting
  truth.

While any proof is blocked, stale, unknown, or failed, the current website and
default remain unchanged. No administrator, support worker, timeout, route,
email link, AI, or prior reviewer bypasses the floor.

### D23-R11 — no second approval platform

D23 is a small composition contract over source facts and the existing Phase 12
authorization/identity boundary. It is not a generic approval engine, task
system, policy DSL, delegation engine, or notification-owned workflow.

Candidate-scoped external review and the protected participant boundary were
reserved as separate founder decisions. D24 and D25 now close them; D23 creates
neither one implicitly.

## Plain-language meaning

Core asks the domain that understands the consequence whether another person
is necessary.

- For ordinary work, an authorized staff member may review work they helped
  prepare. Core adds no second-person ceremony.
- For protected work, Core requires the independent human that the applicable
  source contract calls for.
- If Core cannot prove which rule applies, it stops safely and keeps the
  current website unchanged.

This is more usable than “always another person” and safer than “an authorized
person can always review their own work.”

## End-user and Tenant example

Maria prepares Hope Ministries' ordinary French (Canada) default Plan. Every
applicable source proves that another person is not required, and Maria is
currently authorized. She sees:

> **Ready for your review**  
> You can complete this review. No current rule requires a separate reviewer.

For a zero-public re-review, the action is **Review changes** and the page says
the live website will not change. For final activation, the distinct action is
**Review and make French (Canada) default** and the confirmation says that a
successful commit changes `hope.org/` from English (United States) to French
(Canada); explicit language and Giving URLs stay unchanged.

If Maria materially changed protected Navigation and that source contract
requires a different human, she instead sees:

> **Someone else must review this version**  
> This version requires an independent review because it affects protected
> Navigation. You helped prepare it, so another currently authorized person
> must complete the review. The current website remains unchanged.

Ana receives one private actionable item only if Ana is currently source-
authorized and independent for the exact candidate. Maria's configured route
membership remains useful for future ordinary reviews; it is not deleted or
treated as a permission error.

## Complete staff UX/UI contract

### State 1 — ordinary zero-public re-review

```text
Ready for your review

French (Canada) changed since the last review.
You can complete this review.
The live website will not change.

[Preview current version]  [Review changes]
```

Success:

```text
Review complete

The Plan now uses the current version.
The live website has not changed.
```

### State 2 — ordinary final activation

```text
Ready to make French (Canada) the default

You can complete this review.
After a successful check, hope.org/ will open French (Canada).
English and Giving links remain unchanged.

[Preview French website]
[Review and make French (Canada) default]
```

The final confirmation repeats **Current**, **After this change**, and
**Unchanged**. It never hides public impact behind the word “review.”

### State 3 — another human is required and this person participated

```text
Someone else must review this version

This version requires an independent review because it affects protected
Navigation. You helped prepare it, so another currently authorized person must
complete the review.

The current website remains unchanged.

[View current comparison]
[Manage review coverage]  — only when separately authorized
```

If participation or the protected reason is not safe to disclose, use:

> Current review policy requires another authorized person.

Do not show another person's name or protection detail unless the viewer is
separately authorized for that roster/evidence.

### State 4 — selected for notifications but unable to act

Route management may show, to an authorized manager:

```text
Maria Santos
Selected for future review notifications
Cannot review this version because another person is required
```

Maria receives no actionable item for the current episode. Selecting her never
changes her access. She is not forced out of the route.

### State 5 — no currently qualified independent reviewer

```text
Independent reviewer needed

No currently eligible person can complete this review.
hope.org/ still opens English (United States).

[Review coverage]  — only when authorized
```

There is no administrator override, emergency self-review, direct-database
instruction, timer, repeated alert, or automatic email. The lawful recovery is
now D25's source-authorized candidate-scoped external review, only under the
exact source and authorization boundaries recorded there.

### State 6 — evaluation cannot be proved

```text
We couldn't confirm the review requirements

Nothing changed. Core will not continue until it can safely check this version
and current reviewer access.

[Try again]
```

Unknown is not presented as “no reviewer exists” and does not trigger fallback.

### State 7 — candidate, policy, or access changed

```text
Review requirements changed

Nothing went live. Reload the latest version before reviewing.

[Reload current version]
```

Announce the status politely, preserve focus and non-conflicting draft notes,
and never optimistically show success before the authoritative receipt.

### State 8 — another person completed first or response was lost

Concurrent loser:

> **Review already completed.** This Plan was reviewed using the current
> version. The live website changed only if the separately confirmed final
> activation command succeeded.

Ambiguous response:

> **Confirming whether your change was saved.**

Suppress duplicate submission and reconcile by semantic business-effect key.
Show the actor only when the viewer may see that audit fact.

### Visual, mobile, accessibility, and resilience rules

- Reuse Base Maia, Base UI behavior, Zinc tokens, and shared `@asym/ui`
  primitives; do not build an approval micro-frontend.
- Use normal information/warning treatment, not a generic red danger screen.
- Show one obvious lawful primary action; omit an unavailable effectful button
  instead of leaving a mysterious disabled control.
- Never rely on color, tooltip, hover, icon, or badge alone.
- Server-render authoritative status and durable actions; JavaScript may
  enhance but never own review truth.
- At 320 CSS pixels and 400 percent zoom, stack Current/After/Unchanged in one
  reading order rather than forcing a side-by-side table.
- Preserve visible focus, Core's 44-pixel important touch target, forced
  colors, reduced motion, keyboard-only use, screen readers, RTL, CJK,
  combining characters, long names, and long translations.
- Announce asynchronous state changes through one polite status region; do not
  create chatty or auto-disappearing alerts.
- Do not queue an offline review. Preserve input, reconnect, re-read the source,
  and require current server proof.

## Domain invariants

1. One exact candidate/review epoch has one complete independence evaluation.
2. Every required source is represented or the result is indeterminate.
3. The strictest applicable unsatisfied obligation wins.
4. A satisfied source review is neither repeated nor transferred.
5. Route, item, read state, role name, and UI state never alter the floor.
6. Same-human eligibility never grants source authority.
7. Different-human eligibility uses stable human identity and source-owned
   conflict proof.
8. Current authority and conflict are re-proved at commit.
9. A material change never inherits a prior review.
10. Policy tightening can raise/stale; weakening is successor-only.
11. One semantic effect has at most one winning receipt.
12. Zero-public re-review remains zero-public.
13. Only the D17/D16 final command may activate the default.
14. D23 never changes Giving or financial truth.

## Conceptual database, RLS, and authorization contract

This decision does not authorize a schema. The future design must nevertheless
prove the following structural shape:

- immutable evaluation header scoped by Tenant, environment, Site, Plan,
  candidate, review epoch, and D20 meaning;
- normalized immutable source-coverage rows with source family/scope/head,
  evaluator version, result, closed reasons, applicability, policy epochs, and
  participant proof reference/digest;
- normalized immutable participant rows where the source may expose identities,
  or a source-side actor predicate when copying identities would violate
  privacy/ownership;
- one append-only review/activation receipt with trusted stable human actor,
  exact authorization/conflict evidence, candidate/evaluation, semantic key,
  outcome, and source head;
- same-scope composite keys for Tenant/environment/Site/Plan/candidate and
  restrictive deletion; no orphaning or cascade-erasure of business history;
- a unique current evaluation per current candidate and unique successful
  receipt per semantic effect;
- complete-row count/digest proof before favorable release;
- indexes for current candidate/epoch, source head/policy revision, stable actor
  conflict, current route qualification, receipt reconciliation, and stale
  evaluation discovery;
- no cross-schema CMS foreign key; use typed stable references and revalidate at
  the source command;
- revoked browser/direct child writes and one trusted server mutation boundary;
- equivalent `USING` and `WITH CHECK` tenant/scope predicates, explicit grants,
  and update tests proving an allowed row cannot move to a forbidden Tenant,
  Site, candidate, source, actor, or policy;
- the same boundary through RPC, security-definer, owner, service role, Payload
  Local API, worker, import, replay, support, repair, and migration paths; and
- body-free logs and metrics. Durable business receipts are not replaced by
  traces.

Never store a route-level `self_review_allowed`, a generic risk score,
caller-supplied actor/material/reason/policy fields, full copied source content,
raw permission sets, peer read/open data, mutable approver-name authority, or
Giving/financial identifiers.

## Ruthless adversarial review — all 22 categories

### 1. Problem validity, necessity, and alternatives

**Material concern: Yes.**

- **What could go wrong / why it matters:** A universal second human strands
  one-person teams and encourages broad role grants; universal same-person
  review bypasses protected duties. Vague proportionality yields both outcomes
  depending on implementer.
- **Severity / likelihood:** High / High.
- **Evidence:** D16 rejects a universal second approval; Phase 17 requires one
  for protected publication; NIST AC-5 makes separated duties policy-specific.
- **Effect on choice:** Narrows but does not invalidate Option 2.
- **Permanent fix:** One exact-action, source-owned, three-state evaluator.
- **Exact language:** “D23 SHALL compose only typed source obligations for the
  exact current D20/D17 effect; it SHALL neither add a universal second-person
  gate nor weaken a source-owned different-human floor.”

### 2. Brittleness

**Material concern: Yes.**

- **What could go wrong / why it matters:** “Applicable,” “editor,” and
  “independent” can depend on mutable labels, the last editor, undocumented
  fan-out, or another account. A small refactor can change who may act.
- **Severity / likelihood:** Critical / High.
- **Evidence:** Phase 17 uses whole-candidate/versioned proof; GitLab documents
  creator/committer distinctions and provenance pitfalls.
- **Effect on choice:** Requires immutable typed applicability and stable-human
  provenance.
- **Permanent fix:** Closed source contracts, normalized participant proof,
  version/digest fences, indeterminate fail-closed.
- **Exact language:** “Independence SHALL NOT derive from display names, role
  names, current-user fields, route membership, browser claims, or an
  undocumented last-editor shortcut.”

### 3. Technical debt

**Material concern: Yes.**

- **What could go wrong / why it matters:** App-local booleans or a generic
  approval engine duplicate Phase 12, source publication policy, and D21/D22
  routing. Drift becomes inevitable.
- **Severity / likelihood:** High / High.
- **Evidence:** ADR-0029 requires reference-not-copy; Phase 12 is the sole
  authorization spine; Phase 17 rejects a Tenant workflow language.
- **Effect on choice:** Constrains implementation shape.
- **Permanent fix:** One source composition contract and one source command;
  no generic engine.
- **Exact language:** “No app-local self-review flag, copied author list,
  notification permission, or generic approval DSL SHALL become authority.”

### 4. Edge cases

**Material concern: Yes.**

- **What could go wrong / why it matters:** Creator-only/no-edit, reviewer edit,
  AI assistance, no-op change, imports, source removal, aliases, policy churn,
  protected-reason privacy, locale qualification, suspension, and years-old
  undated Plans all differ.
- **Severity / likelihood:** High / High.
- **Evidence:** D16–D22 already distinguish candidate facts, action meaning,
  route state, suspension, and no-op engagement.
- **Effect on choice:** Expands the required closed test matrix.
- **Permanent fix:** Source-owned materiality; stable identity; event-driven,
  not age-driven, reevaluation.
- **Exact language:** “Every accepted source mutation kind SHALL have an
  explicit material/non-material classification and hostile boundary test.”

### 5. Footguns

**Material concern: Yes.**

- **What could go wrong / why it matters:** Staff may select themselves, relax
  policy, switch roles/accounts, interpret a route as authority, or click an
  ambiguous **Approve** action believing nothing will go live.
- **Severity / likelihood:** Critical / Medium-high.
- **Evidence:** Phase 12 rejects multi-hat self-approval; GitLab separates
  creator/committer policies; D16 requires consequence-led action copy.
- **Effect on choice:** Requires monotonic policy, stable human identity, and
  exact effect wording.
- **Permanent fix:** Successor-only relaxation, no override, Current/After/
  Unchanged confirmation.
- **Exact language:** “Policy relaxation SHALL NOT reduce an existing
  candidate's floor, and every effectful action SHALL state its public
  consequence before commit.”

### 6. Tenant safety

**Material concern: Yes.**

- **What could go wrong / why it matters:** Candidate, route, participant,
  policy, reason, cache, or receipt data may mix Tenants/Sites or leak a hidden
  person's existence.
- **Severity / likelihood:** Critical / Medium.
- **Evidence:** Platform boundaries and Phase 12 make Tenant isolation
  structural; current final Site scope is not yet implemented.
- **Effect on choice:** Blocks runtime until compatible Tenant/environment/Site
  scope exists.
- **Permanent fix:** Composite same-scope constraints, uniform responses,
  privacy-minimized projections, poison tests.
- **Exact language:** “Cross-scope or hidden people/candidates SHALL disclose
  no name, count, reason, timing, eligibility, or existence signal.”

### 7. Database, RLS, and authorization safety

**Material concern: Yes.**

- **What could go wrong / why it matters:** Caller-authored actor, participant,
  reason, policy, or Tenant fields; mutable JSON sets; missing `WITH CHECK`;
  owner/service bypass; or movable rows can manufacture independence.
- **Severity / likelihood:** Critical / High if built conventionally.
- **Evidence:** Phase 12 and repository Supabase rules require trusted context,
  RLS, explicit grants, same boundary through privileged paths, and current
  action proof.
- **Effect on choice:** Requires structural storage and one mutation boundary.

## Strongest alternative and why it loses

**Always require another human** is the strongest plausible alternative. It is
simple to explain and maximizes universal separation.

It loses because:

- D16 explicitly rejects a universal second approval;
- Phase 17 intentionally preserves ordinary self-publish while protecting
  consequential changes;
- NIST requires identified duties to be separated, not all duties;
- it blocks ordinary work in a one-person Website team;
- it encourages broad standing access merely to find a nominal second person;
  and
- it duplicates source review when a protected dependency has already been
  independently reviewed.

The other extreme—always permit same-person review when authorized—loses
because it converts route/role convenience into a bypass around real protected
source and Tenant floors.

## Resolved subsequent Grill decision — D24 protected participant boundary

### Why this required a decision

Core currently says two different things for protected work:

- the active outbound-communications OpenSpec excludes only the **latest
  material editor**; and
- the Phase 17 PRD and traceability record exclude **every substantive author
  or editor** of the exact protected candidate.

Those rules produce different real outcomes. D23 could not safely guess which
one the founder intended.

### Hope Ministries example

Maria makes a protected Navigation change. Ana later fixes the final protected
label. Maria is no longer the latest editor, but her work is still part of the
candidate.

The decision was whether Maria should count as independent and review the
combined protected version.

### Option 1 — exclude only the latest material editor

Maria may review after Ana makes the last material edit.

**Staff impact:** more people remain eligible and small teams have less
handoff. However, staff can rotate the last edit and review each other's
combined work. “Independent” no longer means independent of the whole candidate.

### Option 2 — exclude every substantive participant — recommended

Maria and Ana are both unable to review that exact protected candidate. A
third current qualified human must review. Reading, commenting, routing, or
requesting changes does not disqualify; only a source-defined material change
does.

**Staff impact:** the rule is predictable—**if you materially change protected
work, another person reviews it**. A reviewer who chooses to fix protected
content gets a warning before save and a clear handoff. The cost is that small
teams need the later candidate-scoped reviewer path when no internal person
remains.

### Option 3 — exclude only the protected subgraph each person edited

Maria may review protected parts she did not edit, while another person reviews
her subgraph.

**Staff impact:** theoretically most proportional, but one atomic Plan now
needs partial approval graphs, subgraph attribution, combination rules, and a
hard-to-explain UI. It is the most brittle option.

### Recommendation and founder answer

**My recommendation is Option 2 — exclude every substantive participant for
the exact protected candidate.** It matches the primary Phase 17 decision,
prevents last-editor laundering, and gives staff one simple rule they can
predict before editing.

The founder chose **Option 2 — exclude every substantive participant**. The
completed [D24 primary research](./phase-24-d24-every-substantive-participant-primary-research.md)
and [D24 adversarial review](./phase-24-d24-every-substantive-participant-adversarial-review.md)
record the exact candidate-lineage, source-owned materiality, stable-human,
identity, concurrency, authorization, data, UX, and proof amendments.
