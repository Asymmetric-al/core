# Phase 29 — Shared custody, delivery and source integration

This volume specifies intended behavior for the shared byte platform and every registered consumer. It does not assert that a source command, role, storage adapter, processor, migration or release gate is implemented or qualified. The Phase 29 shared requirements and the complete incorporated owner contracts apply throughout. Ordinary request behavior does not replace public Media, official/financial documents, Support, resource publication, import, care or development-artifact authority.

The shared platform owns immutable bytes, intake, technical safety and processing, copies, source-authorized delivery, access evidence and authorized physical disposition. Each consuming source owns document meaning, currentness, acceptance, audience, purpose and records obligations. A provider stores bytes; it is not a competing access or business authority. Requirements below use logical interfaces and outcomes without selecting new physical tables, provider APIs or a generic workflow engine.

## REQ29-D01 — Preserve document, byte, rendition, copy and use identity

**Story US29-D01:** As staff working across records, I want a file's exact identity and uses to survive organization and storage changes, so that I can trust what was received, reviewed and used.

**Decision basis:** Founder §§7–8,13; Q02/Q28/Q42/Q44/Q50/Q53/Q56/Q61-C02/C07; P18/P21/P22/P23 identity owners.

The platform shall distinguish source business identity, immutable received or generated version, exact stored byte identity, processing rendition/output, provider copy and source use. A typed use binds its actual owner, Tenant/environment, source record and exact version or expressly qualified current-version resolution. A use is not an ownership transfer, byte copy or new reader grant. Source-designated originals shall retain their exact received bytes; source-required raw-discard rules remain separate. Provider locators, paths, names, folders, digests and Payload document versions shall not substitute for business identity.

- **AC29-D01-01:** Rename, move, copy registration and provider migration preserve source identity, version lineage and references. A same-named upload creates no implicit replacement.
- **AC29-D01-02:** Equal bytes can support distinct admitted uses with different actors, receipts, rights and obligations. Digest equality cannot merge those facts or establish authenticity, consent, signature validity or acceptance.
- **AC29-D01-03:** Linking or unlinking requires the actual source command and same-scope references. Removing one use neither deletes shared bytes nor discharges another source's duty. Visibility of one use does not reveal other protected associations.
- **AC29-D01-04:** Required scope and lineage are structurally non-null and immutable where their meaning requires; composite keys/references prevent cross-Tenant or wrong-version binding. Legitimate code-owned global definitions may be referenced without pretending they are tenant-owned records; their assignments remain correctly scoped.
- **AC29-D01-05:** Actual current source facts determine candidate, accepted, superseded, withdrawn, evidence-only and unavailable meanings. Custody or a restore cannot make a version current, accepted or legally effective.

## REQ29-D03 — Bind every transfer to narrow durable intake

**Story US29-D03:** As an authorized submitter, I want reliable progress and recovery for my intended file, so that retrying a connection does not create extra evidence or expose a bucket.

Direct transfer is permitted only through a qualified narrow grant; it is not bucket access or source admission. The source owns upload/resume/cancel/retry semantics; browser navigation alone does not cancel durable accepted work.

- **AC29-D03-01:** The user sees per-file selected, transferring, interrupted, checking, known failed, cancelled and result-unknown states without simulated success. One normal authorized Upload and add intent may include qualified transfer/finalization; no routine second confirmation is required.
- **AC29-D03-02:** Grant issue/reissue and lost responses reconcile against the same intake and actual authority. Repeated calls cannot mint unbounded grants. Resume binds the exact actor, source, intake and object generation; names or browser fingerprints are not that identity.
- **AC29-D03-03:** Retired keys are not reused. No upsert or late transfer may overwrite verified/admitted bytes or recreate an untracked copy after cleanup. Token expiry, client abort and worker-lease expiry do not prove all admitted writes stopped.
- **AC29-D03-04:** Multiple supported local selections retain separate intended file outcomes unless a source explicitly admits an indivisible assembly. Their batching does not import Q52 acceptance atomicity or imply remote all-or-none effects.
- **AC29-D03-05:** Actual finite transfer/session/resume/grant and cleanup bounds are qualified per profile. Exhaustion supplies a useful smaller-selection, retry or source-assistance route without public fallback, indefinite authority or silently dropping selected files.
