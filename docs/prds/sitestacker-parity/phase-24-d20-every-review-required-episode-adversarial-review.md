## Final disposition

**Accept with required amendments.**

The founder direction is the best permanent balance of safety and low noise,
but only after **episode** is made exact. Modern approval and code-review
systems commonly require review again after material reviewed facts change.
That evidence supports renewed review; it does not justify a Core alert for
every edit, revision, retry, or refresh.

The corrected decision is:

> D20 admits exactly three separate, code-owned, in-product review meanings:
> **Ready to review**, **Changed since review**, and **Current default
> changed**. One Site-owned selector evaluates complete current truth for one
> Active Default Site Locale Plan and chooses at most one of them at a time.
>
> A **review-required episode** is one maximal, producer-proved interval during
> which one selected review meaning and its exact direct action remain current.
> Repeated edits, source revisions, current-default observations, retries,
> polling, cache recovery, and elapsed time while that same action remains
> required create no peer occurrence and no new unread pulse. A later genuine
> recurrence after the prior episode ended creates a new occurrence; Core never
> rewrites or marks an old item unread again.
>
> The selector order is: terminal or target already current; proved target,
> serving, safety, or action unavailability; unknown proof; **Current default
> changed**; **Changed since review**; **Ready to review**; otherwise no D20
> item. Site suspension always suppresses Ready/activation. A Changed or
> Current-default item may remain only when its exact zero-public-effect review
> command, fact visibility, and safety are independently proved available while
> suspended. Thus one Plan never produces parallel cards for one next review.
> The specific current-default meaning excludes that fact from the general
> changed-review predicate.
>
> **Changed since review** requires explicit immutable source-owned review
> evidence and a change in its closed material dependency set. Notification
> open, read, Preview, page load, task state, or notification engagement never
> creates review evidence. A current-default difference is measured against the
> Plan's latest acknowledged expected default head, or the Plan's initial
> expected head when no successor review exists. If the target itself became
> current, the Plan becomes **Satisfied elsewhere** and no D20 item is created.
>
> One episode absorbs further changes while its action remains open. Copy stays
> truthful and generic enough not to freeze mutable Page titles or locale names;
> the authenticated destination re-proves and displays the latest exact facts.
> A successful review command compare-and-swaps the current Plan, default head,
> material dependency digest, selector generation, and actor authority or
> changes nothing.
>
> A source-owned end closes the item. Reading, opening, previewing, navigating,
> or merely spending time never does. Transient unavailable proof is
> **unknown**: it suppresses unsafe presentation but neither fabricates an end
> nor starts another episode when proof recovers. Apparent restoration after a
> proved material invalidation never silently resurrects the old review; only a
> source contract that classified the change as semantically equivalent before
> invalidation may preserve the basis.
>
> Each chosen episode commits one semantic source identity and durable outbox
> effect. Canonical Phase 6/17 fan-out then creates the D19 recipient-specific
> items. All D19 authorization, engagement, source-end, accessibility,
> retention, one-writer, and zero-side-effect rules remain controlling. D20
> adds no task, due date, reminder, email, SMS, push, urgency, automatic
> activation, public mutation, Page/Giving mutation, currency, Legal Entity,
> Stripe, settlement, bank, receipt, ledger, or accounting effect.

No Phase 17 key is minted by D20. The three names below are non-executable
planning handles only. After D21, D22, D23, and every exact source, recipient,
fact, action, end, storage, RLS, migration, usability, and proof binding close,
the manifest workflow may mint all three stable keys as **Reserved** in one
generation. None may become Live before its complete proof pack passes.

## Plain-language result

For staff, “every episode” means **tell me once each time a real review action
newly becomes necessary**. It does not mean “tell me whenever something was
edited.”

```text
Ready to review
hope.org · French (Canada)

French (Canada) became ready for final review.
This Plan does not change the website automatically.

[Review planned change]
```

Maria opens the item. Her unread badge clears, but the item remains under
**Needs attention** while the review action is still required.

If a reviewed French Navigation version later changes, the old Ready episode
ends and Core creates one new, clearly different item:

```text
Changed since review
hope.org · French (Canada)

Information used by a previous review changed.
Review the current version before continuing.
This Plan does not change the website automatically.

[Review changes]
```

Ten more Navigation saves while that same re-review remains required do not
create ten items. The review screen shows the latest current facts.

If Spanish (Mexico), rather than the planned French (Canada), becomes current,
the more specific state wins:

```text
Current default changed
hope.org · French (Canada)

The website's default changed while this Plan was active.
Compare the current website with this planned change.
This Plan does not change the website automatically.

[Review changed default]
```

If French (Canada) itself became current, Maria sees **Satisfied elsewhere** on
the Plan; no review notification claims that this Plan caused the change.

## Best-practice verdict

### What current evidence supports

- **Verified external fact:** Microsoft Lists/SharePoint, Google Drive, GitHub,
  and GitLab can invalidate or reset prior approval after reviewable material
  changes. GitHub and GitLab support a renewed review request rather than one
  request per commit.
- **Verified external fact:** Contentful, Sanity, and HubSpot model review as a
  workflow state over reviewable content/version context, not as a notification
  emitted for every underlying edit.
- **Verified external fact:** Salesforce action surfaces and modern
  notification guidance favor contextual, direct-action items. Android,
  Windows, and W3C warn against excessive or interruptive notifications.
- **Verified repository fact:** ADR-0027 requires a new recipient-specific item
  for a later meaningful source transition. It forbids reviving an old item's
  engagement and separates read state from source completion.
- **Verified repository fact:** D17 already owns the three derived review
  states; D19 owns one quiet in-product item per exact recipient and role.

### What current evidence does not support

No primary source establishes “notify for every review-required episode” as a
universal rule for every product. That is a **Core product judgment** supported
by two proven patterns: material changes can make earlier review unsafe, and a
renewed review request can bring the right person back without notifying on
every edit.

The strongest alternative is **Ready-to-review only**. It is quieter and
simpler, but it lets an invalidated review sit unnoticed unless staff happen to
return to **Site → Languages**. Core's long-lived, cross-owner Plan makes that
risk more important than the small bounded cost of two additional precise
meanings. No current Core study proves that staff reliably revisit the Plan or
that renewed review items are too noisy.

Salesforce's strongest approval alternative is to lock the reviewed record.
Core must not import that behavior: Page and Navigation owners need to continue
working while a Plan exists. Core therefore permits source work and makes prior
review visibly stale. It also rejects vendor task assignment, arbitrary
approvers, due dates, reminders, snooze, priority, email, push, and direct
approval from the notification because those conflict with D18, D19, and
ADR-0027.

## Corrected D20 decision — normative language

### D20-R1 — The trigger registry is exactly three meanings

D20 admits exactly these meanings and non-executable planning handles:

| Proposed trace handle (not a manifest key)             | Staff state                 | Proposed direct navigation action                |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| `site_default_locale_plan_ready_for_review_v1`         | **Ready to review**         | `site_default_locale_plan.open_final_review@1`   |
| `site_default_locale_plan_review_basis_invalidated_v1` | **Changed since review**    | `site_default_locale_plan.open_changed_review@1` |
| `site_default_locale_plan_current_default_changed_v1`  | **Current default changed** | `site_default_locale_plan.open_default_review@1` |

Phase 17 forbids minting a stable key before meaning, owner, recipient, source
fence, facts, action, and end are all fixed. D20 therefore freezes semantic
separation, not final identifier spelling. After D21/D22/D23/D24/source closure,
all three keys are minted as Reserved in one generation or none are. There is no
executable `plan_changed`, `plan_actionable`, `plan_review_required`, or
tenant-configured catch-all key. The three meanings may share compatible Phase
17 profile primitives, but they retain separate sources, predicates, facts,
actions, end rules, copy, occurrence identities, and proof packs.

### D20-R2 — An episode is a maximal semantic interval, not a source event

One review-required episode begins when the Site-owned selector newly chooses
one exact meaning after choosing none or another meaning. It remains the same
episode while that meaning and direct review action remain required, even when
its current source evidence advances.

The identity is exact Tenant + environment + Site + Plan lineage + meaning +
monotonic producer episode sequence. Page/default revisions and timestamps are
source fences, not occurrence partitions. A proved exit followed by a later
genuine re-entry allocates the next sequence. Retry, refresh, replay, event
delivery order, wall time, Plan age, and cache recovery never allocate one.

### D20-R3 — One selector evaluates complete truth and applies strict precedence

Independent listeners MUST NOT emit D20 occurrences. One versioned Site-owned
selector recomputes complete current truth and returns one closed result:

1. terminal Plan, target already current, or source-owned no-longer-applicable
   proof → no D20 review item;
2. proved target unavailability, safety restriction, or source-level action
   unavailability → no D20 review item; this source applicability is independent
   of recipient resolution, and zero/unknown recipients never make the source
   action false;
3. incomplete, stale, timed-out, corrupt, or contradictory proof → `unknown`,
   fail presentation closed without ending the episode;
4. current default differs from the acknowledged expected head → **Current
   default changed**;
5. another closed material review dependency differs from the current review
   basis → **Changed since review**;
6. D17 proves **Ready to review**, the Site is serving rather than suspended,
   and its final-review action is available → **Ready to review**; and
7. otherwise → none.

The selector runs against one coherent source snapshot/CAS generation. At most
one unended selected reason exists for one exact Plan lineage.

Suspension by itself does not pretend a zero-public review is unsafe. Changed
or Current-default may remain selected only when the exact source review
contract explicitly proves that its command, all required facts, and safety
remain available while suspended. Otherwise suspension makes that action
unavailable and selects no D20 reason.

### D20-R4 — The three predicates mean different things

**Ready to review** means the Active Plan's current D17 projection proves the
target may enter final review now, no higher reason applies, and the registered
final-review navigation action exists. It does not mean live, approved,
scheduled, or automatically activating.

**Changed since review** requires an explicit eligible source-owned review
basis and a proved material difference outside the current-default-head fact.
It cannot fire before any qualifying review basis exists. If source work is
still required before review can happen, D20 creates no item yet; the owning
source surface remains responsible.

**Current default changed** means the authoritative current Default Site Locale
Version/head differs from the Plan's latest acknowledged expected head and the
planned target is not itself current. Before a successor review revision, the
Plan's immutable initial expected head is the comparison basis. This reason
owns every current-default mismatch and prevents a sibling generic Changed
item.

### D20-R5 — Materiality is a closed, versioned source contract

The later source contract MUST classify the exact dependencies staff must
understand before D16 activation. At minimum it covers the Plan lineage and
revision, target Site Locale, acknowledged current Default Site Locale head,
target homepage/allocation, relevant Page/Navigation/publication heads,
locale-exact Public Site Generation, serving/safety/policy proof, current D16
candidate or review revision, and pinned compiler/profile/dependency versions.

Cosmetic labels, display order that does not affect the candidate, audit or
`checked_at` timestamps, cache refresh, task state, notification engagement,
permission changes that only remove a recipient, Giving enablement, currency,
Legal Entity, Stripe, settlement, and finance facts cannot invalidate review.
An unregistered or ambiguously material dependency fails the selector closed
and prevents D20 key minting/activation.

The source may deliberately classify a revision as semantically equivalent
before invalidation, using a proved versioned digest/patch identity. Once it has
proved a material invalidation and opened an episode, a later apparent
reversion does not silently reinstate the old human review.

### D20-R6 — Review evidence is explicit, immutable, and source-owned

A **Default Site Locale Plan Review Basis** is conceptual source-owned evidence,
not notification state. The eventual design may reference a D16 candidate,
D17 immutable Plan/review revisions, or an equivalent smaller accepted model;
D20 does not pre-name a table. A D16 candidate is eligible only when an explicit
successful source-owned review command/receipt proves the actor reviewed that
exact candidate and material basis. Compilation, candidate existence, Preview,
or page load alone never establishes human review.

The basis references, rather than copies as authority, the exact Plan
lineage/revision, target, acknowledged current default head, complete material
dependency digest/schema, candidate/review revision, actor authority,
predecessor basis, and semantic command receipt. Opening or reading an item,
viewing Preview, loading a page, completing a task, or changing notification
engagement never creates, advances, restores, or supersedes it.

### D20-R7 — Further changes inside one episode never create an alert storm

One open **Changed since review** episode absorbs further admitted Page,
Navigation, generation, or policy changes while **Review changes** remains the
selected action. One open **Current default changed** episode absorbs further
non-target default-head changes while **Review changed default** remains the
selected action.

The immutable occurrence records the opening meaning and fences. Current source
facts remain source-owned and are re-proved by the destination. Notification
copy therefore states the durable reason without freezing a Page title, locale
name, current head, diff count, or other mutable fact that could become false.

### D20-R8 — Higher precedence supersedes safely

When a higher reason becomes true, one D20 producer episode/outbox transaction
ends/supersedes the lower episode and opens one higher episode with a new
semantic identity. It observes authoritative source truth but never joins,
rolls back, or rewrites upstream Page, Navigation, default, review, or safety
transactions. Query-time source applicability hides the lower item immediately
even if Phase 17 end projection lags.

If default and non-default material facts changed together, **Current default
changed** alone appears. Its review destination shows every contract-required
authorized fact or denies review when a required fact cannot safely be shown. A
successful review may consume lower facts only when that exact source review
contract explicitly authorizes, CAS-validates, covers, and presents the
complete material basis; display alone grants nothing. Otherwise one lower
Changed episode opens when the higher reason ends and that lower review remains
genuinely required.

### D20-R9 — Each meaning has an exact source-owned end rule

The later manifest MUST bind these separate end-rule identities:

- `presentation_end.site_default_plan_final_review_current@1` — remains current
  only while D17 still proves **Ready to review** for the exact Plan and final
  review remains applicable; terminality, target current, source-owned adverse
  state, or a higher selected reason ends it;
- `presentation_end.site_default_plan_changed_review_current@1` — remains
  current only while the exact eligible review basis is materially invalid and
  **Review changes** remains applicable; a successful current-basis review,
  terminality, target current, an adverse state that makes this exact review
  action/facts/safety inapplicable, or higher reason ends it; and
- `presentation_end.site_default_plan_default_review_current@1` — remains
  current only while the acknowledged expected default differs and **Review
  changed default** remains applicable; a successful D17 current-default review
  of the latest head/current basis, terminality, target current, or an adverse
  state that makes this exact review action/facts/safety inapplicable ends it.

Opening, reading, previewing, page loading, marking read, task state, and time
never end an episode. End time comes from trusted source truth, is once-set, and
is not accepted from the caller or notification worker.

### D20-R10 — Unknown proof suppresses; it never fabricates recurrence

Transient timeout, stale readiness beyond D17's `300`-second ceiling,
contradictory adapters, or partial source reads produce `unknown`. No new item
is emitted. A current destination fails closed with a privacy-safe explanation,
and current list/count presentation may be suppressed.

Unknown does not once-set source end, mark the episode completed, start the
90-day history clock, or produce a false → true recurrence when proof returns.
If the same source episode is still applicable after recovery, the same item
returns with the same engagement and no unread pulse.

### D20-R11 — Chained review steps remain clear without losing responsibility

A successful **Review changes** or **Review changed default** command compares
the exact latest Plan, head, digest, selector generation, and actor authority.
If it moves the Plan into a genuinely different **Ready to review** state, that
is a new action and therefore a new episode under the founder's choice.

The destination presents this as one continuous review journey: it confirms the
completed re-review and shows a labelled **Next: final review** section with a
**Continue to final review** link. It does not show a toast, sound, second
modal, or force a return to the bell.

A chained Ready occurrence may appear inline as source-owned next-step context,
but rendering it records no notification engagement. D21 decides whether the
reviewing actor belongs to that occurrence's recipient set. If an item exists
for that actor, only deliberately opening that item through the next-step link
or explicitly marking it read changes engagement. Background creation,
prefetch, list/inline rendering, or source command completion never marks it
read. Other selected recipients keep independent unread state.

### D20-R12 — One D20 producer transaction and durable outbox own occurrence identity

One CAS-controlled D20 producer transaction records the selected reason episode,
monotonic sequence, immutable opening fences, predecessor/end reason, PII-free
Phase 6 occurrence token, and durable outbox effect after observing source-owned
truth. It never shares atomicity with or rolls back an authoritative Page,
Navigation, default, review, or safety write. The same semantic command returns
the original episode. The same idempotency key with changed scope, meaning,
Plan, head, or digest rejects.

Phase 6/17 fan-out is a downstream, replayable effect. Failure to compile or
present recipient items cannot roll back review/source truth and cannot trigger
an email or task fallback. Reconciliation reuses the same occurrence token; it
never fabricates a peer occurrence.

### D20-R13 — Canonical Phase 6/17 remains the sole notification writer

All three later Reserved key contracts MUST use the D19-selected
`presentation.source_actionable_then_recent_90d@1`, `prepared.none@1`,
**Attention**, in-product-only posture, one recipient-specific item per exact
Party+role+surface, independent engagement, and 90-day authorized post-end
history.

No D20 code may read or write `notification_queue`, Mission Control attention,
contribution notification ledgers, the static demo dropdown, a Site-specific
inbox, or a generic task queue as notification authority. The three key
contracts are minted and enter one manifest/selector generation together;
partial minting or activation is rejected.

### D20-R14 — Recipient authorization, routing, cardinality, and reviewer independence are closed; synchronized proof remains

D20 inherits D19's exact current view+action authorization at occurrence
compile, list, count, realtime invalidation, detail, destination, and command.
Creator, prior reviewer, assignee, editor, nominal staff/admin, support access,
or historical notification membership grants nothing by itself.

D21 now requires one explicit Default Site Locale Plan review responsibility
route hierarchy: Tenant inheritance or a Site route with visibly configured
Tenant fallback on/off, intersected with current exact authorization. Only
proved zero may advance to the saved fallback; indeterminate, partial,
ambiguous, stale, or over-limit never widens. D21 also permits one initial and
producer-authorized successor routing occurrences inside the same source
episode for explicit current handoff or proved selected-set loss; each remains
immutable and all-before-any, unchanged recipients keep their item/engagement,
and at most one active item exists per exact recipient.

D22 now fixes one deliberately chosen person by default, at most three exact
unordered co-responsible people, personal items for every qualified selected
member, and one source completion ending sibling attention. Route attention is
not an action allowlist. D23 now requires one source-owned proportional
independence floor, stable-human conflict proof, and fail-closed unknowns. D24
now excludes every source-defined substantive participant in the complete exact
protected-candidate lineage, not only the latest editor, as recorded in the
[D24 primary research](./phase-24-d24-every-substantive-participant-primary-research.md)
and [adversarial review](./phase-24-d24-every-substantive-participant-adversarial-review.md).
The later manifest must bind that predicate and all exact resolvers before any
D20 key is minted. A proved zero is a complete routing-generation result and
creates no item. A complete nonzero bounded set releases all-before-any.
Indeterminate, partial, ambiguous, or over-limit releases none and reconciles
the same routing generation without guessing.

### D20-R15 — The destination navigates to current truth and never performs review

Each item exposes exactly one server-resolved typed navigation action. Click
reauthorizes and re-proves Site, Plan, selected reason, source visibility, and
exact action capability. It never submits **Review changes**, **Review changed
default**, **Review and make default**, or any source/public command.

Notification actions use native `<a>` link semantics with descriptive names.
Source review and activation commands use native `<button>` elements on the
destination. The card is not a custom clickable container, ARIA listbox, or
direct-command target.

The destination starts with the latest selected reason and four questions:
**Why review again? What changed? What remains public and unchanged? What can I
do next?** It shows a concise current comparison and progressive disclosure for
the full authorized evidence. Cached notification text or a typed destination
reference never grants access.

### D20-R16 — Copy is consequence-led, minimal, and privacy-safe

Every item shows one plain state label, authorized Site display label, planned
locale label, an unchanged-public consequence, and one action label. It avoids
“invalidated,” “digest,” “head,” “fence,” “occurrence,” “revision,” “CAS,” and
“producer.” It carries no Page body, Navigation body, preview image, private
URL, diff content, staff identity, permission reason, owner list, Giving fact,
financial fact, or provider fact.

Mutable current locale names and detailed changes live only on the currently
authorized destination. Restricted facts produce a privacy-safe generic reason
without count, title, timing, or existence clues.

### D20-R17 — The staff UI is calm, continuous, mobile-first, and accessible

The bell and notification center retain D19's explicit **Unread** versus
**Needs attention · active** distinction. Cards use a single text-first reading
order, familiar Base Maia/Zinc tokens and components, one important action of
at least `44px`, no new visual system, no color-only status, no flags, and no
decorative animation.

At 320 CSS pixels and 400% zoom, content and action stack without horizontal
loss. Keyboard, visible/unobscured focus, screen reader, forced colors, reduced
motion, long/CJK/RTL strings, mobile, offline, slow network, stale state, and
access loss are release gates. Async arrival or state change uses at most one
complete polite status message, does not steal focus, and never announces each
underlying revision.

The reason, current public consequence, and next-action availability
server-render/stream first. Detailed comparison loads progressively or on
demand; Preview and Page/media payloads never sit on the critical path. Layout
reserves space to avoid jumps, and review commands remain unavailable until
fresh proof completes. Slow-network proof records critical-path bytes and
usable-summary time.

Background changes never move focus. After a user-submitted review, focus stays
on the submitted control when it remains; if the result replaces it, focus
moves to the new result heading with `tabindex="-1"`. If a focused notification
disappears, focus moves to the next logical row or the list heading. One
coalesced polite message represents one meaningful transition; badge and live
region do not announce it twice.

### D20-R18 — Storage, grants, RLS, and privileged paths preserve scope

Any later operational design MUST make at most one unended selected D20 reason
per exact Tenant+environment+Site+Plan lineage structurally enforceable. It
uses append-only producer episodes and review evidence, once-set end fields,
monotonic episode sequence, immutable scope/meaning/fences/actor attribution,
restrictive deletion, and same-scope composite relationships. Payload/CMS
references remain typed application-validated soft references rather than
cross-schema foreign keys.

Required indexes cover current reason by Plan, review basis by exact Plan,
semantic idempotency/sequence, unreconciled outbox, source end, exact reverse
material-dependency to Active Plan, current-default head to Active Plan, and
all inherited Phase 17 recipient/feed indexes.

`anon` and authenticated browser roles receive no direct mutation grant to
review evidence, producer episodes, or outbox rows. Any admitted browser reads
apply current authorization and operation-correct RLS: SELECT/DELETE `USING`,
INSERT `WITH CHECK`, UPDATE both. Prefer narrow `packages/api` commands.
Tenant, environment, Site, Plan, reason, basis, digest, actor, reviewer,
recipient, source fences, and end derive from trusted server/source context.

Table owner/`BYPASSRLS`, service role, RPC/`SECURITY DEFINER`, views, workers,
Payload Local API, import, migration, support, impersonation, break-glass, AI,
Data API, and repair paths use the same selector/CAS boundary or repeat every
structural check. Definers use schema-qualified objects, pinned empty
`search_path`, revoked `PUBLIC` execute, and least-privileged grantees.

### D20-R19 — Concurrency, failure, and reconciliation fail safely

Review concurrent with a Page/default/safety/permission change has one safe
winner. A stale review commits nothing. Late or out-of-order events never supply
truth; they trigger a recomputation against monotonic current heads. Source
truth remains independently authoritative. The D20 producer episode/outbox
commit is atomic; Phase 17 delivery is independently replayable.

Before the D20 producer episode/outbox commit, failure exposes no D20
occurrence; independently committed source truth remains and reconciliation
observes it. After D20 commit, a lost response or worker crash returns/replays
the same episode and occurrence. Source false before recipient release prevents
active/unread debt. Source end/read/unread races converge to zero active/unread.
Ambiguous source command outcomes are resolved from durable receipts before
retry.

### D20-R20 — Scale and rollout remain bounded and one-versioned

The selector uses indexed exact reverse dependencies and bounded batches; it
never scans all Tenants, all Plans, Plan age, or every Page after one change.
Occurrence fan-out inherits Phase 17's initial exact bounds and D19's
all-before-any rule. Feed/list/count use keyset pagination and complete Tenant,
Party, role, source, and authorization cache partitions.

All three later keys, selector, materiality registry, fact adapters, end rules,
actions, and resolvers are minted/activated under one manifest generation and
cohort gate.

Maria submits **Review changed default**. The source command compares the exact
latest Plan revision, current default head, material dependency digest, selector
generation, and Maria's current authority. If German replaced Spanish a moment
earlier, the stale command changes nothing and the page refreshes the comparison
without creating another occurrence.

If the command succeeds, it appends the source-owned zero-public-effect review
evidence. It consumes lower facts only when that exact source review contract
explicitly authorizes, CAS-validates, covers, and presents their complete
material basis; display alone grants nothing. Otherwise the selector opens one
lower Changed episode after the higher reason ends.

action has changed to final review. Maria's current page advances to a clearly
labelled **Next: final review** section rather than throwing her back to the
bell. There is no toast, sound, modal, focus theft, or celebratory animation.

```text
Changes reviewed
The review now covers the latest changes.

Next: final review
Confirm the complete website impact before making French (Canada) the default.

[Continue to final review]
```

Rendering this source-owned next-step context changes no notification
engagement. D21 determines whether Maria belongs to the current routing
generation's recipient set. If she has an item, deliberately activating **Continue to final
review** opens that exact item/context and may mark only it read. Other selected
recipients keep their own unread item. This preserves responsibility when the
person who re-reviewed and the person who may activate are not the same.

### 7. Activation remains a separate deliberate command

The separate **Make French (Canada) default** button compiles a fresh D16
candidate and rechecks every current source and authority. Only its successful
atomic receipt advances the Default Site Locale head and makes the Plan
**Activated**. That terminal source truth ends the Ready item. D20 itself never
changes the website. **Make French (Canada) default** is consequence-led UI copy
for D17's one **Review and make default** command, not a second command or
authority.

### 8. Adverse, unknown, and access-loss outcomes are quiet and safe

- Site suspension always removes Ready/activation. It removes Changed or
  Current-default unless their exact zero-public review contracts independently
  prove the command, required facts, and safety still available. Target
  withdrawal or another proved action-blocking safety restriction ends/hides
  the item; D20 does not invent a blocker alert.
- A source timeout or stale projection yields **Could not be checked** at the
  destination and temporarily suppresses presentation without ending or
  re-alerting on recovery.
- Permission loss immediately removes list/count/detail/destination access and
  recent history. Restoration does not revive the old item.
- If French becomes current through another authorized command, the Plan is
  **Satisfied elsewhere** and every D20 review item ends without claiming D20
  caused the public change.

### 9. Mobile, assistive technology, and low bandwidth keep the same meaning

The mobile card and review page use the same text order, consequence, active vs
unread labels, and one action. At 320px or 400% zoom, blocks stack. Long French,
Arabic, CJK, and RTL labels wrap without clipping. Background changes never move
focus. After a submitted review, focus stays on the submitted control when it
remains; if the result replaces it, focus moves to the new result heading. If a
focused notification disappears, focus moves to the next logical row or list
heading. One coalesced polite status message announces a meaningful transition;
individual revisions and the badge do not duplicate it.

Offline or stale destinations show a compact privacy-safe state and retry
control. The reason, current public consequence, and next-action status render
first. Detailed comparison loads progressively or on demand; Preview/Page media
never block the usable summary. Reserved layout prevents jumps, commands remain
unavailable until fresh proof completes, and slow-network fixtures record
critical-path bytes plus usable-summary time. The notification carries no rich
preview payload, so weak connections do not download Page content merely to
render the bell.

## Domain invariants and cardinality

1. One exact Plan lineage has zero or one unended selected D20 reason episode.
2. One selected episode has exactly one of the three stable meanings.
3. One episode maps to exactly one semantic Phase 6 occurrence identity.
4. One occurrence maps to zero or one item per exact resolved
   Tenant+Party+role+surface; zero recipients is valid, partial fan-out is not.
5. One recipient item has independent engagement; no engagement field changes
   source review or public truth.
6. Current-default mismatch and generic review-basis invalidation never select
   simultaneously; the former wins and is excluded from the latter.
7. Changed-since-review cannot exist without eligible immutable review evidence.
8. The planned target already current implies **Satisfied elsewhere** and zero
   D20 review items.
9. Proved terminal, target-unavailable, safety-blocked, or action-unavailable
   truth cannot coexist with a currently actionable D20 item. Suspension always
   excludes Ready and permits Changed/Current-default only under an explicit
   safe zero-public review contract.
10. `unknown` is neither applicable nor ended; recovery cannot allocate a new
    episode for unchanged semantic truth.
11. Repeated source revisions while one selected action remains open conserve
    one source episode and one current routing lineage. They create no peer
    occurrence; only a D21-admitted responsibility handoff may add an immutable
    successor routing occurrence without re-notifying continued recipients.
12. A later genuine episode uses a new sequence and never mutates or re-unreads
    the prior item.
13. A review command either records one complete latest source-owned review
    effect or no effect; no partial basis exists.
14. D20 conserves every public, content, Giving, financial, and accounting fact.
15. Time, notification state, task state, provider delivery, and cache state
    have zero authority over episode selection or source review completion.

## Final disposition after adversarial review

**Accept with required amendments.**

The accepted decision is exactly the block under **Final disposition** and the
normative D20-R1–R22 clauses. “Every review-required episode” must never be
shortened in a later spec to “every change,” “every revision,” “Plan changed,”
or one generic notification key.

The amendments do not weaken the founder choice. They make it usable: one
initial or renewed review request for one exact current action, no duplicate
cards, no change storm, no stale copy, no hidden completion, and no public or
financial side effect.

## Required acceptance criteria and proof

### Trigger, meaning, precedence, and occurrence

1. **D20-AC1 — Unready first state.** An Active, never-reviewed Plan whose
   final review is not currently available creates no D20 item.
2. **D20-AC2 — First Ready entry.** The first proved entry into **Ready to
   review** creates exactly one Ready source episode and one initial routing
   occurrence.
3. **D20-AC3 — Ready deduplication.** At least 100 repeated Ready evaluations,
   refreshes, retries, and duplicate events return the same source episode and
   initial routing occurrence and create no additional unread pulse. A
   separately admitted D21 handoff is not a duplicate Ready event.
4. **D20-AC4 — Ready exit.** A proved Ready → not-ready transition ends the
   Ready item once and creates no D20 blocker item.
5. **D20-AC5 — Ready recurrence.** A later proved not-ready → Ready transition
   creates exactly one new Ready episode sequence; it never revives/re-unreads
   the prior item.
6. **D20-AC6 — No premature Changed.** A material source change before any
   eligible review basis creates no **Changed since review** item. Candidate
   compilation/existence, Preview, or page load without an explicit successful
   source-owned review receipt remains unreviewed and cannot satisfy the basis.
7. **D20-AC7 — Changed after review.** A material non-default dependency change
   after an eligible source-owned review basis creates exactly one Changed
   episode.
8. **D20-AC8 — Cosmetic change excluded.** A cosmetic label or source change
   classified as semantically equivalent by the versioned materiality contract
   creates no episode and does not invalidate review.
9. **D20-AC9 — Operational noise excluded.** Audit time, `checked_at`, cache
   refresh, projection version with equivalent semantics, task progress, Page
   view, notification engagement, and transport state create no D20 episode.
10. **D20-AC10 — Financial separation.** Giving enablement, Designation,
    currency, Legal Entity, Stripe, settlement, bank, contribution, receipt,
    statement, ledger, and accounting changes create no D20 episode.
11. **D20-AC11 — Initial expected head.** A non-target current-default change
    before a successor review basis exists creates one **Current default
    changed** episode against the Plan's immutable initial expected head.
12. **D20-AC12 — Reviewed expected head.** A later current-default change after
    explicit review creates the same specific meaning against the latest
    acknowledged expected head.
13. **D20-AC13 — Target current.** The planned target becoming current produces
    **Satisfied elsewhere**, no D20 item, and no audit claim that D20 caused it.
14. **D20-AC14 — Default precedence.** Simultaneous current-default and
    non-default material changes select **Current default changed** only; the
    destination shows every contract-required authorized fact or denies review
    when a required fact cannot safely be shown.
15. **D20-AC15 — Changed precedence.** Simultaneous generic Changed and favorable
    Ready predicates select **Changed since review** only.
16. **D20-AC16 — Ready exclusion.** Simultaneous Current-default and Ready
    predicates select Current-default only.
17. **D20-AC17 — Mutual exclusion.** No coherent state, event-order permutation,
    or recipient produces two concurrently active D20 reasons for one Plan.
18. **D20-AC18 — One selector.** Static/generated proof rejects any independent
    Ready/Changed/default trigger binding that can bypass the one versioned
    selector.
19. **D20-AC19 — Registered materiality only.** An absent, unknown, ambiguous,
    or unversioned dependency classification fails the selector closed and
    prevents key minting/activation.
20. **D20-AC20 — No generic key.** Build/manifest/runtime proof rejects
    `plan_changed`, `plan_actionable`, `plan_review_required`, arbitrary
    event-type, and tenant-authored trigger contracts.

### Episode lifecycle, source end, and chained review

21. **D20-AC21 — Suspension.** A suspended Site presents no Ready or activation
    item. Changed/Current-default remains only when its exact zero-public review
    contract proves the command, required facts, visibility, and safety still
    available; otherwise it ends/hides. Every shown variant says the Site is
    suspended and not currently public.
22. **D20-AC22 — Favorable restoration.** When a proved adverse source state
    later becomes favorable, the selector creates only the currently correct
    reason and at most one new episode.
23. **D20-AC23 — Target adverse.** Target withdrawal, retirement, missing
    generation, or proved safety terminality ends applicable review items and
    creates no replacement D20 blocker item.
24. **D20-AC24 — Plan terminality.** Cancelled, Superseded, Activated,
    Satisfied elsewhere, and No longer applicable end every D20 reason
    idempotently and cannot reopen the old episode.
25. **D20-AC25 — Unknown creates nothing.** Timeout, partial read, corrupt
    source, contradictory adapters, or stale readiness beyond 300 seconds
    returns unknown and creates no occurrence.
26. **D20-AC26 — Unknown is not terminal.** Unknown temporarily suppresses
    unsafe current presentation without once-setting source end, fabricating
    read history, or starting the 90-day history clock.
27. **D20-AC27 — Unknown recovery.** Same-fact recovery restores the same still-
    applicable item/engagement with no new occurrence or unread pulse.
28. **D20-AC28 — Changed absorption.** At least 100 further admitted Page,
    Navigation, generation, or policy changes while Changed remains selected
    produce one source episode/current routing lineage and no extra initial
    occurrence or unread pulse; only a D21-admitted handoff may create an
    immutable successor for newly selected recipients.
29. **D20-AC29 — Default absorption.** At least 100 further non-target default-
    head changes while Current-default remains selected produce one episode/
    current routing lineage with no peer initial occurrence; the current
    destination shows the latest head. A D21 successor does not create another
    Current-default source episode.
30. **D20-AC30 — Immutable copy remains true.** After repeated changes, the
    original item text remains accurate and contains no stale mutable Page,
    locale, head, diff-count, or staff fact.
31. **D20-AC31 — No silent review restoration.** After a proved material
    invalidation opens Changed, an apparent source reversion does not restore
    the old review basis or end the item without an explicit current review.
32. **D20-AC32 — Higher supersession.** A higher-precedence transition atomically
    ends/supersedes the lower episode and opens exactly one higher episode.
33. **D20-AC33 — Comprehensive review.** Successful **Review changed default**
    against the latest complete source-contracted material basis resolves the
    current-default reason and only the lower facts explicitly authorized,
    covered, and presented by that exact review contract. Merely displaying a
    fact cannot consume its review requirement; a truly covered basis creates
    no redundant Changed item.
34. **D20-AC34 — Legitimate lower follow-up.** If a higher reason ends without a
    comprehensive review and a lower reason remains genuinely actionable,
    exactly one new lower episode may begin.
35. **D20-AC35 — Ready after re-review.** A successful re-review that genuinely
    moves the Plan into Ready creates one new Ready episode. The same page shows
    **Next: final review** without toast/sound/modal/focus theft. Inline render
    and source completion change no engagement; only deliberate activation of
    the next-step/item link or explicit Mark read may mark that recipient's
    exact item read.

### Review evidence, concurrency, temporal behavior, and idempotency

36. **D20-AC36 — Engagement is not review.** Notification open/read, Preview,
    review-page load, list render, prefetch, task completion, or another
    recipient's action creates no review basis and ends no source episode.
37. **D20-AC37 — Explicit source command only.** Only the separately authorized
    source review command can append/supersede eligible review evidence.
38. **D20-AC38 — Complete review basis.** Review evidence pins exact Plan
    lineage/revision, target, acknowledged current head, material digest/schema,
    candidate/review revision, predecessor, trusted actor authority, and command
    receipt without copied content authority.
39. **D20-AC39 — Caller attribution rejected.** Caller-supplied Tenant,
    environment, Site, Plan, reason, actor/reviewer/role, basis, digest, fence,
    recipient, or end is ignored/rejected and cannot alter durable attribution.
40. **D20-AC40 — Stale Plan.** Review against a stale Plan revision commits no
    basis, episode end, occurrence, public, or source effect.
41. **D20-AC41 — Stale default.** Review against a stale current-default head
    commits nothing and returns the latest safe comparison.
42. **D20-AC42 — Stale material digest.** Review against a stale material
    dependency digest/registry version commits nothing.
43. **D20-AC43 — Source/review race.** Review concurrent with a material source
    change has one safe winner and never records review of unseen facts.
44. **D20-AC44 — Default/review race.** Review concurrent with another default
    activation has one safe winner; the Plan converges to the correct selected
    reason or Satisfied elsewhere.
45. **D20-AC45 — Revocation race.** Actor capability/source visibility
    revocation before review commit rejects the command; cached authority cannot
    win.
46. **D20-AC46 — Same semantic retry.** The same review idempotency key and
    exact meaning returns the original durable receipt.
47. **D20-AC47 — Changed retry meaning.** The same key with changed Tenant,
    Site, Plan, head, digest, selector generation, or meaning rejects.
48. **D20-AC48 — Concurrent equivalent reviews.** Concurrent equivalent review
    commands produce one successor basis/effect and deterministic loser result.
49. **D20-AC49 — Duplicate producer events.** At least 100 duplicate producer
    events return one source episode and one Phase 6 occurrence token for the
    same routing generation; they never create a routing successor.
50. **D20-AC50 — Clock invariance.** Advancing wall time, DST, Site/user time
    zones, timestamps, or Plan age without a source transition creates, orders,
    escalates, ends, or reopens zero episodes.

### Ordering, failure, fan-out, authorization, and isolation

51. **D20-AC51 — Late-event fence.** A late older source/default event cannot
    reopen an ended episode, regress precedence, or overwrite current reason.
52. **D20-AC52 — Order convergence.** Every tested permutation of Page,
    Navigation, default, safety, terminal, and review events recomputes to the
    same current selector result and episode history.
53. **D20-AC53 — Pre-commit crash.** Crash before the D20 producer episode/
    outbox commit exposes no D20 occurrence/item. Independently committed Page,
    Navigation, default, review, or safety truth remains authoritative and is
    observed by same-identity reconciliation; D20 never rolls it back.
54. **D20-AC54 — Lost response.** Crash after the D20 producer episode/outbox
    commit but before response replays/returns the same episode, sequence, and
    occurrence token; independently committed source truth is preserved.
55. **D20-AC55 — Duplicate outbox.** Duplicate/out-of-order outbox delivery
    creates one Phase 6 occurrence per admitted routing generation and no peer
    recipient item. Continued recipients in a D21 successor mapping receive no
    new item/unread.
56. **D20-AC56 — Occupied-slot conflict.** Changed scope/meaning/facts under an
    occupied semantic occurrence slot hard-conflict instead of overwriting.
57. **D20-AC57 — Source false before fan-out.** If source actionability ends
    before recipient release, no active/unread debt is exposed; durable source
    history remains auditable.
58. **D20-AC58 — End/read race.** Every source-end/read/unread/realtime order
    finishes with zero active/unread after end and no fabricated read.
59. **D20-AC59 — Query-time applicability.** List/count/detail/destination hide
    a terminal/superseded/inapplicable item immediately even while asynchronous
    end projection is delayed.
60. **D20-AC60 — Exact recipient authority.** Each key releases only to
    recipient+role identities that can currently see all required facts and
    perform its exact direct review action under the eventual D21 resolver.
61. **D20-AC61 — Historical roles grant nothing.** Creator-only, prior-reviewer,
    assignee-only, broad staff/admin, support, service-role, or old authority
    creates no recipient item by itself.
62. **D20-AC62 — Scope poison.** Cross-Tenant, wrong-environment, wrong-Site,
    wrong-Plan, wrong-target, wrong-basis, wrong-occurrence, wrong-role, and
    hidden-resource fixtures fail without count/timing/existence disclosure.
63. **D20-AC63 — Revocation removal.** Recipient authorization loss immediately
    removes active/recent list/count/detail/destination access; restoration does
    not revive old engagement.
64. **D20-AC64 — Resolver result union.** Proved zero recipients preserves and
    releases the current routing generation's Phase 6 occurrence as a zero-
    member occurrence and creates no guessed item. Complete nonzero bounded
    recipients release all-before-any. Indeterminate, partial, ambiguous, or
    over-limit releases none and reconciles/fails the same source episode and
    routing-generation identity.
65. **D20-AC65 — Anonymous denial.** `anon` cannot read or mutate Plan review
    evidence, producer episodes, occurrence/outbox, recipient items, groups,
    engagement, or private review destinations.
66. **D20-AC66 — Browser mutation denial.** Authenticated browser roles cannot
    directly create/update/end/delete review evidence, producer episodes, or
    outbox rows.
67. **D20-AC67 — RLS operation matrix.** SELECT/INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE tests prove
    operation-correct `USING`/`WITH CHECK`, grants, views, functions, and no
    allowed update can transform a row into forbidden scope/state.
68. **D20-AC68 — Privileged poison matrix.** Table owner, service role,
    `BYPASSRLS`, definer/RPC/view, worker, Payload Local API, import, migration,
    support, impersonation, break-glass, AI, Data API, and repair paths cannot
    bypass selector/CAS/scope/actor/source rules.

### Manifest, migration, UX, performance, trace, and zero effects

69. **D20-AC69 — Minting/Reserved closure.** No key is minted until D21 routing,
    D22's closed route cardinality/completion, D23's proportional independence
    floor, D24's protected participant predicate,
    every selector predicate, identifier, action, fact adapter, materiality
    registry, end rule, recipient resolver, capability, retention, RLS,
    migration, usability, and proof binding is fixed. The complete set is then
    minted as Reserved and remains so on any failed proof.
70. **D20-AC70 — Atomic generation.** Partial activation of one/two keys or
    selector/key/resolver/end-rule generation skew is rejected.
71. **D20-AC71 — No historical inference.** Migration creates no review basis,
    episode, occurrence, engagement, or unread item from candidates, Page
    views, tasks, legacy notifications, audit rows, timestamps, or staff
    identity guesses.
72. **D20-AC72 — Mixed-version failure.** Old producer/new manifest, new
    producer/old manifest, old materiality/new selector, and new selector/old
    end rules fail closed without exposing partial items.
73. **D20-AC73 — Safe rollback.** Kill/rollback stops new D20 episodes while
    preserving Plan/source/review/episode/audit truth and current query-time
    source/access suppression; no destructive evidence rewind is required.
74. **D20-AC74 — Indexed scale.** Query-plan/production fixtures prove exact
    reverse dependencies, selector, source-end reconciliation, and feed paths
    use declared indexes/bounded batches and perform no all-Tenant/all-Plan/
    age scan.
75. **D20-AC75 — Production-shaped budgets.** Zero/one/50-recipient, multi-Site,
    noisy-source, cold/warm-cache, permission-churn, and low-bandwidth fixtures
    remain inside inherited `500 ms` p95 feed/read and `300 s` reconciliation
    ceilings without weakening authorization or copying source truth. Selector
    p95 uses its own pre-registered production-shaped budget/window/sample
    before Live; D20 does not misapply the read budget to producer evaluation.
    The slow-network review journey separately pre-registers device/network,
    critical-byte, and usable-summary-time budgets and must meet them without
    omitting required context or authorization proof.
76. **D20-AC76 — Comprehension gate.** A pre-registered representative study
    records roles, ministry size, languages, devices, assistive technology,
    bandwidth, sample, threshold, and confidence rule; participants must
    distinguish first review from re-review, identify the one next action, and
    understand that reading and D20 change nothing automatically. Failed or
    missing evidence keeps the keys Reserved.
77. **D20-AC77 — Accessibility gate.** Keyboard, screen reader, non-color state,
    visible/unobscured focus, forced colors, reduced motion, long/CJK/RTL text,
    320px reflow, 400% zoom, and 44px important actions pass; async updates use
    no more than one complete polite announcement and never steal focus.
78. **D20-AC78 — Channel/task absence.** Every D20 path creates zero email,
    digest, SMS, push, browser/OS alert, sound, Slack/Teams message, reminder,
    due date, task, snooze, configurable/time-derived priority or severity,
    assignment, or provider request; the fixed D19 **Attention** presentation
    remains.
79. **D20-AC79 — Public/content absence.** Every D20 path creates zero Site,
    root, default, locale, Page, Navigation, host, route, redirect, Public Site
    Generation, search, sitemap, public cache, explicit Page/Giving address, or
    automatic activation mutation.
80. **D20-AC80 — Giving/finance absence and trace.** Every D20 path creates zero
    Giving, Designation, currency, Legal Entity, Stripe, settlement, bank,
    contribution, commitment, receipt, statement, tax, ledger, or accounting
    effect; generated trace can distinguish an external authoritative default-
    change receipt from D20's later zero-effect observation and maps all 80
    criteria to founder/ADR/OpenSpec/design/test/release evidence.

## Ruthless synthesis — strongest path forward

### Resolved before recording D20

1. Define **episode** as one maximal interval of one selected direct review
   action, never one source revision or notification delivery.
2. Close the registry to exactly three distinct meanings and proposed trace
   handles without prematurely minting Phase 17 keys.
3. Give one selector strict terminal/adverse/unknown/default/changed/ready
   precedence and structurally allow one active reason per Plan.
4. Require explicit source-owned review evidence and a closed materiality
   registry; notification engagement never becomes review authority.
5. Absorb repeated changes within one episode while current destinations show
   latest facts and immutable cards stay truthful.
6. Specify exact source-end and unknown behavior, including no silent review
   restoration and no recurrence from technical recovery.
7. Make sequential review actions one continuous staff journey without hiding
   a real next-action item from another qualified person.
8. Correct the contradictory **Changed since review** example that previously
   used a current-default change.
9. Mint no key until D21, D22, D23, and every source/recipient binding closes;
   then mint all three as Reserved in one generation and keep them there until
   proof passes.

### Requirements the later PRD/OpenSpec/design must capture

1. D20-R1–R22 verbatim or with traceably equivalent normative language.
2. Exact D16/D17 review-basis/evidence representation, material dependency
   registry/digest/schema, source selector/result union, CAS inputs, episode
   sequence, source-end reasons, and outbox transaction.
3. All three complete Phase 17 key contracts: profiles, facts, forbidden sets,
   actions, end rules, D21 resolvers, capabilities, retention/audit, proof ids,
   and one manifest generation.
4. Same-scope constraints, grants/RLS/privileged-path boundary, indexes,
   immutable/append-only evidence, and source/review/notification audit links.
5. Complete staff bell/center/review destination, continuous chained flow,
   private current comparison, accessibility/mobile/offline/low-bandwidth
   behavior, and comprehension study protocol.
6. Shadow/pilot/cutover/kill/roll-forward plan, dashboards, alerts, runbooks,
   owner roster, and generated founder→release trace matrix.

### Implementation order and dependencies

1. Land the accepted canonical Phase 17 occurrence/item/group/engagement model
   and one-writer cutover. Do not build a D20 substitute.
2. Land D15/D16 public/default contracts and D17 private Plan/readiness/review
   source authority; preserve D18's no-time decision and D19 presentation floor.
