## Recommended next question

**Should Phase 26 ask requesters for a brief assessment of the help they received, and what should initiate that invitation?**

Recommend **A — Tenant-enabled automatic feedback after eligible completed help**, with the feature Off until the tenant explicitly enables its qualified policy. This is one optional feedback capability and one invitation-initiation choice. It does not make every Resolved transition send mail, require three configurable modes, or decide the detailed question wording, scale, schedule, response attribution or reporting design before the founder answers.

Illustration, not a claim about every ministry: Elena asks the Donor care team how to find a receipt. After staff have actually helped and finished that Support work, Elena could receive a brief optional invitation to say how the help went. That assessment is different from whether the receipt legally exists, whether Elena can access another person's gifts, or whether staff replied within a target. Elena can continue ordinary Support correspondence whether or not she provides feedback.

## Why this is a genuine remaining choice

- The current [coverage ledger](phase26-grill-log.md) leaves CSAT and public self-service as separate scope choices. D27 closes staff discovery of selected public guidance; it does not ask requesters to evaluate service.
- [D14 reporting](phase26-d14-adversarial-review.md) explicitly separates reply-time statistics, current backlog and ending volumes from satisfaction, resolution success and staff effort. Faster replies cannot stand in for a requester's assessment.
- [D5-R05](phase26-d5-adversarial-review.md) explicitly says the local ending action sends no satisfaction survey and does not implicitly enroll a requester in one. Future notices require their own separately authorized owner contract and policy. Thus the founder can choose optional feedback here without silently changing the meaning of Resolved or No response.
- [D15](phase26-d15-adversarial-review.md), [D18](phase26-d18-adversarial-review.md) and [D27](phase26-d27-adversarial-review.md) similarly avoid treating following, wording insertions or guidance lookup as satisfaction. No accepted decision supplies the missing requester-feedback lifecycle.

This is a product judgment about a useful remaining choice. Neither the roadmap nor vendor availability proves Asym tenant demand, a minimum response rate, a satisfaction improvement or that a rating is representative of everyone helped.

| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Tenant-enabled automatic feedback after eligible completed help — recommended** | A tenant deliberately enables a bounded policy. The system invites feedback after qualifying human-help completion without staff having to remember an invitation each time. It stays Off for tenants that do not enable it. | More consistent invitation coverage and less staff effort or selective asking than case-by-case invitations.                                                               | Adds optional outward email and a real invitation/response lifecycle. Timing, repeat-contact limits, current eligibility, audience and handling of feedback require careful qualification; automation does not remove non-response or interpretation bias.                      |
| **B — Staff chooses when to request feedback**                                        | Authorized staff deliberately include/request the same brief feedback opportunity for a particular conversation, in the context of their actual correspondence. No completion-triggered invitation runs.                     | Best situational judgment and fewer unsolicited invitations; staff can choose a useful moment in a sensitive or unusual exchange.                                          | Adds an action and depends on staff remembering. Invitation coverage may be inconsistent or selective, so any later score must not be represented as a comparable unbiased team measure. It still needs a real governed invitation/response contract, not just a free-form URL. |
| **C — Ordinary feedback, without a dedicated rating flow in Phase 26**                | Requesters continue replying normally with thanks, confusion or concerns. Staff review those conversations and existing operational reports; no survey invitation or rating page is added.                                   | No additional email or rating burden, and the smallest product/maintenance scope. This is credible for teams that get enough useful feedback through normal relationships. | No consistent structured requester assessment or survey coverage. Staff must interpret ordinary feedback themselves; the system cannot turn silence or response speed into satisfaction.                                                                                        |

These are alternatives for the Phase 26 product direction, not an instruction to build all three as settings. Ordinary replies remain available with every option. A/B are not a broad NPS campaign, donor loyalty score, staff ranking system or required survey on every signature.

## Current primary evidence and its fit

**Front provides a direct comparison of initiation patterns.** Its current CSAT documentation, edited 7 August 2026, describes manual insertion, signature collection and automatic rules. Automatic rules can be scoped to an inbox and delayed; its repeated-request prevention relies on a Survey Sent tag. Front distinguishes repeated manual/signature ratings from the automatic-rule behavior. Its current documented plan is Professional or above, with some legacy access. This supports A and B as real alternatives and exposes the need for durable repeat prevention; it does not justify copying tag-controlled safety, per-reply signature noise or Front's staff-score attribution into Asym. [Front CSAT](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2255).

**Zendesk demonstrates delayed completion invitations.** Its current recipe documents a default invitation 24 hours after Solved and an alternative immediate trigger guarded by satisfaction being Unoffered. This supports the general post-help pattern and explicit prior-invitation state, not a universal Asym 24-hour rule or blanket mapping from D3 Resolved. The retrieved recipe's edition coverage was not established; no price or all-plan claim is made. [Zendesk workflow](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408887007386-Workflow-How-to-send-satisfaction-surveys-when-tickets-get-solved).

**HubSpot supplies a useful CRM-connected contrast, with an important metric distinction.** Its Customer Support survey is **Customer Effort Score (CES)** on a seven-point scale, not its separate CSAT survey. The article, updated 20 June 2026, covers Service Hub Professional/Enterprise, ticket pipeline/status triggers, immediate or delayed sending, and one survey per ticket even after reopening. Those are concrete workflow comparisons. Asym should not import HubSpot's account-user sender selection, Marketing-tool permission dependency or CRM contact enrollment as its own authorization design. [HubSpot customer support surveys](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/customer-feedback/how-do-i-send-a-customer-support-survey).

**Intercom documents the failure pressure behind apparently simple automation.** Its workflow article, dated 25 September 2025 and currently accessible, warns that a generic conversation-rating predicate can refer to the latest rating across a customer's conversations, rather than the current one. Its repeat-customer FAQ describes a tag workaround for frequency control. This supports precise conversation/occurrence attribution and fatigue prevention as eventual selected-answer requirements; it does not justify a global person rating or recreating the vendor workaround. [Intercom rating workflow](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/9634546-ask-customers-for-a-conversation-rating).

These are verified documented product behaviors, not evidence of undocumented vendor schemas, measured nonprofit demand or statistical validity. A's reduced selective asking is a reasoning-based comparison with B; it is not a claim that automatically collected responses are unbiased.
