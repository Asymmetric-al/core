## Reported friction versus verified behavior

Comments on [Zendesk's official article](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408885807258/comments/5446378351642) include staff requests to re-edit an internal comment and suggestions to retain changes in events. These are self-selected experiences, not measured incidence or representative research. They help frame the cost of B, while the official article establishes the product behavior.

No Asym tenant study proves how often notes are corrected, what edit window would be optimal or that one policy measurably improves satisfaction. A is a product judgment combining the founder's low-friction goal, current source ownership and actual modern editing patterns. C is a plausible compromise, not a claimed vendor-standard duration. B is the strongest no-in-place-edit alternative and remains explicitly available.

## Adversarial question check

The choice is properly bounded to human-authored published internal Support notes. It excludes incoming/sent communication edits, bulk changes, third-party/AI authorship, routine hard delete, public conversion and delegated rewriting of another author. Redaction/retention/current access remain mandatory regardless of option. A's visible earlier revisions cannot recreate redacted/expired content; edits cannot reset original chronology or resend old notifications. B/C correction links must remain current-authorized and obvious rather than leaving an isolated misleading original. New circumstances belong to new notes, not retroactive mutation.

The exact schema/authorization/revision/attachment/mention/notification/concurrency/migration tests must be researched and settled in the full answer after the founder selects a policy. No choice of Q35, timing window, general versioning platform or new formal implementation authority is smuggled into a question. The [final independent review](phase26-q35-final-review.md) checks these boundaries, option differentiation and the recommendation before presentation.
