# D29 supplemental UX review — direct contact and optional guides

**14 September 2026. Independent supplemental review of the fully ratified D29 A.** This is the founder's requested re-examination of contextual guidance and direct contact, not an answer to Q35. D1–D34 remain ratified and Q35 remains unanswered. I read the actual [D29 decision](phase26-d29-adversarial-review.md), [requester/maintainer blueprint](phase26-d29-ux-blueprint.md), relevant [data contract](phase26-d29-data-contract.md), [evidence](phase26-d29-evidence.md) and [resolved final UX review](phase26-d29-ux-final-review.md), then refreshed the primary sources below. No root record, product, provider, inbox or public page was changed; no form was submitted.

**Disposition: accept the existing contact-first design, with one narrow required accessibility-proof amendment.** The full-page or app-owned contact form remains immediately available, email/message are required, name remains optional and unsplit, and zero to three exact public guides remain secondary. Current evidence does not justify a public search library, bot, channel chooser, required subject/category, extra account gate or different CRM owner. The new finding concerns the explicit reflow/focus/target proof in R26/P40; it does not change A's product scope.

## New material finding and exact amendment

### UX-RF01 — the explicit accessibility proof stops too early

**Material concern: yes. Severity: Medium; likelihood: plausible in a responsive implementation without the missing proof.** A form can pass at 200% zoom while its side column, long reply email, modal bounds, error summary or sticky footer makes contact unusable at a smaller effective viewport. A person who relies on magnification or touch could fail to find, correct or submit the same ordinary request. This narrows the proof language; it does not invalidate contact-first A or demonstrate a bug in a built Asym form.

**Evidence:** current D29-R26, P40 and the UX accessibility paragraph expressly name 200% zoom. The refreshed W3C Reflow guidance establishes a distinct 320 CSS-pixel test. D29 already promises narrow mobile, keyboard and shared controls, but those general promises do not identify a falsifiable reflow/focus/target boundary.

**Exact supplemental language for D29-R26 and its UX paragraph:**

> Preserve the existing 200% text-resize proof and separately prove reflow at an effective viewport width of 320 CSS pixels, including a 1280 CSS-pixel viewport at 400% zoom. The ordinary form, direct contact methods, optional guides, validation errors, persistent receipt and app-owned panel/full-page route must remain readable and operable without horizontal form scrolling or loss of controls. Long translated labels, international mailbox strings and RTL content must not overflow or conceal Send or contact alternatives. Existing shell headers, footers and overlays must not obscure keyboard focus. Pointer targets must satisfy WCAG 2.5.8's 24 CSS-pixel minimum or a valid documented exception; use a 44 CSS-pixel interaction area for the primary Send and app panel controls where applicable as the product's touch-usability target. Do not enlarge every inline text link or add a new density setting merely to satisfy this proof.

**Exact supplemental P40 acceptance criterion:**

> In both the public full-page form and qualified authenticated app presentation, complete initial entry, a validation-error correction and a successful persistent receipt at 200% text resizing and effective 320 CSS-pixel reflow/400% zoom. Repeat with long translated labels, a long supported email address and RTL text. Keyboard focus remains visible through the existing shell/panel; Send, context Remove, Copy email and maintainer reorder actions meet their actual target-size/spacing contract. Mobile virtual-keyboard opening leaves the focused input and a reachable submission path usable. Run named browser/screen-reader combinations and record actual results; no viewport-size screenshot alone proves completion.

This is one bounded amendment to accessibility qualification, not a new form layout, breakpoint system or preference feature. The virtual-keyboard journey is a user-visible proof extension; the browser's keyboard is not mislabeled as author-created content under WCAG 2.4.11.

## Maintenance and proof priorities

The existing maintenance sequence remains the best fit: identify the exact Help placement, select or repair its actual contact owner, select zero to three currently eligible source references, preview with synthetic data, publish/activate through the respective owner, and display current readiness without conflating publication with delivery. Removing all guides must remain a safe ordinary action. Route and guide failures should lead to their actual owner repair paths rather than support-team SQL cleanup or a blanket Help shutdown.

There is no evidence-based reason to add a public article taxonomy, a standalone contact administration database, department selection, behavioral recommendations, a new tenant-controlled breakpoints editor or an additional email-template editor. The existing bounded input numbers and three-guide limit remain explicit product choices; this source refresh neither verifies them as universal norms nor supplies tenant-volume evidence to change them.

Before release, preserve all D29 P01–P44 and add UX-RF01's exact P40 coverage. The current blueprint remains **untested as rendered software**. At minimum the existing P44 moderated study must observe a guest contacting without opening guidance, someone deliberately reading a guide and returning, a requester changing a prefilled reply address, a context-only expiry case, a phone-absent contact page, a no-JavaScript/slow-network recovery and a maintainer withdrawing the last guide while contact remains available. Test with synthetic data and authorized nonproduction paths. These scenarios elaborate the already-required journey proof, not a new selected capability.

**Final supplemental conclusion:** the accepted product decision, source ownership, direct-contact priority, minimal input, CRM continuity, optional acknowledgement and maintainer journey remain sound after the refresh. Record the narrow accessibility-proof amendment and retain explicit unexecuted browser/assistive-technology/usability evidence. Do not reopen A or treat new vendor features as a reason to expand it.
