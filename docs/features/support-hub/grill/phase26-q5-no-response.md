## The decision

**When staff cannot finish a request because the requester never answers, should Asym allow them to end follow-up—and who should make that decision?**

Maria asks Sarah which donation a receipt question concerns. The conversation becomes Waiting for requester. Sarah does not reply. When Maria's chosen reminder becomes due, D3 already returns it to Open for review. Maria checks the conversation and delivery evidence; no further promised Support action remains unless Sarah supplies the missing information.

The remaining decision is whether that conversation must continue waiting, whether Maria can deliberately end follow-up with a truthful No response outcome, or whether a timed policy can do it. This is an illustrative fixture, not an assertion about ministry workload or how many follow-ups are sufficient.

## Options and tradeoffs

| Option                                                               | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Benefit                                                                                                                                            | Cost and consequence                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Keep waiting until answered or completed**                     | An unanswered request remains active/waiting until the needed input arrives or staff can actually complete the request. Reminders can return it for review.                                                                                                             | Most conservative about ending assistance; avoids classifying silence as completion.                                                               | Some conversations may never finish, creating repeated review and an aging queue that mixes actionable and indefinitely blocked work. No-response ending is unavailable.                                                                          |
| **B — Staff explicitly ends follow-up as No response — Recommended** | After reviewing the outstanding request, commitments and delivery evidence, authorized staff may deliberately end follow-up. The existing Resolved work state carries a visible **No response** outcome and durable history; it is not counted as a confirmed solution. | Keeps the queue honest without letting elapsed time decide donor-care work. Preserves the difference between completing help and ending follow-up. | Requires a staff judgment and one deliberate action. The outcome must remain visible in history/reporting; misuse to clear queues must not conceal promised work or delivery recovery.                                                            |
| **C — Automatically end eligible unanswered requests**               | A tenant-configured policy sends its qualified reminders/notices and ends eligible conversations after a stated interval, with No response recorded and a new relevant reply reopening work.                                                                            | Reduces repetitive handling at sufficient volume and applies a consistent policy.                                                                  | Adds timer, delivery, exclusions, changed-policy and racing-reply complexity; silence can reflect missed mail or unavailable people. Requires Phase 34 vocabulary and Phase 6/17 message qualification. No day count or sequence is assumed here. |

All options retain accountless email continuation, owner authorization and D3 reopening on newly admitted relevant input. C is a credible mature-help-desk pattern, not inherently wrong; it needs stronger evidence of recurring Asym workload and a carefully qualified policy. It must not arise merely because a setting already appears in an old screen.

## Single best recommendation: B

**Choose staff-controlled ending with an explicit No response outcome.** It is the smallest complete way to prevent indefinite backlog while preserving human judgment about donor care, context and remaining commitments. It finishes the lifecycle without adding a fifth normal work state, a generic workflow engine or a mandatory chase sequence.

D3 defines Resolved as deliberate completion of the **Support obligation**, not proof that the underlying problem was fixed. B therefore makes the ending reason explicit beneath that existing work meaning. A restrained proposed display is **Resolved · No response**. The action could be labelled **End follow-up — No response**. This is not a silent ordinary Resolve action with a reason buried in notes. Exact interaction and enforcement receive the full adversarial review after the founder answers.

No response must not be included without qualification in “problems solved,” customer-success or first-contact-resolution reporting. A later relevant reply opens the same canonical conversation for review and preserves the earlier ending episode. The donor does not need to create a new ticket, log in or repeat the request solely because staff ended follow-up.

The strongest argument against B is manual burden and inconsistent judgments when large volumes recur. That makes C worth considering if actual operating evidence supports it. There is no measured Asym volume, demonstrated repeated follow-up burden or established tenant no-response policy here that justifies adopting that complexity now. A is stronger if the product deliberately promises to keep every unanswered request pending until a definite answer or completed result; that is a real choice, with its backlog consequence stated openly.

## Research that changes the recommendation

- **Front, ticket statuses, edited 22 April 2026, current Starter plan or above:** distinguishes waiting with intended proactive follow-up from resolved work needing no action unless the requester replies—even when requested information has not arrived. This supports separating active commitment from problem outcome. It does not establish Front has Asym's proposed No response reason model, and its overall status/Snooze mapping is not copied. [Official documentation](https://help.front.com/en/articles/1300288).
- **Zendesk, automated-reminder workflow recipe, indexed edit 1 May 2026:** documents a configurable reminder-and-solve sequence, including resetting the sequence when someone replies. Its illustrated calendar-day schedule is an example that the article explicitly allows changing. This supports C as a real alternative; it is not evidence that the example duration is suitable for Asym or a default all tenants receive. [Official recipe](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408832749210-Workflow-recipe-Sending-automated-ticket-reminders-to-customers).
- **Intercom, current Snooze guidance:** says to close fully resolved work, yet also permits closure after an illustrative week of silence because the person may have found a solution. That tension is exactly why Asym should distinguish No response from confirmed problem resolution. It supports deliberate review, not assuming silence proves success or importing a universal one-week threshold. Its product-specific reopen and timing defaults remain separate from ratified D3. [Official documentation](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/6564538-snooze-a-conversation).

The [complete source and independent challenge record](phase26-q5-evidence.md) also distinguishes automatic closure of an already resolved ticket from automatically ending a still-unanswered request. Those are different workflows. Vendor practices and community reports inform tradeoffs; none supplies Asym-specific measured user research or overrides Core's owner boundaries.

## Boundaries that every answer must preserve

1. **Silence is not a verified outcome.** No response cannot mean the donor's problem was fixed, a refund completed, a receipt issued or an identity confirmed.
2. **Remaining Support commitments remain work.** Ending follow-up cannot hide an owed promised action or an unreviewed failure/recovery obligation. An unrelated pending owner-domain process is not automatically a reason to keep every Support conversation open; assess the actual Support commitment and preserve the owner's independent authority.
3. **Delivery evidence matters.** Unsent, failed, suppressed or unknown mail is not proof that a requester ignored a question. The reviewer must handle that delivery state truthfully; no open/read tracking is treated as proof of understanding. This is not a demand for proof that a person read every message. Already-reviewed, irrecoverable delivery uncertainty is not an automatic requirement to keep a conversation open forever; retain that uncertainty and any actual remaining obligation without inventing successful contact.
4. **Current authorization and review apply.** A new reply or relevant change racing the ending cannot be overwritten. Use D3's canonical transition/history/reminder boundary; repeat/replayed actions cannot manufacture another episode.
5. **Ending is not deletion or irreversible Closed.** Preserve permitted history and discovery, cancel the applicable follow-up reminder under D3, and reopen on newly admitted relevant input. No archived record grants access to restricted CRM/care information.
6. **A disposition is not an email.** Selecting an ending action does not itself authorize a new automatic closing notice. Optional human correspondence uses D4. C would additionally require explicit notice/sequence qualification; it cannot reuse template insertion as send authority.
7. **No duration is smuggled in.** Reminder presets, minimum follow-up count, automatic-notice policy and SLA effects are separate choices. D4's seven-day prepared-material ceiling is not a requester-response deadline or auto-close timer.
