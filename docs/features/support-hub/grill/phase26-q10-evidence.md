## Why this is the next bounded decision

D9 established CRM discovery around canonical Support conversations. Staff consolidation changes which conversation carries current work, how original references behave, and what CRM displays. Resolving that product choice now avoids building the CRM experience around an accidental permanent inability to reconcile duplicate requests.

D1 transport dedupe, D2 audience/collision control and owner-domain Party merge do not settle whether staff may combine two valid conversations about one support obligation. The coverage register explicitly leaves conversation merge/split decisions open. General splitting, asking another team for help, service-target enforcement and quarantine operations remain meaningful later branches; bundling them into this question would ask several decisions at once. The already-ratified safety layer remains an activation prerequisite regardless of interview order.

No measured duplicate rate, ministry usability study or tenant staffing benchmark is available. Maya's repeated receipt question is a test scenario, not invented research. The recommendation is based on the demonstrated product goal of preventing duplicate replies and keeping email continuation natural, with a credible smaller alternative openly described.

## Reported friction and limitations

In [Front's Unmerge Feature discussion](https://community.front.com/workflows-discussion-47/unmerge-feature-3716), 8 April 2026, a participant described different shipment threads becoming entangled, with attachment/instruction and metric confusion. A Front specialist replied 9 April that the requested feature was unavailable; later discussion disputed what unmerge meant. Current official merge and separate-message-move documentation clarify the distinction.

This is attributable logistics feedback, not donor-care prevalence or an Asym incident. Its relevant failure pattern is treating separate obligations as duplicates without usable correction. [Front's move/split documentation](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2307), 26 February 2026, describes moving one selected email message versus broader non-email splitting. Those operations are not a complete restoration of all pre-merge metadata, recipients, privacy and subsequent actions.

Therefore safe correction is required for a merge feature; generic topic splitting remains a separate decision. Neither adding a Split button nor promising Undo guarantees that a mistaken merge can recall disclosed mail or restore obsolete state.

## Independent challenge and final recommendation

The repository, lifecycle and vendor reviews independently identified duplicate handling as the strongest next question. Initial alternatives varied: two reviews treated close-with-reference as part of keeping records separate, while another proposed a durable duplicate relation that would itself need continuation routing.

The final three alternatives remove that ambiguity:

1. **A:** keep independent work and ordinary coordination.
2. **B:** resolve one with a reference; keep independent routes, so genuine later input reopens the original for review.
3. **C:** reviewed consolidation; one continuing work item receives qualified later input from either original route.

All three are genuine choices. B is smaller because it does not promise enduring continuation routing, not because it can ignore later input. C is the single recommendation because it addresses recurring duplicate coordination and fits D9's canonical CRM discovery. Merely related requests remain separate. No review found a governing conflict with this narrow choice if source identity, current permissions, work obligations and correction are preserved.

No broad auto-merge, similarity/AI service, bulk merge, cross-channel catalogue, primary Party, new CRM store or general split engine is implied. The full review after selection must settle admissibility, current preview/commit, private drafts/in-flight mail, source-history placement, CRM link provenance, status/reminder reconciliation, original-route continuation, correction and measurable release proof. Those are not requirements left to informal implementation after ratification; they are the consequences to resolve in the next selected-answer review.
