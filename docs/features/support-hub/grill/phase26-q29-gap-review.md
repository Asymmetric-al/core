# Q29 — Independent next-gap review: requester help discovery

**13 September 2026. D1–D28, including D27-C and all D28 amendments, are fully founder-ratified by the latest instruction; the root is recording D28. Q29 is unanswered.** This is an independent next-question recommendation, not a new accepted decision, formal specification or implementation.

## Recommended next question

**When someone opens Help, how should they find useful public guidance alongside contacting the tenant's Support team?**

Recommend **A — Answers alongside Contact support**: a compact, optional way to browse/search deliberately qualified public guidance at the existing Help entry, with a direct Contact support action always available. This makes maintained answers accessible where a requester is already seeking help without demanding another destination or a search before human contact.

Illustrative example, not an observed ministry workflow: Maya opens Help because she cannot find a giving statement. She can read an approved public explanation of where statements are found, or contact the tenant immediately. Any actual statement access stays in its owning authenticated surface. If the explanation does not answer her question, moving to contact should retain her place and any entered message without importing private activity into public search.

The remaining choice is therefore **where and how much public help discovery Phase 26 should present**, not whether Asym needs another CMS, whether staff guides should be made public, or whether a visitor must become a CRM contact to ask a question.

## Three real options

These are competing Phase 26 directions, not three configuration modes that must all be built.

| Option                                                  | Requester journey                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Strength                                                                                                                                                                     | Cost and tradeoff                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Answers alongside Contact support — recommended** | At the existing Help entry, see a compact set of deliberately selected public answers and a way to search that qualified help content. Contact support remains available immediately.                                              | Puts useful answers close to the task and preserves easy human help. Fits the whole-product Help journey without requiring another help-center home.                         | Requires a maintained public help selection and careful integration with the contact form, safe context, language, search and return state. Sparse content must never turn Help into an empty detour.                                                                                                               |
| **B — Direct contact and ordinary website guidance**    | Help goes straight to the qualified contact experience. Public instructions remain available through normal Web Studio pages, links and the existing public site's discovery features; Phase 26 adds no requester guidance finder. | Smallest additional scope and most direct route to a person. Strong when the tenant has little reusable public material or its common questions require individual handling. | A requester may not encounter an existing answer at the moment of contact, and staff may repeatedly supply links already available elsewhere. This does not remove D27 staff Guidance or ordinary public content.                                                                                                   |
| **C — A dedicated public Help center**                  | Offer a maintained tenant-branded help destination with article/topic browsing, search and a direct contact route, using the same qualified CMS sources.                                                                           | Strongest destination for a substantial library people need to explore independently, bookmark or revisit.                                                                   | Adds a distinct public navigation/discovery experience and a larger content organization and maintenance commitment. It must reuse Web Studio/public search, not create a second article publisher or duplicate site identity. No evidence yet proves the larger destination is necessary for typical Asym tenants. |

**Why A over the strongest alternative B:** D27 already recognizes the value of maintained guidance. Making a bounded publicly eligible subset visible at the moment someone asks for help can remove an unnecessary staff round trip while keeping B's direct contact route. That benefit is conditional on useful content; it is not a reason to hide contact or force article consumption. C becomes stronger when an actual broad library and independent browsing need are demonstrated, but those facts have not been established.

## Current primary comparisons

**Help Scout Beacon provides a particularly direct interaction comparison.** Its official article, updated **3 September 2026**, distinguishes Neutral (articles and contact options available), Ask First (contact opens first) and Self Service (the visitor must search or interact with an article before contact appears). This verifies that placement and contact friction are real product choices. A borrows the neutral availability principle; B is closer to contact-first. Asym should not import the forced-content gate, which conflicts with the accepted direct-help intent. AI mode limitations in that article do not apply because Q29 proposes no AI. [Work With Beacon Modes](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1296-work-with-beacon-modes)

Help Scout also documents limiting a Beacon to selected public collections and deliberately selecting suggestions. It warns that restricting collections affects which article links can open inside Beacon. That is useful evidence that source eligibility and presentation restrictions can create broken help journeys; it is not authority to copy Help Scout's collection data model or one-site limit into Asym. [Manage Beacon Docs Settings](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1231-manage-beacon-docs-settings), [Customize Content Suggestions](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1264-customize-content-suggestions-in-beacon)

**A dedicated destination is a real larger alternative.** HubSpot's current knowledge-base theme documentation, updated **2 April 2026**, describes homepage, article, navigation, language-switcher and search-bar layouts for migrated knowledge bases. It identifies **Service Hub Professional/Enterprise**, an assigned Service Hub seat and specific permissions. This supports C's distinct navigation/maintenance scope, not an Asym requirement for a separate theme editor, paid vendor dependency or CRM synchronization. [HubSpot knowledge-base theme](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/knowledge-base/customize-a-knowledge-base-theme)

**Zendesk supplies supporting historical-product evidence, with a version caveat.** Its July 2026 article explicitly describes **Web Widget (Classic) as legacy** and distinguishes it from the newer messaging widget. The Classic experience combines article search and a contact form whose submissions receive email replies. This supports a coherent guidance-to-email-contact journey, but its widget implementation, feature sequence and channel suite are not a modern architecture recommendation for Core. [Zendesk Web Widget (Classic)](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408836216218-Using-Web-Widget-Classic-to-embed-customer-service-in-your-website)

These sources establish documented workflows, not observed Asym usability, nonprofit demand, deflection rate or satisfaction improvement. No vendor's undocumented database or CRM architecture is inferred.

## Boundaries to explain in the question, then research after selection

All three choices preserve direct Contact support and ordinary email continuation. None includes a requester conversation archive, a forced search gate, AI answers, live chat, a CMS-private audience or public publication of internal guides.

If A or C is selected, the full review must resolve exact public selection/maintenance authority, Site/locale behavior, safe optional page/task context, source withdrawal, source/search failure, mobile and keyboard journeys, preservation of entered contact content, truthful intake success and qualified owner handoff. Contextual help must not mean automatically feeding a donor's private record or contact-form text into public search. A public browser query must not become CRM personalization or analytics identity.

Those are selected-answer consequences to flesh out, not unasked decisions about taxonomy, exact article counts, layout pixels, schemas, route names or another configurable builder. This question does not claim a tenant should publish new guidance merely to fill an empty Help interface. Direct contact remains useful when no eligible guidance is available.
