## Why this is the next unresolved dependency

[D6](phase26-d6-adversarial-review.md) governs new intake, preserving existing ownership when receiving is Off and explicitly reserving later absence/departure policy. [D3](phase26-d3-adversarial-review.md) makes relevant new input, due reminders and actionable owner/recovery evidence reviewable work; [D5](phase26-d5-adversarial-review.md) permits a resolved episode to reopen without a new-ticket barrier. Therefore a person's existing portfolio can need attention after the moment a pre-leave handoff was prepared.

The decision concerns **timing and amount of existing-work transfer during a deliberate temporary absence**. It is not another choice of initial routing mode or a way to reinterpret Receiving Off. Calendar scheduling, temporary backup roles, permanent offboarding, service commitments and donor notices are separate concepts and should not be silently bundled into this question.

## Independent challenge and final synthesis

The initial hypothesis was A, reviewed handoff, because it reuses one current assignment and requires less automatic policy. The Core-only review still preferred A with genuine ongoing human shared coverage. Its benefits remain represented fairly in the final options; absence automation is not justified merely by vendor feature parity.

The lifecycle and vendor reviews exposed the decisive counterexample: today's unfinished portfolio does not include every old Resolved/No response conversation that can reopen next week. Both waiting sides can also become Open through a due reminder or owner/recovery evidence without another donor email. A can address that only through continuing human monitoring beyond the handoff snapshot. Root recommends B because an explicit bounded coverage cause can consistently surface this later work while preserving quiet waits, fitting the user's emphasis on effortless dependable follow-through.

The offered C was refined from a temporary-backup ownership model to **all unfinished upfront plus later-reopen coverage**. That compares one coherent dimension against B: transfer the whole unfinished portfolio now or only Open now/when it becomes Open. C is strongest where the tenant wants the full portfolio under the covering team's responsibility immediately. Both choices keep one effective current assignee and actual D3 statuses/reminders.

Mandatory qualifications for the selected answer's review:

1. Receiving Off alone remains assignment-neutral; separate coverage intent must be explicit and visible.
2. Treat all current D3 Open, including deferred Open, honestly. Preserve reminders and avoid claiming all transferred work is immediately urgent.
3. Handle previously Resolved reopenings, due reminders and actionable owner/recovery facts as well as requester replies. Auto-replies, unrelated noise and duplicate events are not a new reason to move ownership.
4. Later reassignment uses a distinct coverage cause and current conditional control, never a second initial source or a broad D6 intake sweep. Responsible-inbox Shared is the proposed fallback, not permission to rerun default round-robin on old work.
5. Return/end of coverage does not silently take conversations back from current owners. Late jobs, settings changes, intervening claims/moves/endings, explicit manual assignment during coverage and Pause semantics require precise temporal/concurrency review.
6. Coverage grants no CRM/financial/member-care rights, does not copy another person's private draft and sends no donor message. Known inaccessible work needs safe qualified custody, not a fabricated recipient.
7. A shared queue still needs named coverage. If there is no qualified cover, automation is paused or a transition fails, show incomplete coverage and retain discovery; do not claim Unassigned means answered.

This is enough evidence to ask the founder one consequential question. It deliberately stops short of freezing a calendar schema, duration presets, exception taxonomy, workforce system or complete D7 requirements before an answer.
