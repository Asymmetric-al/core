## Why this is a real product fork

Staff already need to inspect actual attachments received with Support work. D16 governs source restriction and private delivery. D19 allows separately qualified file inspection during intake review. D22 allows a filename match even when its file type cannot be previewed. Those clauses are compatible with several preview scopes; none requires either a full Office viewer or download-only handling.

An illustrative worker investigating a screenshot and an attached PDF needs to examine the files without confusing the attachment with a CRM receipt or verified business record. The screenshot/PDF example is a design fixture, not a claim about the frequency or sensitivity of actual ministry documents. Viewing an attachment remains distinct from extracting authoritative financial facts or completing an owning-domain action.

The unresolved choice is **which formats receive an in-context inspection experience**, and therefore the everyday journey and maintenance burden. It is not whether to scan files, authorize bytes, honor redaction or implement recovery; those requirements remain mandatory under every option. Correcting the current filename-only/staged-metadata UI is also not optional release safety.

## Proposed question and three fair options

**Question:** When staff open a file attached to a Support conversation, how much should they be able to inspect without leaving Support?

All options preserve safe metadata, exact original byte/version identity, current permissions, scanning/readiness, redaction/retention, honest errors and separately authorized download. Metadata itself must be disclosable; a blocked/private filename is not automatically shown, and its owner supplies a neutral or unavailable state where appropriate. No option automatically previews a file on opening a conversation or finding its name. A missing or failed preview must never trigger an automatic download or imply a file is safe. These are alternative inspection scopes, not three tenant modes.

| Option                                                 | Staff experience                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Strongest benefit                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Main tradeoff                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Preview common images and PDFs in Support**      | A deliberate attachment action opens a read-only in-context viewer for the supported common image/PDF set. The worker returns to the same request/draft. Other or unsuitable files retain clear metadata and a qualified download action when permitted. | Convenient inspection of a bounded, useful set without building a general document workspace. It can avoid unnecessary application switching or saved local copies, while making no promise that browser access leaves no local data. | A maintained rendering/accessibility/compatibility surface is still required, and some permitted files need an external viewer.                                                                                                 |
| **B — Show file details and download when needed**     | Clear filename/type/size/status and an explicit qualified download action; staff inspect the file with their chosen local application. No in-Support attachment viewer.                                                                                  | Smallest supported viewer surface and full use of installed tools, especially for specialized formats.                                                                                                                                | More switching and local-copy handling for ordinary inspection; the device may lack the needed application. Download safeguards remain just as necessary.                                                                       |
| **C — Common previews plus broader document previews** | Include A and deliberately support additional useful document formats, such as approved office documents/spreadsheets, through the same source/Phase-29-compatible preview boundary. Qualified download remains available where allowed.                 | Fewer interruptions for teams that regularly receive varied documents and need to inspect them in the request context.                                                                                                                | Adds rendering/conversion fidelity, accessibility, unsupported-feature, derivative-custody and dependency maintenance. It is a larger supported-format commitment, not permission to execute macros or build a document editor. |

**Single recommendation: A.** It provides a useful bounded inspection experience while keeping B's qualified download route for exceptions. C is a credible broader choice, but no actual Asym file-format distribution or staff study presently establishes that its additional renderers are needed. The exact image formats, PDF capabilities, budgets and viewer behavior belong in the selected answer's full review; the question should not silently freeze a library, arbitrary limit, conversion provider or browser-only fallback.

No option changes public requester channels, creates file-content search/OCR/AI, edits attachment bytes, auto-extracts donor/financial facts or copies an attachment into CRM as another authoritative record. Preview support and permission to receive, download, send or retain a format are distinct decisions.

## Current primary research supporting the comparison

- [Intercom: The Inbox explained](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/6258745-the-inbox-explained), opened 13 September 2026, documents an image lightbox for images/attachment uploads with zoom. This supports an in-context visual-inspection pattern. It does not prove PDF or Office support, a universal format set or Asym demand.
- [Front: How to download and preview attachments](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2412), edited 11 August 2026 and opened during this review, distinguishes Mac app QuickLook preview, Windows app temporary download/local-application opening, and web preview in a new tab. This is strong evidence that the word Preview does not guarantee the same journey or locality on every surface. Do not cite Front as proof that its Windows app supplies A's in-Support PDF viewer.
- [Zendesk: Allowing attachments in tickets](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408832757146-Allowing-attachments-in-tickets), opened 13 September 2026, documents linked versus real attachments, first-page image presentation and separately configurable private downloads. This reinforces that a thumbnail, full preview, byte download and email delivery are different capabilities. Its bearer-link/privacy and administrator malware-override policies are not adopted; Core's accepted source/security rules govern.

These verified vendor behaviors establish meaningful alternatives and known friction boundaries. They do not establish that A is empirically optimal for every ministry. The root's separate vendor/UX research can supply stronger broader-document comparisons and reported user friction; it must distinguish user reports from product guarantees and avoid substituting a Zendesk-hosted customer's help site for Zendesk's own documentation.

P17/Email Studio owns actual outgoing content/preparation and P6 actual delivery; viewing a file does not stage it into a reply, change its audience, attach it to a template or send it.

CRM uses the same authorized conversation/attachment source, not a synced copy or a permission grant from the linked Party. Giving, receipts, identity documents and protected care retain their actual owners and purposes. A preview is evidence someone could inspect bytes, not proof an underlying business action occurred or that the document is authoritative.
