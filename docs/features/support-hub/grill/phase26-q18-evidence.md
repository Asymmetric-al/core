## Feedback and uncertainty

A [HubSpot Ideas discussion](https://community.hubspot.com/t/enable-snippets-to-be-made-private-or-shared-with-team/21559) contains historical reports of unrelated snippet clutter and accidental shared editing concerns. A [Zendesk community request](https://community.zendesk.com/ideas/allow-admins-ability-to-manage-delete-personal-or-individual-macros-in-the-user-interface-2261) reports personal-macro management friction. These are useful first-person signals, not current feature inventories, prevalence estimates or causal proof of productivity gains. Official current mechanisms carry the factual comparison.

No verified Asym staff study establishes how often personal variants are needed. B is therefore a product judgment about flexible everyday writing, not an empirical claim that ministries universally want personal libraries. A would be preferable if minimizing duplication and maintenance scope outweighs personal reuse; C would be preferable if broad collaborative authorship is the tenant's deliberate operating model.

## Independent challenge and reconciled recommendation

The [repository gap review](phase26-q18-scope-review.md) established that this is an unresolved fork and caught four important scope errors before presentation: do not re-ask D4 insertion, do not add another approval pipeline, do not treat nullable ownership as privacy proof, and do not turn Save as reply into a D16/D17 archive bypass. The vendor review independently recommends B with A as the strongest simpler alternative.

The final question resolves the overlaps as follows:

1. **Genuine alternatives:** A has a maintained shared collection only; B adds personal reuse plus deliberate shared contribution; C broadens ordinary shared-publication capability to eligible Support authors. C still has deliberate publication; unpublished drafts do not automatically become visible merely because more people may publish.
2. **Proportionate governance:** suitable staff may maintain Shared through existing capabilities. Ordinary qualified self-publication remains available; protected and stricter tenant review remain intact. No new job-title hierarchy or arbitrary approval steps.
3. **Clear personal meaning:** My replies belongs to the current tenant's governed authoring assets. It means personal ordinary use, not cross-tenant portability, private CRM storage or a promise that no qualified administrator can exercise legitimate custody.
4. **One coherent journey:** one scope-aware picker within D4, clean new authoring, editable insertions and deliberate shared candidates. The owner model supplies safety; no new editor, vendor or runtime publishing dependency is selected.
5. **No stale policy copies by default:** keep common guidance convenient to reuse from Shared. Personal convenience does not justify automatic cloning/synchronization of every official response. New insertions follow current eligible source revisions; existing draft/prepared/history semantics remain D4's.
6. **No retention bypass:** source-derived wording/files/links require current authorization and honest retained dependencies. The simplest creation journey starts with generic reusable authoring. A transcript-derived convenience action is not implied before its permanent source-safe design is qualified.
7. **No premature policy detail:** private/shared short-code collisions, exact retirement and departure handling, applicable asset review/retention, safe assets, concurrency and accessibility belong in the selected answer's full review. Their governing safety boundaries are stated now; they are not separate founder database questions.

B offers the best balance because individual stylistic reuse and maintained team guidance have different ordinary audiences. The added ownership scope is bounded and fits an existing authoring owner. It is not automatically worth adding folders, analytics, translation automation, artificial item limits, a knowledge system or macros with business actions.
