## The question

**What should the main reply button do by default?**

Maria might send Sarah three different replies:

- “Which gift do you mean?” — needed requester input.
- “Finance is still checking.” — a progress update while the ministry awaits its own result.
- “Here is the answer.” — a reply that completes the Support obligation, if no other promised step remains.

All three are ordinary outgoing emails. D3 establishes that their work meanings differ. Q4 chooses which action occupies the primary button; every option retains explicit alternatives for other replies. These are illustrative fixtures, not a claim about the measured distribution of ministry support traffic.

| Option                                                | Main button and behavior                                                                                                       | Benefit                                                                                   | Tradeoff                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Send reply; keep current status — recommended** | **Send reply** admits the reviewed reply without performing a work-status transition or cancelling a valid follow-up reminder. | Predictable for progress updates and conversations whose current status is already right. | When the reply changes the next obligation, staff deliberately choose a combined alternative or update status separately.                     |
| **B — Send and wait for requester**                   | The primary action explicitly admits the reply and sets **Waiting for requester**, when eligible under D3.                     | Fast for exchanges asking the requester for needed information.                           | Progress updates, our-side waits and completed answers require another choice. It must not treat every outgoing message as requiring a reply. |
| **C — Send and resolve**                              | The primary action explicitly admits the reply and resolves eligible Support work, cancelling its current reminder under D3.   | Fast for complete answers and keeps the active queue focused.                             | Staff must select another action for questions, investigations and outstanding promises; habitual misuse can prematurely finish work.         |

**Recommend A.** D3 deliberately distinguishes different kinds of work. A stable Send reply meaning respects the status staff have already chosen; clearly named combined actions retain the convenience of sending and updating work together. This is a practical Asym product judgment, not an industry consensus or a quantified claim that it minimizes all clicks.

The strongest alternative is C. It is a mature help-desk convention when most replies finish the request. B is defensible for information-gathering workflows, but the evidence does not establish that requester questions dominate Asym's reply mix. None is automatically forbidden: a button visibly saying Send and resolve is explicit staff intent, not prohibited inference from message direction. It still must satisfy D3 eligibility and D1/D2 sending authority.

## The proposed compact interaction

One primary button and an adjacent **More send options** menu keep the composer quiet. Full action names describe the effect: Send reply, Send and set Open, Send and wait for requester, Send and wait on our side, Send and resolve. The available actions must reflect actual authority and work eligibility. No five-button toolbar, mandatory questionnaire or default inferred by AI is needed.

Under the recommended model, the menu's explicitly named combined commands apply to the current reply; they do not silently become the next draft's default. The primary button and keyboard submission must agree. Opening or navigating a menu does not send. Whether a final design uses direct command selection or stages a choice before the final click must be coherent and visible; the answer's adversarial review should choose one interaction consistently rather than mix both.

The existing D2 personal preference still controls Reply all versus Reply to sender. It does not automatically introduce another personal/inbox/team default for work status, assignment or navigation. No such preference hierarchy or remembered-last-action behavior is selected by this question. If a later founder answer requests a personal send default, it must be explicit, visibly reflected in the button and reviewed with the same stable-draft principles.

## Critical meaning of “keep status”

**Preserving means no work transition.** It does not mean writing the status from the beginning of composition back over a newer current state. D1/D2/D3 still validate the reviewed conversation and reject relevant stale approvals. An old successful command receipt cannot overwrite a later Open state caused by new input.

Plain Send is also not **Send and set Open**. A progress message while Waiting on our side preserves that wait and its valid reminder. An explicitly chosen Open action clears deferral under D3. A waiting-side-only change preserves its reminder; Resolve cancels it. The button must not hide these distinctions behind a generic label.

This choice does not force a fake reopen/resolution cycle for every message on already-resolved work. A genuinely new Support obligation must be represented explicitly, while a permissible courtesy/final clarification need not manufacture a historical completion episode. The founder's selected default will receive the full eligibility/UX review; message text alone cannot determine that distinction.

## Required boundaries under every option

1. **Sending and work intent share one reviewed local admission.** A combined command validates recipient/content authority and D3 work eligibility before admitting its immutable reply, work change, reminder effects and evidence. It cannot be implemented as Send followed by an unrelated status PATCH with hidden partial success.
2. **Local atomicity does not include remote final delivery.** Success means the qualified product operation was durably accepted. Queued, sending, delivered, bounced and indeterminate remain separate truthful member-level facts. A later failure follows D3 causal review without erasing the prior action.
3. **Retries preserve effect identity.** Do not resend a message to retry a status update. Changed audience/content/action cannot reuse an old approval. Original operation result is distinct from current work state.
4. **D2 audience remains explicit.** No selected work action adds recipients, follows a changed preference, reveals internal notes or substitutes a CRM address.
5. **Known invalid combined actions do not quietly degrade.** Retain the draft and explain the blocked combination. Staff may explicitly choose another eligible action; the system does not send anyway while ignoring the requested outcome.
6. **No unrelated side effects.** A Send default does not assign staff, change CRM/giving facts, create a reminder duration, advance the selected conversation or activate a new automation engine. Normal email remains accountless for the requester.
