# D17 independent vendor and UX review

**Independent review input:** The [reconciled D17 decision](phase26-d17-adversarial-review.md) and [final UX](phase26-d17-retention-ux.md) govern adopted amendments. Calendar-unit, pause and other candidate alternatives below are preserved review history, not competing final requirements. D17 amendments remain proposed pending ratification.

Research date: 11 September 2026. Scope: the selected ordinary default, automatic expiry after Support work ends, plus tenant policy administration and CRM continuity. This is an independent review for the parent synthesis, not a ratified decision or implemented UI. No provider accounts, product data, or governed documents were mutated. Existing Q17 evidence was read before targeted new research.

## Disposition

**Accept with required amendments. A is the strongest ordinary default.** The benefit is predictable disposal without recurring review work, while staff retain relevant context during legitimate work. That is a product judgment informed by actual product patterns, not a ministry user-study result or a legal rule. The strongest alternative remains reviewed disposal at the same eligibility boundary: it permits more human judgment but creates a recurring queue and delay risk. Individual-message age is more predictable for each item but can fragment active context. Neither alternative removes the need for D16's authoritative source restrictions, actual preservation handling and truthful cleanup evidence.

Use a small policy experience: one tenant default for ordinary Support text and files, explicit activation, a current impact preview, and quiet content-expiry markers across Support and CRM. Do not turn configuration into a generic workflow builder, a new records-management product, or deletion of CRM people and financial history.

## New consequential evidence and qualifications

### Kustomer now supplies direct recurring-rule evidence

[Manage your platform usage data](https://help.kustomer.com/en_us/manage-your-platform-usage-data-HJkfk28Kkx), official article marked January 2026. Direct page extraction returned no body; targeted searches restricted to this exact official URL returned the article text. It documents daily deletion rules, activation/deactivation and audit history. Conversation deletion removes nested notes and attachments, while separate custom objects, exemplified by an order, need their own rule. Its updatedAt basis, 24-month floor and per-day limit are vendor constraints, not Asym requirements. This **supersedes the limited Q17 search observation** that no stronger native Kustomer policy UI had been verified. It does not invalidate Q17's choice.

Adopt the separation between conversation payload and independently owned business objects, plus attributable policy changes. Reject updatedAt as the ordinary clock. Do not copy its storage-cost framing as the only retention purpose or its numerical limits as proven capacity for Core. The page delegates plan availability to pricing; no exact entitlement was independently verified.

### Zendesk supports preview and deliberate activation, but reveals clock footguns

[Creating ticket deletion schedules](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/6388012977306-Creating-ticket-deletion-schedules) documents approximate preview and inactive schedule creation. Redaction and attachment removal can reset a last-updated schedule. [Managing deletion schedules](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/8301879320474-Managing-deletion-schedules) makes activation separate and asks administrators to acknowledge immediate and ongoing effects. Advanced Data Privacy and Protection changes schedule availability. Adopt reviewed activation; make Asym's preview mandatory for consequential policy changes. Reject the metadata clock and unnecessary AND/OR rule builder. Whole-ticket deletion is broader than Core content expiry. No vendor performance assertion or count limit was independently tested.

### Front supports a concise default with controlled inbox exceptions

[Enabling a data deletion policy](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2083), edited 24 August 2026, says company admins configure a default; enabled shared-inbox overrides are managed by workspace admins. The last message/comment determines inactivity. Multiple inboxes choose the longest period, including Never. A separate trash interval affects the actual disposal date, and an optional setting removes unused contacts. Adopt visible effective policy and purposeful, centrally controlled exceptions. Reject longest-wins, Never, silent routing-based reclassification, contact deletion and hidden date arithmetic. A's required work-ended boundary is an Asym judgment, not a claim that Front enforces that same gate.

### Intercom distinguishes content from reporting, with unsafe cross-work cascades for Core

[Deleting a conversation](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/13885146-deleting-a-conversation), 12 March 2026, documents API-only conversation deletion retaining metrics by default but removing data attributes that may contain sensitive content. Removing metrics has an Unstable-version/scope caveat. Linked back-office tickets and side conversations cascade. Adopt explicit separation of approved reporting facts from payload. Reject cascade into Asym's delegated business actions and retention of arbitrary free text as metrics. This article establishes an API pattern, not a native ordinary-retention settings UI.

[Call recordings and transcripts](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/8488923-call-recordings-and-transcripts), current official documentation, uses an explicit automatic-deletion control with a number of days. This is useful evidence for straightforward duration controls only. Phone recording rules are not an email conversation policy, and adding telephony is outside D17.

### Donor CRM evidence shows why expiry must not invoke record deletion

[Bloomerang interactions](https://help.bloomerang.com/en/articles/12632737-view-add-edit-and-delete-interactions), displayed as updated this week, describes timeline communication records linked to transactions. Deleting an interaction created from a task reactivates that task; deleting an acknowledgement interaction does not itself reset its transaction's acknowledgement status. [Bulk Delete Constituents](https://help.bloomerang.com/en/articles/12632758-bulk-delete-constituents), 2 January 2026, removes associated transactions and timeline data with the constituent. These are verified product behaviors, not Core recommendations. Asym should expire payload through its owner without calling CRM interaction/Party/task deletion. The examples demonstrate consequential coupling; they establish no general donor-care retention interval.

### Other researched products bound the comparison

[Zoho Desk Data Retention](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/desk/user-management-and-security/data-security/articles/data-retention) describes active storage, trash, backups and external integration handling separately. [Using the Recycle Bin](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/desk/data-administration/recycle-bin/articles/using-the-recycle-bin) describes recoverable deleted records followed by permanent disposal. These are not a universal age limit for active conversations. Use truthful state distinctions; do not transplant vendor trash/account-termination periods.

[HubSpot Help Desk](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/help-desk/manage-tickets-in-help-desk), verified in the prior Q17 research, demonstrates CRM associations and consequential reopened-ticket dates. Its separate Conversations Inbox deletion behavior must not be generalized to every Service Hub operation. This pass found no stronger relevant native age-policy behavior; that is a research limit, not proof of absence.

[Freshdesk archiving](https://support.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/212901-ticket-archiving), verified in Q17, preserves readable history and affects reply behavior. Archive is therefore not expiry. Help Scout's early-access masking retention and Kustomer's platform privacy statement are weaker fits than the direct recurring-rule evidence above. No additional competing retention platform is needed.

### Accessibility and proportional confirmation

[W3C error prevention, SC3.3.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/error-prevention-legal-financial-data.html) supports reviewing, confirming and correcting consequential data changes; it does not require confirmation for every ordinary save. [W3C status messages, SC4.1.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html) supports exposing meaningful asynchronous results without inappropriate focus changes. These are normative criteria with informative explanations, not evidence of a tested Asym UI. Use one consequential policy review, inline validation and accessible completion status; avoid default typed phrases, repeated modal stacks, noisy progress announcements or routine disposal emails.

No reliable current user study establishes a universally optimal ministry duration or policy UI. Earlier community anecdotes are not promoted to verified product behavior. The recommendation rests on independently documented mechanisms and Core's existing domain boundaries, with usability acceptance evidence still required.

## Recommended exact policy experience

These are proposed product requirements. Parent must reconcile exact duration/clock semantics with the lifecycle review before adopting them.

1. **Placement:** one `Support content retention` section within the existing tenant settings hierarchy, linked from Support settings. Reuse the shared admin shell, controls, semantic colors and permission model. Do not create a standalone compliance dashboard or duplicate a policy editor inside CRM.
2. **Ordinary form:** `Keep content for` followed by a positive whole-number value and an explicit unit. Prefer Days / Months / Years because administrators may have policies written in those units. Keep the initial value blank until the responsible tenant owner chooses it. No vendor-derived numeric default or supposedly recommended preset. Calendar units require explicit end-of-month, leap-year and timezone behavior and example dates; no silent conversion of a year to365days. If root chooses a simpler days-only contract, label it `elapsed days` consistently, explain weekends count, and do not masquerade as calendar years.
3. **Default scope:** ordinary Support message bodies, internal-note bodies and files share one period. Separate attachment timing is not justified as mandatory configuration by current Asym evidence. A stricter existing owner classification still applies; adding a class-specific schedule requires an actual purpose, not file-extension inference.
4. **Policy explanation:** `After Support work ends, message text and files become eligible for permanent removal after this period. Approved interaction history and records owned by other parts of Asym follow their own policies.` Link a concise `What stays?` disclosure. Do not claim all remaining metadata is anonymous or kept forever.
5. **One effective default:** tenant policy is the default. If an inbox has an approved different content purpose, show `Uses tenant policy` or a named finite exception, with a read-only effective summary to ordinary inbox administrators. Retention-policy capability is distinct from routine inbox management. Scope selection is not implicitly inherited from current assignee, CRM owner, donor category or message recipients.
6. **No silent reclassification:** the original source's approved policy class determines its eligibility. Moving it to another inbox, merging, linking or undoing a merge does not extend its lifetime. An explicit authorized reclassification is a policy action with a before/after impact, not an incidental consequence of a move. No longest-period/never-wins algorithm.
7. **Draft and review:** editing creates an unsaved/draft proposal and has no disposal effect. `Review changes` leads to one review panel/page. It shows previous value, new value, effective scope, whether existing content is included, planned activation, and impact calculated from the same eligibility rules used by execution. No false checkbox suggesting a legal certification has happened.
8. **Existing history:** normal approved scope should explicitly cover both existing retained material and future intake. Preview separates content that is already eligible under the proposal from content that becomes eligible later. Do not quietly exempt old history forever or silently purge it at first save. A staged legacy rollout, if required, is an explicitly approved bounded migration with a deadline, not an unexplained prospective-only loophole.
9. **Preview units:** label distinct original conversations, message/note bodies and files separately where those measures are shown. Do not call a merged group one original or a count of conversations a count of attachments. Counts may be estimates only when labelled as such, timestamped, with known exclusions/completion. A qualified policy administrator may see allowed scope totals without gaining source-body access; content samples require separate read authorization. No subject, filename, identity or hidden-inbox disclosure through a preview.
10. **Confirmation:** final action `Activate policy` or `Apply retention change`. State plainly whether already-old material can begin expiring immediately. Do not make a silent autosave destructive. One explicit review/confirm action is the default; a second approver or typed phrase is not universal. An existing stronger platform requirement still applies if it actually governs the action.
11. **Shorter and longer changes:** both are audited policy revisions. Shortening requires a fresh destructive-impact preview. Lengthening must explain that it applies only where content is still retainable and cannot recover content already removed or override a different mandatory limit. Concurrent policy edits cannot silently overwrite each other.
12. **Operational view:** show active revision, last successful processing result and any actionable failure/exception. Distinguish `Eligible from` from completed cleanup; do not promise that every provider/backup byte disappears at the displayed date. A bounded pause affects only work not past its authoritative irreversible boundary and never restores content; pausing ordinary expiry does not stop D16 correction or mandatory owner disposal.
13. **Conversation detail:** a quiet `Retention details` disclosure can show effective period, original source scope, eligibility status/date and a permitted exception explanation. Avoid an always-visible ticking countdown. Staff should not need this panel to reply or resolve work.
14. **After expiry:** display `Content expired under your retention policy` in the same source-backed presentation in Support and CRM. Preserve permitted facts and original order. Replace unsafe subject/excerpt/filename fields too. Use a generic safe heading if a subject expired. An expired attachment is not a broken download. Search returns only approved remaining facts; it cannot match expired content.
15. **Cross-surface navigation:** opening Support from CRM preserves the user's CRM place; returning preserves filters and scroll. A lack of access to Support is not the same state as expired content. Expiry must not expose whether restricted unrelated records exist.
16. **Reopened work:** when policy permits new work after content expiry, show a quiet explanation that older content has expired and retain current conversation/work identity rules. Do not repopulate old content from an email provider, merge snapshot, backup, AI summary or copied CRM activity. Requester-facing communication, if needed for the actual support task, is a deliberate staff reply rather than an automated expiry notification.
17. **Visual craft:** clear section rhythm, compact summary rows, neutral ordinary states and one semantic destructive action. Do not use red to decorate ordinary active policy; reserve stronger visual emphasis for the specific irreversible confirmation or actionable error. Use shared Core components, not a second style system. Dense tables collapse into labelled rows on mobile; no horizontal-scroll dependency for the period/scope/impact needed to approve.
18. **Input/accessibility:** persistent labels and unit association, keyboard completion, visible focus, announced inline errors, preserved draft on transient failure, no color-only distinctions, accessible status without every-count screen-reader announcements, and no hover-only explanation. A failed preview or timeout must not display zero eligible content or enable a false-success action.

Suggested review copy using an **illustrative tenant-chosen value, not a recommended period**:

> Change ordinary Support retention from 24months to12months.
>
> Applies to existing and future content in the General Support policy class. Viewing, assignment and linking do not restart the period.
>
> Already eligible under this change: [original conversations], [message bodies], [files]. Current preservation and active-work exceptions are excluded. Checked [time].
>
> After activation, eligible message text and files will begin permanent removal. This cannot restore content previously removed. Approved interaction history and official business records follow their own policies.
>
> [Back to edit] [Apply retention change]

Display time/window text must be supplied by the actual implementation contract. Do not invent a fixed grace period or provider cleanup SLA from this mock copy.

## Proof needed before calling this excellent UX

Test with realistic policy tasks, not only screenshots: first activation with old history; shortening with eligible originals; lengthening after some disposal; inbox-default versus named exception; merged originals under different classes; a failed or stale preview; a staff user without policy authority; a policy admin without body access; mobile and low-bandwidth save ambiguity; and CRM navigation to expired versus unauthorized content. Include keyboard/screen-reader completion and representative month-end/leap/timezone inputs if calendar units are selected. Confirm participants can explain what disappears, what stays, when cleanup can begin, and that expiry does not mean a refund or receipt action completed. These are required release demonstrations, not claims of tests run here.

The permanent minimal solution is policy approval plus reliable source expiry and coherent history. A generic legal engine, routine export-before-delete, additional contact deletion, a public expiry portal, automatic AI summarization before purge, and retention of all full-text audit evidence are rejected because they either widen scope or contradict the chosen purpose.
