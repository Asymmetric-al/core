## The next single decision

**Who should be able to keep reusable Support wording, and who should maintain the shared library?**

Illustrative example: Alex often explains how someone can request a corrected receipt. The tenant has a maintained shared explanation. Alex also uses a brief, friendly opening in their own voice and wants to reuse it. Another colleague, Maya, develops clearer general instructions that could help the whole team. Decide whether both staff must go through the shared library, can keep personal wording and deliberately contribute to Shared, or should maintain one shared collection together. These are synthetic examples for comparing behavior, not evidence of a measured ministry workflow. Issuing or correcting the actual receipt remains an authorized action in its owning domain.

Saved replies contain reusable wording. They are not sent messages, confidential case records, a source of CRM facts, or action-running macros. All three options preserve the ability to type and edit a normal human reply.

## Options and tradeoffs

| Option                                                         | What staff can do                                                                                                                                                                                   | Strongest benefit                                                                                        | Main cost                                                                                                               |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Shared curated replies only**                            | Use one shared library maintained by designated staff with shared-publishing rights. Personal wording can still be typed or adapted in each draft, but cannot be saved as a personal reusable item. | Smallest model and clearest common source for reusable guidance.                                         | Repeated personal phrasing must be retyped or put through the shared-library path; maintainers can become a bottleneck. |
| **B — My replies plus a curated shared library (recommended)** | Save reusable wording for personal use within the current tenant, use maintained shared replies, and deliberately offer a personal candidate for shared publication.                                | Individual convenience without making every experiment or stylistic variation everyone's shared content. | Requires clear scope, retirement, departure and duplicate/drift handling. Personal variants can become stale.           |
| **C — One collaboratively maintained shared library**          | Eligible Support authors receive ordinary shared-editing/publishing capability and maintain the common library directly; no personal reusable layer.                                                | Useful improvements become available quickly with fewer maintainer handoffs.                             | More shared churn, duplicate entries and potentially inconsistent guidance; the default editing audience is broader.    |

These are alternative launch models. They do not silently require three tenant modes, a custom permission designer, or an additional approval workflow. Under C, publication still requires explicit capability and the owning policy; assignment or conversation access alone is never sufficient.

**My single recommendation is B.** It supports personal working style while keeping common guidance recognizable and maintained. A is the strongest simpler alternative, especially if the tenant values one curated library more than personal reusable wording. C is reasonable for a small team comfortable maintaining shared content together. No vendor documentation or ministry study proves that B is universally superior; it is the best product judgment for Asym's requested flexibility and clear shared-inbox experience.

“Curated” means designated people may manage shared content. It does not mean every harmless edit requires another reviewer. Existing Email Studio rules permit qualified ordinary self-publication and retain the applicable protected-review and tenant-wide stricter-review rules. An authorized shared editor may publish their own eligible improvement through that existing policy.

## What B should feel like

The existing **Use template** entry offers one searchable library, with clear **Shared replies** and **My replies** groupings. Staff do not switch applications or learn a second editor. Search results identify scope without clutter. Insertion remains editable and preserves the draft; the already-ratified explicit replacement and Undo behavior remains intact.

**New saved reply** starts clean reusable authoring with a title and the same bounded content tools. Ordinary staff can save to My replies. A separate deliberate sharing action creates a candidate for the shared library; a qualified shared editor publishes it under the existing owner rules. It never instantly exposes every personal item or automatically converts personal content during offboarding. The shared item remains tenant-managed when its original author leaves.

For example, Alex can keep “Thank you for checking with us” in My replies, insert the shared receipt instructions, and personalize the current draft. Maya can offer revised shared instructions without first gaining access to everyone else's personal library. Shared wording should remain the convenient choice for common instructions; avoid unnecessary private clones of maintained policy content.

Use **My replies**, not a claim of secret storage inaccessible to authorized tenant governance. The precise administrative access and departure behavior must be explicit and permission-aware. This is current-tenant working content, not a personal library carried automatically between organizations.

The UI follows Core's shared base-maia design, accessible keyboard/touch controls, stable focus and meaningful loading/empty/error states. One scope-aware picker is sufficient. No mandatory folder tree, nested approval pipeline, AI organizer or usage dashboard is inferred by selecting B.

## Boundaries under every option

- **One owner:** reuse qualified Email Studio structured authoring and copy-on-insert Saved Sections. A personal scope is an explicit owner-model qualification, not an already-shipped capability, new system-message catalog key, new Layout Role or Support-only template engine.
- **No hidden actions:** using wording does not send, resolve, reassign, change recipients or update CRM. D4 preparation and Send review remain authoritative; actual sending continues through Phase 6 and the tenant's qualified Resend connection.
- **No case archive:** saving reusable wording must not copy a live conversation, donor details, protected links, uploaded records or rendered CRM values into a permanent library. Clean authoring is the straightforward entry. A transcript-derived shortcut cannot ship until it preserves D16/D17 restrictions, safe generalized wording and source authority; human review is not a claim of perfect automatic anonymization.
- **No access expansion:** personal/shared listing, search, previews, assets, publication and use require current scoped permissions. Library visibility does not grant access to CRM fields or authority to disclose them. Real personalization happens only in the qualified current reply context.
- **No retroactive rewrite:** later library edits do not change inserted drafts, prepared messages or sent history. Actual retirement/revocation still governs new insertion/preparation under D4; a cached asset is not permanent authorization.
- **Safe departure:** access loss stops unauthorized future use. Personal content is not silently made Shared, and deleting a staff record must not incidentally destroy needed independent provenance. Administrative custody, retention and correction follow the qualified tenant owner.
- **No artificial CRM history:** library edits and insertion do not create communication occurrences, new contacts, financial outcomes or donor interaction rows. Only the actual governed communication contributes its proper evidence.

## Evidence behind the recommendation

Zendesk documents personal and shared macros and separately privileged management, including administrative visibility into personal items. Front documents personal and shared templates with a distinction between where they appear for use and who can edit them. Those support a scoped personal/shared model; they do not justify copying a macro action engine or promising absolute personal secrecy. [Zendesk macro management](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408884166554-Organizing-and-managing-your-macros).

Intercom documents personal availability but also permission-removal, export and departure limitations. Zoho Desk separately controls sharing while preserving creator edit/delete authority. These are concrete reasons to define revocation, ownership and offboarding explicitly, rather than equating a Private label or nullable owner field with a safe lifecycle. [Intercom management](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/6433193-creating-and-managing-macros), [Zoho Desk snippets](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/desk/ticket-management/ticket-replies/articles/creating-and-using-snippets-in-ticket-responses).

The [evidence supplement](phase26-q18-evidence.md) includes edition/date qualifications, direct sources, the strongest counterargument to B and current repository gaps. Vendor terminology differs: a macro may run work actions, while a snippet can mean AI knowledge content. Q18 concerns human reusable reply wording only.
