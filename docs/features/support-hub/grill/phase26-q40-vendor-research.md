# Q40 — Unwanted correspondence already admitted to Support

**15 September 2026. Question-stage research; Q40 remains unanswered.** The founder has fully ratified D39 and its amendments; root is recording that ratification. D1–D39 remain accepted. This research compares the next capability scope. It selects no new disposition, glossary term, ADR0040, formal specification, implementation, sender policy or external effect.

**Single recommendation: A — Recoverably set aside the current unwanted conversation only.** Make the current action understandable and reversible within qualified retained content, while later mail continues through normal intake. This provides a clearer way to distinguish unsolicited non-support correspondence from actual completed care without silently treating a mailbox, CRM Party or organization as untrustworthy. B is useful where recurring unwanted mail creates demonstrable effort, but needs a separate future-policy review and false-positive recovery contract. C remains the least-build alternative where existing handling is sufficient.

## The real question and its boundaries

D19 already governs held input **before it enters Support work**, including Release, Dismiss, Return to review, finite custody and separate future-routing authority. It does not supply a post-admission unwanted-conversation command. D3/D5 already govern legitimate work endings, D10/D12 preserve original messages and related/merged work, and D16/D17 govern restriction/redaction/retention. Their obligations cannot be bypassed by a convenient new label.

Illustrative fixture: an unsolicited promotion has already appeared as a Support conversation. Staff want to stop treating it as ordinary support work. A later email from the same address might be a real request. This is a plausible interaction test, not measured ministry prevalence, a claim of identity, or proof that every newsletter is malicious. A legitimate request that is difficult, critical or inconvenient is not unwanted correspondence for this purpose.

The current conversation may contain multiple originals or both legitimate and unwanted material. No option permits a wholesale hide/ending of remaining legitimate work merely because one message is irrelevant. The selected answer must qualify exact affected source/work scope rather than retroactively undo admission, move original messages, destroy history, suppress required recovery or pretend a problem was solved.

### Source contradictions and limits matter

Zendesk's current action article first says deleted tickets remain for 30 days and can be restored together with unsuspending the user. Later instructions say the ticket cannot be recovered. Those statements conflict on the same retrieved page. This research relies on the consistently documented combined deletion/suspension effect and records the contradiction; it makes **no universal Zendesk recovery guarantee or recommended Zendesk-derived retention duration**.

Help Scout's search preview showed an older March13 date while its canonical page says September8. HubSpot query-parameter search variants showed older copies; the canonical July10/September7 pages were used. Source dates are evidence-version boundaries, not assertions about every account's rollout.

Front documents syncing spam classification to Gmail/Office365. That is part of its connected-mailbox product, while D25 explicitly preserves a different receiving boundary. A says future intake rules remain unchanged; it does not promise a provider-wide spam action with no external consequences. Asym must not emit such an action from a local classification.

No vendor source here proves that successful recovery restores all original permissions, reporting populations, notification state or CRM data. No survey-suppression behavior was established from these pages. The selected Asym answer must retain D28 eligibility, communication and measurement rules explicitly rather than guessing competitor CSAT behavior.

## Why A is the strongest fit now

The root problem is **unwanted correspondence already being treated as Support work**, not a newly established need for broad sender blocking. Front proves that the two effects can be explained and exposed separately. Zendesk and Help Scout document concrete ways their coupling can surprise staff; Zoho demonstrates broad effects on people and other tickets. Those sources support a small current-context action with truthful recovery, while Core's shared identity and owner boundaries make copying contact-level spam particularly unsuitable.

B's benefit is real: repeated unwanted mail can consume staff effort. B is strongest if actual tenant evidence establishes repeat senders, enough volume to matter, a trustworthy sender/route match and an accountable held-review path. Its tradeoff remains even with a perfect dialog: shared mailboxes, forwarding and compromised addresses can carry legitimate requests. The feature must not pretend that sending future inputs to review eliminates work or verifies sender identity. This research establishes no representative Asym volume or repeated-sender frequency, so B's additional scope is not yet the strongest default recommendation.

C has a credible low-complexity precedent in Zendesk's documented tag/routing alternative. Asym already has useful work controls and curated labels. However, asking staff to memorize a special “Resolve plus label” convention obscures the difference between non-support correspondence and genuinely completed care. A can make that meaning clear in one bounded action, without reproducing an enterprise anti-spam platform. C remains a fair choice, not an intentionally poor option.

## UX direction without preselecting the answer

Use a plainly labelled current-context action and scope explanation. Staff should understand what happens to this correspondence, what remains unchanged about later input, and where correction remains available. A prospective B future hold is a separate reviewed action, never a prechecked checkbox, implicit consequence or CRM-person ban. No action should silently send a message, change a subscription or claim email was recalled.

Recovery needs a persistent discoverable route while current authority and retained content permit it; a short toast alone is insufficient. Do not freeze a new sidebar folder, purge interval, bulk command, status enum, reason catalogue or numerical operational target at this question stage. Existing Base UI/base-maia interaction patterns should carry the eventual menu/review/recovery UI with keyboard/touch access, clear pending/error/partial results and preserved drafts/context.

For mixed legitimate/unwanted threads or merged originals, the selected-answer review must choose a safe exact scope. A lone irrelevant message cannot become permission to hide an unresolved donation, receipt, login or staff-support obligation. Already sent/received mail remains historical evidence. A bad classification must not require a requester to resubmit legitimate work or staff to recreate a CRM record.

## Platform seams preserved under every option

1. **Support and held intake:** current admitted-source disposition is distinct from D19 pre-admission Dismiss/Release. No retroactive un-admission, copied second conversation or reset input clock. B's future hold must use its qualified owner and actual accountable review; it does not grant release authority or override safety gates.
2. **Current work:** no shared assignment, response target, promised follow-up, assistance, delivery recovery or legitimate work disappears by a label/classification shortcut. A selected answer must reconcile current obligations before any set-aside effect.
3. **CRM and identity:** retain the same permitted Support reader through CRM. Neither a source mailbox nor a matching CRM email proves who sent it or gives authority over a Party, relationship, portal, giving record or care record. No contact deletion, duplicate identity, unsubscribe or account suspension follows.
4. **Email Studio/P17/P6:** a local disposition or recovery creates no draft, prepared email, template publication, provider spam training, dispatch, read receipt or new survey. Actual mail and D28 feedback continue only through their own qualified purpose, recipient, current-fence and immutable-delivery contracts. No silent feedback invitation arises merely because staff set something aside.
5. **Records and reporting:** D16/D17 current restrictions, retention, holds and restore barriers remain. No copied 30/90-day vendor purge. Current classification, admitted intake history and genuine successful handling are different facts; they cannot be relabelled for nicer metrics. Correction must have declared current consequences rather than rewriting earlier history.
6. **Scope discipline:** no AI classifier, global blocklist, provider-mailbox sync, custom Phase34 engine, all-matches batch, universal compliance process or new CRM model is implied. The selected answer receives its full adversarial review before any new term/decision is recorded.

Research is complete for the question's scope and alternatives. No live tenant workflow, provider account, production message, database or participant study was inspected. Exact source eligibility, disposition/return state, mixed-original handling, D28/report treatment, future-input correction, authority, retention and implementation proof remain the selected answer's review—not decisions smuggled into the recommendation. **Q40 remains unanswered; A is the single recommendation.**
