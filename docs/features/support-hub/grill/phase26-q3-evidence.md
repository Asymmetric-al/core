## Best next decision

**Should unfinished support work explicitly distinguish “we need something from the requester” from “the tenant's team still has work to do”?**

This is the valuable product choice beneath a list of status names. “Waiting” cannot safely mean “the last message came from staff”: staff may have said “I am checking this,” which leaves the next action with staff. Likewise a reply successfully queued is neither proof of receipt nor proof the request is resolved.

Illustrative scenario, not a claimed observed ministry workflow: Sarah asks about a receipt. Maria first asks Sarah for missing information, and later tells her “I'm checking with the team.” Both are outgoing messages, but only the first means Sarah must act next. Staff should see that difference without rereading the whole conversation or reopening CRM.

| Option                                        | Staff work model                                                                                          | Strongest benefit                                                                                                                         | Real cost                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Minimal inbox**                         | Open and Resolved, with a timed Snooze action/view. No explicit waiting reason.                           | Fewest labels and choices; good when a small team remembers its few ongoing threads.                                                      | Waiting-for-requester and staff investigation look alike or depend on notes/readbacks; status cannot directly explain whose turn it is.                                                               |
| **B — Explicit requester wait — recommended** | Open / Waiting for requester / Resolved, with Snooze as a timed follow-up that preserves the work reason. | Staff distinguish a requested answer from ongoing team responsibility with one useful extra state. No need for a generalized case system. | Staff must deliberately choose waiting when actually asking for something; timing/return behavior must be consistent.                                                                                 |
| **C — Explicit internal dependencies too**    | Open / Waiting for requester / Waiting on another team or external dependency / Resolved, with Snooze.    | Makes longer handoffs easy to filter and report separately.                                                                               | Another classification to maintain; risks hiding tenant responsibility behind “another team” unless the accountable support owner remains clear. Actual demand for this separate state is unverified. |

**Recommendation is B**, subject to current vendor research and the founder's answer. It gives operationally useful meaning without treating every ministry activity as a ticket. If Maria is consulting another tenant team, the conversation stays Open with its responsible support owner and the existing authorized handoff/context; a separate status is not required to enable the handoff. Choose C only if its distinct queue/reporting outcome is something the founder actually needs, not because Zendesk offers On-hold.

These are user-facing semantic models, not a proposal to freeze database enums, automatically add three board columns, or decide every timer/SLA edge now. All options retain timed Snooze, new-reply visibility, explicit resolution, assignment, failure visibility and the existing domain boundaries. The UI can show a short status and, when applicable, one quiet follow-up time; the user should not be asked to manipulate two competing status systems.

**Ratified D1:** local `docs/features/support-hub/grill/phase26-d1-adversarial-review.md:317-332,563-569` expressly leaves lifecycle vocabulary for a separate decision but requires work status, read state, assignment, timer, local send intent, provider evidence and domain completion to remain separate facts. New replies must become visible owed work; draft, automatic acknowledgment or failed reply cannot count as substantive human answer. Failure/uncertainty stays actionable. These cannot be offered as optional competing designs.

**Ratified D2:** a new relevant incoming message invalidates stale send review while preserving the draft and target. This does not automatically send, readdress the draft, or infer that all work is complete. Work-status transitions must share the D1/D2 authoritative race boundary rather than defeat collision protection.

## Already fixed; do not ask the founder to choose again

- Accountless ordinary email continuation, no requester My messages archive.
- Snooze with a timer and renewed visible work on admitted incoming replies, including after prior resolution. Exact wake/interaction semantics may be refined after the status choice; no “leave it hidden forever” alternative.
- Staff Work state and delivery/read facts remain separate. A queued reply, a delivered email, a note or a payment outcome must not silently stand in for another.
- Resolving Support does not execute or prove a refund, contact change, recurring-gift action, document issuance or member-care action. Those owners retain authorization and authoritative outcomes.
- No automatic close after a fixed number of silent days has been ratified. Do not imply “Resolved” must be immutable or that old replies must always create new conversations.
- Assignment/unassigned, current inbox, status, escalation, unread state, quarantine and failure attention are separate concepts. Quarantine is not a business waiting status; a waiting status is not an access grant.
- A move retains work context under the inherited audited-move contract. New state semantics must migrate without silently rewriting original history.
- Shared workflows and preparation/delivery capabilities retain ownership. A new clock service, CRM master or generic automation engine is not justified by Q3.

After the founder chooses the semantic model, the adversarial pass should settle these bounded consequences rather than launch unrelated discovery:

1. Exact conditions for Open, Waiting and Resolved; which explicit action changes each. An ordinary “I'm checking” reply cannot automatically imply Waiting for requester. No AI/content guessing is necessary.

## Recommended decision axis

Ask whether the staff queue should have **one general Waiting state** or **distinguish waiting on the requester from waiting on the team/another party**. This is one coherent product question about who must act next. Avoid asking the founder to choose a vendor's complete ticket model or a database enum.

**Best recommendation: distinguish the two kinds of waiting, within a small fixed lifecycle.** Present four understandable staff labels: **Open**, **Waiting on requester**, **Waiting on team/third party**, **Resolved**. The last long label can be shortened in the UI after language testing, but its meaning must include internal owner domains and genuinely external dependencies. “On hold” is shorter but hides the useful distinction. “Waiting on us” risks being confused with Open unless its help text says the support worker is waiting on another person or domain; do not use it without that distinction.

This is a product judgment: a support worker waiting for Sarah to provide missing information should not be indistinguishable from one waiting for an authorized finance owner to check something. The additional state does not require an enterprise workflow engine, customizable pipelines, automatic external integrations, a new task framework, SLAs, or a donor-facing status portal. It simply prevents a team from blaming requester silence for work still owed on its own side.

The strongest alternative is **Open / Waiting / Resolved**, with a note explaining the wait. This is credible, mature practice in Front and Help Scout-style products. It costs less categorization and supports small teams adequately when waiting cases are few. Its cost is that teammates must open/read the conversation to know which side can unblock it, and aggregate waiting counts do not convey that difference.

Do not add a weak third option solely to satisfy “a few options.” A fully customizable enterprise taxonomy is possible but has no demonstrated need here; comparing two strong choices is clearer. A two-state Open/Resolved model with snooze can also work, but treating snooze as the only waiting concept blends a reason with a future date. It is not necessary to offer every viable design.

## Concepts that must remain distinct under either option

**Work status** answers whether Support can act now, awaits input, or considers its support work complete. **Assignment** answers who remains accountable. “Unassigned” is a queue derived from assignment; it is not a replacement for Open. Waiting does not relinquish the support team's responsibility to follow up or grant a different team access.

**Snooze/reminder time** answers when the conversation should demand attention again. A waiting conversation can have a scheduled follow-up; it is still waiting rather than solved. An actionable conversation can be deferred for an explicit time without pretending that a requester owes an answer. A personal notification preference must not hide shared-team obligations. The detailed wake-up rules should be groomed with concurrency and visibility evidence after the basic lifecycle is chosen.

**Delivery state** answers what the messaging owner can prove about a particular recipient copy. Pending, retrying, bounced and indeterminate delivery are not work-status synonyms. A failed message must remain visible as work needing recovery even if a worker earlier marked the conversation resolved; no timer may relabel it “waiting on requester” and blame someone who never received it. Exactly how that attention is surfaced or reopens work must preserve D1/D2 history and be tested.

**Business-action state** remains owned by giving, documents, CRM, identity, care, or another domain. “Waiting on team/third party” may point to an authorized owner handoff; it does not create a duplicate refund/receipt/contact-change state machine. Resolving support does not execute or certify that action. The staff label should describe the Support obligation honestly while permission-aware links show the owner fact.

**Resolution and closure reason** also differ. A genuine solved request, a duplicate, withdrawn request, abuse/spam and an unresponsive requester do not prove the same outcome. Do not decide auto-close after an arbitrary number of days or mark all nonresponses “resolved” solely to clear a queue. Q3 can choose the main work-state vocabulary while the future decision on closing/disposition remains explicit. That is a dependency to groom, not an already accepted feature or invented timeout.

**New activity** is not always new actionable requester content. Legitimate admitted replies should become visible promptly and revive waiting/resolved work as appropriate. Duplicate deliveries, automated acknowledgments, quarantined senders, transient typing, and an internal note should not blindly reset the entire lifecycle. D1/D2 message admission, collision review and deduplication continue to govern those events. Reopening must preserve the original resolution event for honest reporting.

## Product judgment and pressure test

The extra waiting distinction is proportionate because it answers a concrete operational question without requiring new customization: “Are we waiting for the person who asked, or is our side responsible for obtaining the next input?” It improves handoffs and queue scanning even when no formal SLA is sold. If these waits rarely occur, the extra choice could be unnecessary; no Asym usage evidence currently quantifies their prevalence. That is why the generic Waiting option remains a strong alternative, not a straw man.

For multiple simultaneous dependencies, no simple exclusive status expresses every outstanding task. The label should describe the next condition blocking Support's next action; authorized notes/owner links explain additional dependencies. Do not solve that uncertainty by creating a generic dependency graph before a demonstrated need. If the support worker can still take useful action, keep it Open rather than treating every incomplete external task as permission to stop work.

The UI can remain one status dropdown and clear queue filters; choosing one of two waiting labels need not open a mandatory description form. A brief optional internal note may clarify the expected input. The snooze control offers an optional follow-up time and displays the resulting exact time/zone; it does not require staff to repeatedly classify a donor. Existing base components and D2 draft preservation remain the implementation foundation.

## Ruthless challenge of the recommendation

### Forgotten follow-up is the real risk

“Waiting” can be a graveyard if it hides unresolved work indefinitely. However, eliminating it does not eliminate that risk: Open can become an undifferentiated backlog, and Snoozed can also lose work when timers do not fire. The permanent invariant is **every unresolved conversation remains discoverable and has accountable support ownership** (including an explicit unassigned queue if ownership is missing). A Waiting view must include outstanding work, not just today's wake-ups. Any promised future staff action must have a retrievable follow-up responsibility. This question need not pick timer lengths or automated reminder policy.

### A label is not enough when behavior depends on it

Ordinary labels are multi-valued and may coexist with contradictory statuses. If “waiting” determines attention queues or follow-up behavior, it should be a clear work fact with one authoritative transition boundary, not a conventional tag that can be forgotten. Labels remain useful for topics, not substitutes for the core mutually exclusive work state. Do not build a generic workflow engine simply to enforce three states.

### A reply does not mean the team is waiting

“I am investigating” is outbound mail while staff still owe action. A final answer may complete support. A queued or bounced question does not prove the requester has received anything. Therefore neither last-message direction nor successful row creation can determine Waiting or Resolved. The precise send/status convenience action comes after the founder selects the lifecycle; defaults remain unchosen here.

### Resolving support is not completing the owning business action

Support may resolve a request when its support obligation is complete, but its status cannot mark a refund, receipt correction, recurring change, protected-document grant or CRM mutation complete. If staff have promised a later action or answer, the support record must preserve that outstanding obligation or a real authorized handoff. Waiting on finance is not a substitute for the finance domain's approval, state and audit history. A finance action completing may create a support follow-up signal; it must not silently send or resolve the conversation unless a separately approved owner contract permits that.

### Failure state and work state are separate facts

Inbound custody/quarantine is not Waiting. Delivery pending/retrying/undelivered is not Waiting. A resolved conversation with a later bounce must not conceal the failure: staff need an attention path even if the work-status resolution semantics are distinct. Do not promise that every provider event automatically reopens or resets timers; that behavior requires exact causal and ownership evidence. Replayed callbacks must not thrash status or fabricate another unanswered human message.

### Reopen semantics require admission, not another founder decision first

The intended baseline should say that an admitted relevant human reply brings unresolved/new work back to staff attention, including a reply to previously resolved work. Auto-responders, bulk mail, quarantine, duplicate webhooks, malicious reference headers and unrelated same-subject mail do not qualify merely because an email arrived. Exact classification and participant-admission rules remain their own bounded decision. This protects the lifecycle question from overreaching into sender-authentication policy.

### Concurrency matters independently of the chosen labels

A worker cannot resolve or defer against a stale version while a newly admitted reply is arriving. A delayed snooze timer cannot reopen a subsequently resolved or re-snoozed conversation. Store the relevant work revision/generation and admit transitions atomically with their authoritative event history; a stale transition has a clear refresh/review outcome. Preserve manual assignment and D2 draft audience through these transitions. The required guarantees apply to A, B and C; C simply adds more transition cases.

### B's biggest counterargument

One Waiting bucket can obscure whether the next step belongs to the requester or an internal colleague. This is a legitimate reason to choose C, particularly if staff daily routing and reporting need that split. But mandatory status specialization is not justified by vendor feature availability alone. Show who/what is awaited through the authorized conversation/handoff context; do not claim this provides the same filter/report precision as C. If that distinction is central to Conrad's intended product workflow, C should win deliberately.
