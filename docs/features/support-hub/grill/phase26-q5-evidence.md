## Root synthesis and independent corrections

The question is who may end a follow-up when necessary requester input remains absent: nobody until another completion, an explicit staff decision, or a qualified timed policy. D3 already determines reminder waking and later relevant-input reopening; D4 already determines Send. Those decisions are not re-asked. B is the single recommendation, not an accepted Q5 answer.

The strongest argument for B is honest finite staff responsibility with a visible No response outcome beneath the existing Resolved work state. The strongest arguments against it are inconsistent staff judgment, premature abandonment and misleading success metrics. A and C are therefore real alternatives, not caricatures. No Asym volume, staff-cost study or response-delay distribution was supplied; the recommendation is a proportionate product judgment grounded in the existing boundaries.

Two independent wording improvements are adopted: (1) A waits for **needed input**, not merely any reply, since an acknowledgement might not unblock work; (2) delivery safeguards preserve **outstanding/unreviewed actionable recovery**, rather than requiring a read receipt or an impossible proof of every historical delivery before any conversation may finish. A reviewed unknown remains unknown. No response is never a claim that the requester ignored successfully delivered mail.

Do not couple every pending owner-domain process to Support status. An actual Support promise/owed action remains work; an independently owned process retains its own status and authorization. This distinction is explicit in the question and overrides any shorthand in the independent reports that could be read as “all pending owner processes prohibit ending Support.” Eligibility must follow the actual current commitment, not merely the Waiting status, because a due reminder may already have made the conversation Open for review.

#### Material challenges and exact guardrails to carry forward

These are bounded pre-question findings, not the future full 23-category adversarial review of the founder's answer.

##### 1. “Resolved” must not claim an answer that never occurred

**Concern: material. Severity medium; likelihood high if all endings share the existing undifferentiated timestamp/count.** A team clearing its backlog can appear to have solved more problems than it did. Later staff and donors cannot tell what happened. Evidence: current report and schema above. **Effect:** narrows B/C, does not invalidate intentional ending.

Proposed clause: “No response is a distinct recorded disposition for an explicit permitted ending of Support follow-up, not evidence that the requested outcome was achieved. Use the existing Resolved work state; retain the disposition and actor/time in the resolution episode. Do not add a fifth status. Operational counts may include it as ended Support work only when labelled honestly; reporting must distinguish it from answered/completed-outcome episodes.”

This is a deliberate narrow refinement of D3's completion eligibility if accepted. It must be recorded across the glossary and feature ADR, not smuggled into UI language. No mandatory reason form for ordinary Resolve is needed: the named action supplies No response. Additional closure taxonomies remain outside this question.

##### 2. Silence cannot discharge an outstanding owner obligation

**Concern: material. Severity high; likelihood conditional on real owner dependencies.** An unanswered donor email may coexist with a submitted refund, required receipt/correction notification, unresolved delivery recovery or an explicit promise. Ending follow-up must not hide them. A pending unrelated linked record is not automatically a blocker; only an active required obligation relevant to this Support work is.

Proposed clause: “No-response disposition is allowed only when requested input is the sole remaining active blocker and staff deliberately ends the otherwise permitted follow-up. It cannot waive a known required owner action, unresolved review/recovery item or promised follow-up. Resolve changes only Support facts. Each owning domain retains its own completion, cancellation, suppression, approval and audit rules.”

Hard guards must use known authoritative owner evidence; staff review handles context the system cannot safely infer. Missing/restricted owner evidence cannot be treated as success. This does not justify scanning every CRM record, building a task dependency graph or claiming the software can prove every semantic promise in free text.

##### 3. No reply is not proof the question reached the right person

**Concern: material. Severity high; likelihood normal when email fails or group evidence is ambiguous.** A question left in draft, queued indefinitely, suppressed or bounced cannot justify claiming the requester ignored a delivered request. This does not mean every fully reviewed, irrecoverable delivery uncertainty creates an eternal Support obligation. D2 expressly distinguishes submission, provider acceptance, mail-server acceptance, per-member delivery and human reading; aggregate group success is not every member delivered.

Proposed clause: “No-response review refers to the actual requested input and its relevant admitted audience, using qualified Phase6 delivery evidence. Unsent, pending, failed, suppressed or indeterminate mail is not proof of successful contact. An outstanding actionable or unreviewed recovery obligation prevents ending; fully reviewed irrecoverable uncertainty does not by itself force indefinite waiting if no Support commitment remains. Preserve that uncertainty and the observed absence of a reply without blaming the requester or inventing delivered/read evidence. Never infer delivery from last-message direction or reading from delivery, or require delivery to every unrelated copied participant.”

A provider-specific Delivered badge is not universal proof of human receipt. The exact evidence predicate needs the already-required D1/D2 provider/member qualification; do not invent a new messaging state or ignore the existing ambiguity rules. No fixed waiting clock is needed for manual B. If C is selected, its clock must originate in qualified actual contact evidence and must not age from draft creation or queued time.

##### 4. Race-safe ending and reopening must preserve context

**Concern: material. Severity high; likelihood normal concurrent intake.** Staff ends while a reply arrives; a stale callback ends the newly reopened work; or a bulk command closes fresh items that were not reviewed. The existing status setter has no reviewed revision/reason contract. D3 already defines the permanent transition boundary.

Proposed clause: “Apply No response through the same tenant/actor-authorized, reviewed-version, idempotent work command. Atomically record the disposition episode and cancel the current follow-up reminder. A newly admitted relevant reply opens review under D3, including a late first-time admission; a replay cannot reopen twice or end new work. Retain the original episode. Freeze exact bulk membership and check each item's eligibility and current revision; preserve durable per-item outcomes.”

Eligibility should not require the literal current label Waiting for requester: a D3 due reminder legitimately opens the item for review while the requester remains the only substantive blocker. Requiring staff to change Open back to Waiting before ending is needless friction. A newly arrived human response is different: it must be reviewed and cannot be ignored by stale no-response intent. A required promised follow-up still cannot be cancelled merely by choosing this action.

##### 5. Keep the action quiet and intelligible

**Concern: material. Severity medium; likelihood high if implemented as another permanent work status/button or mandatory classification form.** A fifth state or cluttered picker would reopen D3 and confuse clean inbox work.

Proposed clause: “Expose the exceptional action as End follow-up — No response within existing secondary work actions, with short consequence text: ends this follow-up, retains the history, and a later relevant reply opens it again. Record the reason directly from the action. Preserve the conversation, draft and focus; show the resulting Resolved state with its reason where useful. Do not require a new form on ordinary Resolve.”

This is a proposed interaction direction, not a rendered usability result. D4's After sending choices remain unchanged; there is no automatic final email or hidden audience change. A separately composed final reply still uses D1–D4. A later automated notice would require an explicit producer/content/dispatch contract, not a hook attached to every status change.

##### 6. Do not preserve a misleading automatic setting as live authority

**Concern: material. Severity high if activated from old seed; likelihood conditional on implementation/migration.** Numeric14 and label Pending cannot identify safe new requester waits, actual contact, promises or meaningful policy. API zero differs from UI min1; neither count has evidence as a launch choice.

Proposed clause: “Inventory existing auto-resolve settings and writers. Preserve historical values as migration evidence; do not execute them against Phase26 work semantics or present them as an active policy unless the founder authorizes and the implementation qualifies that policy. If A/B is chosen, retire or clearly disable the misleading configuration through a compatible rollout. Do not activate old Pending or last-direction inference, seed defaults or stale automation after rollback.”

No need to delete existing data during grooming. If C is chosen, qualified automatic policy must call the same command and state fences, not create a second automation vocabulary or bypass guards because a timer fired.

#### Minimum outcome proof after a decision

1. Qualified requester-only unanswered follow-up can be ended through B; actor/reason/time remain visible; ordinary completed answers retain their existing simple workflow.
2. A required pending refund, failed required notification, unresolved delivery outcome or promised follow-up prevents using No response to erase the obligation. Owner state and task/audit facts are unchanged by Support resolution.
3. Unsent/queued/failed/suppressed/indeterminate requests do not qualify as delivered unanswered questions; actionable recovery remains work, while reviewed irrecoverable uncertainty is retained without fabricating contact or a perpetual obligation. Mixed group evidence does not attribute delivery to an unproven member. Delivery is never labelled read.
4. A due reminder's legitimate Open item can be reviewed and ended without a forced Open→Waiting→Resolved dance. Ending cancels only the current eligible reminder generation.
5. Both reply-before-end and end-before-reply orderings retain new review work; duplicate intake/command/callback cannot alter the later state or duplicate episodes.
6. Reopened history retains its prior No response episode. Report/export labels and totals distinguish work ended from requester outcomes achieved. Legacy unknown outcomes remain unknown, not inferred No response.
7. Bulk eligibility, lost-response reconciliation, permission loss and tenant switching obey D3; one successful item does not mask another item's stale/denied result.
8. Choosing the action sends zero emails and changes zero CRM/finance facts. Any separately requested reply retains exact audience/preparation and D4 work intent.
9. Legacy14day and zero/null settings cannot activate unintended automatic ending in mixed-version rollout or recovery.

No model/test execution, deployed database inspection, provider call, usability study or measured volume evidence is claimed by this pass. The narrow unresolved product decision is **who may decide that unanswered follow-up can end and whether it happens automatically**. Timing, required outreach policy, extra disposition reasons and service commitments must not be frozen accidentally by the option wording.

#### Pressure-testing B: strongest counterargument

The strongest objection is not that manual ending is unfamiliar. It is that **No response can become a polite name for staff abandonment**. A worker may close a difficult request without having asked a clear necessary question, without seeing a failed email, while another team still owes a promised action, or because a low-bandwidth/time-zone delay looks like disinterest. If No response is counted as “problem solved,” an apparently cleaner dashboard rewards the wrong behavior.

A avoids that discretionary boundary. C can reduce inconsistent treatment when a tenant has a proved policy, while still allowing staff exceptions. These are genuine alternatives, not straw men. B remains preferable for Phase26 because existing evidence establishes neither a universal tenant policy nor the volume/consistency need that would justify C's additional automation. The remedy for B is explicit meaning and eligibility, not a speculative manager-approval workflow or a mandatory message-count quota.

#### Required guardrails if B is chosen

1. **One reason beneath the existing state.** Record **No response — follow-up ended** as an explicit staff outcome on the applicable resolution episode. Keep Open / Waiting for requester / Waiting on our side / Resolved unchanged. Do not relabel historical resolutions or create a customizable outcome taxonomy merely for this choice. Show the reason in conversation history/detail and preserve it when a later reply reopens work.
2. **Silence is not proof.** The reviewed record must show what needed requester input remains unanswered and the relevant outbound request. A known failed/undelivered message or unresolved delivery uncertainty is not evidence that the requester chose not to answer. Unseen relevant input, quarantine/recovery evidence or unresolved promised Support work prevents a false no-response disposition through the same authoritative eligibility boundary.
3. **Support remains one domain.** Ending follow-up does not cancel, approve, reject, refund, change or certify any CRM/giving/missionary/member-care obligation. A mixed case with an owed our-side Support step cannot be disposed of as only No response. An independently owned business process may continue under its own authority; its existence and permissions are not rewritten by this outcome.
4. **No mandatory closing email.** An internal end-follow-up decision sends nothing by itself. A staff member may write a useful human follow-up under D4 when appropriate. It must not claim the underlying issue was solved, require a portal, or instruct the requester to create a new ticket to continue. This does not silently authorize an automatic notice/catalog key.
5. **Reuse D3, do not create a second timer.** D3's current reminder becomes Open review when due. B supplies a possible staff decision during that review. A successful resolution cancels the current reminder under D3; old callbacks are fenced. A later admitted relevant human reply reopens review on the existing conversation under D1/D3. Staff can also reach the same review through normal conversation navigation; expiry is not a required waiting-period qualification.
6. **Honest atomic history and reporting.** Apply the conditional work transition, reason, actor and reminder effect together; a race with new input must not hide that input. Same-command retry cannot append another end-follow-up episode. No-response closure is distinguishable from answered/completed work in any claimed problem-resolution or success metric. Do not claim a receipt, remedy or requester confirmation from this state.
7. **Proportionate UI.** Offer a clearly named deliberate **End follow-up — no response** action through the existing completion affordance. Explain the consequence succinctly: no more Support follow-up is planned, and a later email reply returns to the team. #### The adversarial challenge to B, resolved precisely

##### 1. “Resolved” could become a false problem-success claim

**Material concern: yes. Severity high; likelihood high if reporting merely counts the existing status as solved problems.** D3 deliberately defines completion of Support's obligation, so B does not invalidate the word's governed meaning. Its everyday connotation nevertheless creates a UI/reporting risk.

**Permanent clarification to include in B's eventual review:** “No response records that staff ended this follow-up obligation without the needed requester input. It is neither an assertion that the underlying issue was fixed nor that the requester withdrew, was satisfied or received/read our message. Store the disposition with this resolution episode and display it in its history and any relevant outcome reporting. Counting completed work is allowed when labelled as such; do not count No response as confirmed problem resolution.”

The narrow reason belongs to the episode so a later response and true completion do not erase why the prior episode ended. Do not require a broad configurable resolution-code system or a mandatory survey for every Resolve. Historical entries lacking a reason stay unknown; do not backfill them as successful fixes.

##### 2. Staff may label their own failure “No response”

**Material concern: yes. Severity high; likelihood medium under realistic delivery or owner handoff failures.** Known unsubmitted, suppressed, bounced or indeterminate outreach can coexist with no admitted requester answer. A group-level provider event cannot prove every needed participant received the question. A support worker must not be forced to claim delivery to clear a queue, nor allowed to conceal a real repair task.

**Exact guardrail:** “Before ending follow-up, review the current requester blocker, relevant admitted input, contact/delivery evidence and any our-side promise or required recovery. The action cannot hide an outstanding obligation or unreviewed actionable recovery. No response is an observation about absent needed input, not a transport success classification. Delivery uncertainty and its reviewed recovery remain independently visible; do not demand read receipts or fabricate definitive delivery to complete a human review.”

This does not impose endless waiting whenever a provider can never prove a historical outcome. D3 permits an authorized review to determine that no further Support action remains, while retaining the unknown fact. The distinction is **unreviewed/still-actionable obligation versus honestly reviewed unresolved evidence**, not “every email must have delivered before any conversation can finish.” A new adverse result remains subject to D3's fresh-cause rule.

##### 3. A simple age check can erase legitimate current work

**Material concern: yes. Severity high; likelihood medium.** A reply may arrive just before Resolve; an automatic reply may arrive without supplying needed input; the donor's friend on the admitted To/Cc group may supply it; a note/CRM update may change `updated_at`; or a due reminder may already have opened the conversation. Restricting B only to the literal current Waiting-requester status would block the normal due-reminder review flow.

**Exact guardrail:** “Eligibility follows the current reviewed missing-input situation, not only a status label, generic updated timestamp or original CRM contact. Reuse D3's conditional transition/admission and D2's participants. A due review may choose No response from Open. Relevant new admitted input blocks a stale no-response decision; duplicates do not create another resolution episode or revive a cancelled reminder. A later relevant reply opens the same conversation and preserves the previous episode.”

No-response does not mean “no bytes arrived.” A human acknowledgement, partial answer or out-of-office message must be interpreted under the current required-input/ordinary admission semantics, not a new universal text classifier. Do not add AI inference to end work.

##### 4. Ending an episode may generate a misleading customer email

**Material concern: yes. Severity medium to high; likelihood high if generic “your issue is resolved” notifications attach to the status.** A blanket status notice can claim a fix, disclose a staff-only reason to unintended recipients, generate thank-you loops or restart a clock through its own send.

**Exact boundary:** “Q5 does not imply sending a notice. Any staff-authored message uses D4's explicit composer and D2 audience review. Any future automatic reminder/end notice needs its own approved purpose/content/audience/timing and P6/P17 admission/recovery contract. It must explain how to continue by ordinary email without claiming the issue was fixed. Internal disposition and customer-facing copy are different facts.”

##### 5. Capacity/reporting pressure may distort the decision

**Material concern: yes. Severity medium; likelihood medium.** The Zoho rationale shows that vendors may close quiet work partly to free assignment capacity. A similar hidden coupling would encourage premature endings and reward staff for clearing counters rather than fulfilling commitments.

**Exact boundary:** “Assignment capacity, SLA policy, customer outcome and completion of a Support follow-up episode remain distinct. No response is not a successful owner action or satisfaction signal, and automatic capacity rules must not manufacture that disposition.”

#### Focused proof cases for the selected policy's later review

1. Missing required requester input, no remaining promised or actionable our-side work: explicit B resolves with No response; no message, fifth status or owner mutation occurs.
2. Due reminder already made the request Open: staff can deliberately end its reviewed requester follow-up, and the old reminder cannot reopen it again.
3. Needed input admitted immediately before no-response command: stale decision fails without changing work; input admitted after completion opens review and retains the earlier reason.
4. Support still owes a promised follow-through on a refund/receipt correction/CRM update: requester silence cannot be used to dispose of that Support obligation. 8. No response ending followed later by actual completion: two episodes retain their distinct outcomes; completed-work totals and confirmed problem-success totals do not collapse them.
9. Duplicate command, repeated Resolve or delayed response: one episode, cancelled timer stays cancelled, lower revisions do not overwrite current work.
10. A note, branding change or ordinary D4 courtesy update does not reset a hypothetical silence clock or change the outcome by implication. Any later automatic policy must define its own exact clock separately.
