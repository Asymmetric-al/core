## The next single decision

**How should Asym routinely retire old Support message content and attachments?**

Illustrative example: Maya's receipt question is resolved. After the tenant's approved retention interval, Alex no longer needs the old email text or uploaded files for that Support work. The CRM should still show permitted evidence of the interaction, while the actual gift and official receipt retain their own owner policies. A genuinely new email from Maya must still reach staff without resurrecting expired content. This is a scenario for comparing behavior, not evidence of measured ministry incidents.

Now consider a conversation that remains active for a long time. Should its oldest messages disappear individually as they age, should the normal cleanup wait until the Support work ends, or should staff review the eligible content before disposal? These are three alternative ordinary defaults; they are not three settings selected simultaneously.

## Options and practical tradeoffs

| Option                                                                     | Ordinary behavior                                                                                                                                                                               | Strongest benefit                                                                                 | Main cost or risk                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Automatically expire content after Support work ends (recommended)** | Eligible original conversation content expires after the work is resolved and genuinely inactive for the tenant-approved period. Qualified preservation and ongoing-purpose checks still apply. | Preserves working context, then handles routine cleanup without a recurring staff deletion queue. | Needs a trustworthy clock, explicit policy-change behavior and accountable treatment of long-open/repeatedly reopened work. |
| **B — Review eligible old content before disposal**                        | The approved policy brings the same old resolved content to a qualified staff review before ordinary destruction.                                                                               | Adds human judgment where the continuing records value may be uncertain.                          | Creates recurring review work and backlog risk. Pending review cannot override a mandatory disposal deadline.               |
| **C — Expire each message and file by its own age**                        | Each original message/file reaches its own approved expiry, even when the larger conversation remains active, unless a qualified owner exception applies.                                       | Bounds older content's ordinary lifetime without every new contribution extending it.             | Can remove useful context from active work and produce more partial histories, explanations and preservation decisions.     |

**My single recommendation is A.** It is the best ordinary fit for a coherent Support journey: keep the context staff need while handling the request, then apply predictable cleanup after that purpose has ended. B is the strongest alternative where classification needs routine human judgment. C is defensible when age-based minimization matters more than preserving a continuous active thread. A is a product judgment, not proof of a universal ministry preference.

The period must be justified and approved for the tenant's actual purpose and obligations. No retrieved evidence supports copying one competitor interval—or inventing one Asym-wide period—as universally correct. Exact durations, allowed policy overrides and activation/backlog treatment belong in the selected answer's complete review. This question does not silently require all three models to be configurable.

## What A should feel like

An authorized administrator sees one understandable effective retention policy, an impact preview when enabling or changing it, and a clear explanation of existing-history effects. A means an approved, explicitly activated policy; it is not silent destructive cleanup during onboarding. Ordinary staff continue working in Support and CRM without per-message retention prompts. On-demand details can explain the applicable policy and expected content availability.

When content expires, its authorized appearance uses a calm marker such as **Content expired under your retention policy**. The same source state appears from CRM. It must not look like a loading failure, delete the CRM person, imply a refund completed, or generate a routine follower/requester email.

The source owner may retain only permitted communication/work facts under their own policies. “History remains” does not mean all metadata is anonymous or retained forever. An independent giving record or official document does not share a Support attachment's disposal authority merely because the two are linked.

Configurable scheduled disposal, deliberate activation and visible policy effects have primary product precedents. The details vary substantially; they do not establish one universal clock or duration. [Zendesk deletion schedules](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/6388012977306-Creating-ticket-deletion-schedules), [Front deletion policy](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2083).

## Boundaries under every option

- D16 remains the one governed restriction/disposal mechanism. Q17 adds routine eligibility and policy authority; it does not create another purge system, reveal vault, automatic scanner or generic compliance platform.
- Current source permission, actual holds, mandatory disposal and qualified records custody apply under all three defaults. A long-open status or B's uncompleted review is not a keep-forever exemption. Open/repeatedly reopened work needs accountable purpose review under the approved policy.
- Retention binds original sources. Merge/Undo, inbox movement, viewing, following, assignment, labels, CRM linking, redaction and technical retries cannot silently renew old content's lifetime. Genuine new work needs an explicit rule; it never restores already disposed bytes.
- Before destruction, check whether continuing/delegated work still has a justified need for the affected content. A Resolved label or merged-away original alone does not establish that fact or confer financial/records authority: an active continuing issue cannot become disposable merely because one source looks closed. A mere link is not an automatic indefinite hold either.
- The selected model must define scope and units of any impact preview, current eligibility at execution, policy version/effective time, shortening/extension effects, late replies and existing-history rollout. An approximate preview cannot authorize deletion of unreviewed hidden scope by implication.
- Active content expiry, physical cleanup, lawful restricted custody, provider/backup disposal and external-copy limits retain D16's truthful meanings. No healthy routine expiry notification or silent metric improvement.
- Archiving or removing a conversation from a working view is not content disposal. Do not introduce competitor lifecycle states merely for parity or let an expired body cause an unauthorized new CRM identity/conversation.
