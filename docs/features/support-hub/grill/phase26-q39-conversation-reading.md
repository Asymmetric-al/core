# Q39 — Whose reading should the conversation unread cue represent?

**Current answer — 15 September 2026:** A — Personal read/unread is selected. See [D39 selection](phase26-d39-selection.md) and [complete proposed amendments](phase26-d39-adversarial-review.md). The original unanswered comparison below is historical; no Q40 is opened.

**15 September 2026. D1–D38 and all amendments are fully ratified. Q39 is the single researched next question and remains unanswered.** This comparison does not select a reading policy, new glossary term, ADR0039, formal specification or implementation.

## The question

**Should a Support conversation's unread indicator reflect each staff member's own reading, the team's shared reading, or be omitted?**

Imagine Ava and Ben both have access to an Open conversation. A new requester message arrives. Ben views it to understand the request; Ava returns later. No one has completed the Support work, so the conversation remains Open. The choice is what, if anything, should indicate that the message is still new to Ava. This is an illustrative reading journey, not a measured ministry staffing pattern.

| Option                              | What it means in that example                                                                                                                                                     | Main benefit                                                                                                         | Real tradeoff                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Personal read/unread**        | Ben's qualifying viewing changes his own cue. Ava's remains unread until her own qualifying viewing; they can legitimately see different cues.                                    | Each person can identify material new to them. Another person's quick look cannot erase that aid.                    | Requires source-qualified personal state and clear rules for new staff, newly accessible conversations and old history. It must not become an obligation to read everything.        |
| **B — Shared team read/unread**     | One shared cue changes when an authorized staff member qualifies as viewing that activity. Ben's viewing clears the shared cue; no reader name, time or roster is shown.          | Gives the team one indication of whether activity has received an initial look within the relevant authorized scope. | Ava cannot tell what she personally has seen. A glance may clear the team's cue despite unfinished work, and different source permissions require careful scope qualification.      |
| **C — No ordinary read/unread cue** | Omit this conversation-list marker. Keep the existing work status, assignment, latest activity, reply targets, reminders and qualified notifications for their existing purposes. | The simplest ordinary list and no new personal/team reading-state feature.                                           | Staff cannot scan for material new to themselves or unseen by the team; they may need to revisit more conversations. Mandatory new-input review and notification engagement remain. |

These are **alternative policies**, not three settings to implement. B is the strongest competing meaning if shared initial-triage visibility matters most. C is the strongest smaller-scope alternative when existing work and notification tools are enough.

## Single recommendation: A — Personal read/unread

Keep the ordinary reading aid personal, and keep responsibility in the already accepted shared work model. Ava's view should tell Ava what is new to Ava; Ben's viewing should not claim that Ava has caught up. This supports staff returning to a conversation, arriving from CRM or taking over work without an additional workflow, reader roster or public read receipt.

The UI direction is restrained: a clear personal new/unread meaning near the conversation subject, separate from its Open/Waiting/Resolved label. Use existing Base UI/base-maia and accessible text/semantics; do not rely on an aria-hidden color dot alone. Do not add a red urgency count, unread dashboard or another priority system. Exact visual treatment and qualifying read behavior belong to the selected answer's full review.

“Read” here is a product cue about qualified viewing or a deliberate user action, **not proof of comprehension, full historical review, attachment inspection, requester receipt or completed work**. A personal state is also not an instruction to mark every old conversation unread for every employee. That baseline must be made explicit after the policy is selected.

## Boundaries every option preserves

1. **Reading does not complete work.** It cannot resolve/reopen a conversation, change assignment, cancel reminders, satisfy reply targets, accept delegated outcomes or certify CRM/giving actions. A team member's viewing does not remove unresolved work from its proper queue.
2. **Reading does not approve sending.** An ordinary cue or manual reset cannot replace explicit current content/audience/source review under D1/D2/D4/D10. Choosing C leaves those safeguards intact.
3. **Conversation reading and P17 engagement are distinct.** Viewing or marking one does not automatically read/archive an entire notification group or change its source/retention rules. D15 Follow/Stop remains unchanged. No new notification, enrollment or employee-reading analytics follows from the cue.
4. **Current source access applies before disclosure or update.** A boolean/count can reveal hidden activity. Restrict cues to admitted sources the actor may currently access; merge/Undo, revocation and expiry cannot invent viewing or leak restricted originals. For B, a narrower viewer cannot attest to hidden activity in a broader audience.
5. **No reader surveillance or requester tracking.** A is self-only. B shows shared state without naming readers, never a list of viewers, names, timestamps, counts of readers or productivity statistics. It still reveals that someone viewed the activity; in a small team the reader may be inferable. It offers no anonymity guarantee. D34 composing awareness is not evidence of reading. No outgoing-email tracking pixel or recipient read receipt is added.
6. **One capability across Support and CRM.** A qualified Support conversation opened from CRM uses the same authorized source and selected reading state. Opening only a CRM profile, prefetching, loading a hidden pane or fetching content is not by itself evidence of viewing. No duplicated read authority, CRM Activity or provider-mailbox synchronization.
7. **Email Studio and P6 keep their existing roles.** This cue does not prepare, publish, rerender or dispatch email. P17 owns its existing notification presentation/engagement and P6 owns actual delivery/reconciliation. Read state is not delivery evidence. D37/D38 drafts and D35 posted-note edits remain unchanged.

The selected-answer review must settle eligible activity, first-use/history baseline, exact qualifying read action, any manual reset, concurrent arrivals and multiple tabs/devices, source/merge/privacy changes, storage/index/retention, failure states and accessible UI. No timer, total count, filter, schema, bulk action or extra user preference is prematurely frozen here. **Q39 remains unanswered; A is the single recommendation.**
