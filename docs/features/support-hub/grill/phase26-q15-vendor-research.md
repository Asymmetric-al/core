# Q15 independent primary research: deliberate conversation following

Research date: 2026-09-11. Grooming evidence only. No runtime, provider, account, or repository mutation. Core architecture and D1–D14 are evaluated by the parent agent; this note does not claim an independent Core audit.

## Recommendation

Recommend **A — Deliberately follow a conversation**: an authorized colleague can choose Follow without taking the Support assignment or delegated-action ownership. Unfollow stops only optional following. Existing assignment, direct mention, D11 source-required attention, and P17 notification behavior continue under their own contracts.

This is a choice about ongoing interest, not a new general notification policy. The permanent model should keep four facts separate: conversation responsibility, one-time directed attention, an explicit personal follow subscription, and the reader's current authorization. Personal delivery preferences remain P17-owned. Shared/Unassigned work remains visible in its responsible inbox, not dependent on somebody following it.

The strongest alternative is automatic following after participation. It saves an initial click, but current Front documentation demonstrates several independent auto-entry and exit conditions. Those are real complexity and surprise costs, especially for a specialist giving one answer while the Support coordinator remains responsible. Deliberate Follow offers that specialist an intentional, legible choice without turning every mention or contribution into an indefinite subscription.

## Three mutually exclusive launch postures

| Option                                  | Behavior and benefit                                                                                                                      | Cost or limit                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Deliberate Follow (recommended)** | Add explicit Follow/Following/Unfollow for currently authorized staff. Existing directed attention remains. Continued updates are opt-in. | One small deliberate action for someone who wants subsequent updates.                                                                                                                                                             |
| **B — Directed attention only**         | Keep assignment, direct mentions, qualified owner-action attention and shared queues; provide no ongoing personal follow subscription.    | Lowest new state, but specialists must revisit the queue or ask staff to mention them again. Self-mentioning becomes a tempting bookmark workaround.                                                                              |
| **C — Follow after participation**      | Automatically begin ongoing following after defined reply, note, or mention events; allow Unfollow.                                       | Fewer initial clicks, but more unwanted subscriptions, reason-specific stopping rules, and greater need to show why somebody is following. Exact triggers and explicit-Unfollow precedence become part of the permanent contract. |

Illustrative journey, not an asserted ministry requirement: a colleague answers a Support coordinator's internal question and wants to see the eventual requester response without taking over the conversation. A offers Follow; B requires revisiting or another mention; C subscribes the colleague because they participated.

## Boundaries and edge cases to carry into the answer review

These are design judgments from the research, not factual claims about vendors or a final ratified D15:

1. **Follow is explicit personal interest.** The visible control should use Follow → Following, with Unfollow discoverable in the same location. Helpful copy: “Get updates about this conversation. Assignment stays with [coordinator/inbox].” Avoid a generic star that might mean bookmark or priority.
2. **Unfollow affects optional interest only.** It cannot suppress a direct mention, assigned responsibility, required delegated-action outcome, or another source-required notification. P17 determines channel preferences and any mandatory-delivery categories, not Support's button.
3. **Watching is not permission.** Require current access both to begin following and to render/deliver each specific permitted projection. Revocation, inbox move, restrictions, inactivity, tenant switch, caches, exports and deep links must not expose protected content or hidden counts.
4. **A one-time mention is not automatic following.** Notify the mention once through its existing source contract. Offer Follow unobtrusively when the recipient wants subsequent activity. Do not auto-subscribe the note author merely for helping.
5. **One source event, one user's attention item.** A user can be assignee, follower and mentioned on the same contribution. Reuse P17 deduplication and reason attribution; do not multiply emails or notification rows because three recipient derivations matched.
6. **Specify a finite event set before recording D15.** “All updates” is vague and risks every delivery webhook, target tick, attachment scan, CRM write or unrelated linked thread becoming a notice. Keep actual qualifying conversation contributions and materially useful lifecycle outcomes separate from technical events; D14 remains quiet with no per-target alert subscription.
7. **No scope expansion through related records or conversations.** Follow does not spread to every linked Party, D12 related thread, transferred action or restricted original. A D10 merge/Undo must retain exact original follow intent and readable provenance, avoiding accidental subscription to previously unrelated content. The UI should accurately describe which originals are followed in a merged view.
8. **No hidden completion mutation.** Reading/dismissing notification, Unfollow, follow-view filters and turning off a delivery channel cannot change Support status, response coverage, reminders or owner-domain action state. The inbox remains the authoritative work view if notifications are delayed or unavailable.
9. **No extra notification center or delivery engine.** Support contributes source facts and follow candidates through qualified owner APIs; P17 owns the shared presentation/preparation and preference contracts, P6 owns dispatch and delivery evidence, and Email Studio remains template authority where existing notification sends require it.
10. **Avoid false guarantees.** Following promises eligibility for the documented updates under current access/preferences, not successful receipt, human acknowledgement or monitoring. Notification fatigue and missing-alert anecdotes are hypothesis evidence, not proof of current product defects.

## Confidence and unresolved vendor detail

High confidence in explicit Follow/Unfollow as an established option and auto-participation as a genuine competing posture. High confidence that the permission implications differ materially by vendor. No universal consensus on auto-follow defaults or event/channel matrix was established, so A is an Asym product judgment, not a claimed industry standard.

Help Scout's newest text leaves auto-follow triggers unspecified; the older Follow page's edition list may lag current plans. Front's initial mention versus explicit-unsubscribe behavior must be reported with that distinction. Zendesk's general follower access grant and messaging-specific permission statement must not be collapsed into one assertion. HubSpot's Follow depends on related notification topics and does not prove every message emits one record-update notification. No live vendor account behavior, usability session, provider delivery or accessibility implementation was tested.
