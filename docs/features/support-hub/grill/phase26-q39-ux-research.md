# Q39 — Whose conversation unread indication?

**15 September 2026. Question-stage research.** D1–D38 and their amendments are accepted; the owner is recording D38 full ratification separately. Q39 is a genuinely open reader/list choice and remains unanswered. No personal/shared read policy, term, ADR0039, formal specification, issue, runtime behavior or external mutation is selected by this recommendation.

## Why this is the next meaningful question

The [independent gap review](phase26-q39-gap-review.md) verifies that ordinary Support conversation read/unread has not been assigned a personal or shared meaning. D1 and D3 require it to remain separate from work state, assignment and delivery. D16/D17/D35 constrain corrections, expiry and edits from generating phantom new/unread activity. D15 owns optional following and P17 notification engagement; D34 prohibits passive-reader rosters. None of those choices decides whose ordinary conversation indication is cleared by a reader action.

Fresh scoped source inspection found a shared `unreadCount` projection and increment, plus row emphasis, without a qualified mark-read command in the inspected Support app/API paths. The source is a gap to resolve, not evidence that a shared policy has already been accepted. Existing rendering must not be treated as proof of accurate read state.

This question concerns **staff reading Support conversation content**, including that same authorized reader reached through CRM. It is not whether a requester read an outgoing email, a staff notification has been opened, or a reply obligation was reviewed or completed.

## Three fair policies

Illustrative fixture: Ava and Ben can access the same Support conversation. A new requester reply arrives. Ben opens it to understand the request. Ava returns later and needs to distinguish what is new to her. The fixture tests an existing shared-work reading journey; it is not a claim about observed staffing or ministry habits.

| Option                                     | What the indication means                                                                                                                                             | Benefit                                                                                                   | Cost and limitation                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Personal unread state**              | Each staff member has their own indication. Ben opening the conversation does not clear Ava's cue.                                                                    | Helps each person find content new to them while shared assignment/work state keeps responsibility clear. | Requires private per-person state and deliberate rules for newly accessible content, new staff and old history. Different people will legitimately see different cues. |
| **B — Shared team unread state**           | One indication is shared for the relevant conversation scope. An authorized team member's qualifying read clears that shared cue for everyone in scope.               | Gives the team one shared signal for content nobody in the relevant team scope has viewed yet.            | Cannot tell Ava whether she personally caught up. Someone else's glance can clear her visible cue; it still cannot mean handled, understood, assigned or resolved.     |
| **C — No ordinary read/unread indication** | Use existing Open/Waiting/Resolved, assignment, reply targets, reminders, timestamps and qualified notifications without a separate conversation-read classification. | Smallest state and least unread-badge pressure; staff focus on actual work obligations.                   | No visual memory of what each person—or the team—has already opened. Returning staff may need to inspect more conversations to identify unfamiliar content.            |

These are alternative policies, not three settings or a per-inbox hierarchy. B has a genuine shared-triage benefit. C is the strongest smaller-scope alternative when the existing work and notification tools are sufficient. None may remove mandatory work/recovery visibility, or treat reading as completion.

**Single recommendation: A — Personal unread state.** Shared responsibility already has explicit owners in D3/D6/D7/D8/D14. Keeping a reading cue personal avoids another person's browsing erasing the information Ava needs, while requiring no coworker-read roster, extra workflow or public receipt. The recommendation is a product judgment supported by actual help-desk behavior and this clear distinction of purpose, not an assertion that all vendors agree or that every visible item must become unread for every tenant employee.

## Look-alike patterns deliberately excluded

[Help Scout's open-tracking documentation](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/421-about-open-tracking), updated September 9, 2025, discusses recipient-side pixels with false positives/negatives and ambiguous viewers in group mail. That is not this feature and is not evidence of requester comprehension. Q39 introduces no tracking pixel, recipient receipt or delivery-state claim.

[Zendesk's redesigned messaging notifications](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/9615994942234-Announcing-redesigned-messaging-notifications), edited March 2, 2026 with March 2–16 rollout, concerns messaging notifications and explicitly distinguishes other channels. Those notification unread events are not an ordinary email-conversation read frontier. They cannot justify duplicating Core P17 engagement or clearing it through arbitrary Support browsing.

Missive's [label-count FAQ](https://missiveapp.com/docs/core-features/understanding-label-types/faq) says internal comments can create unread state that has no Gmail/Outlook equivalent. The useful point is the mismatch of domains, not that Asym must copy its exact inclusion policy. D25 already excludes a Gmail/Microsoft mailbox-sync product. Do not synchronize Support reading to a provider mailbox or equate the two populations.

Freshdesk and nonprofit CRM marketing were not forced into this comparison: the returned material did not supply a stronger verified personal/shared staff-read contract. Missing evidence is not a claim that those products lack the capability. No new nonprofit workflow is inferred to fill that gap.

## UX direction without prematurely answering implementation details

The ordinary list may distinguish an eligible new/unread conversation with restrained emphasis plus an accessible textual meaning, while work status remains a separate label. Do not turn every unseen item into an urgent warning, red counter or required task. The exact visual treatment follows Base UI/base-maia and the selected policy; this question does not choose a new dashboard, menu hierarchy or all-inbox unread view.

For A, Ava and Ben can legitimately see different cues in the same shared work view. The interface must make its personal meaning clear without exposing either person's state to the other. Opening a source-qualified Support conversation from CRM participates in the same selected reading capability; merely opening a CRM profile, list preview, hidden pane or prefetched route must not pretend the staff member saw the conversation. The exact qualified read trigger is reserved for the selected answer's review.

For B, use wording that accurately describes the shared cue and avoid implying that every person has caught up. It does not name readers, but it does reveal that someone viewed the activity; a small team may infer who. Do not promise anonymity or an uninferable reader identity. Restricted originals and different staff source permissions make scope an actual requirement: a narrower viewer cannot implicitly attest to hidden material. The chosen answer would need to qualify its shared scope, rather than assume every inbox member can read the same history.

For C, continue showing truthful source timestamps and existing required work/attention. Removing a separate read cue must not hide a new substantive reply, a failed send or an overdue obligation. It removes this classification, not the underlying content or existing handling contract.

No option treats a product read signal as proof that someone understood all visible content, read every historical message, examined a file, satisfied a service promise or performed a business action. Timing alone is not comprehension. That distinction keeps the user journey helpful without surveillance or false assurance.

## Dependencies and limits preserved under all choices

1. **Reading and work remain separate.** D3 status, D6 ownership, D14 owed-response/coverage and D31 follow-up retain their exact authority. Marking read cannot resolve or defer a request, claim it, suppress required review or prove a response. Marking unread cannot reopen work, restart a target or become a new reminder engine.
2. **Support source ownership.** Current tenant, real staff identity, original source eligibility and D10 grouping remain authoritative. A personal state is not a copied conversation or CRM property. A shared state is not permission to reveal hidden sources or aggregate unqualified viewers.
3. **P17 notification engagement stays distinct.** Reading a conversation does not indiscriminately mark every related notification read, cancel direct mention duties, enroll/stop D15 Following or alter notification retention. Any existing typed engagement rule remains with its actual owner; no broad linking heuristic is introduced.
4. **Privacy and care boundaries remain current.** D16/D17 restrictions and expiry can remove unavailable contribution/metadata without creating a new read event or resurrecting old content. No coworker reader list, last-seen time, read-duration analytics, manager scoreboard or requester receipt is added. D34 neutral composing awareness is separate.
5. **Drafts remain untouched.** D37 Reply and D38 Note private content, source, files, saved states and pending operations are not cleared, posted, sent or readdressed by reading. Loading or refreshing the cue cannot replace editor content or shift focus.
6. **Email Studio and P6.** The cue owns no template, message preparation, P6 event, provider call or delivery correction. Actual staff replies and separately qualified notifications continue through their already accepted Support/P17/P6 contracts. No automatic “read” email or new notification family is proposed.
7. **CRM continuity.** Qualified Support detail entered through CRM uses the same selected state for that actor/source, preserving return context. CRM Party relationships or owner changes cannot transfer personal reading, disclose private material or fabricate last-contact/activity.

## What the selected answer must settle

If A or B is selected, explicitly qualify what source changes produce the indication; initial historical/new-access baseline; what a read action covers; how late/out-of-order content and open readers behave; whether a deliberate Mark unread action is wanted and its exact meaning; source merge/Undo; source/identity permission changes; stale acknowledgments; current-authorized storage/queries; cache invalidation; bounded retention; and readable/error/unknown states. None of these is answered by an increment-only global counter or a guessed client timestamp.

Avoid a product fork into personal/shared modes, every-inbox timing preferences, read receipts, a new progress ledger or body-per-user duplication. The minimum correct mechanism is chosen only after the policy, reconciled with current source and the already ratified platform. Real keyboard/touch/AT/mobile, two-user/two-tenant, source-restriction, incoming-during-read and CRM-return tasks are needed before implementation release.

Research produced no representative Asym staff study and no live private customer-data inspection. Current primary documentation supports the alternatives; actual behavior and capacity must be proved during later implementation. **Q39 remains unanswered. A is the single recommendation.**
