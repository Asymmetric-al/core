## Corrected decision to record

Support provides one consistent **Follow up** time chooser for its existing shared conversation reminder. It offers **In 1 hour**, **In 4 hours**, **Tomorrow at [time]**, **In 1 week at [time]**, and **Choose date and time**. Every choice displays its actual date, time and timezone before commitment. Tomorrow and one week preserve the currently displayed wall-clock minute on the next or seventh local calendar date; they never imply nine o'clock, Monday, office hours or holidays. Custom provides direct date/time entry and an optional calendar, with an explicit timezone control. Quick choices commit on deliberate activation; Custom has one **Set follow-up** action. No second confirmation is required for an ordinary valid choice.

New choices use the tenant's validated default timezone, with an explicit per-use override and visible UTC fallback. Editing preserves the accepted instant and its recorded zone. One shared server command owns authorization, timing validation, concurrency, atomic history and durable reminder intent. Retry reconciles the original operation; it never renews the delay. Reminder expiry creates the already-ratified Open review work. It does not send a message, complete a business action, pause a reply target or create a CRM task. CRM exposes the same currently authorized Support record and reminder. Personal presets, preset administration, recurrence, scheduled email and AI timing are outside this decision.

The four quick choices are a proportionate **product judgment**, not a measured universal optimum. Custom remains a first-class route. The meaningful alternative is exact date/time entry only; it avoids preset assumptions but adds repeated work. Personal presets solve repeated individual patterns at a real settings and maintenance cost. No evidence justifies that extra system now. A universal Tomorrow9 shortcut is also plausible, but unsupported as a ministry default and avoidably inflexible.

## Exact required decision language

### D31-R01 — One purpose and one shared reminder

“Follow up schedules the existing shared Support conversation reminder. It is optional, visible to currently authorized staff and independent of the conversation's work status, assignment, read state, CRM task, reply target and business outcome. Scheduling/replacing preserves D3 work meaning. It creates no personal alarm, new work domain or generic scheduling framework.”

### D31-R02 — A small fixed menu

“Every permitted entry point offers the same four quick meanings: In 1 hour; In 4 hours; Tomorrow at the displayed current local minute; In 1 week at that minute on local date plus seven. Show actual weekday, unambiguous date including year, time and relevant zone before commitment. Choose date and time remains visible after the four choices. No preset is automatically applied, remembered as a new default, or required before another action.”

### D31-R03 — Two explicit kinds of time arithmetic

“Use a trusted server reference instant. Elapsed choices add exactly 3,600 or 14,400 seconds, then round the resulting future instant upward to a whole minute; this adds less than sixty seconds and the displayed result is the value committed. Calendar choices take the actual reference local date plus one or seven and its displayed HH:mm with seconds zero. Never round the reference across midnight before choosing the calendar date. Calendar days need not equal 86,400 elapsed seconds. No business-day, Monday, holiday or D14 service-calendar adjustment occurs.”

### D31-R04 — Explicit timezone context

“For a new chooser, use valid `tenants.default_timezone` from the owning tenant context. If missing/invalid, visibly use UTC, expose a nonblocking configuration issue to the authorized maintainer and allow a valid per-use zone override. The picker always displays its zone, with the offset applicable at the target date when needed to disambiguate it. Changing this picker zone does not change tenant settings, a missionary profile, CRM data or any existing reminder. Do not derive zone from requester identity, location, IP address, a reporting filter or service-hours calendar.”

“The per-use zone control is available for quick and Custom choices and supports validated IANA identifiers, including UTC. New reminders do not require a new profile preference or a browser-zone authority. On edit, begin with the recorded zone and exact accepted instant. Within Custom, deliberately changing the input zone retains the entered date/time fields and resolves a new candidate; show the changed result and require Set/Update before commitment. This is distinct from changing a read-only display zone, which preserves the instant. A legacy reminder without original zone provenance keeps its instant and clearly uses a current display zone; do not invent historical provenance.”

### D31-R05 — Stable preview without artificial expiry

“Resolve quick offers once on deliberate open or Refresh choices, using one reference and selected zone. Keep the shown instant stable while a person reads, points or types. When relative wording ages, qualify it with its reference time or use its exact calendar label; expose Refresh choices without moving existing targets. A valid explicitly reviewed future candidate has no arbitrary preview timeout. Explicit refresh, new choice or zone edit may produce a new preview, never an automatic new write. Commit validates the exact candidate rather than recomputing a delay from send time or retry time.”

“The server validates local date/time/zone/offset against the submitted instant and returns a reviewable discrepancy if they disagree. A preview is not a security grant or proof someone read a screen. No preview token table, job, cryptographic viewing attestation or separate workflow is required.”

### D31-R06 — Custom with direct entry

“Choose date and time opens one focused form with labelled date, time and timezone; direct entry and calendar selection coexist. A fresh Custom form starts on today's date in the displayed zone with time unset, unless continuing a deliberately selected candidate or editing an existing reminder. Date selection alone does not save. Time is minute-granular for newly chosen values. Show one exact combined preview and one Set follow-up or Update follow-up action. Cancel/Escape before submission has no effects; preserve values on validation/network errors.”

“Use explicit locale-format hints and a calendar representation that maps unambiguously to an ISO date, while displaying familiar localized month/day names and 12/24-hour time. Do not silently interpret ambiguous free-form dates or add a natural-language parser. Invalid dates are errors, not normalized rollovers. Date/year navigation must not require hundreds of month clicks.”

### D31-R07 — DST, political changes and precision

“If a chosen wall time does not exist, keep the requested values, explain the clock change and offer valid nearby choices or another time. If it occurs twice, show the two actual offsets/instants and require a choice. No default earlier/later selection or silent gap adjustment is permitted. A quick calendar offer with such ambiguity opens that focused review instead of committing. A valid elapsed-duration instant is already unambiguous; show its actual target offset.”

“Persist the accepted UTC instant and enough original local input, IANA zone and resolved offset provenance to explain it. Later timezone preference, tzdb, travel or inbox changes do not rewrite an accepted instant. Existing seconds/microseconds are not rounded by opening, viewing or cancelling edit. Deliberately changing a Custom date, time or input zone creates a new minute-granular candidate; show old/new exact values before Update, including any original seconds. Preserve accepted original evidence even if later zone rules render that instant differently.”

### D31-R08 — Validation and due boundaries

“For a genuinely new admission, require a finite supported instant strictly later than trusted server time at the serialized mutation boundary. Custom supports the representable ISO civil-year range 0001–9999 intersected with valid future UTC instants; it imposes no arbitrary seven-day or one-year business limit. Reject overflow, infinity, impossible dates, missing zone, contradictory offset and past/equal-now input without losing the form. Do not use `due_at > now()` as a persistent database constraint: valid reminders must become due.”

“Due execution uses now greater than or equal to the accepted instant. Existing overdue work remains due, never silently moved forward. A long date uses existing durable product scheduling/recovery within the qualified provider horizon; it does not become one unbounded provider sleep. Stored support for a year is not a promise that timezones or staff plans remain unchanged until then.”

### D31-R09 — Existing lifecycle and simple correction

“At most one current reminder exists for a conversation. Change time conditionally replaces its generation. Remove reminder cancels that generation and deferral while preserving work meaning. Work now is the existing explicit Open action and clears deferral. Resolve cancels; an incoming qualifying event follows D3. On resolved work, use an explicit reopen followed by scheduling, or an already-qualified reviewed Reopen-and-remind action under D3; opening the picker cannot reopen work. This decision adds no new compound command. Waiting-side-only changes preserve the reminder. A current due generation clears deferral and creates Open Follow-up due review with the prior reason preserved. Due processing does not directly select an assignee, but D7's applicable active-coverage evaluation still runs and may reassign work under its own rules, including when the original scheduling staff member has left.”

“A Support conversation merge or Undo retains D10's reviewed resulting reminder plan and qualified generation. Do not silently choose earliest/latest or resurrect an old job. A related/new conversation does not inherit a reminder merely through source linking.”

### D31-R10 — Trusted mutation and database safety

“All menu, keyboard, bulk and authorized embedded-detail mutations use the same D3 command in `packages/api`. Derive tenant and actual human profile/system attribution from trusted context, not caller fields or assumed auth-user/profile-ID equality. Recheck current conversation/inbox/resource access and reviewed versions. Enforce same-tenant parent relationships, unique current reminder/generation, finite due data, allowed state combinations and immutable ownership in the authoritative transaction.”

“Use least-privilege grants and RLS. Evaluate both old-row USING and resulting-row WITH CHECK for allowed client mutations, and revoke bypassing direct lifecycle/timer/history writes. A permissive additional policy cannot leave the old bypass open. Check view invoker policy, function EXECUTE grants, fixed search path and service-role authorization separately. Existing tenant FKs/RLS are not alleged absent. Do not add money fields, copy CRM identifiers without tenant-aware relationships, or cascade-delete required history.”

### D31-R11 — Concurrency and replay

“Atomically admit state revision, current reminder/generation, immutable before/after command evidence and required durable dispatch/recovery intent. Concurrent schedule/replace/remove/Resolve/new-input actions serialize or conflict against reviewed versions; no lost update or revived cancelled generation is permitted. Before fresh future-time/stale-state validation, reconcile an identical previously admitted command after checking current access. Return its original receipt and separately current authorized state. Changed input under one identity conflicts. A new deliberate change uses a new identity. Late receipts cannot overwrite newer state.”

### D31-R12 — Dependable execution and honest recovery

“The product reminder remains authoritative; the shared workflow ledger, claims and identifier-only envelopes execute it. A callback checks current tenant/conversation/generation/due/eligible state atomically. Required D7 coverage-evaluation intent commits with due-created Open work; any resulting qualified transfer uses the guarded assignment path. Early attempts remain pending with a durable future attempt. Old, cancelled, applied or superseded attempts are no-ops even after a claim lease expires. Recovery scans bridge commit-before-dispatch crashes and finite provider run horizons. Failed/unknown saves reconcile; reopening the picker does not assume failure. Display intended due and actual processing time separately.”

### D31-R13 — Complete access journey

“Expose a labelled Follow up control beside status and assignment, with existing reminder date visible. Quick activation commits the displayed time; Custom alone opens the richer form. Preserve selected conversation, list position, filters, return destination, scroll/focus and unsent rich-text draft. Keep a successfully deferred conversation open in detail if it leaves the current queue, with truthful membership feedback. Existing follow-up views and D30 recovery navigation remain discoverable; no new overview metric is introduced.”

### D31-R14 — Bulk and alternate entry points

“Keyboard/palette opens the same chooser; typing in TipTap or another input cannot accidentally invoke it. Qualified exact-ID bulk operations show the selected count and one exact shared instant/zone, then report authorized per-item success/conflict/failure using D3/D7's existing contract. Server evaluation occurs per item, including dates that become past during a batch. Never silently recalculate later items to new instants. Retry only the same unresolved intents; don't expand selection to newly matching rows or leak inaccessible records.”

### D31-R15 — CRM and owner-domain continuity

“CRM shows the same canonical Support conversation and current reminder only when both its relationship context and Support data are currently authorized. Entering from CRM preserves the record return path and supported detail context. Support record access does not grant CRM, finance, missionary or care access, and CRM access alone does not expose restricted Support metadata. A requester, participant, authenticated user, CRM person, represented organization, assignee and CRM owner remain distinct. Email matching, labels and reminder assignment confer no identity or authority.”

“Links, merges, unlinking, archive and permission changes use their owning domain's authoritative relationships. A reminder references the conversation, never a copied CRM due field. It neither creates a duplicate person/task nor rewrites contact data. Genuine D11 delegated tasks remain separate linked work. Scheduling, expiry or resolving Support cannot mark a refund, receipt, contact change, recurring gift or delegated business task complete.”

### D31-R16 — Email Studio and TipTap seam

“Opening, previewing, setting, changing, removing and expiring this reminder perform no email content preparation, dispatch, scheduled send or new catalog-notification action. Support owns work/reminder facts; P17/Email Studio owns governed email content/preparation; P6 owns delivery/reconciliation. D4 send review remains a separate deliberately selected operation; plain Send preserves a valid reminder. This choice does not add a combined-send action or weaken an existing one.”

“D24 curated shortcuts cannot store/apply reminder presets. TipTap remains the rich-text editor for qualified replies, notes, signatures and shortcut wording; ordinary date/time fields are shared form controls. Never mount a second rich-text composer in the picker or change recipients, attachments, notes/reply mode, signatures or prepared message material. Existing independently qualified platform failure escalation remains governed by its owner; it is not a new reminder email.”

### D31-R17 — Privacy and minimization

“Reminder APIs, caches, realtime events, history, searches and CRM projections use current tenant/resource authorization. Retained UI state is cleared on logout/tenant switch/revocation as appropriate. Technical timing telemetry excludes message bodies, recipients, donor identifiers and exact location data; trusted business history records permitted actor/cause/operation references. The selected timezone is scheduling provenance, not a missionary location claim. Donor/public surfaces do not expose staff reminder internals. Existing owner-governed retention, redaction, export and backup rules apply.”

### D31-R18 — Visual and accessible interaction

“Compose existing shared `base-maia`/Base UI primitives, semantic Zinc-family tokens and Core typography, spacing, focus and motion. Use readable sentence case, one compact surface, exact secondary timing text, generous hit areas and a distinct but restrained focus/selection treatment. No all-caps microtext, ornamental clocks, new palette, primitive library or app-local calendar fork. Mobile uses a responsive single-column dialog/sheet with visible action and keyboard-safe scrolling.”

“Provide keyboard operation, input labels/format hints, announced validation and save outcomes, focus return, contrast, zoom/reflow, reduced motion, high-contrast support and touch targets. Meet WCAG 2.2 AA; use at least 44-CSS-pixel primary action rows as a product touch target, distinguishing that from the AA 24-pixel minimum and exceptions. Calendar cells may use the shared compact geometry when AA/spacing and real touch tests pass. Reuse does not establish accessibility without testing.”

### D31-R19 — Performance and operational bounds

“The four quick options are constant-sized, calendar rendering is bounded and timezone lookup is searchable without rendering every result at once. No per-conversation scheduler is created on picker open. Batch validation/reads and use indexed tenant/current-due/generation access paths, with separate bounded shared recovery admission for provider horizons. Do not scan all messages or send one preview request per row/keystroke. Preserve tenant fairness and accepted-work recovery when new scheduling is disabled.”

### D31-R20 — Maintenance and future evolution

“Product and shared-UI owners maintain one versioned definition of the four quick meanings, one renderer/resolver contract and common conformance tests. Tenant staff maintain no preset list. Changing future menu labels or zone defaults cannot reinterpret saved instants or receipts. Support operations own stale/failed due work through normal recovery controls; direct database repair is not the routine process. Personal presets or AI suggestions require later researched decisions and must remain inputs to the same human-authorized command.”

### D31-R21 — Migration and release sequencing

“Qualify D3's command, constraints, history, worker and read-model contract before enabling the new chooser. Fence legacy writers, then backfill known instants without reinterpreting them from old labels. Mark missing provenance as unknown. Ambiguous old work follows D3's migration-review rule. Deploy compatible readers before writers, verify mixed versions, activate by controlled tenant scope, and preserve due recovery/history if new scheduling is disabled. Roll-forward fixes must not shift existing due dates; rollback must not restore bypassing old writers.”

### D31-R22 — Traceable proof

“Trace D31-R01–R24 through the glossary/ADR, later authorized OpenSpec/design/tasks/tickets, implementation and release evidence. Keep D3/D4/D7/D14/D24/D26/D30 semantics consistent. Execute all P01–P32 release groups in the proof ledger before claiming readiness. Reference arithmetic and documentation checks are not SQL/RLS/worker/browser/provider or participant proof. Unknowns in throughput and usability become explicit release gates and operating signals, not confident scalability or perfection claims.”

### D31-R23 — Failure states and low-bandwidth use

“While preview resolution is loading, keep the compact surface and usable explicit inputs; do not show a false exact time. Saving shows a single pending operation. A definitely rejected save retains input and gives an actionable cause; an unknown outcome keeps its identity and offers Check status. Cancel before submission is different from closing an admitted operation. Reconnect reconciles before new actions. Custom remains usable for entry during a transient outage, but scheduling is only confirmed after authorized server admission. No offline delayed auto-send or browser alarm is promised.”

### D31-R24 — Independent clocks and responsible promises

“A follow-up time is an internal review commitment, not a customer-visible appointment, service target or guaranteed execution second. It does not alter D14 calendars/targets, D30 historical cohorts, D5 explicit ending, CRM/giving/task deadlines or notification consent. No mandatory chaser, automatic resolution, SLA pause, recurrence or future configuration requirement is inferred from time selection. Any deliberately written customer promise remains governed by the existing Support work and owner-action rules.”
