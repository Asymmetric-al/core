## Why this is the next decision

Phase 26 already includes individual/team assignment, manual assignment, round-robin, CRM-conditioned routing and explicit Unassigned work. D1–D5 separate those operational facts from work status, delivery, recipient authority and owner-domain outcomes. They do not choose the default first individual assignment of a genuinely new incoming conversation.

This question starts after correct tenant/inbox routing and safe intake. It is not a choice to reroute a continuation, reopen, moved conversation or old backlog. It also does not choose whether someone must claim before editing/sending, when absent staff's existing work transfers, or which notifications are sent. Plain Send's no-assignment side effect remains ratified. More-specific valid assignment policy cannot be silently overwritten by a general default.

The three alternatives are manual claim/assignment from visible shared intake; rotation among eligible available workers; and routing by an explicit spare-capacity/workload policy. Selecting a default does not remove the other already-scoped modes. Selecting A is not an excuse to leave the promised automatic modes unfinished or inadequately tested.

## Adversarial synthesis of the recommendation

All three independent reviews recommended A as a first-use default, but the parent assessed the counterarguments rather than treating agreement as proof. A does not solve responsibility merely by displaying an Unassigned label. The responsible tenant team must actually cover the inbox, have complete discoverable work and a clear claim/assign action. A later selected-answer review must make that ownership operationally concrete. No shift schedule, SLA or new manager role is invented in the question.

B's advantage is immediate personal accountability and less claiming overhead. It is the strongest rival, especially for a known pool actively receiving work. The missing current engine is not an argument against building B properly. C can help where limits/data represent real workload, but neither old Open+Pending counts nor number of conversations establishes effort; D3 waits and reminders retain obligations.

The supported default must remain separate from deliberately configured routing. More-specific rules, receiving rosters, team/CRM routing, availability, load weights and notifications still need explicit qualification. This preserves original Phase 26 scope without smuggling every one of those policies into this single question.

Two extra boundary corrections are included in Q6: assignment is not a send lock, so choosing A does not require a claim before every edit/send; and automatic retries may continue the same pending initial-placement intent without being allowed to steal a human claim, rerun on an old continuation or sweep all currently Unassigned work. D4 and the accepted move rule remain intact.
