# Q35 — Correcting a posted internal note

**D1–D34 and every adopted amendment are fully ratified. Q35 is unanswered.** This is the next product choice, not a selected answer or feature ADR0035. It concerns ordinary human-authored **internal Support notes**. Sent emails, incoming messages, system events, CRM-owned notes and D16 privacy redaction remain under their existing owners. Staff-recorded request briefs retain D26's separate correction contract.

**After a staff member posts an internal note, how should they correct it?**

For example, Maya records that a donor requested a callback on Tuesday, then notices she transcribed the request incorrectly: the donor said Thursday. The team needs clear current information and a trustworthy account of what changed. This illustrates an ordinary recording mistake; it is not a claimed observed ministry workflow. If the donor later changes the requested day, that is a new update, not a reason to rewrite the earlier account.

| Option                                                                       | What staff experience                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Strongest benefit                                                                                                                                         | Real cost or limitation                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Authors can edit their own notes, with visible history — recommended** | Maya chooses Edit on her own still-permitted note, corrects it in place and saves. The note shows Edited, who/when, and access to currently permitted earlier revisions. Other staff add their own clarification/correction rather than rewriting her words. | Keeps the main thread clear and accurate without forcing a second note for every typo or transcription fix; preserves attribution and reviewable changes. | Requires a small, trustworthy revision/collision/privacy contract. An edit cannot recall what colleagues already read or received, so material new facts still need a fresh update. |
| **B — Keep posted notes unchanged; add a linked correction**                 | Maya adds a new correction linked to the original. The original clearly points to its correction; readers can follow both in context.                                                                                                                        | Makes chronology explicit and avoids ordinary in-place editing of published notes.                                                                        | Even a small correction adds another entry. The thread must keep correction links obvious so staff do not act on the old text.                                                      |
| **C — Allow a short editing window, then linked corrections**                | Maya can edit her own note shortly after posting, with visible history. Once the window ends, correction uses a new linked note.                                                                                                                             | Allows immediate typo fixes while stabilizing older entries.                                                                                              | Adds a time-dependent rule and two correction journeys; a mistake noticed after an interruption becomes harder to fix. Window duration would be decided only if C is selected.      |

These are alternative product policies, not three tenant settings to implement. **All three retain D16 redaction for sensitive information and D17 finite retention.** None authorizes changing external correspondence, switching internal content to public, silently deleting history or rewriting another person's attributed statement.

## Single best recommendation

**A — Authors can edit their own notes, with visible history.** It is the clearest balance for ordinary donor-care collaboration: current information stays readable, staff can correct their own mistakes with little effort, and the change is visible. Author-only authority avoids a new administrator override for rewriting colleagues' statements. The original posting time and author remain; the edit has its own truthful time/actor. Earlier revisions are available only while current source/privacy/retention rules permit them.

This does not make a note a mutable financial record or official proof of an action. A revised sentence about a refund cannot change the refund itself. A new event, decision or changed circumstance belongs in a fresh update. Material corrections that colleagues need to notice use the existing deliberate attention path; an Edited marker alone is not a delivery or acknowledgement guarantee.

Intercom introduced author-only inline note editing with an Edited marker and a specific permission in March2026. Its current help page says there is no admin override and the feature is web-only. This supports the low-friction author-owned pattern; Asym should retain its own accessible mobile-web journey and source safeguards. [Intercom release](https://www.intercom.com/changes/en/136196-edit-your-internal-notes), [current behavior](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/8838326-conversations-faqs).

HubSpot documents editing, a History panel and consistent note presentation across Help Desk and associated records. That supports coherent cross-surface access; it does not prove Asym should adopt HubSpot's note associations or create synchronization between its own surfaces. [HubSpot notes documentation](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/help-desk/create-edit-and-manage-notes-in-help-desk).

B remains a serious simpler alternative when immutable published chronology is the stronger product priority. Zendesk does not permit ordinary comment editing. C is a compromise, but no investigated primary evidence establishes one universally correct grace period. Its timer adds friction without solving content sensitivity or already-delivered copies. [Zendesk comment policy](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408885807258-Can-I-edit-recall-or-delete-comments-in-my-tickets).

## Recommended A journey, subject to the founder's choice

1. Open the same currently authorized note in Support or its canonical Support reader reached through CRM. Show Edit only for the original human author with current edit/source permission; the server rechecks on save. No email-address matching or assignment-derived ownership.
2. Use the note's accessible action menu. Edit in place with the shared qualified Tiptap/Base UI/Maia controls. Keep an unmistakable Internal note label; preserve the separate reply draft, current source and reading position. No floating modal stack or audience conversion.
3. Save deliberately with truthful saving/error/conflict feedback. Another device's edit or a privacy/source change cannot be silently overwritten. Cancel keeps the posted note unchanged and returns focus predictably.
4. Keep the note at its original chronological position with its original author/posting time and an Edited marker. A reachable history disclosure shows permitted revisions and edit attribution; do not depend on hover alone. This is a narrow note-history view, not a generic collaborative document editor.
5. Use a new update when circumstances changed or new work occurred. Use existing explicit staff attention if a material correction needs action. Ordinary edits do not create a new optional follower message, fake new note publication or reset retention.
6. Current redaction, expiry or access loss dominates the note, old revisions, attachments, search, previews, prepared notification content and all CRM entry paths. Edit/history is not a recovery route to removed sensitive information.

The detailed revision schema, collision command, attachment/mention changes, material-correction attention, legacy author mapping and release bounds are investigated in the full adversarial answer **after selection**. This question does not prematurely choose those implementation details or imply current code already provides them.

## Boundaries that remain settled

- **D16 privacy correction:** removing sensitive material follows its governed source-redaction path. Ordinary editing must not leave forbidden text accessible in old versions. No new routine hard-delete capability is bundled into A or C.
- **D17 retention:** edits keep the existing content deadline and cannot manufacture a new retention lifetime. A newly published correction/update follows its genuine source-occurrence rules; it cannot copy expired/redacted content back into existence.
- **D15 attention:** ordinary edits correct existing presentation without a new optional alert on every save. Already-delivered notification bytes cannot be rewritten. A newly published correction or deliberate mention/attention uses its existing producer and current audience rules, not a new notification engine.
- **P17/P6 Email Studio:** editing a Support note does not prepare or send a donor email or alter external reply/signature content. Any independently qualified staff notification uses the existing P17/P6 contract. A new correction note may have existing attention consequences; it is not falsely labelled as having no possible notification effect.
- **CRM continuity:** one canonical Support note/source under Support authorization, viewed through CRM without a second CRM note store or circular sync. Independently authored CRM notes and financial/member-care facts remain owned elsewhere.
- **D34 composing:** a live composing cue is advisory and private; it is not an edit lock or permission to inspect another person's change draft. Any application to a posted-note edit must follow the accepted current-source/privacy semantics, not silently expand the published cue's meaning.
- **No speculative collaboration platform:** no co-editing, presence roster, AI author/editor, free-form workflow, bulk note rewrite, public-note conversion or administrator editing another author's statement is included.

## Why this is the next unresolved question

D17 explicitly says **“If note editing is supported”** and constrains its retention behavior without selecting it. D16 explicitly separates privacy redaction from general edit history. Current inspected PrivateNote presentation and add-note mutation do not establish an ordinary note-edit/revision capability. By comparison, bulk work and post-send navigation already have accepted decisions and were excluded from this next question.
