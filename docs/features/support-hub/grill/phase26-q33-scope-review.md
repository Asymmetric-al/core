## Proposed single question and actual options

**When repeated quoted emails and signatures make a Support conversation long, what should staff see by default?**

For example, Maya opens a donor-care exchange in which each reply repeats earlier messages and several sign-offs. She needs to find the latest request quickly, while still being able to inspect a correction someone wrote inside an earlier quoted section. This is an illustrative journey, not measured Asym frequency or evidence that every tenant uses long email chains.

| Option                                                | What staff experience                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Strongest benefit                                                                                                             | Real cost or risk                                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Compact reading, with expansion — recommended** | Repeated quoted history and signatures that can be identified conservatively are folded behind a clearly labelled control. Staff can expand the currently permitted content in place. Ambiguous content remains visible. | Reduces repetitive scrolling in the main daily work surface without a setup step or another personal setting.                 | Recognition must not conceal unique inline replies, meaningful changed signature information or uncertain content. A compact result needs an obvious way to inspect more. |
| **B — Full messages by default**                      | Show each currently permitted safe message body in full, including quoted history and signatures. Use ordinary navigation to move through the conversation.                                                              | Predictable visibility, with the smallest interpretation layer; useful when inline replies and unusual formatting are common. | More repetitive scrolling and visual noise, particularly on a phone or in a long exchange.                                                                                |
| **C — Personal compact/full preference**              | Provide the same safe compact and full reading modes, and let each staff member choose a persistent personal default.                                                                                                    | Serves staff who routinely inspect complete correspondence as well as staff who prefer a compact queue workflow.              | Adds preference discovery, persistence and reset/device behavior. It does not remove compact-mode recognition risks or justify a separate content/security model.         |

**Single best recommendation: A.** It solves the narrow reading problem already identified in the roadmap and preserves the full currently permitted information on demand. B remains a serious alternative when minimizing interpretation is more valuable than reducing repetition. C has a legitimate mixed-workstyle benefit, but there is no current evidence that another persistent setting is needed to complete ordinary Support reading. These are alternative product choices, not three tenant modes to build automatically.

Do not freeze a parser package, confidence score, line limit, collapse threshold, personal-default hierarchy or message virtualization strategy before the chosen answer's review. Do not bundle automatic summarization, AI, body truncation, new external-email formatting or a public conversation archive into this question.

## Current external evidence and tradeoffs

Read the current official Zendesk documents on 13 September2026; the retrieved articles do not establish a plan-specific entitlement, publication date or every version's behavior, so none is claimed here.

[Zendesk's delimiter documentation](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408821456922-Adding-and-removing-the-delimiter-from-email-notifications) documents expandable quoted replies and the important failure pattern that a partially inline response may sit inside the expandable region. This supports both the usefulness and the risk of compact presentation. The Asym recommendation is therefore conservative folding with retained inspection, not an assertion that all content below a delimiter is old or unimportant. Do not import Zendesk's delimiter/template settings, size limit or inline-image limitation as Asym requirements.

[Zendesk's original-email documentation](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408832876442-Viewing-the-HTML-and-original-source-for-incoming-tickets) distinguishes normal rich-message presentation from original-email inspection and documents restrictions on originals after redaction. The relevant lesson is that an expansion/recovery path remains subject to source availability and privacy. Asym must offer currently permitted safe content, not promise a permanent raw-email archive or a new bypass around D16/D17. The vendor's exact redaction behavior is not copied.

These are verified vendor behaviors and product tradeoffs, not evidence of Asym's actual thread lengths, parser accuracy, staff preference distribution or task-completion improvement. The companion UX/vendor research can supply wider comparisons; it must keep provider-specific quirks separate from the one product fork.

## What remains invariant across all three choices

- Reversible folding changes presentation only. It does not delete or rewrite original body/signature content, change source retention, generate a new message, conceal an authorization failure or change D22's matching population.
- Expansion means the currently authorized safe source content, not inaccessible/expired/redacted spans, raw transport material or unguarded original-email download. A folded region and an authorization-hidden span have different meanings.
- Search/Find can still locate and reveal the currently eligible original match. Old snippets or cached folded content cannot restore restricted material; no new cross-source matching or AI index appears.
- The same reader and exact original-source identity work through Support and CRM. Attachments retain D32's separate deliberate authorized Preview/Download actions; reading preserves drafts, focus and return context.
- P17/Email Studio continues to own actual governed outbound wording/signature/preparation, with P6 transport. A staff display preference or folded reading region does not alter sent HTML/text, headers, recipients, body material or delivery evidence. No added outbound delimiter, template key or system-message family follows from this question.
