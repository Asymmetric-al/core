# Q36 — Earlier email text in a new reply

**Current status — D36 selection, 14 September 2026:** A — New reply only; add a quote when useful is founder-selected. Q36 is answered at intent level. The [complete proposed amendment package](phase26-d36-adversarial-review.md) is under review for ratification; D1–D35 stay fully ratified. Earlier unanswered wording below is historical. No Q37 is opened.

**14 September 2026. Unanswered.** D1–D35 and all adopted amendments are fully ratified. This question selects the default amount of earlier email text appended to a new human external reply. It does not change Reply/Reply all recipients, subject/threading rules, signatures, the Compact/Full reader, internal notes or the ordinary formatting of what staff write.

**When staff reply to a Support email, how much earlier email text should be included automatically?**

Example: a donor asks about a receipt, the team exchanges two emails about it, and Maya now writes, “I’ve sent the corrected receipt.” The issue is whether the outgoing email contains that new answer alone, that answer plus the message she is answering, or more of the prior exchange. This is an illustrative communication scenario, not evidence that a note or email itself authorizes or proves the receipt action.

## Three actual policies

| Option                                          | What starts in the outgoing reply                                                                                                                | Strongest benefit                                                                          | Real cost                                                                                                                              |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — New reply only; add a quote when useful** | The new answer and governed signature/footer. No earlier email text is appended automatically; staff can deliberately include qualified context. | Focused messages and deliberate control over what previous content is sent again.          | Staff must add context when it matters. A recipient without useful conversation grouping may need to find earlier mail.                |
| **B — Quote the message being answered**        | The new answer plus the qualified body of the exact selected reply-target email, visible for inspection/removal before Send.                     | The question and answer travel together without staff adding a quote each time.            | Repeats text; the selected email may already contain nested older quotes. It is not necessarily a short, one-message excerpt.          |
| **C — Include earlier email history**           | The new answer plus a bounded, reviewable set of eligible earlier external emails from the selected thread; staff can reduce/remove it.          | More of the exchange is understandable from one email, including in fragmented mail views. | Longer messages and a broader source/recipient review burden. Exact bounds and omission behavior would need qualification if selected. |

These are alternatives for one default product behavior, not three new tenant settings. In every option, the complete outgoing content remains reviewable and current-source/audience qualified. B/C are serious convenience choices, not permission to append everything in the staff timeline.

## Single best recommendation: A

**Recommend A — no automatically appended history, with easy deliberate quotation.** The accepted Asym Support flow already provides email continuation and an explicit source/recipient review. A keeps the normal outgoing message focused while giving staff a clear way to include the particular context a recipient needs. It avoids a hidden transcript-growing behavior or a new administrative policy hierarchy.

The strongest alternative is **B**. It reduces staff effort when the specific question should routinely accompany the answer, and familiar email clients use that approach. A's cost is real: threading headers do not guarantee that a recipient sees a complete grouped thread. A short response such as “Done” may be unclear, especially when there is no prior email exchange. Staff should write an understandable answer or deliberately include qualified context; A is not a claim that context is unnecessary.

C is appropriate if carrying more of the exchange in each outgoing message is a deliberate priority. It requires greater care with repeated/nested text, recipient changes, partial eligibility, size and clear omissions. No exact message/byte cap, stripping algorithm or fallback source is selected before the founder chooses the policy.

## Evidence behind the comparison

- [Intercom's history controls](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/3527623-sending-email-notifications-without-conversation-content-or-history) explicitly distinguish included versus omitted history and permit composer inspection/removal. For conversations after12July2023, included history derives from the last message **and its history**, illustrating B's nested-content caveat. Email, API/admin-notification and Messenger-originated behavior differ; do not generalize one to all replies.
- [Zendesk simplified threading](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4565992897562-Understanding-simplified-email-threading) still sends quoted history, often collapsed by recipient clients. A concise-looking email is not proof that fewer historical bytes were sent. Its predefined fallback/limits are not Asym requirements.
- [Front's attachment guidance](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2158), updated6July2026, documents repeated files/signature images accumulating through quoted history. Text quotation must not silently become historical file acquisition or attachment reuse.
- [Outlook's original-message options](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/outlook/change-how-the-original-message-appears-in-replies-and-forwards) document automatic original-message inclusion and an omission option for the listed Microsoft365/2024/2021 flow. This is strong evidence for B's familiarity, not every client's behavior. [Gmail](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/5900?hl=en) permits conversation grouping to be turned off, so A cannot rely on grouping as a universal guarantee.

The [full current UX research](phase26-q36-quote-ux-research.md) includes exact product/date/client qualifications, Help Scout's per-thread inclusion controls and additional primary sources. A [self-selected Intercom forum report](https://community.intercom.com/train-fin-94/fin-strips-quoted-email-thread-from-replies-is-there-a-fix-14882) describes missing context in Fin-generated replies/follow-ups. This is AI-specific reported friction, not measured ministry preference or proof about all human replies or Core's email-first workflow. There is no claim of a universally optimal default.

## Boundaries preserved under every policy

1. **Target and audience stay explicit.** B targets the exact D2 message selected for reply, not whichever is newest at Send. Reply-all preference remains separate. New messages, CRM changes and merged handling views cannot silently retarget or widen the quote.
2. **Read access is not permission to send.** Only currently eligible source context for the actual outgoing recipients may be included. Internal notes and D35 revisions, internal guidance, restricted CRM/member-care material and unrelated merged sources are never automatic external history. A is not the only safe option; all three retain these controls.
3. **Quoted body and mail threading are separate.** Existing qualified Message-ID/In-Reply-To/References relationships remain under their owners. No quote does not mean a new unrelated thread; a quote does not prove identity or guarantee client grouping. No forwarding feature or broad proactive outreach is added.
4. **No historical-file surprise.** Existing D32 file viewing/preview does not attach a file. Any actual asset/file inclusion follows its independently qualified acquisition, source and recipient rules; a quote policy cannot implicitly acquire old attachments or reconstruct extra header/signature blocks outside the reviewed quote. Permitted signature/address text already embedded in the selected source remains part of its complete content/audience review; no automatic stripping algorithm is selected here.
5. **No source means no invented quote.** A first email following a phone/in-person/form request may lack an eligible earlier email. D26 briefs and D29 answers are not fabricated incoming email or automatically echoed by this default. Their existing first-reply source/recipient and deliberate content-use qualifications remain required.
6. **The whole outgoing body is visible and governed.** D33 Compact/Full only changes staff reading; hidden/collapsed text can still be transmitted. Chosen source quotations must be represented in the actual reviewed canonical message and governed preparation, not injected afterward by a template/provider or reconstructed from an unreviewed merged transcript.
7. **Email Studio and CRM retain their roles.** Support selects and authorizes source context; P17/Email Studio governs compatible composition/rendering and immutable complete-message preparation; P6 dispatches/reconciles that sealed material. Current D16/D17 quote provenance/restriction/expiry applies. CRM uses the same qualified composer without copied state or extra action authority. Sent bytes are not rewritten by later reading/default/history changes.

## Why this is the next genuine gap

D33-R18 and its Email Studio seam expressly exclude outgoing quotation policy. D2-R03/R04 fix target/audience and require reviewed quoted-context changes without selecting an automatic amount. D23 governs canonical authoring/signatures; D16-R14/D17-R19 protect source-derived quotes. None decides A/B/C. Current serializer behavior alone is not accepted intent, and its unqualified header/recipient scaffolding must not be mistaken for a finished default.

The [independent gap review](phase26-q36-gap-review.md) verifies those sources and ranks this choice ahead of speculative external side correspondence or proactive outreach. [RFC5322 §3.6.4](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc5322#section-3.6.4) establishes header relationships separately from body quotation; it does not guarantee identical recipient-client presentation.
