The scope reviewer found no qualified general internal-guidance owner. The roadmap's future internal resources and the storage domain's byte custody do not establish one. **Accepted Phase26 facts:** [D18](phase26-d18-adversarial-review.md) already permits safe links in personal and curated shared saved replies, with purpose, audience, provenance and source restrictions. A guidance tool must work in that shared detail/composer without introducing a second CRM editor or broader access.

The product gap is consequently narrow: a worker can use known links today, but has no newly agreed way to discover the right maintained public guidance and inspect its current destination from Support. Whether that convenience is worth the new lookup contract is the founder's question. No evidence establishes how many Asym tenants already publish useful guidance or how frequently staff need it.

## Reported friction, kept separate from verified behavior

A [Zendesk community request dated 17 August 2026](https://community.zendesk.com/ideas/product-feedback-enhance-agent-workspace-knowledge-panel-controls-beyond-existing-article-access-warnings-22251) reports that warnings on inaccessible articles do not prevent staff manually copying sensitive internal content into an external reply. The author asks for stronger separation and controls. This is a recent user's reported experience, not a reproduced defect, measured incident rate or proof of every current permission path.

It nevertheless identifies a plausible human-error mechanism for C: merely placing an “Internal” badge beside a public composer does not make sharing safe. A reduces this source-mixing problem by admitting only public-eligible guidance. It cannot prevent a person from retyping information learned elsewhere, and must not be advertised as universal data-loss prevention.

## The single question and genuine alternatives

**When a staff member needs guidance while handling a Support conversation, what should Support Hub make available without leaving that work?**

Illustrative example: a donor asks where to find an organization's published instructions for obtaining a receipt. Staff should be able to give a concise personal answer and the correct maintained public page, without pasting a long article or making an unrelated CRM update. An internal exception-handling procedure would be a different source with different sharing rules. These examples are possible use cases, not verified tenant frequencies or universal ministry processes.

**One best recommendation: A.** It adds a useful discovery capability beyond the already accepted saved-reply library while keeping content ownership and audience simple. B is a serious no-addition alternative, not a broken experience: a small well-maintained set of saved replies may suffice. C could be appropriate after evidence establishes the internal-guidance need and its owning contract, but importing an internal knowledge system solely for Support now would be speculative expansion.

These options concern staff access to guidance. **None bundles a new public help center, donor portal, ticket-submission form, chatbot, AI search, automatic recommendations, whole-conversation analysis or CRM workflow.** Public self-service is a separate audience and product decision. Selecting A does not ratify C for later by implication.

## Candidate journey if A is selected

This makes A reviewable without pretending the founder has chosen it or freezing implementation details before the answer's adversarial review.

1. **Stay with the conversation.** From the same permitted Support detail used in Support Hub or CRM, open a clearly named **Guidance** action near the established composer insertion tools. It does not open automatically, switch Reply/Internal note, select recipients, run a shortcut or populate a query from private message content. A read-only staff member can consult eligible public guidance; the insert action requires an editable draft.
2. **Find a useful source.** Search the deliberately selected public guidance set, not every campaign, news story, missionary profile or site page. Show title, a concise source-owned description, site and language where they disambiguate. Provide a small visible language/site filter only when the selected set makes it useful. State the active scope; avoid silently broadening to all tenant content after zero results.
3. **Preview before sharing.** Selecting a result opens a safe read-only preview with its public destination and language. Provide **Open public page** and **Insert link**, with keyboard and touch access. Only current public-eligible content is offered for external sharing; staff CMS preview tokens, draft routes and internal administration URLs must never become recipient links.
4. **Insert a normal link into the draft.** Use the source's human-readable title and owner-resolved public URL at the current insertion point. Preserve all existing wording, the managed signature and attachments. Do not copy the full article, embed dynamic page content, overwrite the message, or send. Offer ordinary Undo, and keep the original composer selection/return context. The worker can add a short answer tailored to the request; the link is help, not a demand that the requester do unnecessary reading.
5. **Send through the existing boundary.** The established D4/P17 preparation and final human send still govern. A qualified source check should detect withdrawal or loss of public eligibility; the selected answer must define its exact admission/failure contract. Do not invent arbitrary URL fetching or block unrelated composition because the guidance service is unavailable. Preserve the draft, identify the affected link, and allow removing or replacing it. Routine article edits must not silently rewrite authored draft text or a sent message.
6. **Keep the page current in its owner.** The CMS owner publishes corrections and owns locale, public URL, retirement and redirect behavior. Support's selection determines what is offered for this workflow; it does not grant CMS edit/publish authority or duplicate the article. An authorized **Manage source** handoff returns to the same conversation/draft. Removing an item from the picker must not claim to retract previously sent email or unpublish the site.
7. **Handle no result honestly.** Distinguish no matching guidance from unavailable lookup. Keep ordinary wording and manual-link composition usable. If the organization has published no suitable guidance, show a concise empty state rather than sample content, fake suggestions or mandatory setup. Do not silently build an article from the current donor conversation to fill the gap.

On small screens, the same list/preview can become a full-width sheet or page with a clear Back action; it must return to the same permitted draft. Follow the existing shared Maia/Base UI patterns, visible focus, labelled controls, screen-reader result/status announcements, adequate targets and reduced-motion behavior. A read-only preview does not require a Tiptap editor instance. The inserted link uses the accepted Tiptap purpose profile; this decision does not replace the CMS editor or create another email editor.

## Evidence limits and next proof

No runtime, browser prototype, accessibility audit, email-client test or tenant usability session was performed for this next-question artifact. No vendor session was modified. No quantitative speed, satisfaction, incident-rate or content-volume claim is supported by these documents.

After the answer, the useful proof is a small set of realistic tasks: find the right language/site guidance; preview and insert without losing a draft; return from CRM and source management; recover from a withdrawn source or lookup failure; distinguish a reusable reply from maintained guidance; and complete the same tasks with keyboard, assistive technology and a narrow viewport.
