## Exact owner invariants and concerns

Each material concern below completes or narrows A. Severity is potential consequence and likelihood is a qualitative estimate conditional on an underspecified implementation, not an incident frequency.

### O31-01 — Preserve one shared conversation reminder

**Concern: high severity; plausible.** A visually familiar picker can accidentally introduce personal alarm state, multiple reminders, recurring definitions or a new task per scheduled conversation. That fragments custody and can hide the team's real follow-up.

**Exact language:** “Quick choices and Custom resolve input for the existing single current D3 shared conversation reminder. Scheduling replaces or creates that source-owned plan under its current identity/generation rules; it creates no private alarm, repeating series, CRM task, second due-state model or automatic assignment. A reminder's creator is attribution, not exclusive ownership or authority. Actual delegated tasks continue through D11's shared Mission Control owner.”

**Proof:** two authorized workers see the same accepted reminder and original author/time; a later replacement is one new qualified generation, not an additional private timer; ordinary scheduling creates no task or CRM activity.

### O31-02 — Resolve visible calendar intent through a trusted server boundary

**Concern: high severity; likely if the current fixed-hour browser expression is reused.** A calendar label can produce a different day/time than staff saw, particularly across midnight, DST, slow connections or a stale picker. A caller-supplied due timestamp is not proof of the reviewed input or actor.

**Exact language:** “Each choice has explicit duration or calendar meaning. Before its committing action, show the server-qualified actual date, time and relevant zone, including any needed unambiguous calendar adjustment. The canonical command verifies the exact reviewed meaning/result and current source/work/reminder authority; it does not silently recompute a different time at commit or trust browser now. A changed or now-invalid result requires current correction/review while preserving safe input. Store the accepted UTC instant and sufficient input/zone/resolution provenance; later viewer or source-default changes alter display or future choices, never that scheduled instant.”

This is a consistency requirement, not a mandate for another confirmation modal, persistent preview database or new timezone platform. Use qualified shared controls and date/time owner facilities. Do not infer the zone or workday from a requester's email, CRM address, missionary location, presumed US week or hidden personal settings.

**Proof:** picker crosses midnight; chosen calendar time is repeated/nonexistent; actor/device zone changes after acceptance; delayed commit becomes past; duration and calendar choice with superficially similar labels remain distinguishable. Validate future time at command admission, not through a database constraint that becomes false when a legitimate reminder reaches due.

### O31-03 — Idempotency belongs to the accepted reminder effect

**Concern: high severity; plausible.** Retrying “Tomorrow” or “In one hour” as a newly evaluated expression can postpone a successfully admitted reminder after a lost response. A stale due callback can revive canceled work or overwrite a replacement.

**Exact language:** “A retry reconciles the same immutable accepted command/intent and due instant under current disclosure authority. It cannot renew relative time, create another generation or reapply an old success over newer work. Changed semantic input under the same identity conflicts. A genuinely new correction/reschedule is explicit and receives a fresh qualified generation. Due execution rechecks current tenant, source, topology, generation, state and due instant; canceled, superseded, applied or completed-cycle work cannot execute again.”

**Proof:** lost response then next-day retry; successful reschedule followed by old command replay; duplicate/early worker call; source event cancels pending work; partial bulk retry preserves successful item outcomes and original reviewed time rather than applying again to all selected rows. Retain D3's exact-ID/per-item bulk rules; no new all-matches operation.

### O31-04 — Shared infrastructure executes, but never decides, due work

**Concern: high severity; plausible.** A second worker queue or browser timer can lose reminders during an outage; using workflow-run status as business truth can reopen work incorrectly. Conversely, treating no new reminder notification as a reason to suppress legitimate existing failure escalation loses operational recovery.

**Exact language:** “The product-owned reminder/history and required shared dispatch/recovery intent commit atomically at the existing command boundary. Shared workflow infrastructure consumes identifier-only tenant/source/generation envelopes and re-proves the product claim. The due instant and actual processing instant stay distinct. Ordinary Follow-up due is source-owned work/UI history, not a new catalog notification. Existing independently qualified workflow/owner failure escalation retains its own scope and message policy; this choice neither introduces a per-reminder alert nor disables required recovery.”

**Proof:** crash before/after intent commit; shared executor disabled; early callback retains recoverable due work; late recovery shows the original overdue instant; re-enable never treats a canceled generation as eligible. A source state remains discoverable without requiring a Resend connection or publishing a template.

### O31-05 — Keep compose/send and reusable shortcuts separate

**Concern: high severity; plausible and directly exposed by vendor Send & snooze patterns.** A reminder click can unexpectedly send the draft, or extending a D24 saved shortcut can bypass the already closed patch and review contract.

**Exact language:** “D31 adds no Send-and-remind operation. A reminder chosen while a reply is composed is a separate deliberate use of the existing qualified D3/D4 control. D24 shortcuts cannot store or apply reminder presets. Plain Send reply preserves a valid reminder; explicit Open/Resolve or another admitted work choice retains its existing D3 consequence and may make an older reminder review stale. No keystroke, preset insertion, template save or picker dismissal sends email or applies the saved shortcut.”

**Proof:** unsent/edited reply survives scheduling/cancel; plain send preserves the accepted time; concurrent Send-and-resolve versus scheduling has one honest current-state outcome; a D24 export/import/activation cannot add reminder fields; keyboard Enter in date input commits only its focused permitted operation and not the external composer send shortcut.

### O31-06 — CRM access is a shared source journey, not copied timing authority

**Concern: critical severity; plausible.** Creating a reminder from a CRM record can silently grant Support access, force a Party, copy a task deadline or expose a restricted work item through its date/label/count.

**Exact language:** “Support-first and CRM-first use the same authorized conversation detail and same D3 reminder command. Derive tenant, actor/profile, source and current permission from trusted context; a URL/association/current CRM owner is not authority. Expose due time, provenance and controls only under current Support/source and relevant CRM disclosure rights. Preserve safe return/draft context, not stale permission. Unknown/no-Party staff/form-origin work remains serviceable through its qualified source without fabricated mail or CRM identity.”

**Proof:** CRM record is readable but Support source is not; source becomes restricted while picker is open; same-user tenant switch discards in-flight prior values; unlinked manual-ready conversation schedules without email readiness; a linked donor/task change neither rewrites due time nor duplicates an Activity.

### O31-07 — Do not couple reminder time to task, money or reply-target deadlines

**Concern: high severity; plausible.** A reschedule can look like an extension of a promised response, Finance task or donor action, and “due” can be mistaken for completion of the awaited work. Shared controls do not mean shared facts.

**Exact language:** “The Support reminder is when Support reviews the conversation. A D11 task deadline, D14 internal reply target, owner-domain completion requirement and any actual staff promise retain independent meaning and authority. Scheduling or rescheduling does not pause, fulfill, reset or synchronize them. At due, current D3 work becomes Open for follow-up with truthful cause; no reply, delivery, refund, receipt, recurring-gift change or care action is inferred.”

**Proof:** a valid future reminder coexists with an overdue D14 target and an incomplete Finance task; target due review retains its separately valid future reminder according to D14; changing a task due date leaves the Support reminder unchanged; a fulfilled reply can still have independent actionable delivery recovery.

### O31-08 — Topology and status transitions retain their accepted plan reviews

**Concern: high severity; plausible.** A menu acting on an old original can silently target a newly merged component, duplicate reminder promises during Undo or restore canceled reminders when a related conversation is created.

**Exact language:** “Bind scheduling and replacement to the reviewed current source/component and reminder/work revisions. D10 merge/Undo and D12 continuation keep their explicit resulting-plan review, source attribution and fresh-generation rules. Equal due times do not prove two promises equivalent; no min/max shortcut resolves incompatible plans. Waiting-side-only changes preserve the valid reminder; explicit Open clears deferral and Resolve cancels it as already accepted. Resolved work requires an explicit qualified reopen plan before a new reminder; no hidden timer survives completion.”

**Proof:** merge while picker open; two reminders with equal time but different purposes; Undo requiring Keep for both versus separate plans; late canceled callback; new related request receives no inherited timer by default. No new promise graph or reminder-owner system is needed.

### O31-09 — Preserve complete follow-up discovery and current privacy

**Concern: high severity; plausible.** A reminder can disappear from standard views because it is deferred/unassigned/old, or after processing clears its timer column even though review remains owed. A retained cache can disclose the due state after source access is revoked.

**Exact language:** “D3/D20/D30's all-age follow-up and recovery discovery remains authoritative. A quick choice does not create private hiding or a second reminder-only list. Current due review remains discoverable from source work/cause after timer processing; no query equates an empty old timer field with completed follow-up. Owner-qualified retired inbox work remains available without a new access grant. Current source privacy, finite metadata custody and restore suppression apply to picker state, reads, history, queries and cached due cues.”

**Proof:** due processing clears the deferral but review remains; waiting/unassigned/deferred work remains in its qualified views; old source outside an Overview period is still findable; retained retired source is permitted only under current owner qualification; revocation prevents old cached metadata reappearing.

### O31-10 — Native date/time controls and a bounded common menu

**Concern: medium severity; likely under implementation shortcut or customization pressure.** Rich-text date entry, hidden natural-language parsing, app-local picker forks or a settings hierarchy can make the narrow choice harder to understand and maintain.

**Exact language:** “Use the existing qualified shared native/date/time controls and Core's Base UI/Maia/Zinc system. Tiptap remains for qualified rich-text content, not date/time inputs. The common quick menu is code-owned and small; Custom remains a complete accessible escape path. Labels expose precise resolved meaning rather than hidden parser syntax. Fixing a menu definition changes future selections only. A adds no personal saved preset store, tenant/inbox override hierarchy, registry editor, recurring schedule language or automated inference.”

**Proof:** keyboard, touch, long/RTL locale text, 12/24-hour presentation and screen-reader resolution; the same accepted choice has the same meaning across permitted entry points; a later product preset revision cannot rebase an existing accepted reminder. Actual menu, morning-hour, zone-resolution and bounds must be settled in the root's full selected-answer synthesis; this owner report does not invent them independently.
