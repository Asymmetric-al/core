# Optional labels from a curated Support list

**D21 and every adopted amendment are fully founder-ratified, 12 September 2026. Historical disposition: Accept with required amendments.** D1–D20 remain fully ratified. The complete accepted package and explicit Email Studio seam are recorded below as grooming authority; no formal specification or implementation is claimed.

Staff may apply relevant labels from one curated Support list, but never have to label a conversation before receiving, replying to, handing off or resolving it. Qualified maintainers control the vocabulary. Label application remains simple; catalog lifecycle, source identity and authorization are enforced by the owning service.

The decisive corrections are: separate vocabulary management from ordinary conversation editing; preserve original-source memberships through merge/Undo; replace ambiguous toggles with durable explicit commands; use Archive/Restore instead of destructive routine deletion; preserve international names independently of identity; and make optional-label reports honest about population, missing classification and overlap.

The [complete UX](phase26-d21-labels-ux.md), [data/lifecycle/reporting contract](phase26-d21-data-contract.md), [evidence register](phase26-d21-evidence.md), three independent reviews and [validation](phase26-d21-validation.json) accompany this decision. Current code observations and illustrative experiments are explicitly separated from required production proof.

## Corrected decision clauses

### D21-R01 — Optional categorization, narrowly scoped

Provide optional labels from one flat tenant-owned Support catalog. A conversation can have zero, one or several applicable labels. No label is required for intake, reading, replying, assignment, delegation, ordinary status changes or Resolve. No automatic label, default catch-all, primary-topic field, personal label catalog, nested taxonomy, label-as-folder navigation or required classification mode is added. D20 My/Shared views already solve personal/shared retrieval. The example Receipt question describes a request; it is not a measured ministry need or an automatically seeded taxonomy.

### D21-R02 — Separate applying terms from maintaining them

Reading the catalog requires current authorized staff access to Support in that tenant, not mere tenant membership. Applying/removing requires current authority to edit the exact affected Support source metadata; assignment alone is not an access grant. Creating, renaming, changing description/appearance, archiving and restoring require the distinct delegated Support configuration-management capability in Core's existing IAM model. These are capabilities, not new global roles or a custom ACL per label. The same person may possess both. D18 publication, D19 intake-review designation and D20 Shared-view maintenance do not automatically confer label management. Tenant oversight can maintain/delegate the catalog through its actual administrative authority; no omniscient transcript access is implied.

### D21-R03 — One owner and no second CRM

Support owns catalog meaning and original-conversation label membership in Asym Postgres, through packages/api. Reuse qualified Core identity, authorization, shared UI, audit, configuration lifecycle/reference and query facilities where applicable. The current support_labels and support_conversation_labels are the starting source model, not permission/lifecycle proof. Do not create a generic tagging microservice, duplicate Party tags, synchronize with a second CRM, or force simple labels into a new custom-field engine. Phase9 Party tags and Phase11 taxonomy/field-option contracts retain their subjects; future reuse must preserve these boundaries. There is no per-inbox label schema or second catalog within each CRM record.

### D21-R04 — General vocabulary, not a hidden permission system

The shared catalog is for general Support topics that may be disclosed to eligible Support staff. Catalog names and descriptions must not contain a person's case details, secrets, missionary location or restricted-care narrative. Explain this beside authoring fields; do not claim automated sensitive-data detection. Protected subjects retain the actual Phase10/38/source controls independently. No ordinary private/secret labels or arbitrary per-label visibility matrix are introduced. Where legacy or newly discovered sensitive metadata exists, the actual records/privacy owner can restrict and correct it across all representations; an archived catalog is not a safe vault. A label named Confidential, Urgent or Refund complete cannot authorize access, change D14 targets or attest a business outcome.

### D21-R05 — Stable identity and readable international names

Use an immutable server-created label ID and tenant-aware references. Human names and compatibility slugs are presentation/lookup data, never identity. Preserve Unicode names; do not derive identity through the current ASCII-only slugifier. The initial authoring bounds are a nonblank name of at most 80 Unicode scalar values and an optional plain-text description of at most 140, measured after NFC normalization and trimming; UTF-8 bounds are correspondingly 320 and 560 bytes. These are bounded product choices aligned with the existing form's intended brevity, not vendor or legal limits. Reject control characters/newlines in these single-line fields and show exact validation; do not strip meaningful non-Latin text, diacritics or punctuation silently. Preserve familiar capitalization and natural spaces. A compatibility token, if still needed, is generated independently and kept opaque to normal staff.

Use one server/DB-consistent, versioned NFC/trim/Unicode-casefold comparison key for catalog name collisions across active and archived entries. Do not strip accents or punctuation to infer synonyms. A duplicate prompts use/edit/restore of the eligible existing entry; never merges or creates another record silently. Reserve normalized names across active and ordinary archived definitions while the name is retained under owner policy. Actual privacy disposal may remove name/comparison material without permitting ID reuse; never retain forbidden text merely to reserve its spelling. Legacy collisions and normalizer upgrades require explicit reconciliation before the uniqueness contract activates; no arbitrary survivor or automatic rewrite of saved references.

### D21-R06 — A visible, quiet staff control

Show Labels and Add label in the conversation's existing details area, with selected labels readable and compact overflow. Use a searchable multi-selection control with readable names and optional short meaning; search the eligible catalog rather than slugs alone or just popular/loaded items. Applying an existing label is one deliberate selection, not a modal approval. Keep the picker open for additional selections and preserve search/focus. Zero labels is a neutral valid state. Loading, no matches, empty catalog, permission loss and request failure are different states. No-matches never means Create. Authorized maintainers can reach Manage labels; other staff may use established internal collaboration without a new label-request queue.

### D21-R07 — Simple maintainer journey

Use one reusable management surface in Support settings, reachable contextually by qualified maintainers while preserving return context. Provide search and Active/Archived sections, readable name/meaning, Edit and Archive or Restore. New label uses Name, optional Meaning and optional restrained appearance; no Publish, approval chain or template workflow. Save returns the committed identity/revision. Preserve unsaved input on validation, permission, duplicate-name or conflict failure. A tenant can operate with an empty catalog; absent curatorial staffing must not block Support work. Reuse existing IAM/administrative maintenance rather than requiring a new curator roster or per-inbox backup scheme.

### D21-R08 — Rename never means merge or reclassification

Edit name, description and appearance changes the selected catalog record only under its expected revision. Fixing wording must preserve the label's established meaning. A materially new meaning gets a new label; retire the old one instead of repurposing its ID over history. Same-name rename is a clear conflict, never Zoho/Freshdesk-style transfer-and-delete. No automatic synonym merge, mass reassignment, saved-view predicate rewrite or historical relabelling occurs. Existing accidental duplicates can be deliberately standardized for future use by choosing an active term and archiving the other; historical IDs remain interpretable. Staff can deliberately correct a particular conversation's memberships through R10/R12. A generic bulk label-merge engine is not justified by this decision.

### D21-R09 — Archive and Restore preserve meaning

Archive prevents new application but preserves still-authorized existing memberships and historical/reference meaning. Previously applied archived labels remain visible with a quiet Archived qualifier, removable by authorized handlers, and usable in D20 filters/reports. Archive does not remove memberships, resolve work, change clocks or break a saved predicate. Restore makes the same ID eligible for new application; it does not restore removed memberships, publish to a new audience or replay old commands. Use expected catalog revision/lifecycle fencing so a pre-archive Apply cannot succeed after Archive→Restore merely because the label is active again. Keep a persistent Archived destination; toast Undo may supplement Restore but cannot be the only correction path.

Ordinary catalog UI offers Archive, not irreversible Delete. Used/unused accidental entries share this predictable path. Actual owner-authorized disposal/redaction is distinct, honors live references, privacy and retention, and leaves the required non-content tombstone/availability meaning without preserving forbidden names. It is not a global scrub button in the ordinary manager.

### D21-R10 — Explicit application and removal

The UI sends Add or Remove, never a read-then-invert toggle or replacement of an entire hydrated label array. Bind command identity, label ID, exact current conversation/component, reviewed membership/control revision and source scope. Add is unchanged when already effective, but must still pass current source and lifecycle authority. Remove targets the exact applicable current contributors. A lost response retries the same durable intent, not another toggle; an old successful Add retried after a later Remove returns its original receipt plus current state and cannot resurrect it. A conflicting new intent refreshes the affected controls for deliberate action. No update-as-create, client timestamps/actor fields or ID reuse.

### D21-R11 — Atomic changes, current authority and race ordering

Commit membership changes, minimal durable business history, revision advancement and command receipt atomically through one authoritative mutation boundary. Catalog archive/privacy actions and relevant membership commands participate in a common ordering/control discipline; same-label lifecycle races have one valid order. Membership and D10 topology revisions guard the full affected original set. Use deterministic locking/current conditional writes or an equivalent proven transaction, not a cache snapshot, UI disable flag or app-only multi-call sequence. No-op commands create no phantom domain activity. Index/cache refresh is recoverable secondary work; failures cannot turn a committed operation into a second write or a falsely acknowledged rollback. Browser intent is preserved on recoverable failure but never replayed blindly after tenant/user/access changes.

### D21-R12 — Preserve D10 original-source membership exactly

Keep memberships on original conversation endpoints. A combined conversation exposes the permitted deduplicated union. Add anchors to the continuing origin and is unchanged if already effective; it must not create a second provenance contribution just because the UI shows a combined record. Combined Remove ends the exact reviewed current contributing memberships atomically, under authority for all of them. If topology/contributors change, refresh and retry deliberately; do not partly remove hidden contributors or follow a mutable root silently. Undo partitions current original memberships and preserves later edits. D12 related conversations do not inherit labels. Merge/Undo composition alone creates no synthetic label events, P6 mail or automation. This is the already-ratified D10-R24 discipline, not a new graph.

### D21-R13 — All authorization paths enforce the same contract

Trusted server context establishes tenant, actual actor/profile and current memberships/capabilities. Database constraints enforce non-null tenant/ID/state/version and same-tenant label/conversation endpoints, unique active membership per original pair, valid bounded fields and legal lifecycle values. Protect immutable attribution/endpoints from arbitrary updates. Raw browser grants and RLS cannot retain blanket staff catalog CRUD or mutation paths that bypass curation/source checks. Prove SELECT/INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE, both USING and WITH CHECK, invoker/definer functions, execute grants, fixed search_path, table-owner/BYPASSRLS behavior, service-role callers, exports and restore jobs. Revoke raw mutation access where the approved server-only boundary requires it. A new narrow policy must not leave an old permissive policy that ORs access back open. Constraints complement, not replace, source permission checks.

### D21-R14 — Protect metadata and all its representations

Catalog text, associations, revisions, search terms and audit evidence are potentially sensitive data. D16/D17 and actual records-owner restrictions apply to source-derived content and permitted structural metadata according to their own qualified purposes. Routine label activity never renews a body or metadata lifetime. Finite configuration/membership/audit/receipt/query-state retention and restore barriers must be qualified before activation; do not copy the message-body interval or retain every old name forever by assumption. Privacy restriction can supersede ordinary archive visibility. Caches, exports, search, labels on CRM projections, history and any existing AI consumer must honor current owner availability; raw rejected input is not copied into logs. Restores cannot resurrect previously forbidden names or membership disclosures. No new AI classification/retrieval capability is introduced.

### D21-R15 — D20 views remain exact

Reference stable label IDs with explicit Any selected, All selected and No labels semantics. Across facets retain AND. Archive preserves reference meaning; actual missing/purged/forbidden reference is unavailable/needs attention, not a dropped clause or empty result. Deliberate Add/Remove can change current matches; catalog maintenance cannot silently broaden a predicate. No labels means no effective label membership in the qualified source scope, including ordinary archived memberships; unknown/unavailable metadata is not assumed empty. Do not disclose hidden labels or their counts. If authorization cannot provide a complete answer, use the established partial/unavailable treatment. Evaluate complete authorized predicates before count/page and deduplicate continuing roots before result pagination.

### D21-R16 — Report current categorization honestly

The label report answers **what labels the selected conversation cohort has now**, not what staff knew at resolution or what was applied during the date range. Name that basis in the interface and export. Use one server evaluation instant, qualified current Support scope and the existing source-owned conversation-start fact, preserving D10 original timing and current grouping rather than using label edited_at, merge time or provider callback arrival. The companion data contract defines the exact cohort, incomplete-data behavior and denominator. No ad hoc browser aggregation over truncated hydrated rows, no arbitrary ID fallback display, and no historical snapshot warehouse is added.

Expose total distinct conversations N, conversations with at least one effective label L, and conversations with none U, with L+U=N only where metadata is complete. Each label count is distinct conversations carrying it, not number of source contributions. Percentages use N and are undefined rather than zero at N=0. Multiple labels overlap; summed label counts/percentages can exceed N/100%. Show unlabelled coverage explicitly. Missing/forbidden evidence cannot improve coverage or be presented as a completed population. Ordinary archived labels remain in current results with status; current privacy wins.

### D21-R17 — Report navigation and historical limits

Use the existing Support report surface with a horizontal bar/table, explicit period/timezone and scope, compact coverage summary and text explaining overlap. Avoid a pie/100%-stacked chart implying exclusive categories, gamified staff tagging rates or a leaderboard. Selecting a label opens report-context drilldown through the shared query/list capability using the exact component-first report predicate and source scope. D20's ordinary createdAt filter is not an equivalent predicate after merge; never substitute it or drop the date constraint. D21 does not add saving this report cohort as a My/Shared view. Broader Open in Support is explicit navigation. Chart/count/drilldown parity applies to one evaluation; a later live drilldown shows a new As of instant and may legitimately differ. Current recalculation may change after deliberate membership correction or merge/Undo. Audit history answers who changed a label when; it is not silently used as a complete historical topic dataset. Export requires the actual export capability, complete authorized query and current restrictions, without raw internal IDs or source text leakage.

### D21-R18 — CRM continuity without tag propagation

Support requester, message participant, authenticated actor, CRM Party/person/household/organization, represented organization, support handler and CRM owner remain distinct. Matching email, a shared address, an unresolved requester or acting on behalf of someone never establishes a Party or label transfer. A conversation can be labelled without a CRM identity. In the CRM's authorized Support projection show the same eligible conversation labels, with preserved return context and independent CRM/source checks. Label edits do not tag the Party, move its owner, add CRM interaction/last-contact events, change receipts/refunds/gifts or mark delegated tasks done. Linking/unlinking, Party merges, deletion, archived records and permission changes do not silently reassign conversation labels or widen access.

### D21-R19 — Email Studio, Resend and automation stay separate

Labels and views are configuration/source metadata. Email Studio owns D18 reusable wording and P17 presentation/preparation; Support owns a current draft/admission; Phase6 owns actual delivery through the tenant's qualified Resend connection. Label create/edit/apply/remove/archive/restore neither publishes wording, changes recipients, sends email, creates a follow nor restarts D13 confirmation. D15 routine label changes stay quiet. No new label email template or notification channel is introduced. Existing macro/automation/import/provider paths must not retain bypasses; D18 content insertion remains content-only, and Phase34 is the sole separately qualified configurable automation vocabulary. No AI/keyword/provider tags create vocabulary or memberships automatically in this decision. Local operational exceptions use existing qualified ownership/attention patterns only when there is a real failed obligation, not every catalog edit.

### D21-R20 — Performance follows complete bounded queries

Use tenant-leading indexes for label identity/name lookup and both membership directions needed by source projection/filtering. Query selected/eligible rows on the server; do not scan the entire tenant label-join table to render one conversation. Catalog search is complete, bounded and paginated; selection survives page changes and no-match is declared only after a successful complete search. Reports aggregate source-authorized distinct identities before limit, with cheap existence checks where appropriate. Register the exact supported query/name/membership/payload/page/time/concurrency budgets in the existing platform governor before activation and test boundaries with skewed tenants, heavily merged components and many memberships. No invented universal label quota or latency claim, per-label worker, external search service or all-catalog count fan-out. D20 current tenant/principal/scope/revision/lifecycle cache discipline applies to catalog, source membership and reports.

### D21-R21 — Durable minimal history, separate diagnostics

Record real create/edit/archive/restore/apply/remove/correction outcomes with trusted actor, tenant, immutable IDs, admitted time, command/revision and minimal before/after facts needed to explain that change. Origin contributions are explicit for combined Remove. No-op retries do not add fake edits. Keep catalog changes and source metadata history separate from customer-visible conversation messages and P6 communication events. Sensitive old names are governed content, not mandatory forever audit blobs. Technical traces report code, latency, retry/correlation and safe IDs, not message bodies/private label text. Staff can inspect currently authorized history and recover a known outcome without developer database repair.

### D21-R22 — Proportionate operations

Use the five named controls O01–O05 below. Zero tolerance for known cross-tenant/unauthorized disclosure, silent predicate broadening or a false completion receipt is a release/incident rule, not a monitoring substitute for prevention. Catalog upkeep uses existing tenant Support administration; sparse optional classification alone is not an alert or a defect. No per-label expiry timer, mandatory curator-response SLA, duplicate-name AI, automatic cleanup or additional governance platform. A real missing/failed technical obligation gets an accountable owner and bounded response through existing operations.

### D21-R23 — Additive migration and safe rollout

Inventory actual persisted labels/memberships and all readers/writers before activation, without assuming fixtures equal production. Add stable identity/lifecycle/revision/receipt/history/name-key fields and qualified constraints additively. Preserve existing tenant and original endpoint IDs. Backfill only supported facts; do not invent actors, historic application times, coverage or primary categories. Unknown metadata provenance remains explicitly legacy. Reconcile collisions/empty slugs/inaccessible references under a bounded owned report; do not silently merge or rename. Fence old toggle, blanket CRUD, whole-array, destructive-delete, macro and fixture writers before enabling curation. New shared components replace duplicated manager/form behavior. A tenant flag can stop new label mutations while allowing safe current reads/archived recovery; actual privacy revocation remains immediate. After new lifecycle data exists, rollback cannot re-enable old destructive semantics; prefer roll-forward repair and guarded compatible readers. No production migration is executed during grooming.

### D21-R24 — Traceable proof before activation

The accepted answer, pending exact amendment clauses, glossary, feature ADR, future OpenSpec/design/tasks/issues, implementation and release evidence must carry the same D21 identifiers and semantics. P01–P36 are falsifiable outcome gates; all remain unexecuted unless the evidence explicitly says otherwise. Register actual permission keys, budgets, retention owners and installed DB/runtime details in implementation design, not by guessing them in product copy. Do not call this production-ready because source pointers, a small helper experiment or documentation formatting passed. No Q22 is advanced before this complete D21 package is reviewed and ratified.
