# D23 independent signature and rich-text UX research

**Research date: 12 September 2026. Status: A selected; this is an independent research and adversarial input to the complete D23 amendment package, not founder ratification or runtime proof.** Scope: inbox-managed signatures with responder personalization; professional email presentation; authoring and everyday reply journeys; Tiptap in suitable Support rich-text surfaces. No production account, provider, inbox, message or implementation was changed.

## Conclusion

**Accept A with required amendments.** Make an inbox's human-reply signature a governed part of its Email Studio presentation. Personalize it using the actual responder's approved public identity; retain an explicitly configured team-only presentation. Staff should see the exact signature while composing and normally do nothing to configure or choose it. The authorized maintainer gets a small, capable Tiptap editor inside Email Studio, a trustworthy preview, draft/publication separation and a clear list of affected inboxes. Support provides the contextual entry point and controls its inbox's selection; it does not own a second editable signature library.

The strongest simpler alternative remains B, team-only everywhere. It removes the public-personalization dependency but gives up human continuity unnecessarily for ordinary teams. C, personal signatures, is a legitimate model but multiplies setup, variation and departure/coverage problems without a demonstrated Asym need. A's team-only exception stays inside the same shared model; it does not introduce three parallel signature systems.

Professional means readable, accurate, restrained and dependable in actual email clients. It does not mean a large promotional panel. This is a product judgment supported by the source comparison below, not a measured claim that one signature increases donations, trust or satisfaction.

## A complete but small user journey

### 1. The ordinary responder starts with no signature setup

When an authorized responder opens Reply, the managed signature appears after the editable body and before any deliberately included quoted history. It is readable and selectable, but not an accidentally editable piece of the body. Normal caret movement, Select all, paste, Replace, saved-reply insertion and Undo affect the intended body region; they do not silently remove, duplicate or replace the managed block.

One quiet **Signature details** control identifies the inbox, effective presentation and source of the public name. It is available by keyboard/touch rather than hover alone. The usual state needs no warning badge, dialog, extra confirmation or spinner. Existing D4 full-email preview remains available; there is no mandatory preview modal for every reply.

The secondary Signature menu can offer **Hide for this reply** and **Restore signature**. This is a deliberate omission of the ordinary signature block, not permission to rewrite shared presentation or hide a required organization/legal footer. It helps a short follow-up or a reply that already contains a deliberately written closing without adding personal signature variants. The hidden state stays visible with Restore available, needs no confirmation dialog, belongs only to that draft and resets to the inbox default on the next reply. It is bound to reviewed content and cannot be switched after approval. Do not use a small hover-only X, persist a personal default or guess that pasted/quoted text is a duplicate signature.

The responder writes the actual answer and presses the existing Send reply or deliberately staged Send-and-work action. The inline signature is part of what they review. It is not appended for the first time after Send. Internal notes do not carry an external signature; their staff attribution remains internal.

Illustrative example, not an observed ministry workflow:

```text
Maya Chen
Donor Care · Example Ministry
example.org/contact
```

The inbox/team line establishes context. The name is Maya's approved public form, not a first-name guess, legal name, assignee name or CRM record owner. If Jordan is coordinating the conversation and Maya replies, Jordan remains coordinator and Maya is the responder.

### 2. Inbox configuration makes the effective result obvious

At **Support settings → Inboxes → [Inbox] → Reply signature**, show the effective published presentation as a card: internal name, language, text preview, **Published** status and a concise usage explanation. Show ordinary capabilities based on current rights:

- **Choose signature** selects an eligible published presentation for that inbox. Selection is an explicit Support-owned configuration command, not a publication or send.
- **Edit in Email Studio** preserves a return path and the inbox context. It opens the same owned document; it does not copy an HTML field into Support.
- **Create in Email Studio** is available only where authoring permission exists. The inbox context can be preselected, but cannot grant publication or signature-assignment rights.
- A viewer without edit rights sees a clear read-only effective result. Do not send them through an empty editor or tell them generically to contact support.

Use an effective **Team only** presentation when the tenant deliberately chooses that voice or needs to avoid putting staff identity in the signature. Do not offer misleading “Anonymous” wording: From display, manually typed body, past messages or attachments can still identify someone. A requirement for team-only external identity must be checked through the existing qualified Sender/Reply Identity and full-email preview; signature selection cannot silently change headers.

An approved team-only fallback handles absent public-name data. Its use is visible before approval with concise repair guidance where useful. There is no silent fallback to login email, private phone, legal name, another agent or arbitrary profile fields. A tenant that forbids that fallback must receive a fixable preflight error instead of fabricated identity.

### 3. Signature creation is structured authoring, not template programming

Email Studio opens a short form with **Name**, authored language/direction, the Tiptap signature editor and a synthetic preview. The internal name never leaks into mail. Start from an attractive, text-first shared presentation already compatible with the human-reply frame. Names and descriptions support search; a large signature gallery is unnecessary.

The default starter includes the typed **Responder public name** token and approved team/organization context. Do not populate unapproved employee contact information to make the preview look complete. A well-labelled synthetic long-name fixture demonstrates wrapping; it is not a real recipient or an authority to send.

Qualify a published Email Studio Saved Section for this signature purpose and copy its exact approved revision into a managed draft slot with source/provenance. This reuses existing authoring while defining the signature's bounded purpose and insertion position. It is not a new Role Layout, signature compiler, mutable live reference or body fragment repeatedly appended during rendering. The general frame's protected footer remains separate.

The signature profile needs text paragraphs/line breaks, emphasis, descriptive links and typed approved variables. A governed logo control can add or replace one small static mark without asking the author for markup, remote hosting or CSS. Font, scale, spacing, color and placement come through compatible shared Email Studio presentation controls. No raw HTML/CSS/script input, arbitrary third-party image URLs, nested layout builder, social-icon tray, animation, survey widget or marketing-banner builder is required. Professional branded signatures remain possible through quality defaults and governed presentation, not unrestricted editor nodes.

Do not invent numeric image limits from a competitor's defaults. Reuse the compatible owner's asset/compiled-message limits and display exact accepted types, dimensions/byte limits and repair messages when qualified. The editor must validate them on paste/upload and publication. A browser-sized image does not prove a safe attachment or an acceptable compiled message size.

**Save draft** saves an authoring candidate and keeps the currently published presentation active. **Publish** runs the current Email Studio review/authorization floor and displays the affected inbox/consumer set. No second Support-specific approval system is added. Where an author lacks publication permission, show the existing review-request action and current review state.

Publication does not itself activate that signature on a new inbox. If the author also holds Support configuration rights, the UI can provide a separate clearly named **Use for [Inbox]** action after publication; otherwise the inbox selection remains pending. If those effects are separate and the second fails, show “Published; inbox unchanged” and offer a safe retry. Never claim the inbox changed because publishing succeeded.

### 4. Editing and maintenance preserve continuity

Editing a published presentation makes a candidate. A concise comparison shows copy, variables, logo, language and presentation changes before Publish. The affected-use list is permission-aware; an author without inbox visibility does not learn restricted inbox names from counts or errors. Existing owner rules govern publication and dependency fan-out; no live mutable signature fragment is introduced.

Normal publication makes a new revision available, but does not automatically advance inbox bindings or change already admitted/sent replies. Deliberate application changes the signature selected for future draft copies. A draft pinned to a valid previous revision does not change while someone is typing merely because a maintainer corrected punctuation. If an actual responder, sending inbox, required public identity dependency or safety fence changes, D4 provides a visible review refresh. A changed actor is materially different from an ordinary new template revision.

Rename changes the internal library label. Duplicate creates a separate draft only when explicitly requested, not an implicit synchronization relationship. Locale variants are independently authored and governed through existing Email Studio semantics; no automatic translation or guessed language from a surname is added. If a required locale is missing, expose the approved fallback in review or block where none is authorized.

Archive removes a presentation from new selections according to the owning publication contract; it is not a deletion of historical mail. The final D23 contract chooses an exact active-binding rule: **Replace and archive** validates the complete current affected binding set, expected revisions, source and inbox permissions and replacement/posture eligibility, then applies replacement and archive atomically. If this cannot complete, ordinary archive does not happen. Safety withdrawal is a distinct operation and uses current no-send/recovery fences without waiting for all replacement permissions. Do not label destructive withdrawal as ordinary Archive.

Author departure does not break valid shared presentation or turn the former author's personal assets into a prerequisite. Staff departure affects their authority to send, not the organization's historical signatures. A public-name correction uses the actual identity/profile owner; a signature maintainer cannot update staff or CRM identity by typing a token value.

### 5. Exceptional and degraded states are quiet and truthful

- **Editor save fails:** keep permitted unsaved work under the existing draft-custody policy; show unsaved state and Retry. Do not claim Saved, publish partial content, or fall back to an ungoverned local HTML store.
- **Another maintainer publishes:** stale revision detection prevents overwrite. Preserve the candidate and show a reviewable conflict; no silent last-writer-wins publication.
- **Permission revoked while open:** recheck on save/publish/selection/preparation; preserve only permitted recovery material and show the narrow unavailable state without exposing another tenant's content.
- **Asset unavailable or unsafe:** block the affected new preparation or use only an already-authorized text-only alternative visible in review. No hidden external-image fallback.
- **Connection or render unavailable:** use D4's truthful preparation/recovery state. The signature editor cannot invent a new sender path or bypass Resend ownership.
- **Copy from another rich editor:** retain permitted semantic wording, visibly report material dropped structure, preserve recoverable original input under the source policy, and never silently save a stripped required token.

## Professional presentation and accessibility

A compact stacked signature is the safest starter. The responder/team identity must remain real text. A small logo can support recognition; it cannot be the only place the name, team or contact method appears. Default to useful public team contact paths that actually work for this inbox. A personal job title can be included only when intentionally sourced and approved; an IAM role is not an outward-facing job title. The current profile adds no per-person email/phone token; approved team contact wording and links remain available. Do not automatically add an office address, staff location, headshot, pronouns, religious title, campaign link, donation CTA, social strip or lengthy disclaimer.

That is not a claim those details are universally inappropriate. A tenant may have a justified public communication requirement. Such content should use the existing governed authoring/presentation scope and owning policy, rather than creating a product-wide rule that all donor-care messages contain it. A signature is also not an electronic signing workflow, a receipt, proof of a refund or evidence that a delegated action finished.

[Microsoft's Outlook guidance](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accessibility/outlook/make-your-outlook-email-accessible-to-people-with-disabilities) recommends meaningful link text, accessible typography and text alternatives; it cautions against essential information existing only inside images. [Classic Outlook blocks automatic external-picture downloads by default](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/outlook/block-or-unblock-automatic-picture-downloads-in-classic-outlook-email-messages). [Gmail lets readers control external images](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/145919?hl=en). Therefore the complete reply must remain understandable and navigable with images unavailable; image loading is not a reliable proof of recipient attention. No open-tracking pixel or recipient-specific tracking URL should be introduced by signature authoring.

[W3C image guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/) distinguishes informative, decorative and functional images. A logo duplicating adjacent organization text can be decorative; a linked logo needs a meaningful accessible destination, or should remain unlinked beside a clear text link. Avoid announcing repetitive “logo” text that adds no meaning. Plain text output must retain meaningful names, contact values and link destinations through the same compiler; it cannot be a screenshot or generic “view this email” link.

The editing UI retains Core's exact `base-maia`, shared Base UI primitives and semantic tokens. It does not adopt Tiptap demo styling or a second component library. Keyboard, screen-reader, touch and mobile access must reach every action, including logo replacement/removal and token editing. Use a predictable labelled toolbar and restore focus/selection after link/token dialogs; follow the [W3C toolbar pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/toolbar/). Selection-only bubble controls cannot be the sole access path.

Verify non-excepted editor and preview content at [320 CSS-pixel reflow and 400% zoom](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html), with an IME, a mobile keyboard and horizontal/vertical focus order. [WCAG 2.2 target sizing](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum) requires appropriate size or spacing; inline text links have specific exceptions, so do not mechanically stretch every email contact link into a button. Test long public names, mononyms, punctuation, non-Latin scripts, RTL/LTR mixtures and contact URLs. [W3C international name guidance](https://www.w3.org/International/questions/qa-personal-names) supports preserving the public form rather than splitting or reordering it by Western assumptions.

The app preview is not an email-client certification. Required release proof covers the exact compiled HTML/plain text in representative current Gmail, Apple Mail and classic/new Outlook paths, including image-blocked, dark/light and reply/forward behavior. The owner must record versions and actual outcomes. Do not promise pixel-identical rendering or claim an untested client matrix passed.

## Tiptap scope and limits

The founder explicitly selected Tiptap. Use a shared Tiptap authoring capability with purpose-specific schemas, not independent editor stacks per tab and not one unrestricted schema everywhere. [Tiptap 3 schemas](https://tiptap.dev/docs/editor/core-concepts/schema) support constrained nodes/attributes; unsupported input can be stripped, so stored-content loading and paste need explicit error/loss handling. [Tiptap JSON/HTML output](https://tiptap.dev/docs/guides/output-json-html) is useful interchange, not proof that browser HTML is safe email output. Core ADR0030 still owns the canonical document/compiler rule.

## Proof and remaining evidence

These are falsifiable outcomes for the later formal design/implementation, not tests executed by this research:

1. A responder without signature-authoring rights can reply with the correct published signature; a signature author without Support configuration cannot bind it to an inbox or send.
2. A designated maintainer creates, saves, previews, submits/publishes and deliberately selects a signature with no raw HTML, copy/paste between systems or extra signature library. Failed final selection reports the published-but-not-selected result.
3. Unlinked requester, on-behalf-of participant and CRM relink do not change the responder or create CRM identity/history. From/Reply-To remain under their prior owner.
4. Assignment change alone does not change attribution; authorized responder change cannot send a stale reviewed name. Team-only preview discloses any still-personal From name honestly.
5. Missing/revoked public name, disallowed phone/title, locale fallback, source revocation and inaccessible asset each produce the exact approved alternative or a repairable block before admission.
6. Insert, Replace, Paste, Undo/Redo, Select all, Hide/Restore, keyboard send and note/reply switching preserve purpose, at most one current managed signature, the correct draft and the visible D4 action; required footers remain present and the next reply resumes its default signature.
7. Ordinary publication, concurrency conflict, connection loss, refresh/recovery and retry preserve pinned content and truthful lifecycle; no live mutable fragment or changed-byte resend occurs.
8. Complete keyboard/screen-reader/mobile/IME journey covers editor, token picker, logo control, preview, publication and inbox return navigation. Test reflow/zoom, long/non-Latin names and RTL.
9. Actual compiled client fixtures cover plain text, blocked images, safe link destinations, logo fallbacks, dark/light rendering and forwarded/replied history; record clients/versions rather than claim universal rendering.
10. Search/redaction/retention read the admitted source and respect D16–D22; editing a signature never rewrites an admitted reply, and branding/privacy withdrawal follows the actual owner's disposal/recovery rules.

No live Asym tenant usability study, donor-care diary study, measured performance benchmark, production browser check, database/RLS test or provider send was performed. The source comparison gives defensible defaults; real usability acceptance must prove that staff can complete these tasks unaided and without identifying the wrong responder. This research does not introduce signature-specific open/click analytics or a new compliance regime.
