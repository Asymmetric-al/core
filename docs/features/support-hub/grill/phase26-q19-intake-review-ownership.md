## The next single decision

**Who should normally review incoming email that Asym has retained but held before it enters ordinary Support work?**

Illustrative example: a church sends a legitimate question to the donor-care inbox. Asym retains the message, but a possible-spam check holds it for review. Alex needs to understand why it was held, inspect only permitted safe evidence, and decide whether that particular message can enter normal intake. Recognizing a church name alone does not prove identity or override a safety restriction. This example compares user journeys; it is not a claim about measured ministry incidents.

The question concerns ordinary held mail with a trustworthy tenant boundary and a safely established responsible inbox or qualified routing path. Unknown-tenant input, missing provider payload, malware and care-classified material are not automatically ordinary Support-review cases. They remain with their actual authorized owners.

## Options and practical tradeoffs

| Option                                                                           | Default responsibility                                                                                                                       | Strongest benefit                                                                                                                    | Main cost                                                                                                             |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Designated reviewers for each inbox, with tenant oversight (recommended)** | The tenant names eligible people or a team to review each responsible inbox's held mail. The same qualified team can cover several inboxes.  | Review stays close to the work and context, without requiring every Support worker to monitor it or granting account administration. | Needs clear coverage, backup and attention when reviewers leave or lose access.                                       |
| **B — One central tenant intake-review team**                                    | A common eligible team reviews routine held mail before it reaches the appropriate Support inbox, escalating anything outside its authority. | One staffing responsibility and consistent review practice across the tenant.                                                        | More handoffs for specialist context; central responsibility cannot imply access to every private source.             |
| **C — Qualified tenant administrators only**                                     | Administrators with the required source/review rights handle routine review; other staff refer issues to them.                               | Minimal additional delegation for a very small tenant.                                                                               | Competes with administrative work and can become a recurring bottleneck; admin status alone is not content authority. |

These are operating defaults over one qualified review capability, not three separate queue engines or mandatory parallel modes. Under A, appointing the same qualified team for all relevant inboxes supports central staffing without forcing every tenant into it. The selected answer must make ordinary tenant configuration and responsibility changes clear; no option can weaken source permissions, preservation or security.

**My single recommendation is A.** It fits the responsible-inbox model already established for Support, gives appropriate staff ownership close to the work, and permits central staffing when that is how a tenant operates. B is the strongest alternative when a ministry deliberately wants one common intake operation. C is credible for low-volume tenants but is too dependent on administrators as a general default. This is a product judgment, not a universal industry consensus or measured ministry preference.

## What A should feel like

The responsible inbox has a clearly labelled **Intake review** view with a short explanation: **Held email needs review before it enters Support.** Designated eligible reviewers see safe reasons, current destination, age and only the content/actions they may access. This is separate from Open/Waiting/Resolved conversation status and separate from technical delivery/retrieval repair.

A reviewer can inspect one item and, when the reason and current evidence permit it, choose a clearly scoped action such as **Release this message to Donor care**. Normal intake then performs the correct original identity, correlation and admission once; the action does not automatically create a new conversation if the message is a valid continuation. The result identifies what actually happened. Items requiring other authority have an owned next step rather than a misleading universal Recover button.

Tenant oversight can monitor authorized coverage and unresolved work. It does not receive every held body merely to show a health summary. Routine review stays in the existing staff work experience, without per-item email storms or repeated copies of private material. Coverage loss creates an explicit operational exception; messages cannot disappear because the last reviewer left.

## Boundaries under every option

- **Responsibility is not access.** Designation never grants inbox, source, CRM, giving, missionary or member-care access. Reviewer eligibility and each actual read/disposition remain currently authorized.
- **Held is not necessarily spam.** Show a truthful reason without equating an uncertain authentication signal with malicious identity. Non-overridable restrictions and unsafe attachments remain enforced by their owners.
- **One-message release is not future trust.** It does not allowlist a sender, alter routing defaults, unblock future mail, verify/register a person, clear consent restrictions or delete CRM records. Broader policy changes need their own explicit authority and review.
- **No guessed tenant or destination.** Unbound/conflicting tenant evidence remains in the separately authorized platform recovery boundary. A technical retry cannot stand in for an evidence-based release.
- **Preserve source and temporal truth.** Retain original receipt, actual Support-ready admission and reviewer attribution separately. Do not present the reviewer as message author, fabricate earlier readiness, lose files through a copied recovery, or duplicate the logical input.
- **No automatic-confirmation replay.** D13 may operate only under its original source/policy/current gates and utility window. Releasing old mail cannot mint a fresh fifteen-minute window or revive an excluded/possibly-sent confirmation. Email Studio publication is not intake-release authority.
- **No imported purge policy.** D17's ordinary ended-work clock does not automatically govern pre-admission held content. The selected answer's full review must define the bounded owner class, disposition and recovery requirements without copying a competitor's fourteen- or thirty-day period.
- **One coherent platform.** Shared Core intake, authorization and work capabilities remain authoritative. No separate mailbox service, new CRM, generic rule builder or provider-switching strategy is introduced.

## Evidence informing this question

Zendesk documents a dedicated suspended-ticket view and regular review, but some editions require broad ticket visibility and bundle suspended-item deletion with access. That supports explicit review responsibility while showing why Asym should keep permissions narrower. [Zendesk recovery](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408893392922-Viewing-recovering-and-deleting-suspended-tickets), [review guidance](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408832102042-Guidelines-for-reviewing-suspended-tickets).

Front keeps shared-inbox spam in that inbox and explicitly distinguishes current-conversation spam from future-sender blocking. That supports clear location and action scope, although its mailbox-provider synchronization is not Asym's architecture. [Front spam workflow](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2251).

Help Scout documents that marking or unmarking spam affects future sender treatment, and warns about classifying legitimate automatic mail as spam. Asym should not silently make that broader decision while reviewing one held input. [Help Scout guidance](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/307-manage-spam-and-unwanted-email).

The [evidence supplement](phase26-q19-evidence.md) adds HubSpot, Zoho Desk and Kustomer comparisons, actual Core recovery/routing behavior, edition/date qualifications and the strongest counterargument to A.
