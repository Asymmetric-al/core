## Strongest current support comparisons

Sources were checked 11 September 2026. Publisher dates/availability are reported only where established. No licensed workspace was exercised, and vendor object names do not define Asym architecture.

**Intercom internal work.** [When to use Back-office tickets](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/8300293-when-to-use-back-office-tickets), 18 May 2026, documents separately handled internal work linked to a customer conversation; customer replies remain in that conversation. [Creation guidance](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/8300297-how-to-create-a-back-office-ticket), 29 August 2025, supports multiple linked internal items and imposes its own contact/type requirements. Its sharing and note/status propagation are configurable vendor behavior.

Adopt the distinction between requester coordination and an internal action. Reject copying a new ticket hierarchy, mandatory contact, optional donor portal sharing or unrestricted cross-posted private notes into Asym. Core already governs shared tasks and owner records. The documentation supports the pattern, not a measured claim that A always outperforms direct transfer.

**Zendesk tracked side work.** [Using side conversation child tickets](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408836521498-Using-side-conversation-child-tickets/) documents separately assigned child work, independent status and configured public/internal comment propagation; administrators must activate the feature. Current retrieved body did not establish an exact edit date or universal edition entitlement.

Adopt separate internal responsibility where useful. Reject parent/child ticket recreation, brittle external-ID linkage, automatic copying or inherited disclosure rules. These are two objects within a vendor help desk; Asym should use its own shared work/owner contract and minimum necessary context.

**Direct transfer is also established practice.** [Intercom assignment guidance](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/6561699-assign-conversations-to-teammates-and-teams), displayed “updated over a week ago” when checked, explicitly supports handing a conversation to a teammate/team. Its role and routing details remain vendor-specific. This supports B as a genuine stronger choice when the specialist should conduct the entire exchange, not a deliberately weak alternative. Do not import assignment side effects already rejected by D6–D8.

Two focused alternatives are better than adding a third unrelated channel/split feature to this question. No new proprietary integration, paid add-on, AI classifier or secondary workflow engine is recommended.

## Why general topic splitting is separate

Research initially examined multiple independent issues in one email thread. The reviews disagreed because that wording could mean either internal subwork or a new requester-facing issue. Root synthesis narrowed the question to internal specialist assistance before choosing a topic-split product.

Official [Front email splitting](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2307), 26 February 2026, moves one selected email message, leaving later messages; it cannot divide two topics inside the same message without a different model. [Help Scout's message controls](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/32-edit-threads-and-notes), 3 January 2026, offer New Conversation on a customer message; that is a separate operation, not proof of Core's desired task boundary or its underlying storage behavior. These comparisons are not recommended message mutations here. D10 source affinity and merge Undo remain settled.

Independent requester work may justify a linked new conversation and a qualified new outward thread, but that needs its own founder choice and source/routing/CRM review. It is not required merely to ask Finance for an internal check. No message copying, reparenting or implied D10 amendment is authorized by Q11.

## Independent challenge and recommendation

The data and lifecycle reviews confirmed that the final coordination default is not already decided by D3's waiting states or D6 initial assignment. The vendor review confirmed that private consultation, tracked internal work and a whole-conversation handoff are different interactions. Early independent-topic alternatives are retained in working research as history, not presented as competing final Q11 recommendations.

**Recommend A: retain current Support responsibility, delegate only tracked internal work when needed.** It gives the requester one coordinated answer while permitting a specialist to own a bounded action with appropriate context and authority. Use an existing owner operation/task where it already represents that action; do not always create another task. Quick clarification remains possible through qualified notes/mentions. If the original worker can perform the owner action directly, no ceremonial delegation is needed.

**B remains strongest when the specialist should own the whole conversation.** It avoids a relay and can let the expert ask the requester direct questions. Its recipient must be eligible for the whole affected Support conversation as well as the specialist action. Neither model creates new access; both retain existing explicit transfer capabilities.

Unresolved consequences belong in the selected answer's full review: exact assistance/link/admission capability, identifying existing owner work without duplicate creation, minimal context and source/audit ownership, current outcomes and stale/reopened/canceled work, reminders versus task deadlines, assignment/review/coverage, notification restraint, D10 merge/Undo, transactional replay and qualified CRM/history. No physical schema, new task taxonomy, automatic status change or response-time promise is selected by this question. Final independent review clarified that prohibiting fabricated requester correspondence does not prohibit actual authorized staff notifications or their P6 evidence; notification policy remains part of the selected-answer review.
