## Technical and UX implications that cannot be assumed away

[RFC3834, Recommendations for Automatic Responses to Electronic Mail](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3834.html), August 2004, is directly relevant standards guidance despite its age. It distinguishes responder purposes and warns against indiscriminate responses, loops, amplification, invalid/null response destinations and privacy disclosure. Human reply recipient rules cannot simply be applied to automated notices. Do not import its vacation-response intervals as the Support confirmation budget without classifying the actual responder purpose.

The selected answer's full review must settle exact recipient authority and forwarding/authentication evidence, including the difference between From, Reply-To and transport return identity; current loop/suppression budgets; owner admission and staff-reply races; original occurrence identity across replay/move/merge/reopen; held-message release and late applicability; optional service-hours interpretation; P17 approved content/sender; P6 actual history; and recoverable failure.

An acknowledgment should describe recoverable receipt, not human review or a guaranteed response deadline. It must not force a portal, echo private message content, become a charitable-gift receipt, trigger CRM creation or count as a substantive staff response. The existing personal Reply-all preference is a human-composition decision and supplies no automatic-notice audience authority.

The strongest case against A is redundant mail when staff respond promptly. The strongest case against B is that service hours do not establish immediate human availability, while maintaining calendars/holidays/time zones has a real cost. C minimizes automation but leaves the requester without confirmation during waiting. These are reasoned tradeoffs, not measured response-time claims.

## Independent synthesis and scope

The data review confirmed this policy is expressly unresolved in D1 and deferred to Phase 26/P17 source qualification. The lifecycle review separated eligible new requester intake from replays, continuations, reopenings and D12 staff-created work. The vendor/UX review confirmed the three meaningful options and highlighted hidden-history, sender, rule-interaction and repeated-response pitfalls.

All reviews support asking Q13 now, with A as the single recommendation. Final question review clarified that “new request” means a new requester conversation created by qualified inbound intake, and that B explains configured service hours without promising when a particular staff reply will occur. Quarantine/loop protection must precede activation, but their full administrative operating model need not block choosing the product's confirmation policy during grooming. The question does not enable sending, reopen D12 or decide exact numerical throttles/recipient rules prematurely.

Recommended framing: **one concise confirmation for an eligible new email request, with a tenant-controlled inbox policy**. Keep the choice understandable: new-request confirmation, outside-hours-only, or staff-reply-only. No separate tenant workflow builder is warranted solely to express those modes. A draft confirmation example in the question is illustrative, not a published P17 template.
