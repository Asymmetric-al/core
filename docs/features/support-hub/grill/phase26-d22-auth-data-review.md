## Disposition and minimal permanent design

**Accept with required amendments.** The selected scope provides useful retrieval without making Support a document-search or AI platform. The permanent design is a registered Support subject behind Core's shared query/API and source-authorization infrastructure, with typed source-local searchable units and derived candidate indexes in Asym Postgres. Keep source ownership, plaintext availability and restriction/revision evidence authoritative. Do not freeze a dedicated search vendor, invent a generic microservice, copy CRM records into a Support index or treat P6 history as readable content.

The root's intended literal contract is coherent: all whitespace-separated literal fragments must match within **one eligible source item**, with quoted exact normalized phrases, a pinned NFC/Unicode-casefold interpretation, and no stemming, stopword removal, fuzzy matching or caller SQL/wildcard syntax. Candidate acceleration may differ internally, but it must not lose valid matches or decide final membership. A correct exact verifier establishes the source match and safe highlight offsets.

## Governing exclusions that cannot be weakened

1. **Readable does not always mean indexable.** Ratified Phase10 A4 and the exclusion at line188 forbid restricted-tier material from search indexing. A security clearance or direct source read does not override that floor. Search eligibility is the intersection of admission, current availability, permitted indexing/exposure purpose and current actor/source read authority. Ordinary search must not direct-scan index-forbidden content as a fallback. [Phase10 A4](https://github.com/Asymmetric-al/core/blob/7abd2c11ffd4ed70c6775c4fd6f51c996e4350dd/docs/prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-10-sensitive-data-safety.md#L98), [explicit exclusion](https://github.com/Asymmetric-al/core/blob/7abd2c11ffd4ed70c6775c4fd6f51c996e4350dd/docs/prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-10-sensitive-data-safety.md#L188).
2. **Recent copy is not a search corpus.** ADR0031 expressly excludes its encrypted recent sent copies from search/export corpus use, even while an individually authorized user may reveal one. Do not index Recent copy or prepared artifacts to fill gaps in Support source history. [ADR0031](https://github.com/Asymmetric-al/core/blob/7abd2c11ffd4ed70c6775c4fd6f51c996e4350dd/docs/adr/0031-body-free-history-with-expiring-recent-copy.md#L41).
3. **P9's CRM-body-search expansion is not implicitly selected.** P9 A15/B13 define the cross-kind Party search and its exclusions. Notes/comms body search is an explicit upgrade trigger in that CRM scope. D22 qualifies Support-local sources, not Party notes, giving history, member care or generalized global search. [P9 A15](https://github.com/Asymmetric-al/core/blob/7abd2c11ffd4ed70c6775c4fd6f51c996e4350dd/docs/prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-09-full-crm-depth-relationship-graph.md#L539), [P9 search upgrade boundary](https://github.com/Asymmetric-al/core/blob/7abd2c11ffd4ed70c6775c4fd6f51c996e4350dd/docs/prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-09-full-crm-depth-relationship-graph.md#L758).
4. **D16/D17 source rules apply before matching.** A stale index cannot contribute a hit, rank, facet, count, preview or match explanation after restriction/expiry. No searchable shadow archive of filenames, old subjects, raw MIME, quoted copies or body-derived fields is permitted. D20 saved source coverage remains versioned; expanding the default query cannot silently reinterpret old saved searches.
5. **Future broader/AI desire is not current authority.** No attachment parsing, OCR, archive processing, embeddings, semantic answers or AI tools are added. Phase29 supplies qualified byte mechanics; Phase40 retains its generalized/global/AI scope. Filename discovery is not byte or extraction permission.

The browser receives only the bounded authorized result and approved match context. Do not fetch all bodies for local filtering, use loaded rows as the corpus or present a capped subset as complete. The same typed query contract serves list, table, saved view, search continuation and permitted drilldown.”

**Proof:** at least 2,001 conversations with a matching oldest item; more than 1,000 related rows under the local Data API ceiling; body-only, note-only and filename-only hits; no body payload fetched for nonmatching results; exact count/page/result parity. ## Finding 4 — Source revision and expiry must fence index admission and every read

**Material concern: Yes. Severity Critical; likelihood Medium under asynchronous rebuild, deletion or restore.** Indexing creates another content representation. A worker can read an allowed old source, then publish it after redaction/reclassification/expiry. Purging an index asynchronously without a current-serving gate leaves an exposure window. Rebuilding from restored backup or P6 Recent copy can resurrect restricted data.

**Exact amendment:** “An index generation is derived from an exact authorized source generation and index-purpose version. Recheck source indexing authority at publication; stale work cannot publish as current. Reads require the indexed generation to equal the current permitted source generation and the current source/file availability barrier to allow use. Redaction/restriction/expiry becomes effective independently of index cleanup or queue lag. Rebuilds, retry, import and restored backups obey the same barriers before any content is searchable. Retire old indexed text and offsets under the actual D16/D17 owner inventory; no search-retention exception.”

Prefer transactionally maintained searchable projections where the selected semantics and workload fit; this reduces an avoidable asynchronous state machine. If an asynchronous projection is necessary, use existing durable admission/recovery primitives with source-generation dedupe and explicit readiness. New indexing success never extends content life, creates a conversation message or modifies CRM last contact.

**Proof:** source edit/redaction/reclassification/expiry between extraction/publication/query/snippet/open; duplicate/out-of-order worker delivery; delayed cleanup; index writes after source deletion; restore containing old vector/text; epoch/race rollback; partial redaction preserves safe remainder only after exact new-generation qualification.

## Finding 5 — Completeness is source coverage, not a successful HTTP response

**Material concern: Yes. Severity High; likelihood Medium with missing source payload or projection lag.** Some newly admitted messages or known filenames may not yet have usable searchable text. An empty search result after excluding stale/missing units does not prove no matching content exists. A bare global backlog count can leak restricted scope or force unrelated tenants into failure.

**Exact amendment:** “At an evaluation instant qualify the eligible source scope before the text predicate and establish whether that scope has complete current searchable representation. Known missing/stale representation makes search incomplete for that scope; show available authorized results only with truthful partial/refresh treatment and withhold an exact all-matches count or definitive No matches claim. Do not count excluded or inaccessible sources as discoverable missing hits. A unavailable query is not a successful empty query. Expired/index-forbidden sources are excluded by contract, not a reason to preserve old bytes for completeness.”

A source gap cannot be tested for query matching until indexed; therefore completeness assessment is over the eligible **pre-text-filter** scope, not only the returned hits. No requirement to build a global completeness warehouse: use owner readiness/source revision evidence already necessary for index publication and the query's allowed scope. If source/index updates are atomic, that proof can be correspondingly simpler.

**Proof:** admitted but unindexed recent unit; metadata-only source currently available while body pending; missing filename representation; other-tenant backlog; unauthorized source backlog; zero complete matches versus zero known indexed matches; source recovery resolves coverage without a new business effect.

## Finding 6 — Literal matching must remain exact under candidate indexes and chunking

**Material concern: Yes. Severity High; likelihood High if generic FTS/fuzzy defaults are assumed equivalent.** PostgreSQL full-text dictionaries can stem, discard stopwords/punctuation and constrain lexeme/position sizes. That does not implement the chosen literal substring-fragment contract. Trigram similarity is also not exact membership. Breaking long text into independent chunks can lose a phrase spanning a boundary or a query whose fragments occur far apart within one source item.

**Exact amendment:** “Use one pinned normalization/parser contract for query, stored searchable text, candidate verification and highlight mapping. Preserve meaningful accents and punctuation; validate malformed scalar/quote input rather than silently substituting semantics. Candidate indexes must yield a superset of valid source-unit matches. Final exact source-unit verification enforces every literal fragment/quoted phrase. Never AND all fragments within a single storage chunk; evaluate them within the same complete logical source unit. Chunk boundaries cannot truncate eligible source coverage or break a bounded valid phrase.”

Minimal feasible PostgreSQL path: normalized text with parameterized literal substring matching; qualify `pg_trgm` indexes as an accelerator where useful, with the shared API/query contract remaining authoritative. Escape `%`, `_` and the chosen LIKE escape character when using LIKE; alternatively use exact `position` verification. Do not use `%` similarity or English `tsquery` as the final filter. No current migration enabling `pg_trgm` was found; P9 documents an intended foundation, not a verified installed extension.

For chunked candidate storage, derive candidate **parent units** from a safely selected fragment/chunk and exact-verify the complete unit or a proven streaming equivalent. If overlap is the chosen phrase technique, normalize before splitting and provide overlap at least the maximum permitted normalized phrase length minus one, in the same defined character unit. Handle larger source units using certified bounded indexing/verification rather than silent prefix-only coverage. Do not build chunking speculatively if full logical-unit indexing satisfies demonstrated bounds.

**Proof:** Thai/CJK/no-space strings, combining marks, casefold expansion, punctuation-only and mixed-digit identifiers, literal `%`/`_`/backslash, quotes, stopword-only text, long single tokens, repeated terms, all fragments in one source but different chunks, phrase across a chunk boundary, and matching offsets back to original display text.

## Finding 7 — Short/common queries require explicit performance handling

**Material concern: Yes. Severity High for incomplete service; likelihood Medium/High under broad or skewed workloads.** `pg_trgm` patterns without extractable trigrams can degrade to full-index scans. Two-character or punctuation queries can be valid under the product contract and still provide poor selectivity. Whole-corpus ranking/counting over all long messages can exceed a bounded request even with an index. Arbitrary row caps would recreate the current defect.

**Exact amendment:** “Apply permitted structured scope early, use actual query plans and a certified finite query budget, and exact-verify candidates server-side. If a valid request cannot be completed within that budget, return a clearly incomplete/too-broad outcome with safe refinement/retry rather than truncated successful results or false zero. Do not silently raise minimum input length, strip punctuation, omit long units or change literal semantics to make an index convenient. A deployment profile qualifies source/query size, page, time, concurrency and tenant-fairness boundaries before activation.”

A fixed small query-length minimum may be a deliberate product choice, but cannot be inferred merely from trigram indexing. Use B-tree exact ID lookup for qualified references and selective supported metadata constraints where available; no hidden widening to CRM. Do not force a new dedicated engine or asynchronous export job solely to make every arbitrary broad query succeed.

**Proof:** exact supported minimum and maximum lengths, short non-Latin fragments, punctuation-heavy filenames, common two-character queries in a large tenant, many large messages in one component, narrow filters versus all permitted scope, timeout/cancel/retry, distinct-root count cost and unrelated tenant fairness. Register actual latency/throughput thresholds; none is measured by this audit.

## Finding 9 — Merged roots, rank, cursor and match location retain exact source meaning

**Material concern: Yes. Severity High; likelihood Medium under D10 changes or result refresh.** Multiple original messages can match one continuing conversation. A deep link to a mutable root can open the wrong location after merge/Undo. A cursor carrying only time/id is not sufficient if scope/query/version changed.

**Exact amendment:** “Qualify source units before matching/ranking, map each permitted hit through current D10 topology and count/page distinct continuing roots. Preserve the exact original source/item/generation and match explanation for opening. New index refresh or label edits do not imply new human-message chronology. ## Database and index invariants

- Source identity is tenant-qualified and references the actual original, message/brief/field or attachment occurrence. Composite foreign keys or equivalent validated tenant-aware source constraints prevent cross-tenant relationships, including privileged writes.
- Searchable representation identity binds source kind, content/metadata generation, projection/normalization/coverage version and current eligibility. No plaintext derivative is an independent source record.
- Only the source/index owner writes projection control. Raw browser mutation and old direct source writers cannot bypass generation/restriction advancement. History is body-free and attributable; a reindex is technical evidence, not a message or CRM interaction.
- Current index-policy permission and current user-read permission are distinct. Unknown policy or stale generation cannot participate in hits, ranking/counts or excerpts.
- Every valid match satisfies the exact selected contract in one logical source unit. Candidate filtering has no false negatives within supported bounds; chunk storage never defines product source identity.
- The result count identity is the current permitted continuing root. Exact original match locations remain independent from that grouping and are requalified on open.
- Metadata/source expiry and D16 restriction are authoritative at their boundary even if index cleanup is delayed. Cleanup/replay/restore cannot revive old authority.
- Neither filename matching nor source visibility provides arbitrary URL, file-content, Party/relationship, giving/care or AI access.

## Migration, recovery and proof sequence

1. Register exact searchable source kinds/fields, corpus exclusions, source-policy/current-read predicates and D20 coverage version before authoring an index. Declare how Staff context and queued/failed admitted replies are represented; never infer from arbitrary stored JSON.
2. Qualify shared source projections and current generation/restriction gates. Fence legacy mutable source/index paths and broad content-table grants. Supply exact immutable tenant-aware index references and writer authority.
3. Implement the common parser/normalizer and exact matcher; then qualify candidate indexes against its truth set, bounded source sizes and query workload. Do not retrofit semantics to match a convenient English/Fuzzy index.
4. Backfill only still-eligible source generations, with durable source/version identity and progress. Do not reconstruct disposed content or invent admission/identity/classification facts. Serve old metadata-search semantics until the new scope's coverage is certified, or label partial coverage truthfully.
5. Bind search, source refinements, matching-source navigation and complete current-scope Find to one contract. Preserve D20 URLs/saved scopes and CRM anchors; do not fetch whole tenant content.
6. Exercise cross-tenant/per-source grants/RLS, source/index forbidden cases, delete/expiry/reclassify races, message/file admission partials, indexes/source generation failure, Unicode/chunk truth cases, complete counts/keysets, migration/restore and browser/AT journeys.
7. Roll out with a stop control for the new search scope while normal Support work and safe already-qualified lookup remain available. Do not silently degrade a saved body search to metadata-only under the same name. Rollback cannot restore index-forbidden generations or weaker source grants; roll forward shared owner repairs.

## Current technical documentation and limits

PostgreSQL17 `pg_trgm` supports indexes for LIKE/ILIKE and notes that patterns with no extractable trigrams become full-index scans. It ignores non-word characters in trigram extraction, so candidate use must retain literal final verification. This justifies the query-budget and exactness gates, not an engine choice or speed claim. [pg_trgm](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/pgtrgm.html).

PostgreSQL17 full text reduces words via dictionaries and can discard stopwords/punctuation. Its published limits include a tsvector below one megabyte, lexemes below two kilobytes, positions through 16,383 and at most 256 positions per lexeme. It cannot be assumed equivalent to D22's literal-fragment semantics or a lossless arbitrary long-source representation. [Text search controls](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/textsearch-controls.html), [limits](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/textsearch-limitations.html). These bounds are vendor technical limits, not new Asym file/message/query caps.

No `pg_trgm` enablement/index was found in the current migration scan. The existing subject GIN uses `to_tsvector('simple', subject)`; current list q does not invoke it. These source facts are not installed-database diagnostics. PostgreSQL17 is the local configured major; deployment version and extension availability must be checked before implementation.

All proof above remains required, not executed. No new money-precision, provider-send, mailbox/DNS, public knowledge publishing or AI mutation hazard is introduced by this narrow query feature; their governing exclusions and actual downstream action boundaries remain intact.
