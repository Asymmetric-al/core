# Q35 — How should staff correct a posted internal note?

**14 September 2026. Unanswered.** D1–D34 and all amendments are fully ratified, including the renewed D29 review, X01 and its three supplemental proof groups. This resumes the same genuine unresolved Q35; the D29 re-review neither answered nor replaced it.

This question concerns **ordinary human-authored internal Support notes**. For example, Maya writes that a donor requested a Tuesday callback, then notices that she transcribed Thursday incorrectly. That is a correction to her account of an event. If the donor later changes the requested day, the team should add a new update under every option. This example illustrates the distinction; it is not claimed as an observed ministry workflow.

## Three actual policy options

| Option                                                       | What happens in the example                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Benefits                                                                                                                                            | Costs and consequences                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Authors edit their own notes, with visible history**   | Maya corrects Tuesday to Thursday in place. The original author/posting time stay; the note shows **Edited**, edit attribution and currently permitted earlier revisions. Other staff add their own clarification rather than rewriting Maya's statement. | Keeps the conversation clear and current. Small mistakes are easy to fix without another entry. Changes remain attributable.                        | Requires a bounded revision, current-author permission and conflict contract. Editing cannot recall what someone already read or received; consequential corrections may still need deliberate staff attention. |
| **B — Posted notes stay unchanged; add a linked correction** | Tuesday stays in the original. Maya adds a correction saying Thursday. The original and correction visibly link to each other.                                                                                                                            | Explicit chronology; no ordinary in-place revision of a published statement. A serious alternative when stable historical entries are the priority. | More entries even for minor mistakes. Correction links must remain visible so people do not act on the original in isolation. Adding a correction can have the normal new-note attention consequences.          |
| **C — Brief editing window, then linked corrections**        | Maya can use A shortly after posting. Once that window ends, she uses B.                                                                                                                                                                                  | Convenient immediate typo repair with stable older notes.                                                                                           | A timer and two journeys, including interrupted edits and saves near expiry. It does not solve already-delivered copies or privacy by itself. No duration is selected at question stage.                        |

These are alternatives for one product policy, not three configurable modes to build.

## Single best recommendation: A

**Choose author-only editing with visible history.** It gives ordinary collaboration the clearest current text and a straightforward way to correct a mistake while preserving attribution. The history stays governed by the note's current source, access, privacy and retention rules. No administrator gets authority to rewrite another person's statement merely by having a broader role.

Use an accessible **Edit** action on the note, deliberate Save/Cancel, a stable chronological position and a reachable Edited/history disclosure. Reuse the qualified Tiptap/Base UI/Maia controls and preserve a separate reply draft and the reader's position. Resolve a concurrent edit explicitly rather than silently overwriting it. Those are the proposed user-visible outcomes; the detailed revision command/schema and edge cases require the full adversarial decision review after selection.

The strongest alternative is **B**. It avoids ordinary in-place changes, but linked corrections still require source/privacy/retention and attention rules; it is simpler in one dimension, not cost-free. **C** is reasonable if a time limit is itself a desired product rule. No reviewed evidence establishes a universally correct window, and the extra boundary adds friction to a user returning after an interruption.

## What current research supports — and does not

[Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/8838326-conversations-faqs) documents author-only inline editing, an Edited timestamp, no administrator override and a web-only feature; its [release was26March2026](https://www.intercom.com/changes/en/136196-edit-your-internal-notes). It does not document the full prior-body revision contract recommended for Asym. We adopt the low-friction author-owned interaction as evidence to evaluate, not its undocumented implementation or platform limitation.

[HubSpot](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/help-desk/create-edit-and-manage-notes-in-help-desk), updated23April2026 for Service Hub Professional/Enterprise, documents inline editing and **property history**, plus consistent note presentation across Help Desk and associated records. That supports continuity; it does not prove immutable body history or justify synchronization between Core surfaces.

[Zendesk](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408885807258-Can-I-edit-recall-or-delete-comments-in-my-tickets) prohibits ordinary editing of posted comments, supporting B as a real policy alternative. [Help Scout](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/32-edit-threads-and-notes) allows edits but explicitly does not retain original note text; A deliberately does not adopt that loss of prior meaning. Modern vendors make different tradeoffs. A is a product judgment for Asym's stated clarity and low-friction goals, not an assertion of unanimous practice or measured ministry preference.

## Boundaries unchanged in every option

- D16 privacy redaction stays separate. Editing/history/correction links cannot expose redacted or expired text, attachments or earlier revisions. No ordinary hard-delete or public-conversion capability is bundled here.
- D17 retention and original chronology remain in force. An edit does not reset a content deadline. New circumstances and new actions belong in new notes, and cannot restore forbidden source content.
- D15 routine-edit semantics remain: no new optional alert on every save and no false recall of delivered notifications. Independently authorized new correction notes or deliberate staff attention use their existing rules and may have their normal effects.
- **P17 Email Studio/P6** retain qualified staff-notification preparation/delivery. An ordinary internal-note edit creates no donor email, new external reply, signature change or automatic resend. The new D29 confirmation profile and its controls are unrelated to note correction.
- **CRM** reads the same canonical currently authorized Support note. It does not acquire another note store, overwrite right or inherited financial/member-care permission. A note about a refund never changes the refund itself.
- D26 staff-recorded request briefs, incoming/sent mail, system/AI events and CRM-owned notes are outside this question. D34 awareness is advisory, never an edit lock or draft-access grant.
