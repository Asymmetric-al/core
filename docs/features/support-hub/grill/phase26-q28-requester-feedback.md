# Q28 — Inviting feedback after Support help

**12 September 2026. D1–D27 and all adopted amendments are fully ratified. Q28 is unanswered.** This asks one product decision: whether Phase 26 should include a brief requester-feedback flow, and what initiates its invitation. It does not select the detailed scale, delay, repeat window, recipient cardinality or reporting formula before the answer is reviewed.

**Should Support invite requesters to say how the help went, and how should that invitation begin?**

Imagine Elena contacts Donor care for help finding a receipt. Staff provide assistance and finish the Support work. Asym already knows the conversation's work status, actual correspondence and response timing. Those facts do not establish whether Elena found the help useful. She may also be helped by a team whose relationship-based follow-up already supplies enough feedback. This is an illustrative case, not evidence that every ministry wants surveys or has the same workflow.

| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Optional automatic feedback invitations — Recommended** | Add a brief feedback flow that a tenant must explicitly enable. Once enabled for its qualified scope, eligible completed human-handled Support work can generate an invitation through a separate feedback policy. | Gives requesters a consistent opportunity to respond without making staff remember or choose each invitation. Avoids one source of selective asking. | Adds optional outward contact, a response page and a real invitation/response lifecycle. Eligibility, reopening, duplicate prevention, preferences and low-volume interpretation require care. Automation does not make responses unbiased. |
| **B — Staff chooses when to ask**                             | Add the same kind of supported feedback opportunity, but an authorized worker deliberately requests it for a particular conversation. No completion-triggered invitation runs.                                     | Gives staff control over context and timing, especially for unusual or sensitive exchanges.                                                          | Another recurring staff action; invitations may be forgotten or selectively sent. It still needs secure response collection, retention, delivery and reporting.                                                                             |
| **C — Ordinary feedback, without a dedicated rating flow**    | Add no structured feedback invitation or rating page in Phase 26. Requesters continue to reply normally with thanks, concerns or questions; staff use those conversations and existing operational reporting.      | Least additional contact, lowest complexity and a credible fit for teams that learn through direct relationships.                                    | No consistent structured requester assessment. Silence, response speed and closed work cannot be reported as satisfaction.                                                                                                                  |

These are alternatives, not three modes that must all be built. Ordinary replies continue in all three. No option creates a donor loyalty score, missionary assessment, employee ranking, NPS campaign, mandatory signature survey or AI sentiment system.

**My single best recommendation is A, Off until explicitly enabled by the tenant.** A provides a useful feedback opportunity with less routine staff effort and less handler-selected coverage than B. Keeping it optional respects C's legitimate lower-contact preference. The eligibility policy must use neutral work/correspondence facts, never whether staff expect a positive rating; completed human handling does not mean a verified fix or satisfied requester. Tenant enablement is not proof of recipient consent or permission to contact every conversation participant.

Current product documentation establishes real alternatives: [Front](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2255) supports manual and automatic invitations, while [Zendesk](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408887007386-Workflow-How-to-send-satisfaction-surveys-when-tickets-get-solved) documents completion-based invitations with a prior-invitation guard. [Help Scout](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/386-gather-feedback-with-satisfaction-ratings) instead puts ratings on each enabled email reply, measuring a different unit and adding repeated prompts. Their existence supports the comparison; it does not make any vendor's scale, timer or attribution formula correct for Asym.

## What A would feel like

An authorized tenant manager deliberately enables feedback after reviewing its scope and recipient-facing invitation. Staff continue handling and ending work through the existing commands; there is no compulsory extra closure question. A separately qualified policy may schedule an invitation, with a truthful pending/sent/unavailable state and current checks before sending. The requester can ignore it or open a short accessible response page and deliberately submit a response. Opening a link alone records no rating. Staff can read permitted feedback in the same Support detail reached from CRM and decide whether ordinary follow-up is appropriate.

This is a proposed direction, not a finalized survey journey. Exact timing, question wording/scale, response-change/expiry rules, invitation cardinality, which recipient qualifies and how negative feedback receives accountable attention will be resolved in the selected answer's full review. No result is inferred from silence. No backfill blast to previously resolved requests or repeated reopening-driven invitations is implied.

## Boundaries that make the comparison honest

**D5 remains intact.** Ending Support work alone sends no survey and does not enroll anyone. A would add a separate explicitly enabled policy and qualified communication effect. No response/administrative closure is not proof of completed successful help. B is also a deliberate new invitation capability, not an ordinary pasted URL with hidden powers. C adds neither.

**Email Studio remains the message owner.** Support would own feedback purpose, eligibility and the response record; Email Studio/P17 would own governed invitation wording, branding/rendering, qualified action material and immutable preparation; P6 would own dispatch and reconciliation. A/B therefore require a separately qualified invitation/action/response contract. Existing signatures, saved replies, Guidance links and shortcuts do not already supply it. A narrow coded policy does not require a general Phase 34 workflow/form builder or second mail provider.

**CRM remains authoritative for its own facts.** Feedback may be visible through the authorized canonical Support detail, but would not create a new CRM contact, duplicate sentiment store, last-contact event, donor score, record permission or financial action. Shared addresses, forwarded invitations and on-behalf-of requests do not prove which CRM person responded. Feedback access cannot expose the underlying private conversation through a public invitation link.

**Reporting remains honest.** Explicit requester assessment differs from agent speed, work ending, Customer Effort Score and inferred sentiment. A reduces handler selection but retains eligibility and voluntary-response bias. Counts/cohorts and the absence of responses matter; a small sample is not a reliable staff league table. B has additional discretionary invitation bias, while C intentionally provides no structured score. [AAPOR's survey guidance](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/) supports transparent populations, recruitment and nonresponse limits; commercial benchmark targets are not imported.
