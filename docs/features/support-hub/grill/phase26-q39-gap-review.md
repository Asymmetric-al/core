## One practical user decision

Illustrative fixture: Maya and Alex can both access an Open conversation. A new requester message arrives. Maya opens it, then Alex looks at the inbox. The conversation is still Open because reading alone completed no Support work. The question is what, if anything, should tell Alex that this material is new to Alex. This is an existing-reading fixture, not a claim about observed ministry staffing or habits.

**Question:** How should Support show whether conversation activity has been read?

| Option                              | Concrete experience                                                                                                                                                                                 | Main benefit                                                                                                                  | Real tradeoff                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Personal read/unread**        | Each staff member has a private reading cue. Maya viewing the currently qualified activity changes Maya's cue; Alex's stays unread until Alex views it.                                             | Helps each person find what is new to them; a colleague's quick look cannot clear their personal reading aid.                 | Adds source-qualified personal reading state; many existing or newly accessible conversations need an explicit, restrained baseline in the selected-answer review. Unread is not work urgency.                               |
| **B — Shared team read/unread**     | One shared cue without naming readers changes once an authorized staff member views the activity. Maya's view clears it for the shared team view; nobody's name, reading time or roster is exposed. | Shows whether the incoming activity has received an initial look somewhere in the team, without personal unread accumulation. | Alex cannot use it to tell what Alex has seen. Someone merely opening the conversation can clear the team's cue even though nobody has completed the work. Shared representation also needs qualified source/audience scope. |
| **C — No ordinary read/unread cue** | Omit ordinary conversation unread markers; use the already accepted work status, assignee, reply targets, reminders, latest activity and explicit notifications for their existing purposes.        | Simplest ordinary list and no new reading-state persistence product.                                                          | Staff cannot scan the list for content new to them or not yet opened by the team; they need to open/revisit context. It does not remove current send-review guards or P17 notification unread.                               |

These are three alternative default meanings, not personal/team tenant settings or a three-mode feature. A is the single recommendation; B is the strongest competing meaning when shared initial-triage visibility is more useful than individual reading continuity. C is a legitimate smaller UI/data scope, not an instruction to erase required notification engagement or collision evidence.

## Why recommend A

The ordinary reader is used by individuals, including those arriving from CRM or taking over existing work. Each needs to distinguish what is new to them even if a colleague has already opened it. Personal read/unread provides that narrow orientation without making a shared dot stand in for assignment, first response, resolution or duty. It also avoids suggesting that a shared cleared marker means all staff read the material.

This is a product judgment, not universal vendor consensus or measured Asym usability proof. Current external comparisons are researched separately by the UX reviewer. The reading marker must remain private self-service information; no staff-read history, performance measures, requester read receipts or passive viewer roster is justified. A is not a security claim that browser presentation proves a human understood a message.

## Non-negotiable boundaries across the options

1. **Reading is not work completion.** The cue cannot resolve/open work, change assignee, cancel follow-up, satisfy D14 reply targets, admit D11 owner-result review, send mail or certify a giving/CRM action. No queue disappears merely because a teammate opened it.
2. **Conversation reading is not P17 notification engagement.** Marking/viewing one does not automatically read/archive the other's whole group, end its source duty or reset retention. D15 Follow/Stop and its 30/90-day policy remain unchanged. Following is not a new unread counter.
3. **Send review remains authoritative.** A cosmetic unread reset or automatic reading update does not prove explicit current content/audience/coverage review under D1/D2/D4/D10/D14. C's no-dot choice keeps those send guards intact.
4. **Source permissions remain current.** Read metadata can itself disclose activity. Authorize original content contributions, current tenant and relevant staff scope before counts/boolean cues or updates. Restricted/expired/hidden source material is not exposed as an unread total or neutral badge. Merges/Undo preserve exact original sources and cannot silently fabricate personal reading of a newly included source.
5. **No surveillance or external read receipts.** A shows only the current person's reading aid. B offers only its shared aggregate meaning without naming readers, not reader identities, timestamps, attendance, rosters or per-staff statistics; it must not reuse composing presence as proof of a view. The shared cue reveals that at least one authorized viewer qualified; small-team circumstances can permit inference about who that was. Absence of displayed identity is not guaranteed anonymity. No donor/requester email open pixels, tracking or public Seen indication is added.
6. **Actual presentation, not incidental fetch.** Any eventual read transition must use a deliberately qualified reader interaction rather than prefetch, background cache refresh, notification delivery or a hidden mounted component. Exact triggering, message eligibility and reset behavior require the chosen-answer review; no dwell timer, browser event or all-history mark is selected here.
7. **Same source through CRM.** Authorized CRM Support context consumes the same source reading meaning rather than a duplicate state store. CRM relationship/record access grants no Support reading mutation or hidden source permission; viewing creates no Activity, last-contact fact or owner action.
8. **Email Studio/P6 remain separate.** Support owns ordinary conversation reading if selected. P17 owns governed content and notification presentation/engagement; P6 owns admitted communication/delivery evidence. Reading the conversation creates no prepared email/template publication/notification resend or provider “read” fact. D23/D36 external reply preparation and D37/D38 private drafts remain unchanged.
9. **No speculative expansion.** No new unread navigation destination, count badge, broad saved-view predicate, manual mark-unread action, custom settings hierarchy, all-history backfill policy, retention number, database table, exact latency or AI feature is selected at the question stage. The chosen-answer review must close necessary source/lifecycle/UX proof without turning unread into a second work queue.
