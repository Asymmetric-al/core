## Recommendation and question coherence

**Recommend A: deliberately remove selected text and whole affected attachments permanently, keeping the useful remainder and a content-free correction record.** A best addresses the example of a receipt question containing an unrelated private paragraph and the wrong document. It preserves the actual support request without retaining a routine revealable original merely to make the action reversible.

The evidence establishes that selective permanent correction, whole-message removal, and reversible hiding are each real product patterns. It does not establish a universal vendor consensus or measured ministry preference. The recommendation is an Asym product judgment, conditional on the shared owners implementing genuine removal across all controlled representations.

Suggested question scope: **How should authorized staff correct accidentally shared private content while keeping the useful conversation history?** The founder is choosing the normal correction granularity and whether ordinary correction retains a revealable original. They are not choosing whether Core must obey its owning domains' mandatory disposal, retention, hold, or emergency-containment rules.

| Option                                                    | Ordinary staff action                                                                                                                                                   | Benefit                                                                                             | Cost or qualification                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Remove selected content permanently (recommended)** | Select exact text and whole files, inspect the resulting message, then confirm permanent removal. Whole-body removal remains available when the whole body is affected. | Preserves the useful receipt question and real context while removing unnecessary private material. | More exact selection and canonical-content work than B; requires reliable rich-text, quoted-content, metadata, and derived-copy handling. No ordinary post-commit Undo.                                                                                                      |
| **B — Remove the affected message body or file in full**  | Remove a whole body, an entire affected file, or both, keeping message identity/history and unaffected messages.                                                        | The simplest predictable boundary; avoids fragile partial-text selection.                           | Loses useful context in the same body and can require another question to the requester. A body-only action must not silently remove unrelated files; the confirmation lists every selected object.                                                                          |
| **C — Hide content with restricted recovery**             | Conceal exact content from ordinary views while an expressly authorized owner may temporarily reveal or restore a retained original.                                    | Recoverable correction and temporary restriction when original retention is permitted and useful.   | Additional restricted custody, reveal authorization/audit, restoration, expiry, and disposal obligations. It is masking, not erasure. It cannot preserve content that the owning rules require removed, create a password vault, or replace mandatory irreversible disposal. |

Common to all options: current tenant and object authorization; immediate qualified containment where necessary; owning-domain retention and holds; actual required erasure; external-copy limitations; audit that does not repeat the removed content; no change to assignment, response targets, participant audience, CRM identity, or the historical fact that an email was sent. An immutable-evidence requirement preserves event identity and admissible facts; it does not authorize retaining material whose owner requires removal.

## Verified vendor behavior

### 1. Zendesk: strongest manual selective-removal comparison

Zendesk's Agent Workspace guide, edited **1 May 2026**, documents a message-menu redaction pane, marking multiple selected strings and attachments/images, removing mistaken selections before commit, and an irreversible final operation. It requires Agent Workspace and appropriate permissions; role details vary by plan. It records a redaction event. The guide warns that selecting one repeated phrase occurrence does not remove its other occurrences; formatting may change. It also documents merged-ticket copies, external email/channel copies, and browser-resident summaries as distinct cleanup hazards. Those are documented limitations, not inferred architecture. [Zendesk, Redacting ticket content](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408846470170-Redacting-ticket-content/)

**Fit:** adopt deliberate selection, a preview, correction before commitment, and truthful permanence. Require Core's source projections and merge/Undo behavior to avoid surviving copies; do not copy a vendor limitation into Asym as acceptable behavior. Treat raw original MIME, previews, indexes, summaries and exports as explicit owner-managed representations. No claim that a local redaction recalls Gmail, another recipient's mailbox, or a prior export.

### 2. Kustomer: a second independent selective workflow

Kustomer's official _Message options_ article describes highlighting text, applying Redact, then **Commit Redaction**, and describes the result as permanent. Its separate access guide identifies **Redact Messages** as an additional conversation permission and a permanent action. These pages' indexed official text was available; direct article readers returned empty bodies, so no exact article-update date or plan entitlement is asserted. [Kustomer, Message options](https://help.kustomer.com/message-options-H1tSk3QOI), [Kustomer, Define user access](https://help.kustomer.com/define-user-access-S17nw7IJv)

**Fit:** supports making correction a distinct authorized action with a preview/commit boundary. It does not prove Asym should inherit Kustomer's customer/timeline data model or authorize all conversation readers to remove content.

### 3. HubSpot: distinguish three different surfaces and operations

The **22 July 2026** CRM Inbox guide documents Enterprise-only **Hide message**, a placeholder in the inbox, removal from a logged CRM timeline, and **Unhide** by a Super Admin or the original hider, restoring it to previously logged records. This is reversible hiding, not proof of erasure. [HubSpot, A guide to the conversations inbox](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/inbox/use-the-conversations-inbox)

The **22 January 2026** security guide documents an Enterprise Sensitive Data scan of CRM activities, with Super Admin review and permanent replacement of selected detected values. It scans the preceding 60 days and allows one scan per 30 days; redaction requires Sensitive Data enabled. It is not a continuously available per-message Help Desk correction contract. [HubSpot, Scan and redact Sensitive Data](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/account-security/scan-and-redact-sensitive-data)

The current Help Desk management guide documents whole-ticket deletion, deletion of the associated conversation, and a recovery limit. It does **not** substantiate a claim that the Help Desk surface offers the same per-message Hide/Unhide or selective manual redaction UI. [HubSpot, Manage tickets in help desk](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/help-desk/manage-tickets-in-help-desk)

**Fit:** coherent CRM reflection of corrected Support presentation is desirable; erasing the entire CRM identity is not. Reversible hiding is a credible C tradeoff, but needs an honest label and justified owner custody. Do not conflate the Security, CRM Inbox, and Help Desk feature sets or generalize a detected-value scanner into manual arbitrary-text correction.

### 4. Help Scout: masking and expiry, with explicit maturity and attachment limits

Help Scout's _Advanced Privacy Controls_, updated **9 September 2026**, is explicitly **early access**. It automatically masks detected content in new conversations after activation, retains an original temporarily, allows qualified users to reveal it, and permanently removes originals after a configured period. Existing conversations are not automatically updated. The documented masking permission does not restrict attachments: all team members can view them until retention removes them. [Help Scout, Advanced Privacy Controls](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1754-advanced-privacy-controls)

**Fit:** supports distinguishing masking, permissioned reveal and eventual permanent disposal. Reject unrestricted attachment exposure as a model for Asym and do not claim this newly documented early-access feature is proof of mature, broadly deployed UX success. Automated PII detection is a different choice from the requested manual correction boundary.

### 5. Intercom: message-level API and a separate intake automation capability

The current Intercom API reference (default **2.16**) supports redacting a conversation part with a body or a source message created by a contact. The request addresses the part/source, not arbitrary text ranges. Unsupported sources return `conversation_part_not_redactable`. This is useful evidence for a message-level correction boundary, without assuming undocumented attachment or external-copy guarantees. [Intercom, Redact a conversation part](https://developers.intercom.com/docs/references/rest-api/api.intercom.io/conversations/redactconversation)

The **4 March 2026** guide separately describes irreversible intake-time redaction with built-in and custom matching rules, including inbound email. It explicitly warns about unrecoverable false positives; regex patterns are validated and an overlong rule execution is skipped. This is prevention through automated detection, not the same operation as a person correcting an existing arbitrary private paragraph. [Intercom, Redacting sensitive data with custom rules](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/13925174-redacting-sensitive-data-in-conversations-with-custom-rules)

**Fit:** B has a real, bounded comparator. Do not introduce a regex platform, NLP dependency, automated erasure, or a claim of complete PII detection merely to implement A. Any separately adopted intake protection would need false-positive, channel, failure and review contracts of its own.

### 6. BoldDesk: clearest whole-message deletion and tombstone comparison

BoldDesk's current undated official guide documents an authorized message-menu Delete, explicit confirmation, permanent removal of that reply/note and its attachments/inline images, and a replacement marker preserving continuity. It says deletion neither recalls already-sent email notifications nor sends a new notification. It includes separate public/private permissions and customer-editing rules. [BoldDesk, How to delete a ticket reply or note message](https://support.bolddesk.com/kb/article/266/how-to-delete-a-ticket-reply-or-note-message)

**Fit:** B can be a usable smaller solution without deleting the conversation. Adopt the visible correction marker and external-email honesty. Do not copy the guide's requirement to retain one public reply as a rule that could prevent required erasure; do not import its customer deletion entitlements. Asym's body/file choice should remain explicit rather than inheriting deletion of every attachment automatically.

## Proposed Asym UX direction for A

This is original design judgment, not a claim that a vendor UI is already sufficient or accessible.

1. Keep the ordinary conversation clean. Put **Remove sensitive content…** in the existing, keyboard-accessible message action menu, visible to qualified actors. Other authorized staff need a narrow safe route to the responsible handler when they cannot perform removal themselves; avoid a new generic approval platform.
2. Open a focused selection/review surface in context. Identify the original message, author/time, and current body revision. Select exact text ranges or whole files. Offer whole-body selection for a wholly affected message. Selecting files does not implicitly select the body or other files.
3. Show **After removal** before commit. Make selected ranges editable/unmarkable and list selected attachment count. Do not require retyping or pasting the sensitive value as confirmation. Permit Cancel and preserve the conversation draft/scroll; selection itself is not removal.
4. Use one meaningful confirmation: **Permanently remove selected content**. Explain that it cannot be restored in Asym and does not recall copies already sent outside Asym. If a hold or dependency means only containment is currently allowed, change the action and resulting status truthfully instead of promising completed removal.
5. Show a neutral **Content removed** or **Attachment removed** marker. Keep permitted message identity, time, communication history and service facts. Do not show a secret-bearing reason or filename in the marker. The permitted audit can explain who acted and why using a bounded reason category and content-free scope.
6. Removal cannot silently re-render the old original in CRM, search, summaries, notifications, attachment previews, quote affordances, exports, or merge Undo. Existing open views must stop presenting newly forbidden material when they next interact or receive the owner's invalidation; do not claim the system can erase a human's already-seen browser pixels, downloaded file or memory.
7. UI validation must include keyboard and touch selection, long messages, quoted/repeated occurrences, HTML text versus links/attributes, emoji/grapheme boundaries, combining marks, RTL, localization, narrow screens and zoom. Whole-message/file fallback protects the outcome when exact partial selection cannot be proven safe.

An important distinction for future design: **remove an occurrence** is not **find and erase every semantically similar fact**. Exact canonical representation copies should be covered automatically by the owner contract. Other independent messages that repeat the material require an explicit reviewed scope or a separate owner erasure process. Never imply a selected phrase erases all occurrences everywhere.

## Pressure test of the options before asking

| Concern                                                         | Effect on the next question                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| “Permanent” conflicts with routine retained originals           | A/B mean no routine reveal/restore; owner-qualified holds and disposal orchestration remain distinct. C must explicitly say retained original and does not satisfy required erasure.                                           |
| B deletes the entire conversation or all unrelated files        | Correct B to whole affected body/file granularity, preserving unaffected conversation history.                                                                                                                                 |
| C becomes a strawman that illegally retains credentials         | Use a private paragraph/wrong-document example; C is only available where owner rules permit temporary custody. Mandatory disposal is common to every option.                                                                  |
| A requires image/PDF pixel editing                              | Limit initial ordinary attachment action to whole-file removal. Selective attachment editing would be an additional capability with separate evidence and proof.                                                               |
| A's arbitrary selection cannot be mapped to raw/derived content | Require owner-qualified representation mapping; otherwise remove the affected whole canonical body/file or contain pending owner action. Never display a black rectangle over recoverable underlying text and call it removed. |
| A silently rewrites history or clears timers                    | Keep admissible message identity and work facts; separate content correction from send reversal, reply coverage, resolution and CRM mutation.                                                                                  |
| Merge Undo brings content back                                  | Correction authority follows original content identity through all projections; topology changes do not restore removed data.                                                                                                  |
| “Best practice” means all vendors agree                         | Say there are established patterns with different tradeoffs. Recommend A for Asym's demonstrated history-preservation objective, not by vendor vote.                                                                           |

## Suggested concise rationale

**A is the best fit.** It lets Alex remove the unrelated private paragraph and the mistaken document while retaining Maya's receipt question and the conversation's useful history. A preview and one clear irreversible confirmation prevent hurried mistakes; the lasting marker explains the gap without repeating what was removed. B is simpler but discards more context. C allows recovery where retention is permitted, at the cost of another protected copy and a more complicated lifecycle.
