# D27C independent UX research — public and internal Support guidance

**12 September 2026. Founder selected C, revising D27's public-only scope. Q28 feedback remains unanswered and paused.** This report researches the revised Guidance experience and proposes amendments for the parent's adversarial synthesis. It does not claim those amendments are ratified, a general internal wiki exists, or an implementation has been completed. The previous D27 public-source contracts remain relevant except where the founder's explicit scope revision changes them.

**Recommendation: accept C with required amendments.** Keep one manually opened Guidance surface with clear **Public** and **Internal** tabs. Public retains the qualified public-page finder and deliberate link insertion. Internal provides published, reusable Support instructions for consultation, with a narrow Support-owned content lifecycle and the shared Tiptap editor for authoring. Do not turn internal guidance into customer-ready wording, a private CMS draft library, a general wiki or an AI corpus by implication.

The public CMS editor is not replaced by Tiptap because Support adopts Tiptap. A new narrow internal-guide authoring profile should reuse its qualified structural nodes and editing machinery, while readable published guides use a safe static renderer.

The independent data reviewer recommends a single common audience: current members with the tenant's qualified **Support-reader** capability. This is not every tenant account, CRM user, donor, missionary, record owner or person mentioned in a conversation. Internal content is reusable guidance for that audience, not actual restricted case records, credentials or a second member-care repository. Publication to that common audience needs tenant-scope authority; an inbox-only wording curator cannot silently widen access.

## Reported friction and maintenance evidence

A [Zendesk community request dated 17 August 2026](https://community.zendesk.com/ideas/product-feedback-enhance-agent-workspace-knowledge-panel-controls-beyond-existing-article-access-warnings-22251) reports that internal article text/screenshots can be copied into public replies despite link-access warnings. This is a user's report, not a reproduced vulnerability or quantified incident rate. It makes the human-error path plausible. C must address sharing explicitly; it cannot promise to prevent every retyped sentence or external screenshot.

Front's [article review scheduling](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2648512), **6 August 2025**, current Professional-or-above, offers reviewer due dates, review history and reminders, including notification when a reviewer loses access. This demonstrates a maintenance problem worth solving, but does not require a new recurring-review scheduler in Asym. Name responsibility, show publication/change history, provide a clear correction path, and reuse a qualified owner task/reminder only when applicable. No arbitrary periodic reapproval timer is selected here.

Zendesk's [flagging workflow](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408826160922-Flagging-articles-for-updates-while-working-on-tickets) creates a ticket with source/article references; assignment to an article owner is Enterprise-dependent. Do not make a Support ticket the organizing model for editorial maintenance. An existing content-owner handoff is preferable; an issue state on a guide can remain narrow if the parent selects one, without introducing comments, voting, subscriptions and another inbox.

## Choose explicit tabs over mixed results

| Design                                                        | Strongest advantage                                                                                                                                                                                             | Real drawback                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Public / Internal tabs with preserved query — recommended** | Makes source audience and available actions apparent before selection. Each source owner keeps its query, pagination, eligibility and failure semantics. One switch explores the other source without retyping. | A worker must choose a source class or switch tabs when uncertain; careful empty-state copy and preserved query are important.                                                            |
| Mixed list with an audience badge on each row                 | One query can discover relevant material without requiring staff to decide the source class first.                                                                                                              | Small badges can be overlooked. Cross-owner ranking, partial failure and pagination become harder to explain; adjacent identical actions can conceal very different sharing consequences. |
| Grouped Public and Internal results in one All view           | Keeps both source classes visible and can avoid interleaving audiences.                                                                                                                                         | Adds a third browsing mode, two partial-result states and more controls in a narrow panel. The extra surface is not needed for the current bounded corpus.                                |

This recommendation is product judgment, not proof that tabs outperform mixed results in every study. Test it with representative tasks. Use equal, visible Public and Internal tabs in the existing Guidance surface, not two new navigation destinations. Public is the initial first-use tab; preserve an explicit tab choice within the current tenant/session and across preview/return. Do not derive the source tab from Reply versus Internal note: message purpose and knowledge audience are different concepts.

Preserve the typed query when switching tabs but run only the active source's qualified lookup. Keep source-specific filters honest: Site applies to public Pages; it must not remain as a hidden filter over internal instructions. Language remains explicit where variants exist. Current authorization precedes results, snippets, counts, suggestions and page boundaries. Draft/archived internal material belongs in authorized management, not ordinary finder results.

Keep both catalogs flat at this scope. Title, concise summary, content-language and full eligible body search are the useful organizing tools. Public retains its existing exact-site/locale selection. Internal has one defined Support purpose/audience. Do not add a folder tree, freely created categories, personal guides, aliases, popularity scores or an inbox-to-guide ACL builder simply because vendors offer them.

## Internal consultation versus insertion

**Recommend consultation-only for internal guides in this revision.** Staff read published instructions, follow the existing owning-domain workflow, and return to their conversation. Internal preview has a persistent text label such as **Internal · Support staff only**, with Back/Close and an authorized Open guide action. It has no Insert into reply, Copy article, Quote section or automatic switch to Internal note.

The strongest alternative is a typed reference inserted into an Internal note. It could help a handoff without copying the guide body. However, it adds source-aware note serialization, changed-audience/retention behavior and an external-egress rule; the target must reauthorize each reader and reveal no denied title. It must never grant guide access from conversation membership. That is feasible, but is not necessary to satisfy direct consultation and should not be quietly bundled into a normal public Insert link control.

Normal accessible text selection and a full-page internal route are not claims of copy-proof content. Any application-recognized native copy/export must preserve applicable source restrictions; manually retyped information cannot be comprehensively detected. The product must not advertise prevention it cannot prove. This is a boundary for supported insertion and disclosure, not a new surveillance or clipboard-blocking product.

D18 Saved notes remain wording deliberately intended for insertion. They are not the internal guide store. A guide explains how staff work; a saved note supplies a reusable note structure. Reading a guide does not enroll a checklist, mark steps complete, execute a shortcut, mutate CRM or satisfy a business approval.

## Complete normal journeys

### 1. Read public and internal guidance while answering

1. Open the existing Support conversation or its D26-authorized shared detail from CRM. Guidance remains an auxiliary surface with a recoverable Back path; do not add a fourth persistent pane or navigate staff to a wiki home page.
2. Deliberately open Guidance. Keep conversation, recipient, sending inbox, Reply/Internal note mode, signature, draft and cursor intact. Neither source is queried from an extracted private message or AI prompt.
3. Choose Public or Internal. Search explicit words. Show concise text results, actual source language and clear current scope. Avoid “no guides exist” when the source is unavailable or coverage is incomplete; avoid revealing hidden draft counts.
4. Open Public to use the previously ratified passive preview and source-qualified link action. At a caret, Insert link uses the public title; with eligible selected text, Link selected text keeps staff wording. Public site/locale/current-generation and source-loss checks remain unchanged.
5. Open Internal to read the current published revision. Show the title, summary, actual language and last publication/change metadata without promising permanent correctness. Present headings, numbered steps, lists and links as ordinary readable documentation. Steps are instructions, not an executable workflow or mutable per-conversation checklist.
6. If an instruction references a CRM, giving or other application operation, follow the actual owning-domain route under the staff member's own permissions. The guide is not an approval or a bypass; a generic link to a tool cannot reveal restricted records or auto-fill a sensitive case from context.
7. Return to the conversation or CRM with the permitted draft and previous location intact. Internal consultation inserts nothing and does not change status, assignment, due targets, notes, notifications or last contact. A revoked capability stops further reads and clears denied rendered/cache state under existing session rules.

A person with Support read-only access may consult eligible guides but still cannot create a draft or execute work actions. A CRM-only user does not gain Support/internal-guide access from the CRM record. Donors, missionaries and other users accessing their own surfaces are not members of the internal audience merely because they have authenticated accounts.

### 2. Add or maintain public guidance

Continue the accepted public-selection journey: authorized curator selects exact current public Page/Site/locale references; CMS authors edit/publish in Web Studio; Support does not copy bodies or gain publication authority. Shared-by-link public remains publicly accessible, not internal. Remove from Guidance affects selection; source unpublish/withdrawal has its existing stronger consequences. Old safe copied links and finder restoration stay separate under the corrected D27/P17 contract.

The management surface's Public tab may offer Add published page or the existing owner selector. Its Internal tab must not offer the same action with a hidden switch to CMS drafts. Public source publication does not auto-select arbitrary Pages into Guidance.

### 3. Create and publish an internal guide

1. An authorized author enters **Manage guidance → Internal → New internal guide**. Show the fixed purpose/audience: reusable instructions for current Support staff in this tenant. There is no Public/Internal conversion dropdown or field suggesting an internal guide can later be made public with one click.
2. Enter a clear title, concise summary, content language and body in the shared Tiptap authoring profile. The profile supports the qualified structural formatting useful for readable instructions. Exclude live recipient/CRM fields, mail-merge tokens, executable code/forms, arbitrary HTML, interactive checklists and remote embeds. Any media/file support must use a qualified internal attachment owner and current delivery/retention checks; do not introduce arbitrary public uploads as a shortcut.
3. Save a draft. Autosave, if provided, saves draft state only and truthfully reports Saving/Saved/Unable to save. Publication never follows a debounce timer. A failed save or stale concurrent version does not discard permitted author input or display success. Draft visibility is limited to the author and qualified guidance managers/reviewers under the owner's actual model, not ordinary Support readers.
4. Preview exactly the draft to be published in a passive read-only rendering. Make its audience visible. The author may inspect changes from the currently published version through existing qualified comparison/revision behavior; no paid collaboration or tracked-change engine is required.
5. A staff publisher with tenant-scope authority chooses **Publish to staff**. If the author lacks that capability, use the existing qualified review/owner handoff; do not invent a mandatory two-person chain for all tenants. The command validates the exact draft generation, author/publisher context, source purpose and content/asset eligibility.
6. Only the acknowledged published revision appears to ordinary eligible Internal readers. Publishing an internal Support guide is its admission to this Internal finder; requiring a second Add-to-guidance action would duplicate curation. This differs from selecting a public Page because the internal guide exists specifically for Support.
7. Return to the published view with an ordinary success confirmation and management/history action. If search convergence is pending, show that accurately rather than claiming instant findability. Do not send a tenant-wide email or auto-subscribe every reader merely because a guide was published.

This is a narrow internal guidance owner, not a generic document service. Actual confidential cases, donor records, locations, financial information, credentials and private communications remain in their owning protected domains. The phrase “Internal” is not permission to put every sensitive fact into a common staff-readable guide.

### 4. Update and maintain an internal guide

Open a published guide with management authority and choose Edit draft. Keep its currently published revision available while ordinary edits are saved. Save and Publish changes are distinct. The changed title, summary and body go live together as one accepted revision; source history records who changed/published it and when. An editor's departure must not strand a team-owned guide or erase historical attribution; qualified tenant oversight can reassign maintenance within current authority.

The management list shows Title, language, Draft/Published/Archived status and last publication/change with a simple filter. Reader view shows the live content, not every draft or old version. A clear responsible owner/capability and correction route are necessary. A date is evidence of an edit/review event, not an automatic assertion that all instructions remain correct. Do not invent an annual expiration, daily reminder stream or stale-content scoring system without a demonstrated maintenance need.

When staff notice an error, let them reach the authorized guide owner or existing correction workflow with a safe reference and concise concern. Do not automatically copy the current case into a content request, start a new Support ticket, or add comments/voting/subscriptions as an implicit social knowledge product. A serious unsafe instruction can be withdrawn through the source owner's safety action rather than waiting for ordinary editorial review.

### 5. Archive, withdraw, restore and correct

| Intent                                       | Clear action and consequence                                                                                                      | Necessary distinction                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Stop offering outdated internal instructions | **Archive guide** removes the published guide from ordinary finder/read eligibility while retaining only owner-permitted history. | Archive is not a public-page operation, an invitation to reconstruct older content, or a cascading delete of linked business records. |
| Repair an archived guide                     | **Restore as draft**, review/update, then explicitly Publish to staff.                                                            | Restore does not automatically republish an unsafe or stale revision. Current permissions and asset eligibility apply again.          |
| Correct a published guide                    | Save changes in a draft and explicitly publish the reviewed revision.                                                             | Readers retain the current published revision until publication; autosave cannot expose an unfinished correction.                     |
| Return to an earlier revision                | **Restore revision as draft**, then review/publish deliberately.                                                                  | An old version is not authority to restore removed confidential material or old attachment access.                                    |
| Withdraw unsafe content immediately          | Qualified source safety action removes current and stale readable/search representations under its owner contract.                | An eventually removed search row is not enough. Historical and cached copies remain subject to privacy/redaction and current access.  |
| Change a title or URL-friendly label         | Keep stable internal guide identity and route ownership.                                                                          | Bookmarks cannot retarget to another guide with the same title; title text is not identity.                                           |
| Remove a staff account/capability            | Current authorization governs every read, preview, asset and command.                                                             | No automatic grant from former authorship, old links, CRM ownership, inbox membership or a cached positive decision.                  |

No generic Delete-all category action is needed. If permanent deletion is later part of the owning lifecycle, it must retain the platform's existing holds, retention, redaction and audit semantics; ordinary guide maintenance should not require direct database repair or an external support ticket.

## Presentation, empty states and accessibility

Use the existing auxiliary panel and one list → preview → return stack on narrow screens. Public/Internal tabs have visible text; Internal content retains an audience label in both preview and full-page reading. Do not depend on a lock icon or a different background alone. There is one relevant primary action: Insert public link, or return from internal consultation. Avoid a disabled Send-like button on internal guides, which implies that external sharing is available with another permission.

Keep Search guidance, Language and the active source tab visible. Site is public-only. A tab switch preserves the query but not inapplicable hidden filters. Loading and source failures are local; a failed Internal reader does not block Public lookup or ordinary composing. Background responses must not replace a newer query, re-open a dismissed preview or steal focus. Do not put raw query strings into public URLs, shared saved views or analytics.

Suggested copy:

- Internal empty set: **No internal guides are published yet.** Authorized managers see New internal guide; ordinary staff can continue working.
- Complete query with no match: **No internal guides match this search.** Keep the query and visible filters; offer the other tab without silently switching.
- Reader unavailable: **Internal guidance couldn't load. Your conversation is unchanged.** Retry.
- Archived or inaccessible direct link: **This guide is unavailable.** Give only detail currently authorized to that actor; no hidden title/body/count leakage.
- Draft save failure: **Changes could not be saved.** Preserve permitted text and provide Retry/conflict recovery.
- Publication success: **Published to Support staff.** Show the actual live revision; do not claim it was emailed or read.
- Removed access mid-task: **Your access changed.** Return to the permitted surface without claiming another draft or message was not sent.

Keyboard/screen-reader behavior uses the shared tab/dialog/list conventions. Preserve IME composition, proper heading order, text selection, zoom/reflow, RTL and long localized titles. Escape/Back returns through one layer at a time and restores focus. No hover-only preview, drag-only organization or animation that makes keyboard navigation wait. Authoring has an accessible toolbar and proper validation announcements; reading does not hydrate an editor instance per guide.

## Required proof and final synthesis

Before declaring this revision ready for release, prove at least these independently observable outcomes:

1. Public and Internal queries produce only their respective current authorized corpus, including body-only matches and all relevant pages beyond initial caps.
2. Switching tabs preserves query/return context, removes inapplicable filters, does not change composer purpose and never queries private conversation text automatically.
3. A staff-only source never appears in public CMS search, sitemap, donor/missionary surfaces, unauthenticated routes, external email serialization or a public preview cache.
4. Read-only Support staff can consult but cannot edit/publish; CRM-only or another-tenant users receive no internal title/body/snippet/count/asset.
5. Save/autosave remains draft-only; failed/concurrent save or publication preserves permitted author text and admits no stale revision.
6. Publication makes one complete current revision available to the intended Support audience; editing a new draft leaves readers on the existing published revision.
7. Public link insertion and its Undo/provenance behavior remain unchanged; internal consultation inserts nothing, executes nothing and changes no work/CRM fact.
8. Native copy/export, attempted internal-source insertion, source metadata removal and pasted internal routes follow the selected egress contract without falsely claiming global clipboard policing.
9. Archive/withdrawal, restore-to-draft, re-publication, source deletion and permission revocation do not leave stale readable content or resurrect withdrawn history/assets.
10. Safe generic links to other Core tools preserve their own authorization; instructions cannot perform or attest a gift/refund/receipt/contact/care action.
11. Offboarding preserves lawful attribution and allows a currently authorized maintainer to continue; an old author or link retains no access by itself.
12. Slow/unavailable source readers, late responses and navigation across CRM, Guide detail and editor leave the right draft/selection/focus intact without blocking unrelated work.
13. Keyboard, assistive technology, touch, narrow widths, zoom, IME, RTL and reduced motion support full use and maintenance, not just opening the panel.
14. Representative staff can distinguish a public link, internal guide and Saved note, publish the right audience, and recover from an unavailable guide without facilitator intervention or accidental disclosure.

The permanent path is to preserve D27's public half, qualify one narrow internal Support source and draft/publication contract, then compose the two through a clear shared Guidance interface. Use existing identity/capability, Tiptap, storage, audit and search mechanisms where sound; do not copy their ownership into a universal knowledge platform. Consultation-only internal use gives the required staff help without adding a new outbound material family merely for convenience.

The source inventory, actual guide volumes, language needs and staff usability remain empirical inputs. No browser, database/RLS, provider, live tenant or assistive-technology proof was executed for this revision. Research supports the recommended direction and exposes the required safeguards; it does not certify perfect UX or current implementation. Only this research artifact was written. **Q28 feedback remains paused and unanswered.**
