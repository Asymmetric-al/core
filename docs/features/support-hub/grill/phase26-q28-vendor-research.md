# Q28 research — how Support feedback invitations begin

**12 September 2026. D27 is fully ratified. Q28 is unanswered.** The parent and independent gap reviewer confirmed this as the next topic. This artifact compares invitation initiation; it does not select a rating scale, survey duration, exact delay, repeat policy, recipient cardinality, feedback schema or general survey product. Those details require the selected answer's adversarial review.

**Recommend A — Tenant-enabled automatic invitations after eligible completed human help.** This is an explicit opt-in policy, separate from ordinary work-state transitions. It should not mean that every closure, administrative ending or imported old conversation automatically sends a survey. B, deliberate staff-selected invitations, is a credible more discretionary alternative. C, retaining ordinary replies and operational reporting without a dedicated rating, remains the smallest valid scope.

[D9/D26](phase26-d26-ux-blueprint.md) preserve shared CRM access to the same Support detail; feedback must not become a duplicate CRM sentiment/interaction store or grant access to private support content.

P17/Email Studio remains the governed authoring/preparation owner; Support supplies the qualified feedback purpose and eligibility; P6 remains delivery/submission/reconciliation authority. Reuse shared capabilities without creating a general survey builder, survey-provider integration or a second mailing stack.

## Reporting and evidence cautions

There is a consequential documentation conflict in Front. Its August2026 [main feature article](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2255) attributes a conversation rating to all teammates who replied before resolution, while its February2026 [CSAT score definition](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2399) describes signature/last-sender attribution. The newer page is more recent, but these documents alone do not establish every deployed report's behavior. Asym must define its own historical attribution rather than claim either behavior is universally proven.

Help Scout's [Happiness report](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/383-reports-happiness), updated **22 May 2024**, shows response counts and the percentage participating, scopes access to permitted inboxes, and indexes its ratings by the reply date rather than submission date. Its “Happiness Score” subtracts negative from positive percentages. This is not the same as an ordinary percent-positive CSAT score. The important transfer is transparent denominator/date/cohort meaning and access-aware drill-through, not copying its formula or lifetime retention.

[AAPOR's survey guidance](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/) recommends first deciding whether a survey answers the research question, using clear questions and response choices, and limiting respondent burden. Its [response-rate guidance](https://aapor.org/response-rates/) cautions that response rate alone does not reliably establish survey quality or nonresponse bias. A consistent automatic invitation policy may reduce discretionary selection by handlers, but it does not make respondents representative of all donors, missionaries or requests.

Product judgment: show actual response counts and the eligible/invited/responded context, and avoid individual staff league tables or strong trend claims from tiny samples. Do not invent a universal minimum sample size or claim “no bias” above a convenient number. Low-volume feedback can still reveal a concrete friction point through a comment. A score is not proof that Finance completed a business action, nor that the handler caused every aspect of the person's experience.

## One question, three real options

**After staff complete eligible Support help, how should the requester be invited to give feedback?**

Illustration only: a person receives help finding a statement and the team completes its Support obligation. The organization may want to know whether that help was useful. A separate conversation ended because needed information never arrived is not automatically evidence of completed successful help. No source establishes how often either scenario occurs across Asym ministries.

| Option                                                     | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                       | Strongest benefit                                                                                                               | Main tradeoff                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Optional automatic invitations under tenant policy** | After the tenant deliberately enables the feature, eligible completed human-help conversations can generate a brief feedback invitation through a separate policy and the normal messaging owners. | Consistent collection without adding another decision to every closure; reduces selective invitation by handlers.               | Requires careful recipient/ending/recency/reopening/suppression eligibility, trustworthy cancellation and duplicate prevention. It must not contact everyone merely because work was marked Resolved. |
| **B — Staff choose when to invite feedback**               | Authorized staff deliberately initiate a supported feedback invitation for a suitable conversation; there is no automatic post-completion invitation policy.                                       | Human judgment controls timing and sensitive or unusual cases; no unsolicited follow-up from a bare status transition.          | Adds a recurring staff step and can produce uneven coverage or selective invitations. Both invitation safety and a real response lifecycle are still needed.                                          |
| **C — No dedicated rating feature**                        | Continue receiving ordinary replies and reviewing existing work/delivery/reply-target information; add no structured feedback invitation or survey response capability in Phase26.                 | Smallest scope and least communication burden; may suffice where teams learn through direct relationships and ordinary replies. | No consistent structured account of requesters' experience. Operational speed/completion metrics cannot substitute for what the person actually thought.                                              |

**Single best recommendation: A.** Make it an optional, deliberately enabled tenant capability with narrow eligibility, not a silent default. It provides a consistent opportunity to hear from people without making every worker remember or choose whom to ask. B is preferable when discretionary timing is the intended operating model and its uneven sampling is understood. C is valid where an additional feedback request would not produce useful action. No current evidence justifies requiring every ministry to survey or scoring staff by commercial-help-desk benchmarks.

These are alternative initiation policies, not three modes that must all be implemented. A does not silently include B as a second manual-send system, signature surveys, reminders, AI sentiment scoring, an NPS product or an arbitrary workflow engine. The selected answer determines the full next review; the rating question, scale, precise timing and cardinality remain unselected here.

## Candidate A journey and constraints to examine after selection

1. **Deliberately enable the policy.** An authorized tenant manager sees a concise description of eligible completed help, the recipient-facing invitation and the operational consequences. Enabling is distinct from editing reusable wording, configuring a signature or resolving a conversation. No backfill blast to old resolved work is implied. The exact inbox coverage and activation rules must be explicit in the selected answer.
2. **Complete actual Support work.** Staff use the existing truthful status/ending commands. If the policy creates a pending invitation, show a quiet understandable state without claiming it has been delivered. Eligibility considers actual correspondence and ending meaning; D5 No response or mere manual recording must not masquerade as human help successfully delivered.
3. **Recheck before sending.** A newly reopened request, merge, recipient change, policy disablement or existing suppression may invalidate a pending invitation. The authoritative lifecycle—not a mutable display tag—must decide. Ambiguous external submission is reconciled through the original effect rather than retried as a fresh survey. A merge must not duplicate invitations or silently credit the wrong request/worker.
4. **Invite the right person without granting access.** Use qualified actual communication context, not automatic selection of the CRM record owner, household, represented organization or everyone in Reply-all. Shared addresses and forwarded invitations require truthful identity limits. Possession of an invitation link is not proof of Party identity or authority to view the underlying conversation. The selected answer must settle recipient cardinality and how ambiguous recipients are handled.
5. **Offer a short voluntary response.** The person opens a minimal, accessible page. Opening a URL or image does not itself submit a rating. Use an explicit final submission, optional comment and clear success/error state. Avoid personal-data re-entry, login creation, a new ticket portal, attachments or a general form builder just to answer one question. This is a recommended pattern, not an accepted questionnaire design.
6. **Let feedback lead to appropriate human attention.** Staff can inspect the response with current conversation authorization and use existing Support/CRM navigation. Receiving a rating alone should not silently grant access, change a donor profile, score a missionary, execute a financial action or certify resolution. Negative feedback may merit review, but any automatic work-state change or follow-up message must be explicitly evaluated rather than assumed.
7. **Present results honestly.** Keep eligible, invited, submitted and unanswered states distinct, with clear cohort/date/attribution definitions and permission-aware drill-through. A no-response outcome is not a negative rating; a positive rating is not verified business completion. No new open/click tracking, AI-inferred score or employee surveillance is necessary to answer this question.

These constraints keep the option concrete without prematurely freezing a physical model, arbitrary limits, timer values or a complete unselected feature contract. Both A and B require shared-purpose qualification and privacy/retention behavior; a reusable URL alone cannot safely supply them.

## Privacy, communication purpose and accessibility

Current primary guidance does not justify declaring all feedback email transactional simply because it follows a support request. The [FTC's CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) distinguishes commercial, transactional/relationship and other content, and says primary purpose and narrowly defined categories matter. The selected review should preserve the platform's purpose/suppression rules and examine the actual invitation content. This is not a blanket legal classification of nonprofit surveys or authorization to add promotions, giving appeals or new marketing enrollment.

Product judgment: an optional invitation should respect applicable recipient communication preferences and provide a proportionate way to decline further feedback requests. Tenant enablement is not the recipient's consent. Do not bundle consent to future marketing with submitting feedback, expose sensitive case text in the invitation/link, or load third-party tracking merely to measure a response. Legal applicability and exact preference ownership are selected-answer obligations, not reasons to invent an elaborate compliance console now.

The [W3C forms tutorial](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/), updated **27 March 2026**, recommends short forms, labelled/grouped controls, clear instructions and perceivable success/error feedback. For Asym, visible text must identify rating choices; emojis or colors alone are insufficient. Use Core's shared accessible UI on the response page, keyboard/touch support, meaningful focus and a usable narrow-screen experience. Link expiry is a security/lifecycle decision; it is different from imposing an unnecessary countdown while the person is completing a form.

## What the selected-answer review must prove

Research found meaningful failure mechanisms, not prevalence estimates. Whichever invitation option is chosen, the full review must independently resolve:

- Completed-help eligibility versus administrative/no-response closure, human assistance versus automated messages, and manual-origin work with or without later correspondence.
- Tenant policy activation, permissions, existing versus future conversations, editing/disabling and source/template version changes.
- Correct recipient/identity limits, shared addresses, changed recipients, multiple participants, forwarding and cross-tenant links.
- Pending invitation cancellation/requalification, reopen/reclose, merge/split, duplicate jobs and ambiguous provider success.
- The exact invitation/response unit, submission/update/expiry behavior, scanner-safe opening and durable idempotency tied to business effects.
- Separation of feedback from ordinary messages, CRM identity/relationships, P17 wording/preparation and P6 delivery; no second authoritative customer score.
- Privacy, suppression, retention, redaction, export, audit and permission-aware response/comment access through CRM and Support.
- Clear question meaning, scale interpretation, denominator/cohort/time/attribution, voluntary response bias and low-volume reporting restraint.
- Staff and requester mobile/keyboard/assistive-technology journeys, clear failure recovery and operational ownership of feedback that needs attention.

No field schema, fixed rating scale, timer, legal classification or universal survey frequency has been ratified by this research. No source/runtime, provider, inbox, DNS, CMS or GitHub mutation occurred. No actual survey was sent, and no browser/usability or production-shaped test was performed. Current primary sources and Core boundaries support presenting this decision; they do not certify the eventual implementation or prove that every tenant needs it.
