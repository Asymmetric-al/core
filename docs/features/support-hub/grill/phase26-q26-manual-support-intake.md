# Q26: Support requests received by phone or in person

**Historical Q26 evidence: the founder selected and fully ratified A and all D26 amendments on 12 September 2026.** Earlier unanswered/recommendation/no-ADR text records the question stage only. [Complete ratified D26](phase26-d26-adversarial-review.md) and [Email Studio seam](phase26-d26-email-studio-integration.md) govern current acceptance.

**D1–D25 and every adopted amendment are fully founder-ratified. Q26 is unanswered.** This is a researched product choice, not a new ratified capability. It concerns manually recording an actual request that needs continuing Support work. It adds no calling service, audio recording, transcription, SMS, chat, offline device synchronization or AI.

## Practical context

Illustrative example: a donor calls about a receipt discrepancy. The staff member needs another authorized colleague to investigate and promises to follow up. The donor has not emailed Support, and the worker may not yet have a usable email address or a confirmed CRM match. The receipt's actual correction remains the giving/receipt owner's job. The open question is where staff record and track the Support request.

A courtesy call that is complete, ordinary relationship cultivation, a general staff task or restricted member-care work does not automatically belong in Support. A phone update about an existing Support request should remain with that work rather than creating another request. The example establishes the decision's meaning, not how frequently Asym tenants encounter it.

## The three actual options

| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Record Support follow-up directly**                         | From Support, use **Record a request**; record the request, its source and responsible inbox, then save without sending. The same action can open from authorized CRM context or an existing interaction, reusing that reference. | Places ongoing team handling in the existing Support work surface without requiring an intermediate CRM record or an email. | Adds a bounded staff-recorded intake origin and requires explicit source, retention and shared-history contracts. It must stay out of general call logging.                                                                      |
| **B — Use CRM notes and shared tasks for now**                    | Record the interaction using the qualified owning CRM/relationship capability and assign the needed shared task. Standalone off-email Support creation stays outside Phase 26.                                                    | Smallest Support scope; appropriate when ordinary owner tasks already cover the work.                                       | These requests use a separate follow-up experience from Support's shared queue, assignment and requester-work states. Only actually qualified notes/tasks may be offered; a planned interaction log is not a shipped capability. |
| **C — Record the interaction first, then open Support follow-up** | Start with the qualified shared interaction journey, then deliberately create linked Support work from that saved source.                                                                                                         | Makes interaction history the consistent first step, useful if that is already the team's normal practice.                  | Adds a mandatory intermediate step and requires that owner to handle unlinked or unidentified requesters safely. Current Party-keyed CRM records cannot simply be assumed to cover that case.                                    |

These are three product alternatives, not three tenant settings to build. A and C both reuse existing encounter records; **C requires one first**, while A permits direct Support capture. B includes real task follow-through, not merely passive notes. None should fabricate an incoming email, create a CRM Party automatically, or send a message merely because work was saved.

## Single recommendation: A — Record Support follow-up directly

Choose A because the demonstrated product need is ongoing shared Support handling, which already has an owner, work states, assignment, access rules and reminders. A uses that existing capability while allowing the worker to begin where they are. It is preferable to making a caller's CRM completeness or email availability a prerequisite for recording legitimate work. It is also more coherent than imposing a separate CRM task queue for the same kind of Support obligation simply because it arrived by phone.

This is product judgment from the actual Core boundaries and documented comparable workflows. It is not a measured claim about tenant demand, task speed or conversion. B is the strongest simpler alternative when owner notes/tasks suffice; C is strongest when staff already record each interaction first. Neither circumstance has been established for every tenant.

## Candidate A journey to pressure-test after selection

1. **Begin in context.** A clearly named Record a request action in Support opens a focused form with current tenant and permitted inbox context. Authorized CRM or interaction context can launch the same form and preserve a return location. This is separate from writing a new outbound email; it is not a button that initiates contact.
2. **Reuse existing work when appropriate.** Where current authorized context identifies relevant Support work, offer to open that work. Do not reveal restricted matches, infer identity from a similar name or phone number, or require an exhaustive CRM search before capture. A new source can relate to existing work without cloning the encounter.
3. **Record only the needed brief.** Capture the received request, its phone/in-person source, the reported encounter time and current staff attribution separately. Use the accepted bounded Tiptap note profile for the brief. Keep unknown requester, person represented, staff recorder, CRM association and eventual email audience distinct. Names need not be split into assumed first/last structures; no fake email or Party is required.
4. **Make responsibility clear.** Review the responsible inbox and existing qualified handling/follow-up controls. Saving creates the actual Support work and its attributable source record; **No email will be sent** is plain adjacent help. No silent self-assignment, automatic inbound-routing reenrollment, acknowledgement, hidden task duplication or business mutation is implied. Exact defaults receive the selected answer's review against D3–D8.
5. **Work in the normal conversation.** A source card identifies the staff-recorded brief; it is not a caller-message bubble or transcript. No native incoming mail time/counter is invented. Current work and follow-up remain visible. An authorized CRM timeline consumes an appropriate source reference/projection; it does not receive a second editable copy of the call.
6. **Contact the requester deliberately if needed.** A later email uses the existing qualified first-thread/audience review, P17 wording/signature/preparation and P6 tenant delivery. A phone-only requester can still have internal follow-up tracked; the product never claims Asym placed a call. Saving a brief neither proves identity nor authorizes a receipt, refund, recurring-gift, contact or care action.
7. **Correct and resume safely.** Cancel before admission makes no work record. An unknown save outcome reconciles the same operation instead of creating duplicates. An incorrect association is corrected through its owner; later Party merge/access loss/redaction/expiry cannot leave stale exposed copies. Mobile and keyboard users retain form input, clear field errors and return focus without a nested modal flow.

This journey is a concrete candidate for the founder's choice, not a hidden ratification of field names, defaults, physical tables or every edge rule. The selected answer receives the complete adversarial review before its amendment package is recorded as ratified.

## CRM, Email Studio and measurement boundaries

Core's one shared interaction history is an ownership guardrail, not permission for Support to build a second CRM. Phase 9's intended timeline composes owner facts; its seeded system-event taxonomy is not already a general call-log contract. Phase 28 requires one shared interaction log, but that does not prove the owner capability is implemented. For A, preserve **one authoritative encounter/source fact with qualified references and projections**. If an encounter already exists, reference it; if Support records the request first, do not duplicate its text into CRM Notes to create a permanent shadow archive. D9's authorized Support summary is distinct from a recorded communication event: creation or linking alone does not emit an email interaction, update last contact or publish a brief across CRM. Physical ownership and new-origin retention require explicit qualification during the answer review. [Core Phase 9 timeline contract](https://github.com/Asymmetric-al/core/blob/7abd2c11ffd4ed70c6775c4fd6f51c996e4350dd/docs/prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-09-full-crm-depth-relationship-graph.md#L599), [Phase 28 shared log](https://github.com/Asymmetric-al/core/blob/7abd2c11ffd4ed70c6775c4fd6f51c996e4350dd/docs/prds/sitestacker-parity/roadmap.md#L3127).

D12 currently permits staff-created related work from a reviewed incoming email; it does not already authorize this new origin. D14 reply targets remain email-specific: a staff-recorded call alone creates no first/next email-response obligation or fulfillment credit. D3's actual follow-up promises/reminders still apply. D16/D17 must govern the new brief's restrictions and finite lifetime without erasing independent owner records or copying text around expiry. D25 email readiness must be distinguished from permission and qualified handling for a manual source: the eventual design must not require a fake receiving address just to save work, or permit real email before the actual mail gates pass. The founder is choosing scope now; no silent amendment to those meanings is made.

Email Studio does not own the offline encounter or receive a message to prepare when staff saves the request. Shared Tiptap editor components do not transfer data ownership. P17 becomes relevant only for an actual governed email draft/preparation; P6 for its transport and outcome. D13's incoming-email acknowledgement, D23 signatures and D24 reply-and-work shortcuts cannot fire from manual capture. This question adds no message-template key, Role Layout, transport or credential.

## Research and evidence limits

Help Scout documents internal phone notes that save without sending email and can later receive an email reply. Zendesk also documents private staff-created requests. These support separating recording from contacting. [Help Scout new conversations](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/12-create-a-new-conversation), [Zendesk staff-created requests](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408882462618-Creating-a-ticket-on-behalf-of-the-requester).

HubSpot separately supports logged offline activities and optional task follow-up; creating a ticket manually does not automatically associate a conversation. Blackbaud Raiser’s Edge NXT supports constituent interactions and planned tasks through Actions. These substantiate B/C as alternatives, not an architectural template for Asym. [HubSpot activity logging](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/manually-log-activities-on-records), [HubSpot manual ticket creation](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/help-desk/create-respond-to-tickets-in-help-desk), [Blackbaud Actions](https://webfiles-sc1.blackbaud.com/files/support/helpfiles/rex/content/en-gb/content/bb-actions-overview.html).
