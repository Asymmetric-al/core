# Q33 — Long email conversation reading: independent UX research

**13 September 2026. D1–D32 are founder-ratified. Q33 is unanswered.** This report evaluates the next proposed choice: how much repeated email material staff see by default when reading a Support conversation. It does not change retained content, email sending, search eligibility, signature authoring, redaction, message order or attachment inspection.

## A genuine remaining choice

The accepted decisions protect source truth and access but do not settle the default presentation of repeated email history/signatures:

- [D22-R05](phase26-d22-adversarial-review.md) explicitly permits current qualified quoted text to match under its own source custody, without a repeated-text relevance boost. [D22-R08/R09](phase26-d22-adversarial-review.md) require exact-source navigation and Find across retained messages beyond mounted pages.
- [D23](phase26-d23-adversarial-review.md) preserves manual/quoted content and distinguishes managed signature authoring from historical message content. A team-only signature does not guarantee anonymity because other message parts may identify staff.
- [D32](phase26-d32-adversarial-review.md) supplies separate deliberate file Preview/Download behavior and preserves Support/CRM draft-return context. It does not choose email-body folding.
- Current [EmailMessage](https://github.com/Asymmetric-al/core/blob/7abd2c11ffd4ed70c6775c4fd6f51c996e4350dd/apps/admin/features/support-hub/components/detail/timeline/EmailMessage.tsx#L134) passes its renderable body to `RichTextViewer`. The [body selector](https://github.com/Asymmetric-al/core/blob/7abd2c11ffd4ed70c6775c4fd6f51c996e4350dd/apps/admin/features/support-hub/components/detail/timeline/EmailMessage.tsx#L259) chooses available HTML, then text. No explicit quoted-history folding control was found in the inspected Support surface. This is scaffold behavior, not proof of a qualified full-content reader or an intentional decision against folding.

The independent scope reviewer confirms this gap. Conversation priority, source subject identity, search, signatures and preview formats are already decided and should not be reopened through a reading preference.

**Collapsed for reading must remain distinct from restricted/hidden source content.** A visual disclosure does not make current permitted text ineligible for D22, and expanding it cannot reveal redacted, expired or unauthorized material. “Full” below means the complete currently allowed safe message presentation, not raw unsanitized transport source or a retention bypass.

## Three real alternatives

| Option                                                         | What staff actually experience                                                                                                                                                                                             | Strongest advantage                                                                                                                                           | Real tradeoff                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Compact by default, with clear expansion — recommended** | Clearly recognizable repeated quoted history/signature material is folded behind a labelled control. New or uncertain content stays visible. Staff can expand a section or use an obvious full-reading action when needed. | Reduces repeated scrolling in ordinary email work while preserving deliberate access to detail. One shared default is easy to learn.                          | Needs conservative recognition, exact source mapping and a full-content escape that does not miss inline replies. Staff who regularly want every permitted detail may expand repeatedly.                                                  |
| **B — Full permitted message content by default**              | Messages show their full safe permitted body, including quoted history and signatures, using ordinary bounded thread loading. No automatic quoted-region folding.                                                          | Most predictable reading surface; avoids mistaken quote detection and the additional step of revealing detail. Strong where every part is routinely examined. | Repeated histories and signatures can dominate a long thread, especially on mobile. Staff must scroll past them to locate current text and files. Full display still needs safe loading, search and source restrictions.                  |
| **C — Each person chooses a Compact or Full default**          | Supplies A's compact machinery and B's full presentation, with a narrow personal reading preference. Temporary expansion remains available within a conversation.                                                          | Accommodates staff who repeatedly prefer different reading density without changing anyone else's view.                                                       | Adds persisted preference scope, initialization/reset behavior and another supportable setting; it does not reduce the hard content-recognition work. No current evidence establishes that this additional preference is needed for Asym. |

These are alternative product defaults/scopes, not a mandate to build three tenant modes. A does not depend on AI summarization, deleting duplicate text or changing what recipients receive. B is a serious simplicity/trust alternative, not an intentionally unusable wall of unbounded HTML. C is a legitimate broader preference option, not a layout builder; its persistence must use a qualified product preference boundary rather than inventing a new owner of message content.

**Single best recommendation: A.** Repeated email chains are a demonstrated interface problem in current support products, and clear reversible expansion addresses it without requiring personal setup. Keep recognition conservative: a quotation marker alone is not proof that a block contains only old text. This is a product judgment informed by sources, not measured proof that every Asym tenant has long threads or that one exact folding algorithm is optimal.

## Reported friction, distinguished from verified capability

An [Intercom community report dated 23 July 2025](https://community.intercom.com/inbox-9/collapse-quoted-content-in-conversation-11077) describes repeated quoted sections forcing extensive scrolling when they cannot be collapsed. A labelled Intercom Support reply on **24 July 2025** confirms that quoted sections commonly use an ellipsis control and says formatting edge cases can prevent it. This is a vendor employee's support explanation and a self-selected user's report; it does not establish that the defect persists in every current edition or measure its frequency.

The same thread includes a user's CSS/JavaScript workaround that hides blockquotes. **Do not adopt it.** A blockquote may contain a newly answered question, a first forwarded request or meaningful in-line context. Client-injected `display:none` is not a source-aware product contract, and silent display hiding is not redaction.

These observations support testing excessive scrolling, missed inline replies and repeated expansion effort. They do not prove that donor-care or missionary-support teams universally receive a certain number or length of emails. A worked example may use a staff member reviewing an ongoing donor-care request; that is an illustrative journey, not a claim about actual ministry case volume or sensitive-data access.

## Recommended ordinary journey, without preselecting implementation details

1. **Open the existing conversation.** Maintain the current authorized Support or CRM context, message ordering and draft. The subject, sender/recipient identity, message kind, timestamp, delivery state and attachment access remain understandable; this decision does not replace those accepted controls.
2. **Read the current content first.** In A, confidently recognized repeated material is folded. Uncertain regions, inline-edited answers and first/only available forwarded context should remain readable. Do not manufacture a summary or call a heuristic boundary “the new answer” without qualification.
3. **Expand deliberately in place.** Use a clear text label such as **Show quoted history**, with an accessible expanded state. Avoid relying solely on an unexplained ellipsis. Restore content from the same currently allowed safe source projection; do not open raw transport HTML, another conversation or a retention archive.
4. **Read broadly without repetitive work.** A clearly scoped full-reading action can reveal permitted folded content for the current conversation. Its wording must distinguish all messages from repeated regions and already loaded from retained eligible content. The exact placement and default nesting are design work after the choice, not a requirement to add a toolbar full of modes.
5. **Find and navigate.** D22 still searches qualified quoted content. A result or Find navigation reveals the relevant currently permitted source span so the person sees why it matched. Fold state cannot create a false no-match or require a manual hunt through collapsed sections. Do not count inaccessible matches or expose restricted history.
6. **Return to the task.** Collapse/expand changes only the reader's presentation. It does not change the source, recipients, quoted content in a draft, signature policy, prepared email, reply intention, read/review state or CRM record. Preserve reading position and the actual unsent Tiptap draft.

C adds a personal default-choice journey to these same controls, with predictable scope/reset and no shared side effect. B opens in the full permitted view without automatic folding. All options preserve source privacy, safe rendering, bounded loading, mobile/keyboard use and D22 exact-source behavior.

No exact number of initially open messages, detector threshold, fold-depth cap, personal-preference scope, shortcut, or pagination size is selected by this research. Those are not needed to make the three current options concrete and fair. No option promises perfect quote recognition, unrestricted “all history,” zero browser exposure or a tested perfect UI.

## Conclusion for the next question

Q33 is a valid single product decision. Present A, B and C visibly, with **A as the single recommendation**. Explain A's conservative behavior and full permitted expansion, B's predictability, and C's legitimate repeated-personal-preference benefit alongside its additional setting. Preserve all already-ratified owners and defer neither safety nor honest source presentation to a future AI system.

This report searched current primary behavior and checked the actual source and accepted decisions. It does not prove detector correctness, real tenant frequency, task-time improvement, browser accessibility or runtime performance. No D33 answer, formal specification, code, ticket, external mutation or real email was created.
