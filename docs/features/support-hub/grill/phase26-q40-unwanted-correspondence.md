## The decision

**When unwanted email has already entered Support work, what should staff be able to do?**

Illustrative example: Maya opens a conversation that turns out to be an unsolicited sales pitch, not a request for help. It passed the existing intake checks. She wants it out of the team's ordinary work, but should be able to correct a mistake. Whether later email from that address should also be held is a separate consequence that must be clear. This is an example for comparing the options, not an observed ministry workload or spam-volume claim.

| Option                                             | What Maya can do                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Main benefit                                                                                                                                      | Real cost or limitation                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Set aside this conversation, with Restore**  | Choose a clear contextual **Mark unwanted** action for the currently reviewed unwanted conversation. It leaves ordinary work and remains recoverable under current access and retention. Later mail goes through normal intake checks; this action changes no future-sender policy. | A direct, understandable cleanup path with recoverable mistakes and no implied judgment about the person or every future email.                   | Repeated unwanted messages can require another decision. Adds a narrowly owned disposition/recovery capability and its truthful reporting rules.                                                                                               |
| **B — Also offer separate future-sender controls** | Have A, plus a separately authorized and explicitly reviewed action to hold future mail from the narrowly specified source through the qualified inbound-policy owner. Setting aside the current conversation does not enable that rule automatically.                              | Reduces repeated manual work when a sender consistently produces unwanted mail, while keeping current cleanup and future policy visibly distinct. | Adds rule scope, permissions, matching, exceptions, review coverage, recovery and readiness dependencies. A shared or spoofed address can also carry legitimate mail. This cannot become a new Support-local rules engine or CRM-person block. |
| **C — Use Resolve and optional labels**            | Continue using existing work completion and an optional curated label to remove the current item from active work and find it later. No special unwanted designation or recovery view is added.                                                                                     | Smallest product scope and fewest new controls. Existing reopening and label tools remain familiar.                                               | Cleanup uses general work tools and label discipline. Retrieval/reporting must not imply that a non-request was a successfully handled Support request; future mail remains governed by ordinary intake.                                       |

**Single best recommendation: A — Set aside this conversation, with Restore.**

This gives the everyday action a precise scope: deal with the item in front of you, preserve correction, and do not quietly change how future mail is treated. It also gives non-support material an honest meaning instead of asking staff to infer it from an optional label. B is the strongest broader alternative if reducing repeated sender noise is important enough to accept the additional policy/operating dependency. C is a valid smaller scope; it should not be dismissed simply because commercial help desks have spam folders.

These are alternative capability scopes, not three tenant settings. A/B's recoverable designation is proposed here; neither is a fifth Support work status or an extra permission tier already accepted by D39. Names such as **Mark unwanted** and **Restore** are clear candidate copy, not ratified new domain definitions.

## Why this is the next question

[D19](phase26-d19-adversarial-review.md) governs input **held before it enters Support work**, with Release/Dismiss/Return and separate future policy. Its mistaken-release correction already preserves wrong-link, duplicate and unsafe-content owner paths. It does not settle an ordinary staff action for nuisance or non-support correspondence already admitted as a conversation. [D3](phase26-d3-adversarial-review.md) separates genuine work from automated/malicious intake; [D39](phase26-d39-full-ratification.md) settles private reading, not whether something belongs in ordinary Support work.

The [independent gap review](phase26-q40-gap-review.md) verifies that distinction against the accepted decisions and current source. Priority/naming, generic split, ordinary bulk work, drafts, read behavior and configurable automation were screened rather than reopened. Print/export has no sufficiently established new need to make it the next question merely for feature parity.

## What the research changes

Front explicitly separates current-conversation spam classification from blocking future sender mail, with different shared-inbox permissions. That supports making scope visible. Its mailbox synchronization is a separate vendor integration and is not proposed for Asym. [Front: Spam actions](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2251).

Other products couple these actions more strongly. Help Scout documents future filtering learned from marking a conversation spam and warns against doing so for legitimate automated correspondence. Zendesk couples spam marking with requester suspension; its current page contains conflicting recovery wording, so no universal recovery duration is inferred. HubSpot's documented blocking flow can also delete associated contact/ticket records under stated conditions. These are reasons to evaluate the whole consequence of the action, not copy a familiar button label. [Detailed current comparisons and limitations](phase26-q40-vendor-research.md).

No vendor behavior establishes a measured Asym demand, safe email-to-person identity mapping or permission to suppress legitimate complaints. A is a product judgment supported by the separation of current handling from future trust, not a claim of universal best practice.

## Boundaries preserved in every option

1. **Unwanted is not difficult or unresolved.** A complaint, donation problem, repeated legitimate question or sensitive case cannot be sidelined to hide work, improve metrics or avoid required review. Existing legitimate commitments and owner-domain tasks remain visible and independently owned.
2. **Current content is not future policy.** A does not block a sender, domain, CRM Party or all later replies in the thread. New input follows current intake rules and legitimate new work cannot disappear merely because earlier content was unwanted. B requires a separate explicit policy decision and the existing qualified owner; no implicit training, domain-wide rule or default account suspension is bundled in.
3. **No whole-thread shortcut over mixed work.** A conversation containing genuine Support work or merged originals cannot be discarded wholesale because one message is unwanted. Exact source/action eligibility and mixed-content recovery must be qualified in the selected answer's review; D10 original-source/Undo and D19 mistaken-release corrections stay intact.
4. **Recovery is honest.** A/B preserve a durable current-authorized recovery route while content is lawfully available; they do not promise forever retention, provider recall or undeleting destroyed bytes. D16/D17 restrictions/holds/expiry and existing source history remain authoritative. No new deletion period, trash lifecycle or bulk action is selected here.
5. **No false completion or hidden reporting change.** Setting aside junk is not successful human Support resolution, a met reply target, an excuse to erase history or a feedback invitation trigger. D28's actual eligibility/exclusions and truthful denominators remain required. C's optional label does not become a new authority to bypass those rules.
6. **Authority stays with each domain.** Current tenant/inbox/source/action permission applies to metadata, commands and restoration. Staff assignment, a CRM link or matching email is not an authority grant. No donor/contact deletion, unsubscribe, consent change, login suspension, giving/member-care action or copied CRM record follows from handling unwanted correspondence.
7. **Email Studio remains a content/preparation owner.** Support owns any selected current disposition/recovery; qualified inbound policy owns any separately selected future hold. P17/Email Studio retains governed content and separately owned notification engagement, while P6 retains actual delivery. Marking or restoring sends no notice, publishes no template, prepares no mail, changes no delivery evidence and clears no notification groups. Any genuinely new communication purpose needs its own existing owner path, not a message assembled by a cleanup handler.
8. **Existing safeguards and work-in-progress survive.** D35 note history, D37/D38 private drafts, D39 reading and mandatory send/result review are not silently erased by a disposition change. The selected-answer review must close stale tabs, pending sends, dirty drafts, new arrivals, restore, merge/Undo, authorization and retention races without a new generic workflow platform.
