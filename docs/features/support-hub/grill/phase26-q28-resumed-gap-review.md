## The next single question and three real options

**Should Support invite requesters to say how the help went, and how should that invitation begin?**

For example, Elena receives help finding a receipt and staff finish the Support work. Reply timing and work status are known, but neither says whether she found the help useful. This is an illustrative comparison, not an assertion that every ministry wants a survey or lacks useful direct feedback.

| Option                                                        | Actual experience                                                                                                                                                                    | Strongest benefit                                                                                                                   | Cost or limitation                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Optional automatic feedback invitations — recommended** | A tenant explicitly enables a separately qualified policy. Eligible completed human-handled Support work can generate a brief optional feedback invitation. It is Off until enabled. | Consistent opportunity without staff having to remember or choose each invitation; reduces one source of handler-selected coverage. | Adds outward contact and a real invitation/response lifecycle. Eligibility, repeat contact, recipient authority, privacy and voluntary-response bias remain material. |
| **B — Staff chooses when to ask**                             | An authorized worker deliberately requests the supported feedback opportunity for a particular conversation. No completion-triggered invitation runs.                                | Strongest control over context and timing, especially for unusual exchanges.                                                        | Adds recurring staff effort and selective/forgotten invitations. It still needs qualified response collection, custody and delivery; it is not just a pasted link.    |
| **C — Ordinary feedback, without a dedicated rating flow**    | No dedicated invitation or rating page is added in Phase 26. Requesters reply normally; staff use those exchanges and existing operational reporting.                                | Lowest additional contact and implementation/maintenance burden; credible for teams learning through direct relationships.          | No consistent structured requester assessment. Silence, fast replies and work ending cannot be reported as satisfaction.                                              |

These are alternative product directions, not three modes that must all ship. Ordinary replies remain available under all three. No survey scale, delay, response lifetime, repeat window, participant cardinality, reporting formula or employee-ranking model is selected at this question stage.

**A remains the single strongest recommendation.** It offers a consistent feedback opportunity without a repeated handler task, while explicit tenant activation respects the legitimate lower-contact preference behind C. This does not claim automatic collection is unbiased, that feedback improves donor retention, or that all tenants need it. Neutral eligibility must not mean “ask only when staff expect praise,” and completed human handling must not mean a proven fix or satisfied requester.
