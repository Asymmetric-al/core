# Designated intake reviewers with tenant oversight

**D19 and every adopted amendment are fully founder-ratified, 12 September 2026. Historical disposition: Accept with required amendments.** D1–D18 remain fully ratified. The complete accepted package and explicit Email Studio seam are recorded below as grooming authority; no implementation or formal specification is claimed.

Give each responsible shared inbox a deliberately designated, currently eligible review pool. Give a named tenant oversight owner visibility into qualified coverage and unresolved work, with a qualified backup route. Reviewers inspect retained, reviewable incoming email in **Needs review**, then deliberately **Release to inbox** or **Dismiss** that one input. Release resumes canonical intake once; dismissal leaves a correction path until the original content deadline. Neither changes future sender trust, persistent routing, CRM identity, consent, recipients, or business records. Restricted safety/classification, technical recovery and unbound-tenant operations retain their own authority.

This solves an established Phase 26 requirement: retained legitimate input must not become invisible because ordinary staff cannot act on it or nobody owns review. The strongest alternative is one central qualified tenant team. A accommodates that team covering every inbox while making local responsibility explicit. Administrator-only review is suitable staffing for a small tenant, not a mandatory product bottleneck. No evidence establishes that every ministry has separate donor-care reviewers or that one staffing model improves measured satisfaction. Those are product judgments, not invented user research.

The [complete UX](phase26-d19-intake-review-ux.md), [data/lifecycle contract](phase26-d19-data-lifecycle-contract.md), [evidence and conflict register](phase26-d19-evidence.md), three independent reviews and [documentation validation](phase26-d19-validation.json) accompany this decision. R01–R30 are exact corrected grooming language; the category findings reference these clauses instead of creating conflicting duplicate rules.

## Corrected decision clauses

### D19-R01 — Scope and one accountable operating model

Support Hub owns the ordinary business-review decision for accepted input that is already bound to a trustworthy tenant and a qualified review scope. Designate reviewers per responsible inbox, backed by tenant oversight; one eligible team may cover multiple or all inboxes. Reuse Core membership, teams, authorization, attention and durable work capabilities after qualifying them for this purpose. Do not create a new omnipotent reviewer role, a second help desk, CRM, classification engine or tenant workflow builder.

### D19-R02 — Reasons and authority determine available actions

Retain independent evidence for every current gate. Present one plain-language primary reason plus expandable additional reasons; compute allowed actions from the intersection of all gates, not the first or least restrictive reason. Routine reviewable uncertainty can be decided by eligible intake reviewers. Missing content/provider retrieval uses technical recovery; unsafe files, non-overridable security restrictions and care classification use the actual safety/classification/care owner. Conflicting or unknown tenant binding remains separately authorized platform recovery. A generic release command cannot clear those gates. Closing one reason leaves the item held if another still blocks admission.

### D19-R03 — Configuration that shows effective coverage

In the existing inbox settings, use one **Intake review** section: **Reviewers**, **Backup**, and the tenant **Oversight owner** reference. Support named existing people or teams, not copied users or email addresses. Select only qualified candidates within the configuring actor's authority; show effective eligible coverage, not raw team size. Assignment of review responsibility alone grants no body, conversation, CRM or restricted-data access. One eligible reviewer is sufficient; multiple eligible people or a qualified backup are recommended, not a mandatory two-person approval rule. The same person can cover a small tenant's roles with a clear single-person coverage indication.

Before new real inbox activation, require a viable review route, finite owner-qualified content schedule and an accountable oversight route. Configuration edits use versioned guarded commands and show resulting coverage before save. Removing the last qualified coverage route through settings requires a replacement; security revocation or offboarding always proceeds immediately and creates a coverage exception rather than being blocked to protect staffing.

### D19-R04 — Absence, team changes and oversight

Evaluate current tenant membership, relevant inbox/review scope, source classification and action permission on every list, preview and command. Team membership changes cannot silently confer a missing base permission. Planned absence uses existing availability/coverage information where sound; do not build an on-call scheduler. A backup may act whenever independently eligible and remains visible as coverage. Stale or unavailable primary coverage raises one source-owned attention condition for the named oversight owner. That owner can repair staffing under actual administration permissions or use a separately authorized review capability; oversight does not imply message-body access. Coverage loss does not discard retained mail, fabricate a completed review, or grant replacement staff access automatically.

### D19-R05 — Quiet, source-authorized queue

Show **Needs review** within Support Hub and as an inbox-scoped view for eligible staff. Default to items requiring a business decision; keep technical recovery visibly separate as **Processing issues** for qualified operators. List authorized sender/address, subject or safe fallback, responsible inbox, plain reason, received age and content expiry. No body snippets by default. An informational header says: “Incoming email held before it enters Support work.” It does not call every item spam or a ticket. Include **To review**, **Processing**, and **History** views; history filters distinguish Released, Dismissed and Expired. Counts, search matches, pagination and empty states are authorized at source, including metadata. “No messages need a decision” requires successful loading of the stated scope and must not conceal authorized pending releases. Unknown or partial scope is explicit without leaking concealed records. Processing here means pending release only; Processing issues remains the qualified technical-recovery view. They reference the same source rather than duplicate it.

### D19-R06 — Inspect without executing incoming content

Use a read-only text-first preview; provide qualified sanitized formatting only when useful. Block scripts, forms, active embeds, remote images/tracking, automatic link fetching, mailto actions and browser navigation from the message. Link text and actual destination can be inspected as inert text. Normal review does not expose raw provider download URLs, raw MIME downloads or open unscanned files. Attachments show safe names or neutral labels, actual availability and scan status; preview/download requires the separately qualified asset path and current source permission. A failed or pending scan is not a clean file. Provider file fetching uses the qualified connection, bounded size/type checks and permitted URL/redirect policy; it is not an arbitrary URL-fetch proxy. Displaying body content is itself authorized sensitive access. Store original permitted evidence separately from its display representation; do not recover by copying plain text and dropping original HTML/files.

### D19-R07 — Authentication is evidence, not identity

Derive tenant/provider binding and machine verification evidence from trusted receiver context, not user-editable email fields. A signed Resend webhook proves the provider event's provenance, not the human sender's identity, business authority or harmlessness. Authentication-Results headers require a qualified receiver trust boundary; text supplied by the sender cannot certify itself. Present useful distinctions such as “Sender checks could not be confirmed,” with technical details on demand. A familiar name, matching CRM email, shared address, valid domain signature, quoted forward or reviewer recognition alone grants no identity/CRM access. Routine false-positive review can admit an eligible input without promoting uncertain evidence to verified identity. It cannot override a separately non-overridable rule.

### D19-R08 — One deliberate release

In the preview, show **Release to [inbox]** with the qualified destination and whether intake will create a new conversation or continue an identified authorized conversation. If that outcome is not yet provable, say it will be checked during processing; never promise “new ticket” or silently attach to an arbitrary record. Show concise scope copy: “Only this message. Future mail rules stay the same.” An explicit review of this summary is the ordinary confirmation; do not add a second generic “Are you sure?” dialog when scope is unchanged. Admit a source-version-bound decision, recheck every gate and record one durable processing obligation. Show **Release requested** until canonical admission is proven, then **Released** with the authorized destination link. Release is not Send, Resolve, registration, email verification, allowlisting, a refund or any other business action. When D13 confirmation may still apply, show “The usual new-request confirmation may follow if this message is eligible”; never promise no email can follow admission.

### D19-R09 — Destination and continuation corrections

A reviewer may choose a one-item destination only when separately authorized for the source, destination and review action. Missing or ambiguous routing goes to the qualified tenant routing owner; it never chooses the first candidate, searches message text for a tenant or exposes unauthorized inbox names. Qualified continuation uses D1/D2 correlation and current restrictions; subject/sender similarity does not prove a thread. Do not offer free-form forced attachment to an arbitrary conversation. Unresolved correlation remains reviewable by its proper owner; choosing a genuinely new conversation, where permitted, is an explicit decision preserving original references and not guessing identity. Persistent **Change future routing** is a separate owner action with its own scope/impact review, never a checked default beside one-item release. It must not automatically grant held-content admission or supersede another reviewer's dismissal.

### D19-R10 — Dismissal and correction

**Dismiss** means “Keep this message out of Support work.” It records one typed reason and removes the item from To review; it neither blocks future mail nor deletes a CRM contact or immediately destroys content. Show “Recoverable until [exact original deadline]” and a persistent **Return to review** action in History while current authority and content lifetime permit. A transient Undo can invoke that same command, but is never the only recovery route. Returning to review does not release, renew content retention or clear safety gates. At expiry the action becomes unavailable with an honest explanation; no background lag extends the recovery promise. Preserve Dismissed as the historical decision and Content expired as current availability; expiry does not rewrite who decided or imply a missed business review.

### D19-R11 — Bounded bulk work

Allow bulk Dismiss for explicitly selected, currently authorized reviewable items, with one reviewed reason and an exact selection summary. Limit selection to the loaded page of at most 50 items; no “all matching,” bulk release, future-sender policy or permanent deletion. Fifty is a proposed bounded UI/command safety limit, not a vendor performance claim. Revalidate each selected source and return per-item outcomes; never claim whole-batch success after a partial conflict. No body-prefetch requirement or cross-tenant batch. Individual release and one-item recovery remain the deliberate normal path. Reassess adding other bulk effects only for demonstrated work and independent authorization.

### D19-R12 — Qualified handoffs and technical recovery

**Ask the responsible team** uses existing restricted staff work/attention with a source reference and typed reason, not emailed copies of the body. It transfers or establishes accountable review scope only after destination authority is proven; the source cannot lose its sole accountable owner before destination custody/obligation is durable. Do not create a second Support conversation merely to investigate intake. Technical **Retry retrieval** repairs body/file availability and runs current source/expiry guards; it does not approve safety, future policy or identity. Safe attachment-only inputs are not rejected merely because text is empty. A genuinely unavailable essential body stays visibly unavailable; an optional unavailable file may follow D1's qualified partial-content path without implying the file passed checks. No blanket “ignore all errors.”

### D19-R13 — One durable input and admission effect

Use the canonical accepted-input identity and its source generation, with a separately recorded review-decision sequence. Provider retries, duplicate webhook IDs, repeat commands and worker retries must reconcile that input's single current admission result. One source may legitimately map to permitted recipient occurrences under D1; never duplicate a conversation/message merely because a transport request ID changed. Atomically commit the review decision, source version and durable dispatch/outbox obligation. Canonical Support admission atomically commits its own source linkage/custody and receipt once. If a shared database permits one transaction, use it; otherwise keep the durable intermediate state and reconcile by authoritative effect identity. No provider I/O occurs inside the transaction. The stable admission-effect identity belongs to the canonical input and each D1-qualified recipient occurrence; source/gate revisions, review decisions and attempt IDs are fences and lineage, not reasons for another admission effect. Cancel release invalidates its pending decision/continuation generation. A later eligible release records a new decision and attempt against the same stable admission-effect identity: old canceled workers cannot act, and an earlier canceled attempt cannot permanently suppress valid re-release.

### D19-R14 — Trusted attribution and tenant-aware data

Derive tenant, actor authentication ID, actual profile/membership ID, owner capability and decision time from trusted server context. Do not substitute auth user ID for profile ID merely because development rows happen to match. Stored tenant-owned review/source/inbox/conversation/team/route endpoints enforce equal tenant identity structurally. Global auth/profile identities, where applicable, bind through authoritative tenant membership and the actual acting-profile relationship rather than a copied tenant identity. Foreign keys establish relational integrity; current authorization/classification remains required at each command and admission fence. Use composite tenant-aware foreign keys or an equivalent constrained authoritative boundary, non-null tenant for tenant-review rows, no self-contradictory state combinations, positive monotonic versions, exact typed reason/status values and unique durable business-effect keys. Unbound input remains outside tenant review, not tenant-null rows exposed through a broad fallback. No cascading parent delete may erase required evidence or detach policy authority silently.

### D19-R15 — RLS, grants and privileged execution

Keep raw intake, review commands and sensitive mutation server-owned through packages/api. RLS-enabled tables with no anon/authenticated grants are a valid server-only design; they do not need permissive browser policies. Privileged services must still prove exact tenant, current source and action scope. Revoke direct review-state, tenant, attribution, admission and audit mutations from general staff paths. Any approved direct-read projection uses current tenant and source predicates. If an UPDATE policy exists, USING must qualify the old row and WITH CHECK the new allowed state; immutable scope/actor fields cannot be transformed through an allowed update. Qualify every RPC/view/storage/cache/export path and SECURITY DEFINER search path/execution grant. Never treat UI filtering or a generic admin/staff label as the boundary.

### D19-R16 — Durable history without a content archive

Keep source occurrence, original receipt evidence, review reason/policy revision, actor/membership attribution, chosen scope, decision sequence, actual admission/outcome and correction lineage as typed owner-qualified history. Append corrections rather than rewrite historical decisions. Durable business history is distinct from technical logs and notification engagement. Audit carries no copied subject/body/raw headers, credentials, provider URLs or arbitrary pasted sensitive reason. If explanatory text is truly required, it is bounded classified content with its own permitted lifetime; default reasons remain typed. Body-free facts are potentially identifying and need an owner horizon, access and anonymization/disposal contract. Existing mutable support_audit_log grants are not append-only proof.

### D19-R17 — Concurrency and expired authority

Release, Dismiss, Return to review, gate changes, destination changes, permission revocation and admission/expiry share current source-version guards and a defined serialization point. Stale decisions conflict with a readable current outcome instead of overwriting. At an exact content deadline, admission is forbidden; admission must commit strictly before the deadline. A later fetch, retry, browser tab, route repair or policy lengthening cannot rehydrate expired content. Outbox replay rechecks current gates, content frontier and approved scope before positive effects. At admission the decision must remain qualified for its deciding actor/designation and current source/destination; the worker's service privileges do not substitute. Revocation before admission blocks the pending effect and records an owned current outcome. Admission that already committed retains truthful historical attribution, with current restrictions governing subsequent access/effects. Use the qualified owner fences, not a new global platform version counter. A worker cannot dismiss/expire a source using an obsolete policy version. Missing proof fails closed for that effect, with an accountable recovery state; uncertainty is not fabricated success.

### D19-R18 — Correct a mistaken release truthfully

Before canonical admission commits, a qualified **Cancel release** may stop a pending release through the same current source guard. If admission has committed or cannot yet be reconciled, the UI must not promise Undo or deletion: reconcile first, then offer the authorized conversation/source correction path. Existing D9/D10/D16 owner commands handle wrong links, actual duplicates or unsafe admitted content; stop unsafe pending effects through their actual owner. Never unsend mail, erase valid staff work, delete CRM facts or make a delivered message disappear to simulate reversal. Preserve original review and correction evidence. A later security restriction always applies even if the earlier decision was legitimate.

### D19-R19 — Finite ordinary held-content schedule

Propose **14 elapsed days from the first qualified provider receipt** as the ordinary business-review content schedule, published through the actual intake/records owner. This is a product judgment providing two calendar weeks for routine review and short absences; it is not a universal legal period or a claim of measured optimality. The earlier applicable mandatory owner ceiling wins. Missing trustworthy original receipt uses a bounded owner-reconciliation exception, not now, migration time or email Date as a fresh timestamp. Reads, touches, review reopening, retries, rule changes, dismissal and repeated receipts never renew the anchor.

Use one tenant-level owner-qualified held-content policy, displayed from inbox settings; no per-reviewer or per-inbox timer hierarchy and no Never. Tenant changes select only published owner-qualified finite schedule variants/limits; the proposed default is 14 days, and no additional arbitrary duration is authorized by this decision. Publishing or changing a qualified variant requires the actual intake semantic owner and records/private-byte contract, not a generic tenant timer. An authorized schedule change requires the existing policy-owner review/publication contract, showing old/new periods, affected existing unexpired scope and exact effective-time semantics; reviewers cannot extend an item. Shortening cannot silently dispose hidden or unclassified scope. Lengthening strictly before expiry can affect remaining permitted life; at/after expiry it cannot revive bytes. Technical, security/care and unbound input are separately owned classes with explicit finite access/review/disposal or restricted-preservation schedules. Do not force them into ordinary dismissal or the D17 Resolved-work clock. For unknown original receipt, use a separate restricted timing-recovery class: anchor it once to trusted first durable platform acceptance where known, otherwise to the explicitly recorded first restricted-recovery registration for legacy evidence. Proposed normal recovery access ends no later than 14 elapsed days from that recovery anchor, earlier known source ceilings still win, and source-owned review attention is due at 24 elapsed hours. Registration never fabricates provider receipt, resets prior restrictions or grants routine body access. Qualify original evidence before ordinary release; if its true cutoff has passed, ordinary admission remains forbidden. At recovery cutoff, ordinary recovery access ends, while actual required preservation or unknown-preservation handling stays restricted with a finite owner review date; no repeated registration renews it.

### D19-R20 — Expiry, holds and copy custody

At the ordinary cutoff, end routine content visibility and release/recovery authority even if cleanup workers lag; record **Expired before admission** or the actual dismissed-expired outcome. Admission approval alone cannot establish a new D17 lifetime: only actual qualified Support admission/custody strictly before cutoff can do so, with its truthful readiness event. Dispose redundant intake copies under their actual transient-copy schedule; do not retain duplicate MIME forever after admission. Cleanup retires only the redundant occurrence/copy, never a shared authoritative blob still needed by the admitted source or another qualified owner.

Phase 26 owns the meaning of its intake schedule; the Phase 21 catalog does not classify intake or become its semantic owner. Reuse D16 source restriction and the existing qualified records/private-byte execution mechanisms for disposal and restore barriers to bodies, headers, safe previews, attachments, derived text, review exports, caches and retained provider copies. A legal/records/security preservation obligation can retain remaining required bytes in restricted custody without ordinary review access; unknown preservation blocks destruction and raises an owned exception, not an unlimited Reveal. Do not claim physical deletion, provider recall or backups erased until independently proved. Provider-retained bytes cannot become a rehydration route. Before live activation, every retained class/copy must have an owner, finite normal horizon, hold behavior, recovery boundary and disposal evidence contract; an unqualified class blocks its activation rather than being silently omitted.

### D19-R21 — Honest clocks and downstream eligibility

Keep first qualified provider receipt, first durable platform acceptance, gate/readiness transitions, review decision, canonical Support-ready admission and processing outcome separately. D14 reply-target time starts at its qualified source-ready event; queue age remains visible so a review delay cannot be concealed. Do not backdate readiness to provider receipt or reset original age to release time. D13 automatic request confirmation remains eligible only under its original rules and strictly before qualified first provider receipt plus 15 minutes; review never starts a new utility window, clears automation/loop suppression or creates a second confirmation. Reviewing, dismissing or admitting input is not a human reply, resolution, satisfied business action or achieved target.

### D19-R22 — CRM continuity and minimum necessary context

Held input is not an ordinary CRM conversation/communication row and must not update last-contact, create or merge a Party, establish a related-record association, assert verified identity, or perform a CRM mutation automatically, register a user, change consent or verify an address. Authorized read-only identity lookup follows the existing owner semantics; a candidate never establishes person identity, representation or a related-record association. D9-R02 correspondence eligibility still requires genuinely admitted source-message evidence and is not created by a held-item lookup. An eligible reviewer may open already-authorized CRM context when necessary through the normal owner surface; recognizing a correspondent is not permission to expose their giving, location or care data. On actual admission, D1/D2 identity distinctions and D9 qualified source-backed CRM discovery apply exactly once. Requester, message participant, authenticated user, CRM Party/relationship, assigned worker, reviewer and CRM owner remain distinct. CRM merge/deletion/link changes never rewrite original evidence or trigger re-ingestion. No synchronization between two Asym-owned CRM copies is introduced. Actual financial, receipt, recurring-gift, contact or care actions retain their domain permissions, approvals and truthful histories.

### D19-R23 — Email Studio, Resend and attention

Review UI is not an email composer. Keep D18's ratified Email Studio ownership of reusable content and prepared human replies, and D13's separate qualified **Support request received** template. Intake Release/Dismiss/Retry use neither a canned reply nor an ad hoc email template. Resend remains the qualified receiving provider; tenant/provider authority must be proven before fetch/replay, and Phase 6 remains the only external dispatch/evidence owner. Raw provider URLs and connection secrets stay server-side.

Use the shared in-product attention capability for coverage failure, aged unresolved review and expiring unresolved content; qualify a bounded source meaning and the existing source-actionable presentation policy before enabling it. Group by tenant/inbox/condition and reauthorize at display. Read/archive is not source completion. Default reviewer attention is in-product with no per-message email, external sender notification, push/body digest or dedicated notification engine. Any later authorized email channel must use an exact Email Studio meaning/template plus P6/tenant Resend; it cannot be assembled by a queue worker or extend source lifetime.

### D19-R24 — Coverage, aging and reporting

Show live authorized backlog by reason and oldest received age, plus distinct Processing issues, coverage gaps and approaching expiry. Default ordinary reviewer attention is due after **24 elapsed hours**, including weekends, from the once-set first routine-reviewable admission; also surface inputs already older than 24 hours at that point. This is an internal operational threshold, not a donor promise, business-hours SLA or D14 reply target. Before expiry, surface unresolved ordinary content with **48 elapsed hours or less remaining**; already-shorter schedules surface immediately. Changing responsible inbox, reviewer, reason or returning from dismissal does not reset that first-reviewable time. Do not send repeated alerts per item. One grouped active condition stays actionable until resolved; no per-user leaderboard.

Report received occurrences, business-reviewable occurrences, released admissions, dismissed decisions, expired-before-admission, corrections, unresolved age and technical delay as distinct units. A release request is not released. Show cohort, timestamps, scope, unknowns and excluded classes. “Released after review” is an operational outcome, not a measured false-positive rate; actual correctness needs qualified sampling evidence. Do not count hidden records in unauthorized totals or erase previous delays/misses when responsibility or policy changes. Use current actionable owner for recovery and immutable original attribution for historical evidence.

### D19-R25 — Reuse qualified capabilities and retire conflicting paths

Extend the canonical intake/source workflow, guarded API boundary, existing team/permission system, source-backed CRM read model, Email Studio/P6 and records/asset owners. Requalify rather than blindly reuse current retry, route-resume, first-match threading, audit or broad staff endpoints. Preserve actual provider evidence and legacy input identity while consolidating the older parallel Support path. The existing future-route command cannot be relabeled single-message release. Leave Phase 34 as the sole configurable rule-engine owner. No queue-specific CRM, new sender registry, local template/compiler, custom security scanner or generic review/approval platform is introduced.

### D19-R26 — Bounded performance and abuse resilience

Use tenant/review-scope/state/deadline indexes and stable cursor pagination; page size defaults to 25 with a hard maximum of 50. Fetch body/asset details only on authorized inspection. Counts are bounded projections with stated freshness and current authorization; never label an incomplete count zero. Separate attachment retrieval and cleanup into bounded owner work with tenant fairness, durable retry and rate-limit-aware backoff. Review responsibility does not bypass intake abuse controls or trigger one notification per hostile email. The inherited workflow's current retry/concurrency numbers are code facts, not demonstrated D19 capacity. Before release, record the tested tenant/queue/attachment/burst/skew profile, concurrency and measurable latency/error/backlog limits, then require capacity to complete work within active content/review windows; no unsupported scale promise is ratified.

### D19-R27 — Safe migration and activation

Ship additive source identities/constraints/decision history before activating new readers or writers. Reconcile legacy tenant/reference/actor/timestamp gaps; do not guess values, backfill reviewer approval, generate duplicate messages, restart old clocks or retroactively purge unknown mail. Invalid legacy items remain in an owned bounded reconciliation class with restricted access. Stop conflicting old writers before new authority is enabled. Activate per tenant/inbox only after qualified coverage, copy schedules, source/asset safety, provider contract, authorization and proof gates pass. Use a write kill switch and separate read/cleanup controls; disabling dispatch never removes restriction/expiry enforcement. Rollback preserves new decisions, tombstones and negative controls; old binaries unable to honor them cannot resume writes. Prefer forward repair after irreversible effects.

### D19-R28 — Operational ownership and recovery

The controls O01–O08 below are required operational contracts after release gates pass. Source owners handle business correction; platform owners handle transport/storage/durable-work failures; security/records owners handle restricted preservation and disposal. Provide scoped retry/reconciliation and receipts in normal tools, not recurring direct SQL repair. An alert is resolved by correcting its source condition, not marking a notification read. For irreversibly missed intake, the source-owned operational review records a typed cause and remedial action or explicit no-further-action conclusion under oversight authority; that completes the review obligation without changing the original expiry/miss or rehydrating content. A deliberately dismissed item later expiring has no missed-business-review obligation. Incidents preserve permitted evidence and publish an accurate status to affected qualified staff without copying sensitive content.

### D19-R29 — Proof and traceability

Preserve the founder selection, all required amendments, considered alternatives, evidence classifications and limits in the grill record, proposed feature ADR and glossary. Map each clause into the later explicitly authorized specification/design/tasks/tickets and test/release evidence; do not create those implementation authorities during this session. P01–P42 are required user-visible/domain proof groups, not tests executed by this review. Documentation consistency and read-only source observations are separately reported. No “complete” claim may convert source inspection, a mocked screen, current broad grants or a vendor screenshot into actual tenant/runtime safety proof.

### D19-R30 — Deliberately limited scope

The complete launch journey is configure coverage, inspect, decide, correct, recover and oversee. It does not add a requester quarantine portal, automatic trust learning, AI release decisions, reply-from-quarantine, mass allowlists, mandatory two-person review, routine legal approval, mailbox synchronization, enterprise security console or another workflow platform. A person may receive review authority and remain unable to read a restricted conversation or CRM record. International names/addresses remain intact; translated labels and exact local dates support comprehension without transforming identifier equality or pausing server clocks.

## Full adversarial category review

Severity describes the plausible impact without the amendment. Likelihood is qualitative engineering judgment about implementation/operation exposure, not measured ministry incidence. The evidence register distinguishes inspected facts, external documentation, inference and product judgment. Each finding changes or narrows A as specified; none requires abandoning designated responsibility.

### 1. Problem validity, necessity, and alternatives

**Material concern: Yes. Severity: Medium; likelihood: High.** A new review role or admin-only queue could duplicate existing capabilities and leave retained legitimate mail unowned. The roadmap/D1 establishes recoverability, but no ministry staffing study proves separate teams are universally needed. Central qualified review is the strongest alternative and is retained as a staffing configuration. **Effect:** narrows A to per-inbox accountability over shared capabilities. **Permanent fix/exact language:** R01–R04/R30; one eligible pool, real coverage and oversight, no compulsory hierarchy. **Proof:** P01–P04.

### 2. Brittleness

**Material concern: Yes. Severity: High; likelihood: High.** Hardcoded admin roles, remembered team membership or a last-updated timer fail after staff leave, security scope changes or a worker arrives late. Existing broad wrappers do not implement D19 scope. **Effect:** changes eligibility from static assignment to current effective authority. **Permanent fix/exact language:** R03/R04/R17/R19; dynamic coverage and source fences. **Proof:** P03/P04/P19/P25.

### 3. Technical debt

**Material concern: Yes. Severity: High; likelihood: High.** Wrapping existing retry/route CRUD as review would preserve wrong command semantics, mutable audit and duplicate old Support paths. Source inspection demonstrates those mismatches. **Effect:** changes implementation reuse conditions, not product choice. **Permanent fix/exact language:** R13–R16/R25/R27; one canonical boundary, append-only evidence, retirement of conflicting writes. **Proof:** P13–P18/P36/P37.

### 4. Edge cases

**Material concern: Yes. Severity: High; likelihood: High.** Attachment-only input, shared addresses, forwarded questions, multiple recipients, unknown original time, duplicate callbacks and multiple gates can be lost or misrouted. Current empty-body and fallback-time behavior is insufficient; no invented ministry frequency is assumed. **Effect:** narrows what one reviewer may release. **Permanent fix/exact language:** R02/R07/R09/R12/R19/R21/R22. **Proof:** P05/P09/P10/P12/P21/P28/P29.

### 5. Footguns

**Material concern: Yes. Severity: High; likelihood: High.** A seemingly harmless Not spam button can change future sender policy or CRM data; competitor documentation confirms such coupling. A mass release or false Undo can expose content or erase evidence. **Effect:** changes action vocabulary and scope. **Permanent fix/exact language:** R08–R11/R18; single release, recoverable dismissal, separate policy and truthful correction. **Proof:** P07/P08/P11/P20/P31.

### 6. Tenant safety

**Material concern: Yes. Severity: Critical; likelihood: Medium.** Guessing tenant from message content, cross-tenant references, background service bypass or global counts can leak one ministry's mail into another. Some review references lack structural tenant equality. **Effect:** required boundary amendment. **Permanent fix/exact language:** R02/R05/R14/R15/R22; bound scope, composite references, explicit unbound recovery and source-authorized metadata. **Proof:** P02/P06/P14–P17/P30.

### 7. Database, RLS, and authorization safety

**Material concern: Yes. Severity: Critical; likelihood: High.** Review IDs, current broad role wrappers and caller/actor assumptions could authorize invalid state changes; current audit grants are mutable and auth user/profile identity differs. Server-only intake tables are a sound grant pattern and are not falsely accused of missing browser policies. **Effect:** requires exact mutation/grant qualification. **Permanent fix/exact language:** R13–R17; tenant-aware constraints, trusted actual profile, current old/new predicates and protected audit. **Proof:** P13–P19.

### 8. Overengineering

**Material concern: Yes. Severity: Medium; likelihood: High.** A new approval engine, rotating on-call system, AI gatekeeper or universal email security console would expand this narrow problem. Vendors do not establish necessity for Asym. **Effect:** narrows scope. **Permanent fix/exact language:** R01/R11/R23/R25/R30; shared capabilities, finite simple UI, coded policies. **Proof:** P01/P32/P42.

### 9. UX/UI and user friction

**Material concern: Yes. Severity: High; likelihood: High.** Calling all held inputs Spam, hiding expiry or moving users through repeated dialogs encourages mistakes and uncertainty. Hover-only meaning, color-only warning and remote-content previews fail safety/accessibility. **Effect:** adds complete specified journeys. **Permanent fix/exact language:** R05–R12/R18/R24 and linked UX; quiet reason-aware queue, exact action scope, safe preview and persistent recovery. **Proof:** P06–P12/P20/P38–P40.

### 10. Source of truth, ownership, and domain invariants
