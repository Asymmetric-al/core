## Strongest primary comparisons

**Help Scout** explicitly searches subject, body, notes and attachment filenames. Its article was updated 13 November 2025 and checked in this research on 12 September 2026. This is a directly comparable A-like scope. Adapt conversation coverage, not its global customer/Docs tabs, owner-wide visibility or query-bearing bookmark URLs. Exact plan entitlement is not established by this page. [Search in Help Scout](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/771-search-in-help-scout).

**HubSpot Service Hub Professional/Enterprise**, documentation updated 13 February 2026, searches message/comment text and provides Message/Note refinements with a matching-source preview. This supports helping staff understand why a result appeared. Its associated-contact fields require separate Core field authority; its globally shared column settings do not override D20 personal preferences. The article does not establish attachment indexing. [Help-desk search](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/help-desk/search-for-tickets-in-help-desk).

**Front**, documentation edited 31 July 2026, demonstrates the strongest broader alternative: text inside PDF/CSV/XLS/XLSX/DOCX/TXT email attachments, with documented channel limits. Its source filters, visible inbox scope and separate Find-in-conversation mechanism are useful. C is therefore neither speculative nor equivalent to OCR/AI; exact Asym file formats and qualified extraction would be selected in the answer review. No Front number, format list or proprietary grammar is imported automatically. [Search coverage](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2131), [Find within a conversation](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2415).

**Zendesk** documents subject/comment retrieval, permission-aware results, indexing delay and explicit result/comment limits. These are warnings against describing capped or delayed coverage as a complete zero. Its first 1,000-result/first 500-comment and other published limits are vendor behavior, not Asym acceptance thresholds. Searchable archives do not override D17 expiry. [Searching Support data](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408894221594-Searching-Zendesk-Support-data), [search reference](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408886879258-Zendesk-Support-search-reference).

**Intercom's original and React Inbox documentation differ.** Current-view versus wider scope and visible filters are useful; exact syntax/seat and English-optimized matching descriptions must retain their version qualification. They do not establish universal attachment/internal-note coverage. [Original Inbox](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/6516006-inbox-search-and-filter), [React Inbox](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/16393491-search-and-filter-conversations-in-the-react-inbox).

**Kustomer** indexed official documentation supplies Message Body predicates and separate object-search scopes, but direct extraction was empty. It supports a qualified comparison, not claims about exact plan, index refresh cadence, attachment coverage or undocumented architecture. The [independent vendor report](phase26-q22-vendor-research.md) records that limit. Zoho's general module-search scope evidence is weaker for this exact indexing choice and is not forced into the decision.

These sources demonstrate practical mechanisms. They do not establish measured ministry search frequency, a universal best UX, comparative performance or production readiness in Core. Filename scope is an explicit proposed Asym choice supported by Help Scout, not a feature attributed to every vendor.

## Recommendation and independent challenge

The [gap review](phase26-q22-gap-review.md) confirms this decision is unresolved, preserves D20/D21 and checks the Phase29/40 boundaries. The [refreshed search research](phase26-q22-vendor-research.md) independently compares the three choices and scope/match-navigation/retention implications.

Recommend **A — Conversation text and attachment filenames**. It addresses remembering what was actually said, without requiring staff to predict ideal subjects or labels. **B — Details only** remains the strongest smaller alternative where reference-based lookup normally suffices. **C — Also supported attachment contents** is strongest when key retrieval clues are routinely document-only. No representative Asym workload, measured extraction-readiness or query benchmark was found that makes C's additional source processing a demonstrated baseline requirement. That absence supports A as a product judgment, not a claim C is inherently overengineering.

The question deliberately does not freeze tokenizer, exact phrase operators, matching/ranking weights, snippet length, parsing library, engine, quota, supported file list, processing SLA or global search architecture. Those are qualified in the selected-answer review. Neither A nor C may index a restricted source merely because the actor can see its conversation shell. Current note/body/attachment metadata authority must precede every visible hit, count, ranking contribution and excerpt.

The [D21 Email Studio addendum](phase26-d21-email-studio-integration.md) remains accepted: ordinary source/query metadata has no publication or delivery effect. Searching admitted conversation history is distinct from searching reusable library content, private drafts or prepared artifacts. A later authorized reply follows its usual P17→Support→P6/tenant Resend path without rerendering or sending anything merely because search ran.
