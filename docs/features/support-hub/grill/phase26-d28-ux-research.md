# D28 — Sampled requester feedback: research and UX recommendations

**13 September 2026. Founder selected A, with Off and an adjustable random invitation percentage. This is an independent research and grooming recommendation; detailed amendments await the root's synthesis and founder ratification.** No product, provider, DNS, GitHub, canonical specification or real message was changed.

**Recommendation: accept with required amendments.** Provide one optional automatic feedback policy, Off until deliberately enabled. The root synthesis adopts a **25% starting invitation rate**, a **24-hour wait after genuinely finished human-handled work**, and a **30-day minimum gap between feedback invitations to the same recipient email address within the tenant**. Keep the first release's editable controls to Off/On and the percentage; explain the timing and repeat-contact protection in the same panel. These are proportionate proposed Asym defaults, not universal help-desk standards or legal safe harbors. The root also adopts a send deadline 24 hours after the due time, a frozen 14-day action lifetime from issuance/preparation rather than delivery, the existing P17 D6 doorway, and one final logical response. Those integrations are reflected below; they remain amendments pending founder ratification.

The percentage reduces invitation volume; the gap protects repeat correspondents. Neither produces a representative sample of all people served. Random selection must not be replaced by every fourth ticket, an agent-selected subset or a new draw on every retry.

## Complete administrator journey

1. Open **Support Hub → Settings → Requester feedback** through the normal tenant settings shell. A qualified policy administrator sees Off and a concise explanation: “Invite a random share of eligible completed conversations to give optional feedback.” Support readers without policy authority may see the effective status where useful, but cannot enable or change it. Inbox assignment, CRM ownership and Guidance publication grant no such permission.
2. Select **Set up feedback**. Use one compact page, not an enterprise survey builder. It contains On/Off, **Invitation rate** with a labelled numeric percent input, the effective inbox coverage established by the domain contract, and one sentence explaining the fixed delay/gap. A small menu of common values can supplement the number field; never make a slider the only accessible input.
3. At 25%, show: “Randomly select about 25 of every 100 eligible conversations. Actual invitations may be fewer because of contact limits and delivery eligibility.” Add “The same email address receives no more than one feedback invitation from your organization in 30 days.” Do not say “25% of donors”, “every fourth request” or “25 guaranteed invitations”. Explain that an invitation delayed more than 24 hours beyond its due time is skipped, so an outage does not produce stale catch-up mail. Show no guessed recent volume. If a genuine body-free estimate exists, label its exact date range, denominator and limitations.
4. Show a concise **How invitations work** summary: qualified human-handled work has finished; wait 24 hours; one selection per original opportunity; suppress unsuitable recipients/repeated contact; participation optional; no reminders. No agent selection based on whether the requester seems happy. Manual-only work without a qualified email exchange does not gain an address or contact record just to qualify.
5. Provide **Preview invitation** and **Preview feedback page** with desktop/mobile widths and the actual qualified tenant public identity, From/Reply-to material and supported locale. P17 supplies governed invitation presentation; the selected scale, safety text and action meaning cannot become arbitrary template variables. A preview is visibly a preview and cannot issue a live token, send mail, consume the cooldown, submit a rating or enter analytics. Production test mail, if later qualified, remains a separately reviewed P17 test effect; no new ad hoc test-send path is created here.
6. **Enable feedback** performs one current-authority save/admission. The final summary shows the concrete rate and that it applies only to newly eligible work. “Feedback is on. New eligible conversations use a 25% invitation rate.” If the response is lost, reconcile the same save, do not display success from client state or start another operation.
7. Later **Edit settings** retains the current published policy while edits are local. **Save changes** shows effective future scope. Percentage edits apply to newly created completion candidates; each candidate freezes its rate at closure, before any draw. The stored draw and sampled historical percentage for prior opportunities do not change; no reroll, retroactive flood or hidden catch-up happens when increasing the percentage or turning back On.
8. **Turn off feedback** stops new and not-yet-submitted invitations. Pending work must obey the current Off gate, while a provider-accepted or ambiguous submission is reconciled honestly. Copy: “New invitations are off. Already-sent feedback links remain available until they expire.” A separate safety restriction may revoke public actions; ordinary Off must not deceptively promise recall or delete collected feedback.
9. Provide a quiet **Activity** disclosure for policy changes and invitation problems, current actor/time/version and body-free counts. An administrator can understand why sending stopped without searching application logs or editing labels. Configuration failure does not stop the underlying Support inbox or ordinary reply path.

One tenant policy avoids per-agent percentages and inconsistent competing rates. The root selects qualified inbox participation through explicit reviewed collection scope, never a free-form exclusion builder or ACL substitute; record scope changes so reports do not imply continuous comparable coverage.

## Staff and requester journeys

### Staff finishes work

The existing D4/D5 action label and sending effects remain intact. A local ending action does not itself send a survey; a separate enabled policy may later identify eligible ordinary Resolved work using neutral actual-handling evidence. The root's defined population excludes No response endings and must report that coverage limitation; it does not call absence of a reply successful help. Do not add a survey checkbox to every reply, a Give feedback variable to D18, or a permanent D23 signature link. The agent cannot reroll or force a selected happy case into the sample.

In the canonical Support detail, an unobtrusive feedback event/status can show **Scheduled**, **Not selected**, **Skipped — contacted recently**, **Submission being checked**, **Sent**, **Received** or **Expired**, according to the qualified lifecycle. Do not render every skipped event as an alarming timeline message. Details belong behind a quiet disclosure. Eligibility exclusions and identities must respect the viewer's current permissions; do not expose other private inbox activity to explain a tenant-wide cooldown.

At ordinary eligible closure, create only a completion candidate with its frozen policy/rate and due time. Draw only after 24 elapsed hours when it matures and still qualifies. A reopen before sampling invalidates that candidate; later genuine completion may create a new candidate and quiet interval because no original opportunity has yet been sampled. After sampling, invalidating the resolution generation before submission must cancel the definitely-unsubmitted effect without restoring it at reclose or creating a second draw; this exact terminal clarification is recorded in the independent final review. Possible submission remains an effect to reconcile, not a recall claim. Duplicate merge/Undo and policy edits do not produce new chances. A contact-limit suppression is not delayed mail held until day 31: the stale opportunity ends rather than becoming a later catch-up invitation.

### Requester gives feedback

1. The person receives one concise email with a clear tenant public identity, neutral question and **Give feedback** CTA. Avoid case title, quoted content, CRM/giving fields, staff personal photos, requesters/CCs, tracking pixels, social links, fundraising asks and promotional banners. The subject may be “How was the support you received?” The invite can be safely ignored and makes no claim of mandatory completion.
2. Opening the link first uses **the existing P17 D6 generic doorway**. The person presses **Continue** before entering the small responsive feedback form; no alternate survey-specific credential/session doorway bypasses that shared contract. The form shows approved organization identity and a short explanation that feedback is linked to the Support request and visible to authorized staff. It is not a public conversation view and is not described as anonymous. No login, repeated name/email fields, new CRM record, attachments or rich-text editor are needed. The credential authorizes only this feedback action; it does not verify the person or a represented organization.
3. Choose one labelled option, optionally type a comment, then press **Submit feedback**. Example comment label: **“Anything you'd like us to know? (Optional)”**. A short, proportionate instruction can discourage sensitive details without an alarmist disclaimer. Tiptap is appropriate for governed rich invitation authoring if P17's purpose allows it; the requester comment is plain text.
4. Before submission, every choice remains editable; state **“You can review your feedback before submitting.”** There is no automatic rating on click, hover, GET, link preview, Continue or scan. The Submit action saves one final logical response atomically and is idempotent. Identical-payload retries return the same receipt; a later changed payload conflicts. If the connection fails, preserve unsent input in the current page and permit a safe retry; do not say it was received until reconciled. Avoid retaining free text in long-lived browser storage or URLs.
5. The confirmation says **“Thank you. Your feedback has been received.”** No new email is sent. It must not promise a response unless a genuine reviewed owner obligation exists. Show a clear ordinary Support contact path for anyone who still needs help. That action starts/continues the existing authorized support journey; submitting a score alone does not reopen, create a task, refund money or send an agent reply.
6. A repeat or expired visit is truthful and does not expose the previously submitted comment or conversation. Example: **“Feedback has already been received for this invitation.”** / **“This feedback invitation has expired. You can still contact the team if you need help.”** The frozen expiry is 14 days from qualified issuance/preparation, never promised as 14 days after delivery. Current expiry or safety removal is enforced server-side even when an old page remains open. Do not let apparently successful controls discard late submissions silently. A later correction or privacy request follows the existing qualified Support/privacy path; no post-submission Change rating control is offered on this single-use action.

### Requester declines future invitations

Provide a separate plainly labelled **“Stop feedback invitations from [Organization]”** link in the invitation and feedback page. It need not require a rating, reason, account, new email or marketing preference change. Its scope is the actual receiving mailbox for this tenant: in a shared mailbox it affects further invitations to that mailbox, not a proven person's other addresses. The page explains this before the explicit stop action. Opening the link alone does not change preferences.

Confirmation must distinguish the purpose: **“Feedback invitations to this email address are off. This does not stop replies about your Support requests.”** The final preference owner must preserve other existing communication restrictions and cannot let a worker silently clear a requester opt-out, CRM merge or changed Party reset it, or a feedback opt-out accidentally suppress a needed human reply. The stop action needs its own usable lifetime and current authority; do not let expiry of the rating action strand the person's ability to stop future invitations. Applicable delivery/legal classification belongs to the qualified platform contract, not a claim that every nonprofit feedback email is transactional.

### Staff reviews what came back

Use **Support reporting → Requester feedback** as a narrow list/report, with conversation-level links and simple date, inbox and rating filters plus **With comments**. New feedback appears as a typed event in the same canonical Support detail, including when that detail is viewed through permitted CRM context. It is not a copied CRM activity body or contact-level sentiment score.

All ratings remain accessible according to source permissions. A focused review list can help staff read comments or dissatisfied responses without hiding other answers from reports. Recommend a minimal **Reviewed** marker with actor/time on the feedback occurrence, and a **Needs review** filter, so teams can distinguish examined feedback from an unattended queue. Marking reviewed never edits the score, deletes the comment or asserts that the underlying issue was resolved. Existing Support reopen/reply/assignment commands perform any genuine follow-up with their normal authority and visible effects. If another existing qualified review marker already satisfies this exact outcome, reuse it instead of adding parallel workflow state.

Notify through the existing permitted notification surface only where a real reviewer is responsible; avoid an email to every follower for every rating. A quiet badge and actionable review list are enough for ordinary feedback. A negative score should not automatically reopen resolved work, reassign it or create a promise to call. A relevant comment may reveal continuing work; a staff member can act after reading it. A person who only has CRM access does not gain access to comments, ratings or the original conversation through reporting. Assignee, last sender and current CRM owner are not reliable sole causal authors of a rating.

## Sampling and measurement clauses

**UX-01 — Actual unit.** “The configured percentage is the probability that an eligible original-conversation feedback opportunity is selected. It is not a quota, a percentage of unique people or a claim of representative feedback.” Use neutral base eligibility, a stable random draw once, then current recipient/cooldown and P17/P6 gates. Store the historical policy rate and qualification evidence. Display actual counts separately.

**UX-02 — No repeated draw.** “Repeated jobs, lost responses, reopen/reclose, merge/Undo, changing the rate or toggling the feature shall not create another draw or invitation for the same opportunity.” A fair bounded integer draw can implement whole-percent probability; the root data contract owns its exact range and comparison. Randomness is server controlled and independent of ticket sequence, handler, sentiment and caller inputs.

**UX-03 — Contact protection.** “Before a submission becomes externally possible, atomically reserve the tenant plus owner-normalized recipient endpoint. A concurrent invitation or uncertain provider outcome shall not permit another invitation during the protected interval.” Never guess Gmail alias equivalence, combine household/Party records or use a CRM person ID as proof of mailbox uniqueness. Retain the minimum restricted contact-control data for the actual purpose/window, not raw email in permanent analytics.

**UX-04 — Final checks.** “A selected opportunity is not a sending entitlement. Current Off state, reopened work, disallowed recipient, changed egress restrictions, cooldown, duplicate lineage, expired opportunity, content/action safety and provider qualification can suppress it.” No worker bypass or fallback recipient. Feedback suppression leaves human Support replies and other legitimate domains' actions unaffected.

**UX-05 — Optional participation.** “Feedback is voluntary and must not gate Support, donor services, missionary resources, identity access or any financial/business action. No response does not mean satisfaction. A rating does not establish consent for marketing, contact identity or a completed business action.” No incentives or better service tied to positive ratings.

**UX-06 — Honest denominators.** Report eligible opportunities, randomly selected opportunities, suppressed selections by permitted reason, submission-may-have-begun issued invitations, accepted submissions, known delivery failures, unresolved submissions and responses. For the same invitation cohort, show **responded issued invitations / issued invitations whose submission may have begun** with that exact unit explained; only invitations in the denominator can contribute to the numerator. A valid response can arrive while P6 still reports an indeterminate submission, so dividing all responses by accepted-only invitations would inflate the rate and can exceed 100%. Show accepted, unknown and delivery outcomes separately, and distinguish response-date browsing from invitation-cohort conversion. Separately show the selected/eligible fraction and qualitative distribution. Do not call responses / all completed work a response rate, or claim provider acceptance is reading or actual delivery.

**UX-07 — Time interpretation.** Invitation-cohort reporting and response-received reporting are different. Label which date controls a view; make later responses to an earlier cohort understandable. A changing sampling rate, collection scope or question version must not silently rewrite historical denominators or combine incomparable definitions. Cohorts still within their response window remain open to more responses.

**UX-08 — Small numbers.** Always show counts beside percentages. Zero responses is **No feedback yet**, not zero satisfaction. One satisfied response can be “1 satisfied response” rather than an unqualified 100% headline. Do not invent a universal `n=30` reliability rule, promise margins of error, infer all-requester sentiment from a voluntary subset, or rank employees from this result. Operational timing and ordinary conversations remain complementary evidence.

## Disciplined completion and monitoring

Before recording the amended decision, the root must finish documenting the exact neutral eligibility and opportunity definition, recipient authority/cardinality, policy scope, stable draw, final cooldown reservation and qualified opt-out owner. These are product/domain semantics, not details to leave to each implementer. The root has now selected the numbers, deadline and one-response correction/expiry integration described above; record them honestly as proposed product defaults with tradeoffs, alongside required proof.
