# Q23: Signatures on human Support replies

**Historical Q23 evidence: the founder selected and fully ratified A, Tiptap and all D23 amendments on 12 September 2026.** Earlier unanswered/no-ADR language below records the question stage only. [Complete ratified D23](phase26-d23-adversarial-review.md) and [Email Studio seam](phase26-d23-email-studio-integration.md) govern current acceptance.

**Q23 is unanswered. D1–D22 and every adopted amendment are fully founder-ratified.** This question concerns the ownership and default presentation of the short signature on a human external reply. It does not reopen sender identity, sending authority, recipient review, internal notes, reusable replies or automated acknowledgements.

## The decision in everyday use

When a staff member replies from a shared inbox, should the closing signature be managed for that inbox, identical for the whole team, or managed individually by each staff member?

Illustrative example, not an observed ministry workflow: Maya answers a donor's question in Donor Care while Jordan is the assigned coordinator. A personalized signature should identify Maya as the responder, using her approved public name and the team's approved contact details. It must not identify Jordan merely because Jordan is assigned, or expose Maya's login email or private phone number. Replying still returns to the already-qualified tenant inbox.

| Option                                                          | What the staff member and recipient experience                                                                                                                                                 | Strength                                                                                                        | Tradeoff                                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Inbox-managed signatures with responder personalization** | The inbox uses one governed signature presentation, normally showing the actual responder's approved public name plus consistent team details. It appears automatically in the reviewed reply. | Personal, consistent and almost no routine setup or choice for staff. One shared correction updates future use. | Requires a trustworthy public-name source and explicit team-only fallback; staff cannot freely redesign the shared block.                                           |
| **B — One team-only signature per inbox**                       | Every reply ends with the same team identity, such as Donor Care, without automatically identifying the individual responder.                                                                  | The simplest administration; suits intentionally team-voiced or privacy-sensitive communication.                | Less personal continuity in the email itself; a staff member may need to introduce themselves in the actual answer. Internal actor attribution still remains exact. |
| **C — Staff-managed signatures within the shared presentation** | Each staff member maintains their own closing signature, optionally with inbox-specific variants; the common Email Studio frame still applies.                                                 | Most flexibility for distinct roles, languages and individual professional identity.                            | More onboarding, stale contact information, duplicated variants and review burden; a suitable signature must be resolved when staff cover another inbox.            |

These are alternative product choices, not three configuration modes to implement. Under A, a deliberate team-only presentation for an inbox is a bounded exception within the same managed model, not a second personal-signature library. B would make team-only the universal product approach; C would delegate signature-body authoring to individuals.

## Single recommendation: A

Recommend **inbox-managed signatures with responder personalization**. The shared inbox already provides continuity while people cover one another. One managed presentation can keep team contact information consistent while making ordinary replies feel human. It fits D4/P17's existing presentation boundary without creating a separate signature system. Help Scout documents an inbox-owned signature personalized with user variables; HubSpot's help desk also supports team signatures with sender tokens. These are verified product patterns, not proof of higher Asym satisfaction. [Help Scout](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/547-create-and-manage-mailbox-signatures), [HubSpot Service Hub](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/help-desk/connect-channels-to-help-desk).

B is the strongest simpler alternative when the tenant intentionally wants correspondence to come from a team. C is a legitimate model in products such as Zoho Desk and Zendesk, but there is no demonstrated Asym need yet for an individual signature library with per-inbox variants. Start with the shared model and an approved public responder name. Do not auto-extract a first name, force a Western name structure, copy a legal name or assume every ministry permits identifying staff externally. [Zoho Desk](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/desk/faqs/personal-settings/articles/faqs-personal-settings), [Zendesk](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/8713325400986-Adding-your-agent-signature-to-ticket-comments-and-email-notifications).

## The proposed A journey to pressure-test after selection

1. An authorized person reaches **Support settings → Inboxes → [Inbox] → Reply signature**. The page explains that this changes human reply presentation. It shows the effective published presentation and a concise preview. Selection/configuration uses Support authority; reusable authoring and publication use Email Studio authority. Having one capability does not grant the other. A contextual Edit in Email Studio link preserves return context and identifies the inbox consumer; there is one underlying presentation owner.
2. A normal responder writes a reply and sees the actual signature inline below their text. There is no signature picker on every reply, no hidden append at Send and no mandatory extra preview modal. A small labelled Signature details control can explain the managed block and resolve a problem without filling the composer with settings. Full-email preview remains available through D4.
3. The recommended starter is restrained text: approved public responder name, inbox/team name and only useful approved team contact information. No default private employee email/phone, headshot, location, social-icon strip, promotion or long disclaimer. Images, if qualified, cannot carry essential identity alone. Long names, RTL, narrow screens, zoom and real plain text must work. This is a product judgment supported by Help Scout's documented image-blocking caveat and W3C's guidance on clear labels and reflow. [Help Scout](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/547-create-and-manage-mailbox-signatures), [W3C labels](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/labels-or-instructions.html), [W3C reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html).
4. A deliberately configured team-only presentation is available where staff identity must not be disclosed. Missing public-name data uses only an explicitly permitted team fallback visible before approval; it never falls back to a login email, someone else's identity or an arbitrary CRM field. Exact capability/source qualification and exceptional removal behavior belong in the selected answer's review, not in a hidden implementation assumption.
5. Changing the actual responder, inbox, presentation or public-name dependency before approval visibly refreshes the affected draft under D4. An inbox reassignment alone never impersonates its assignee. Once prepared/admitted, the approved content and dependencies remain fixed under D4; retries do not rerender. A later ordinary name/presentation edit does not rewrite sent history. Actual revocation/safety fences retain their existing no-send/recovery meaning.

## Owner boundaries that every option must preserve

Email Studio/Phase17 owns qualified reusable presentation, structured authoring, preview and immutable preparation. Support owns which eligible presentation an inbox consumes and its live conversation/draft/recipient/actor admission. Existing IAM/profile ownership supplies any approved public identity; Support must not create a parallel employee or CRM identity. Actual source availability is unproven until implementation qualification.

Phase6 continues sending prepared HTML and plain text using the tenant's qualified Resend connection. A signature is visible message content, not a From/Reply-To setting, authentication proof, digital signature or authorization token. Front's signature feature also exposes sender-header configuration; copying that coupling would conflict with Core's separate qualified Sender/Reply Identity. [Front](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2263).

Human replies do not become system-template catalog entries. D13 automated confirmations remain their own Email Studio template and never claim a human responder simply by using a signature token. D18 reusable fragments must not append another managed signature; Notes remain local and carry internal actor attribution rather than an external email footer. No CRM creation, profile mutation, contact-history write, follow, target completion or business action follows from configuring or displaying a signature.

This question authorizes no new Support-owned signature compiler, arbitrary HTML store, mandatory per-reply approval workflow, generic signature marketplace or expanded message channel. It does not change existing draft, redaction/retention, restore, concurrency or audience safeguards.
