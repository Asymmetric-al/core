# Q33 — Reading repeated email history

**Unanswered — 13 September 2026.** D1–D32, including every amendment and final correction, are fully founder-ratified. This is the next single researched product choice; no D33 answer, ADR or implementation is created.

## The next question

**When older quoted emails and signatures make a Support conversation long, what should staff see by default?**

Consider an ongoing donor-care exchange: each reply includes several earlier messages and sign-offs. Staff need to find the current request, but someone may have answered a question inside an older quoted paragraph. This is an illustrative use case, not a claim about measured ministry email volume or formatting.

| Option                                                    | Experience                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Strongest reason to choose it                                                                                                            | Tradeoff                                                                                                                                     |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Compact reading with easy expansion — recommended** | Fold conservatively identified repeated history/signatures behind a clear label. Keep new, changed or uncertain material visible. Expand a section in place or deliberately switch this conversation to full reading. | Less repetitive scrolling with no setup. One consistent default, with the detail still available.                                        | Recognition and expansion must be reliable; people who routinely inspect everything may expand often.                                        |
| **B — Full messages by default**                          | Show each currently permitted safe message body, including quoted history and signatures, with ordinary bounded loading. No automatic folding of repeated regions.                                                    | Most predictable visibility and the smallest interpretation layer. Particularly useful when unusual or inline-edited replies are common. | Long repeated chains take more scrolling and space, especially on a phone. Full display still needs safe rendering and efficient navigation. |
| **C — A personal Compact or Full default**                | Provide A's conservative compact view and B's full view, then let each staff member save their preferred default. Temporary changes remain possible.                                                                  | Accommodates staff who repeatedly prefer different reading density.                                                                      | Adds a setting, persistence/reset behavior and preference scope to maintain. It retains all of A's recognition work.                         |

These are alternative product scopes, not three tenant modes to build automatically. “Full” always means the currently permitted safe message presentation; it does not mean raw transport source or a route around removed/expired content. None changes chronological order, the composer layout, outgoing quoting or message retention.

## Single best recommendation

**A — Compact reading with easy expansion.** It addresses ordinary reading friction without requiring personal configuration. B is the strongest simpler alternative because it avoids interpreting repeated regions; its cost is repeated scrolling. C is justified if enduring personal preference differences are important enough to add another maintained setting, but current Asym evidence does not establish that need.

The crucial qualification is conservative folding, not an assumption that every quotation block is old text. Zendesk documents that an answer inserted inside a quoted message can appear behind its expansion control. This is a concrete failure pattern to prevent, not a reason to remove the content. [Zendesk delimiter and inline-reply documentation](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408821456922-Adding-and-removing-the-delimiter-from-email-notifications).

Front documents a conversation-level Expand all action because expanding messages one at a time adds reading effort. Adopt the low-effort full-reading escape; its whole-message control is not evidence of a particular quote detector or default. [Front: Expand all messages](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2416).

## Recommended A journey to pressure-test after selection

1. **Open the same Support conversation**, directly or from an authorized CRM record. Keep the existing order, draft, audience, message identity, timestamps and delivery state. Do not open another inbox or create a CRM copy.
2. **Read immediately.** Ordinary content remains visible. Only qualified repetition folds; a quotation marker alone is insufficient. If safe classification fails or the text may contain a new inline answer, meaningful changed contact detail or first/only forwarded context, show that material. A less compact view is preferable to an absent answer.
3. **Inspect a folded section in place.** A text label such as **Show quoted history** explains the disclosure; a bare ellipsis is insufficient. The same current authorized safe source expands without moving the draft or opening raw email HTML. The control exposes its expanded state and works with keyboard and touch. This follows the disclosure interaction described by [W3C APG](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/), not a new widget system.
4. **Read the whole exchange when needed.** A clear conversation-scoped full-reading action avoids opening many folded sections individually. “Full” describes a reading mode across eligible content, not a promise to mount the whole history at once or fetch every attachment. Preserve bounded loading and show actual loading/failure states.
5. **Find a detail without hunting.** Existing D22 search/Find still includes eligible quoted text. Navigation opens the exact permitted matching region, including a folded or not-yet-mounted region. A match cannot remain invisibly folded, and expired or redacted text never reappears through old snippets.
6. **Continue the work.** D32 file Preview/Download stays separate and deliberate; its qualified attachment controls remain findable. Expansion does not change reply content, recipient lists, current managed signature, work status or another person's view. Return from CRM/file preview preserves place, focus and the actual unsent TipTap draft.

B starts at the full-reading presentation without automatic repeated-region folding. C adds a personal default choice and predictable save/reset behavior to the same safe views. No exact detector, folding threshold, initial open-message count, keyboard shortcut, preference storage schema or pagination algorithm is selected by this question.

## Accepted boundaries that apply to every option

- **One source:** originals and current restrictions remain authoritative. Folding is a reversible display choice, not deletion, source correction, retention extension or a second message body that other systems can treat as authoritative.
- **Current access everywhere:** tenant/source permissions apply before metadata, text, expansion, search, delayed responses and any cached projection. Visually collapsed text is different from authorization-hidden content. No count, heading or fragment may disclose an inaccessible source.
- **Search continuity:** D22 matching and exact-source navigation remain intact. Display folding cannot exclude eligible text or join words across authorization gaps. Browser DOM-only Find does not replace the already-ratified complete conversation Find.
- **Email Studio:** P17 retains governed outbound wording/signature/preparation; P6 retains delivery. Reading produces no new preparation/send, template change, recipient change, outbound delimiter or system-message family. It cannot change what gets quoted in an outgoing reply. TipTap remains the qualified authoring tool; reading history uses its qualified static presentation contract, not one editor per message.
- **CRM:** the same Support source appears through authorized CRM continuity. Linked identity or Support assignment does not grant new CRM, giving, missionary or care access. Reading neither changes a CRM record nor establishes that an underlying business action completed.
- **Accessibility and stability:** visible controls, meaningful labels, expanded state, stable focus/order, mobile/text zoom, mixed RTL/LTR and long international text. No hover-only recovery, forced motion or unexplained nested controls. A loading or rendering failure cannot masquerade as an empty message.
- **Scope:** no AI summary, new AI/search corpus, editor, original-email export, archive, generic preference engine, message splitting, retrospective content rewrite or new requester surface.

The older roadmap's phrase **quoted-reply stripping** must be reconciled as non-destructive presentation/derived processing under the accepted source rules. It cannot authorize removal of searchable current source content. The current source renders the chosen HTML/text body through a shared viewer; it does not establish that all historic provider/import paths preserve the complete body. These are source and implementation gaps to prove, not safety options to vote on. [Scope review](phase26-q33-scope-review.md).
