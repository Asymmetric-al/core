## UX evidence and intended adaptation

Intercom and Zoho make the proposed recipient of a handoff explicit in the removal journey. Front and Zendesk document assignment/history effects that would otherwise surprise staff. Asym should combine a compact authorized impact summary, an optional deliberate handoff and a persistent result in its existing owner flow. These are design judgments drawn from documented interactions, not observed vendor internal architecture or measured Asym usability.

W3C's [Understanding WCAG 2.2 SC 4.1.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html) explains how status messages can be conveyed to assistive technology without moving focus, and cautions that excessive live announcements can become disruptive. Apply that to meaningful completion/error/progress updates; do not announce every background row. WCAG guidance does not by itself validate the proposed screen's accessibility or aesthetics.

The clean presentation should distinguish three facts: access is revoked; handoff processing may still be incomplete; current work still has its own Open/Waiting meaning. Routine shared work belongs in the normal inbox. Only actual unresolved handoffs need an additional attention state. Keep both Waiting categories discoverable, meaningful counts permission-aware, and exceptions visible beyond transient toasts. Do not expose a departure reason or send an unsolicited donor notice.

The Zendesk reassignment FAQ is evidence of a documented potential surprise, not a prevalence study. No attributable independent report found for this exact choice establishes Asym demand or proves one default improves satisfaction. Representative staff/admin, keyboard, screen-reader and narrow-screen tasks remain necessary once the interaction is designed.

## Independent challenge and synthesis

Three independent reviews covered actual Core/DB/authorization seams, vendor/UX behavior, and whether this is the right next unresolved decision. They converged on A but materially narrowed the question and alternatives:

1. Use **confirmed inability to handle the affected work**, not departure alone, an inactive flag, unknown permission lookup, Receive Off or a removed automatic candidate.
2. Include **all unfinished work**, because D7's Open-only temporary transfer cannot be reused for quiet obligations after actual access loss.
3. Make B a **designated reviewed successor**, not an invented load-balancing/offboarding engine. This is a stronger and simpler alternative supported by actual vendor flows.
4. Make C **visible administrator-reviewed reassignment**, not continued apparent normal ownership by someone whose rights are gone.
5. Keep the current permission owner, canonical assignment/history boundary, D3 reminders and D4 prepared-mail authority. Transfer neither another domain's permissions nor its facts.
6. Explicitly identify the D6/D7 **Pause and Keep interaction** for the selected answer's review. Revocation never waits; a new assignment-repair exception must not be smuggled into ratified controls.
7. Do not choose collision-blocking as another yes/no question: D1/D2 already require it. After this ownership branch, concrete collision-review UX and Support–CRM identity/context continuity are stronger priorities than speculative workforce settings.

Recommend **A as the normal shared destination, with deliberate successor handoff available**. Its main residual product cost is shared review/claim work. B reduces that step where a real successor is known; C provides tighter review at greater operational cost. Qualified Shared recovery exceptions are mandatory safety behavior under any option, not evidence to require a separate review queue for every ordinary item.

This resolves the question-level research, not the selected answer's detailed lifecycle. Its later adversarial review must establish the actual authoritative event and reconciliation path, exact scope/current-control semantics, full authorization matrix, Pause treatment, late reopenings, immutable history/mail, visible partial outcomes and production proof. Those are engineering consequences to research, not discoverable facts to ask the founder to supply.
