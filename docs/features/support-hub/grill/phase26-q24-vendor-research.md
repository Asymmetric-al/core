## The candidate gap is real, but the need is bounded

[D4](phase26-d4-adversarial-review.md) distinguishes a Saved reply from a work-changing macro and requires disclosed effects through the same authorized transition/send boundaries. It explicitly prohibits resolving a conversation merely while inserting unsent wording. [D18](phase26-d18-adversarial-review.md) ratified My replies plus a curated shared wording library and excluded a business-action macro builder. Neither decision chooses whether staff should have a reusable library of manually selected multi-action shortcuts, how that library is governed, or whether existing separate controls are enough.

That makes this a valid next question rather than a reopening of D18's content library. The product choice is whether repeated combinations of already legitimate Support actions deserve a curated shortcut. It is not whether Asym should build general workflow automation, a script engine, cross-domain mutation bundles or more email templates.

No Asym tenant observation supplied here proves the frequency of such combinations or measured time savings. The recommendation is a product judgment grounded in established help-desk patterns and Core's existing work boundaries. The strongest no-build alternative must stay visible: D18 saved wording plus D4's named Send-and-work action and the normal label/assignment controls already handle individual conversations.

## CRM-related alternatives do not justify a second workflow owner

[HubSpot snippets](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/conversations/use-snippets), updated 2 June 2026 and available across plans with differing limits, are reusable text inserted into records, emails, chat and notes. This is the category D18 already addresses. It is not itself evidence that a snippet should perform Support status changes or CRM writes.

[HubSpot playbooks](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/playbooks/use-playbooks), updated 1 May 2026, are interactive record cards for standardized notes and questions. The page identifies Sales/Service Professional/Enterprise with seat/permission requirements and specifically calls property-updating questions an Enterprise capability; a later paragraph is less precise about editions. Logging can create engagement history and update selected record properties. This solves guided data collection, a different problem from a small manual Support shortcut. Asym should not add playbooks, duplicate CRM interactions, or turn Support into the owner of donor/giving/property updates to support this question.

The good cross-surface pattern is continuity: a person viewing Support from a permitted CRM record reaches the same shortcut, draft and admission path with the same access checks and a preserved return context. CRM contact/organization ownership, relationship changes, giving actions and delegated work keep their independent command boundaries. A label like “Refund inquiry” never proves eligibility for a refund or its completion. No need to integrate two vendor CRMs exists inside one Asym platform.

## Three genuine alternatives

The labels below are a proposed comparison for the root session, not an answer selected by the founder. After the independent gap review, the strongest fork concerns **what a shortcut can do**, rather than whether to add a personal action library. Shared curation is already a coherent Asym convention; content/send coupling is the material additional product and engineering decision.

| Option                                               | Everyday behavior                                                                                                                                                                       | Strength                                                                                        | Cost/tradeoff                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Curated shared Support-only action shortcuts** | Staff choose a named combination of already-qualified local Support actions, inspect its changes, then deliberately Apply. It does not insert text, add a note or send.                 | Reduces repeated triage/organization steps while keeping the effect local, visible and bounded. | Adds a small action collection and review/commit surface; staff still choose saved wording and send separately.                                    |
| **B — Existing actions plus saved wording**          | Staff use native label/assignment/status controls, D18 My/Shared replies and D4 Send-and-work without an action-shortcut library.                                                       | Smallest product and maintenance burden; excellent for occasional or varied work.               | Repeated multi-action combinations require repeated selection and cannot be curated as one reusable operation.                                     |
| **C — Curated reply-and-work shortcuts**             | Staff select qualified D18 wording together with staged Support work changes, review the response/effects, then deliberately Send and apply through an extended qualified D4 admission. | Strongest reduction in repetitive reply-and-update work; familiar in mature help desks.         | Couples shortcut lifecycle to audience, source wording, draft purpose, preparation and sending; creates more failure, conflict and recovery cases. |

All three preserve the existing personal saved-reply library. None adds personal action recipes, automated triggers, branching, new business commands, cross-domain actions, direct provider calls from shortcut configuration, or bulk execution. A and C both use a curated shared collection; they are alternative scope choices, not three configuration modes to build.

**Single recommendation: A, curated shared Support-only action shortcuts.** It can save repeatable local work without changing D18 wording semantics or turning the composer into a new action-bundle editor. The current D4 Send-and-work already covers the common reply-plus-status choice; C should be selected only if the founder wants reusable combinations that materially exceed that accepted capability. Zendesk's evaluation/submission separation supports A's review-before-effect principle; Front/Freshdesk show that manual local work combinations are a legitimate distinct category. None proves observed Asym time savings.

B remains the strongest simpler alternative if teams rarely repeat the same action combinations. C remains a genuine strong alternative, evidenced directly by Intercom's queued actions, Zendesk's staged ticket update and HubSpot's Send and apply. C is not unsafe by definition: it requires additional exact qualifications that A avoids. The original personal-versus-shared fork was weaker because it introduced another custody/privacy subsystem instead of isolating the meaningful workflow choice.

For A's concrete example, use a clearly illustrative **Receipt question triage** shortcut that adds an existing curated topic label and assigns the conversation to an eligible Support worker. The review shows the actual old/new assignment and label change before Apply. It neither issues a receipt nor creates a refund/task/CRM record, sends an acknowledgement, changes the signature, or completes a delegated business action. Do not preinstall invented ministry policy or assume this combination is needed by every tenant. The actual allowed local action catalog must be specified after the founder chooses the model.

For C's contrast, **Ask for more information** could insert approved D18 wording and stage Waiting for requester plus a topic label, then require the normal reviewed explicit send-and-apply. The comparison must make this extra capability clear rather than describing C vaguely as “advanced.”

## Scope guards if A is selected

These are proposed boundaries for the next question, not a preemptive full D24 decision.

1. **Select, review, Apply.** Selecting an action shortcut shows an explicit current-state preview; no field, text, note, reminder or communication changes just from exploring the picker. Apply commits the approved local Support combination through its authorized owner. There is no hidden Send, auto-submit or default shortcut execution.
2. **Only existing local Support commands.** Candidate actions are already-qualified assignment/coordination and curated labels, with any status/reminder addition requiring explicit selected-answer review of its invariants. The macro catalog must not become “any writable field.” Recipient/From/Reply-To/requester/CRM links, new Party creation, delegated-domain actions, giving/care/financial mutation, redaction/retention, arbitrary scripts, webhooks, generic workflows and destructive commands are outside this proposed scope. A shortcut named for a receipt or refund conveys no domain authority.
3. **A is content-free.** No reply/note body, template variable, attachment, signature or P17 prepared artifact belongs to its reusable action definition. D18 still supplies wording independently and D23 still supplies the signature. This avoids a second content store or compiler and is why A is simpler than C.
4. **Show meaningful before/after changes.** The staff member sees the named current conversation, exact target assignment/labels and consequences beside Apply. Inapplicable actions have safe, specific reasons; do not silently skip or downgrade. The collection has a predictable labelled entry point, scoped search and useful descriptions rather than a permanent row of macro buttons or a rule-tree editor.
5. **One reviewed combination.** Start with one selected recipe at a time. Another selection replaces/re-evaluates the preview and cannot silently stack conflicting mutations. No action-order programming, branch execution, nested macros or arbitrary transaction builder is required.
6. **Every action keeps its own authorization.** Shared visibility does not grant assignment, inbox, label or CRM permissions. Evaluate and admit against the actual actor, tenant, target and current recipe revision. A creator cannot lend stronger privileges to the person pressing Apply.
7. **Atomic declared local effect.** The complete bounded local combination is admitted together with its history and durable command identity, or none of those requested local changes is applied. Recheck expected state and current permissions at the effect. Do not run sequential field API calls and report Applied after partial success. No transaction spans provider I/O because A has no send action.
8. **Failures are visible and recoverable.** A removed label, departed/ineligible worker or changed state prevents an invalid operation rather than dropping that action. Preserve the safe preview and provide re-evaluation/repair or the normal standalone controls. Replay of the original admitted command reconciles its result, not a new write that restores obsolete state after someone else's later update.
9. **Preserve the open draft and work truth.** Applying a local action cannot overwrite reply/note text, recipients, signature or a staged D4 choice. If an accepted assignment or routing operation changes access or invalidates a current draft, use the already-qualified owner transition and safe draft-recovery behavior. Preview must warn about the actual known consequence rather than inventing new access guarantees.
10. **One behavior on desktop and mobile.** Responsive web users need the same complete effect summary and explicit Apply; keyboard/IME and assistive technology must reach every control. No silently incomplete mobile execution. A native client that cannot honor the contract must make the shortcut clearly unavailable before use.
11. **Small administration.** Shared stewardship, meaningful name/description, scoped availability, immutable usable revisions and ordinary archive/restriction use the actual Support configuration owner conventions. Favoriting a shared shortcut is a personal preference, not a private action recipe. Do not add another always-two-person review, a new department hierarchy or an automation designer solely for this capability.
12. **Truthful minimal history.** Opening/searching, previewing, abandoning and successfully applying are different. An Apply receipt identifies the recipe revision, current actor and admitted local changes; existing source events remain authoritative. Shortcut use is not a reply, a delivered email, a CRM interaction or business-task completion. No body export or surveillance dashboard is needed.
13. **Inert migration.** Existing mixed macro/raw-HTML paths are not authority. Convert only deliberately reviewed eligible local action combinations into this scope; preserve unsupported legacy objects for controlled remediation. Migration never executes them, sends mail, publishes wording, changes status or grants permissions.

## Additional qualifications if C is chosen instead

The content/work combination would reuse the same curated governance but requires a distinct, precise proposed contract: only qualified D18 immutable shared wording is copied through the existing source-aware Tiptap flow; Reply and Note purpose never silently switch; D23 signatures are not duplicated; missing required values block the applicable send; recipient/actor/locale/source changes refresh approval; and the declared local actions plus exact ready P17 preparation are atomically admitted through D4 before P6 dispatch.

A send failure is not a reason to rerun local actions or claim delivery. Provider uncertainty remains exact-effect reconciliation. No workflow enrollment, external connector or protected CRM action becomes safe merely because a human clicked Send and apply. The reviewed list must never say “one action skipped” while sending changed content. Its composition, conflict, source-revocation, mobile and retry tests are a real additional cost, not a reason to call C categorically wrong.

## Evidence limits and handoff

This pass searched primary current product/API documentation and inspected D4/D18's accepted boundaries. The main comparisons are Zendesk, Intercom, HubSpot, Freshdesk and Front; Deskpro adds a directly relevant permission rule. Zoho's surfaced macro documentation supports a broad automation category but adds no stronger narrow pattern than these sources, so it is not forced into the findings. Nonprofit donor software did not supply a more relevant verified manual macro pattern and is not used as substitute evidence.

Search results contained qualitative reports about macro discovery, missed required fill-ins and confusing public/private behavior. Those were not needed to establish the documented workflow differences, and no prevalence, quantified efficiency or current product defect is inferred from them. No live vendor trial, actual Asym workflow timing, browser/mobile execution, SQL/RLS test or message was performed.

The next founder question should decide A/B/C at this product level with a concrete example. If A or C is selected, its independent adversarial pass must settle the allowed action catalog, single-conversation scope, preview/commit boundary, actor/target races and honest failure recovery before anything is recorded as ratified. C additionally needs exact content/purpose/preparation/send handling. Do not silently advance Q24 merely because this research recommends A.
