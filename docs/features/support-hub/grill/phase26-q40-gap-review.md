## Concrete ordinary journey

A worker opens a newly admitted conversation and finds an unsolicited sales pitch that contains no real Support request or known outstanding obligation. The worker wants it out of ordinary work without accidentally deleting evidence, classifying a donor as untrustworthy, blocking a shared address or suppressing a later legitimate inquiry. If the worker makes a mistake, they need an understandable authorized correction path.

This is an illustrative task derived from the existing intake/work distinction, not a claim about measured ministry prevalence. A legitimate requester who is frustrated, persistent, difficult to answer or unable to provide information is not thereby an unwanted sender. Mixed legitimate and unwanted content is a separate source-correction edge within this same proposed capability, not permission to dispose of the whole legitimate request.

## One question, three real capability scopes

**When unwanted correspondence has already reached Support, how much should staff be able to do from the conversation?**

| Option                                              | Actual capability                                                                                                                                                                                           | Benefit                                                                                               | Real tradeoff                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **A — Set aside this conversation, with Restore**   | A clear recoverable current-conversation Unwanted action. Future mail continues through the ordinary qualified intake policy; no sender policy changes follow.                                              | Clear correction for a mistaken ordinary admission without a permanent future-contact consequence.    | A later unwanted message may still need review; this does not itself reduce repeat-sender intake.                                                                                                            |
| **B — Set aside, with a separate future-mail hold** | The same current-conversation correction plus a separately reviewed, appropriately authorized narrow sender/intake-policy action that holds matching future mail for existing review.                       | Helps an inbox experiencing repeated unwanted mail while preserving held-input review and correction. | More configuration and false-positive risk, especially shared or spoofed addresses. Separate owner authority and exact scope must be qualified. The current action never silently enables the hold.          |
| **C — Use Resolve and an optional label**           | Add no special Unwanted capability. Staff use the current authorized work-completion and optional-label controls when their existing requirements are genuinely satisfied; future intake remains unchanged. | Smallest product and maintenance scope; no additional classification or sender policy.                | Less explicit distinction between an actual completed Support request and material that should not have entered ordinary work. Staff must not use No response or waive obligations simply to tidy the queue. |

**Single best recommendation: A.** It addresses the concrete current-conversation gap and keeps correction understandable. C is the strongest simpler alternative and should remain fairly presented: it can be adequate where the existing Resolve semantics genuinely fit. B's repeat-intake benefit is real but adds a more consequential future policy; no Asym workload evidence yet makes that extra capability essential. Do not offer all three as runtime settings. The founder selects one scope; the ensuing adversarial review then qualifies the full selected journey.

## Boundaries the eventual question and selected review must preserve

1. **Work and classification remain distinct.** A must not quietly add Unwanted as a fifth ordinary work status or rename successful resolution. A nuisance disposition needs a truthful relationship to existing work, target and report meanings. Known actual obligations, promised follow-up and recovery cannot disappear because someone chooses it. The subsequent review must spell that relationship out rather than freeze a schema now.
2. **Current means the reviewed current source scope.** No automatic mailbox/address/Party/domain expansion, no hiding a legitimate merged original, and no silent reparenting, deletion or mutation of old source messages. A mixed conversation cannot be treated as wholly unwanted merely to skip inconvenient work; use already accepted source/duplicate/link/privacy corrections as appropriate.
3. **Future intake is still future intake under A/C.** Later input follows current qualified intake, original threading and legitimate-reopen rules. Do not turn a current label/disposition into an implicit future block or make the old Unwanted classification automatically bury newly qualified relevant content. B's future hold is a separate visible owner action with a defined matching scope, not trust learning or a bundled default.
4. **Correction preserves history and safety.** Restore must be a current authorized correction, not historical rollback. Actual source, permission, retention, merge and independently admitted effects remain authoritative. No new trash timer or blanket force-reopen is selected by this question.
5. **Feedback and measurement remain honest.** A nuisance disposition is not solved service or human correspondence evidence. It must not create D28 invitation eligibility, conceal a previous real failure, erase prior human work or rewrite a genuine already-issued feedback effect. Reporting should distinguish actual exclusion/correction from completed service through its existing owner semantics.
6. **Support is the only current-work owner.** CRM uses the same authorized source; no contact deletion, Party tag, do-not-contact flag, consent change, blacklist, merged identity, financial/care action or duplicate CRM Activity follows. Email similarity proves neither identity nor authority. A worker's ability to change Support handling does not grant protected records access.
7. **Email Studio and P6 stay separate.** Setting aside, restoring or inspecting prepares/sends no email and creates no new template, automatic explanation, feedback message, provider read receipt or unsubscribe. P17/Email Studio retains actual governed authoring/preparation; P6 retains original submission/delivery/reconciliation truth. Any legitimate already-admitted communication or required safety cancellation follows its existing owner boundary, not a pretend unsend.
8. **Existing safety owners remain in force.** D19's held review, D16's restricted-content correction and actual abuse/security policy are neither weakened nor reimplemented. B is not a tenant-authored trigger builder, cross-tenant denylist, bulk sender ban, domain-wide block or CRM suppression system. If a qualified narrow future-policy owner cannot be established, B cannot silently rely on a new local registry.
