Any use while editing a posted note must remain within its accepted private-source boundary. [Accepted composing contract](phase26-d34-adversarial-review.md).

## The three alternatives remain fair and distinct

**Question to resume:** After a staff member posts an internal Support note, how should they correct it?

| Option                                                                       | Product behavior                                                                                                                                                                               | Benefit and real cost                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Authors can edit their own notes, with visible history — recommended** | The currently authorized original author can correct the note; current readers see edit attribution and can inspect only permitted earlier revisions. Other staff add their own clarification. | Corrected information remains easy to read without an extra note for every transcription fix. Requires trustworthy revision, conflict, current-access and retention behavior. It cannot recall information colleagues already saw.       |
| **B — Keep posted notes unchanged; add a linked correction**                 | A new, attributed correction is visibly connected to the original, without ordinary in-place editing.                                                                                          | Strongest explicit chronology; a valid smaller policy choice. It adds another entry even for small corrections, and linking/display must prevent readers missing the correction. It is not simply “do nothing” or already-shipped proof. |
| **C — Allow a brief author-edit window, then linked corrections**            | Author-only editing with visible history is available initially; after the window the correction uses a new linked note.                                                                       | Supports immediate cleanup while stabilizing older text. Adds an extra time boundary and two journeys; interruptions can make an otherwise simple correction harder. No duration is selected at question stage.                          |

These are alternative scopes, not three tenant settings. B's ordinary unchanged text still obeys D16 redaction and D17 expiry; A's history is never a permanent forbidden-content archive. C must not be presented as intrinsically safer solely because it has a timer.

**Single recommendation remains A.** It addresses a recording error at its source while preserving original authorship, original posting time and visible correction evidence. A newly changed real-world fact belongs in a new update instead. That distinction is a product judgment and an instruction for clear use, not an AI classifier or a claim that all changes can be automatically categorized. Required substantive-correction attention still uses existing qualified paths rather than relying on the Edited marker to notify everyone.

Fresh primary reads on 14 September support the alternatives without making a vendor authoritative: Intercom's 26 March 2026 release documents editing one's own posted notes, an Edited timestamp and a dedicated teammate permission. Zendesk documents that ordinary ticket comments cannot be edited and directs sensitive-data correction to redaction. Neither source establishes Asym's precise revision-storage architecture, grace period, tenant demand or measured usability. [Intercom release](https://www.intercom.com/changes/en/136196-edit-your-internal-notes), [Zendesk comment policy](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408885807258-Can-I-edit-recall-or-delete-comments-in-my-tickets).

## Boundaries common to all choices

1. **Exact human source and current authority.** Scope is an ordinary human-authored internal Support note. Incoming mail, sent replies, system events, CRM-owned notes and D26 staff briefs retain their own contracts. Authenticated staff/source authority is not inferred from email, assignment, a CRM relationship or D34 awareness.
2. **Authorship and external evidence remain truthful.** No ordinary capability rewrites another person's attributed statement, converts internal content to public or edits P6 communication evidence. [ADR0032:19–38](https://github.com/Asymmetric-al/core/blob/7abd2c11ffd4ed70c6775c4fd6f51c996e4350dd/docs/adr/0032-immutable-prepared-message-and-whole-message-recovery.md#L19) freezes recipient/channel intent and prepared material; a source-note correction must not mutate those facts. This does not prohibit a separately attributed new source revision if A/C is selected.
3. **Redaction and expiry dominate history.** Current and prior content, attachments, search, CRM views and notification material obey D16/D17. Edits do not reset the content deadline. A genuinely new correction note has its own true publication consequences and cannot restore removed material.
4. **Attention is not indiscriminate republication.** D15 routine edit behavior remains quiet. New correction notes or explicit attention may have their existing source-authorized notification consequences. No claim that every option has zero possible staff notifications; no edit-spam, donor email or new notification engine.
5. **One Support source through CRM.** The same qualified note is viewed through either entry. No duplicate CRM note, shadow timeline, synchronization or inherited financial/care authority. A sentence about a refund never changes the refund or proves its completion.
6. **Editor reuse without shared drafts.** Use qualified Tiptap/Base UI/Maia where an edit is selected, preserving the separate reply draft and current reading context. D34 is advisory, not a lock or access to another author's unpublished changes. No co-editing, generic editor-history service, bulk rewrite or AI authority is implied.
7. **No premature implementation commitments.** After selection, the required full review must settle conditional save, revision/attachment/mention provenance, legacy authorship, material-correction attention, privacy, lifecycle and release proof. This scope check selects no table, CAS algorithm, token, grace duration, storage horizon or new permission enum.

Bulk selected-work changes and post-send Next remain settled by D3/D4/D20/D21. D12/D26 already cover genuine received-request continuation and first outgoing email; unrestricted proactive outreach or third-party forwarding would require a demonstrated new scope need. Those alternatives are weaker next topics than the explicit conditional gap in D17.

**Conclusion: Resume Q35 unchanged with the three actual alternatives and A as the single best recommendation.** No founder answer has been recorded, and D29 reaffirmation adds no answer by implication. Only this new scope-check artifact was created.
