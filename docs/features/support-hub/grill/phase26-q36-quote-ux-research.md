# Q36 — Earlier email context in outgoing replies

**14 September 2026. Research for the next unanswered question; no Q36 policy is selected.** D1–D35 and their amendments remain ratified. This report concerns what earlier email text accompanies a new human external reply. It does not reopen D2's Reply-all preference, D33's staff reading mode, signatures, actual email threading or source privacy. No product, provider, mailbox, GitHub state or prior decision file was changed.

**Recommendation: A — Append no previous email text automatically; let staff deliberately include qualified context when useful.** B, automatically quoting the selected source email, is the strongest competing policy. A keeps the default outgoing message focused and avoids silently redistributing earlier material, but it is not universally more convenient: a recipient without usable conversation grouping may need to find older mail. C, bounded permitted external-thread history, gives more self-contained context at a higher repetition/review cost. The founder must choose the policy; source-specific implementation details are not selected here.

## The gap is real

- **D33 explicitly excludes outgoing inclusion.** [D33-R18](phase26-d33-adversarial-review.md) and its [Email Studio seam](phase26-d33-email-studio-integration.md) say Compact/Full and reading disclosures do not decide which quoted material is sent. Folding is presentation, not removal from email bytes.
- **D2 preserves a reviewed reply target and context, without choosing an amount.** [D2-R04](phase26-d2-adversarial-review.md) preserves the draft and requires reviewed audience/quoted-context changes after an explicit target change. It does not decide whether old body content appears by default.
- **The inspected current serializer does not establish an approved policy.** `apps/admin/features/support-hub/components/detail/composer/serialize-payload.ts` takes current editor JSON, selected attachments and signature inputs, not a selected source-message/history argument. Its permissive scaffold is already unqualified under D23; an absent autoquote implementation is not evidence that A was ratified.
- **P17 does not settle this producer choice.** The inspected `docs/prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-17-system-messages-template-management.md:1535` explicitly leaves response sending/threading to Phase 26. P17 still owns compatible governed preparation and P6 actual delivery. Existing owner reuse is not a quote-default decision.

These local source statements were read in the Phase 26 WSL worktree with its directory verified. The independent data reviewer confirmed the same gap. The root's source/PR registry owns live baseline verification; this research does not claim a new deployment or provider experiment.

## The important nested-history distinction

Selecting one source email does **not** mean sending only words newly authored in that email. Its permitted body may contain earlier quotations, copied address blocks or forwards. B therefore names the **selected source**, not a reliable one-message byte count. Any future promise to extract only its newest authored content needs its own source-qualified extraction and uncertainty behavior; D33's conservative reader folding cannot supply that authority.

C would choose multiple permitted source messages from the selected external email thread. That is a genuinely broader source-selection policy, even if B's single source sometimes already contains a long nested chain. C must never mean the entire merged Support conversation, internal notes, staff briefs, system events, unrelated branches or CRM context. The final question should state this clearly rather than promising an unlimited transcript.

## Three meaningful alternatives

Illustrative example: a requester asks a follow-up about their statement, and Maya writes the answer. The choice is what previous email text, if any, Asym automatically places under that new answer. It does not decide whether Maya can continue the real email thread or whom she is replying to.

These are alternative product defaults, not three simultaneous tenant settings or a vote to add a quote-rule builder. No count, byte limit, historical-attribution format, extraction algorithm, notification behavior or new permission is selected by this comparison.

## Why A remains the best recommendation

Start with the content the worker is intentionally saying now. Maintain the existing real email lineage; when prior wording is useful, make its inclusion deliberate and visible in the same reply review. This fits the founder's preference for clean low-friction surfaces while reducing accidental historical disclosure and repeating source material. It leaves the operational and technical complexity with the existing content/recipient/preparation owners rather than creating a “send all history” shortcut.

The strongest challenge is recipient context. A terse “Yes, that works” without the relevant question can be confusing, especially after an interruption or with conversation grouping disabled. The fix is not to pretend the recipient client always solves it. The reply should be understandable, and staff need an easy deliberate source-qualified context path. B would be the better choice if the founder considers always carrying the selected question worth the extra repeated content. C is strongest when a self-contained sequence is more valuable than the additional review and payload burden; no current tenant evidence establishes that as the universal need.

No published Asym user study measures these preferences. A public [Intercom community question about missing context in Fin replies](https://community.intercom.com/train-fin-94/fin-strips-quoted-email-thread-from-replies-is-there-a-fix-14882), dated **26 June 2026**, reports recipient-context friction but concerns AI replies, not all human replies. It is a self-selected experience, not a measured incidence or reason to add AI here. It supports taking B/C's contextual benefit seriously without overruling the primary documentation.

## Boundaries every option must retain

1. **Content scope is distinct from delivery identity.** Quote inclusion changes neither D2 Reply-all/Reply-to-sender initialization nor the reviewed To/Cc envelope, sender, Reply-To or RFC lineage. Old quoted recipient headers are not new recipients or proof of current authorization. A qualified minimal attribution is different from automatically copying old header blocks.
2. **Only allowed external context is eligible.** Internal notes, D26 staff briefs, D27 internal guidance, CRM/giving/care records, system events and unrelated merged-source content are never auto-quoted. New or removed participants do not acquire old disclosure rights. Every chosen source/copy must remain permitted for the exact reviewed audience.
3. **Preview means the actual material being sent.** A collapsed visual block still contributes bytes when included. Staff can inspect/remove included context; D33 reading preferences never decide or conceal outbound inclusion. After source/audience changes invalidate the reviewed material, require review and the appropriate new preparation rather than silently patching sealed content.
4. **No automatic file reuse.** Historical inline images/files are not reacquired because old body text was selected. Existing D4/D16/D23/D32 source and attachment rules continue. Unsupported source rendering is a truthful limitation, not silent extraction success or a reason to fetch external assets.
5. **P17/P6 keep their roles.** Support owns exact target/source/audience and admission; P17 compiles the approved immutable content with compatible signature/footer and source dependencies; P6 owns actual dispatch, retry and outcome evidence. No second Support mailer, provider template or external-original editing is added.
6. **Privacy and temporal truth remain mandatory.** Quote copies retain source provenance and current D16/D17 restrictions. Expired/redacted/history-only material cannot be revived from caches, old drafts, received originals, prior templates or CRM. Already delivered emails cannot be recalled by removing a later draft's quote.
7. **First outreach from received manual/form work has no invented parent email.** D26/D29's first human response uses its real source/recipient contract. B/C cannot manufacture earlier external mail or copy the private intake brief merely to fill a quote slot.

The selected answer's full adversarial review must determine the exact eligible-source selection, insertion/removal/Undo journey, nested-quote interpretation, attribution, plain/HTML rendering, budgets and uncertainty, privacy-correction propagation, current-recipient review, saved-draft behavior and end-to-end proof. This question freezes none of those details prematurely and introduces no new provider, CRM or public portal.

## Presentation-ready short options

**Question:** How much earlier email text should Asym include by default when staff reply?

- **A — New reply only:** no automatically appended history; add useful permitted context deliberately. **Recommended.**
- **B — Quote the source email:** include the selected message's permitted body, reviewable/removable; it may contain nested history.
- **C — Include thread context:** include a bounded permitted external-email sequence; more self-contained context, more repetition/review.

Present all three alternatives together with their benefit and cost. Do not substitute the recommendation or a research link for the actual choices. Q36 remains unanswered; this report records no new default, ADR0036, formal specification, ticket, implementation or external effect.
