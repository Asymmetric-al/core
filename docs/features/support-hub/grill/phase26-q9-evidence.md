## Reported friction and UX inference

[JoelV's HubSpot contact-picker report](https://community.hubspot.com/t/contact-picker-for-associations/150012), 4 June 2026, describes difficulty selecting the right contact when a ticket is created from a company but the association picker shows the wider CRM, including similar names. The consultant response is not authoritative proof of current product limitations. The report is a concrete friction example, not a prevalence study.

For Asym, the useful inference is to rank/display permitted contextual candidates clearly while retaining broader authorized search and disambiguating details. Do not restrict search to an assumed household/company relationship, automatically link suggestions or treat membership as representative authority. Staff must be able to inspect permitted context and return without losing their draft or queue position.

The related records should be optional and contextual. It adds value when the request-specific relationship is not adequately represented by the existing CRM graph or linked owner record. If a gift already provides the proper beneficiary context, another Party link should not be required. Even under B, main would provide staff orientation only; it must not silently select recipients, protected-action targets, template variables or timeline publication. Under C, list order or expanded-card state must not become an implicit primary authority.

## Independent challenge and bounded recommendation

Three independent slices considered whether this was an unresolved decision, actual Core identity/association boundaries, and current vendor context/side effects. The main corrections were:

1. Do not reask no-auto-create, identity-from-email or collision blocking; those are already settled.
2. Preserve D1's existing plural business-record links in option A. A is a genuine simpler model, not “forget everyone else.”
3. Additional links mean optional conversation-specific Party relevance, not copied CRM relationships, a required extra form or a relationship-role builder.
4. C avoids a primary-designation step. Its cost is the lack of an explicit focal record; a compact related-record group must remain easy to scan. Protected target selection remains necessary under all options.
5. Sender, requester, main context, related Party, legal donor, assignee and action-authorizing principal remain separate under every option.
6. Do not infer vendor architecture or import recipient/portal/deletion side effects from customer-association controls.
7. Explicitly record the stale Twenty OpenSpec conflict instead of treating all committed prose as mutually consistent.

Recommend **C — optional explicitly related records without a main designation**. Two independent reviews preferred B for its stable orientation; the Core review challenged the extra primary-subject classification as unnecessary for the presently demonstrated Support requirements. The final recommendation adopts that challenge: the sender remains the clear communication anchor, one related record remains simple, and several can be presented compactly without requiring a primary choice. This is a product judgment, not unanimous independent endorsement or measured usability superiority.

B remains the strongest alternative if the founder values an explicit focal record enough to justify selecting and maintaining it. A is the smallest model where existing CRM/owner-record navigation supplies enough context. No primary-Party requirement is inherited from Phase 9's separate primary staff-owner model, and no option obtains recipient, representation or action authority from context. The technical proof obligations for authorization and history remain under every choice.
