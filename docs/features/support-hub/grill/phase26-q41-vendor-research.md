15 September 2026. **D1–D40 and every amendment are founder-ratified. Q41 is an unanswered next-stage choice, not a new Support feature.** The independent candidate screen is `work/q41-ux-candidates.md`; the parent is completing the cross-decision coverage audit and recording D40. ## Conclusion

**Recommend A — Begin formalization with `/to-prd`, preserving the accepted validation gates and making the remaining evidence gaps explicit.** That recommendation is a product/process judgment about this session: forty ratified decisions now need one coherent, inspectable contract. It is not a research finding that writing a PRD proves usability, or that specification-first is universally superior.

The strongest alternative is **B — Review a focused interactive research prototype first** when the founder wants to evaluate the combined reading, composing, navigation and recovery experience before formalization. **C — Study intended staff doing their current work first** is strongest when the dominant uncertainty is whether the designed workflows match actual ministry practice. Both produce valuable but different evidence. They are choices about what to do next, not mutually exclusive lifetime development methods or three product settings.

The necessary documentary reconciliation happens now under the existing request. It is not an optional quality check hidden inside one option. Formalization must preserve, not waive, outstanding actual-user, accessibility, database, concurrency, provider, recovery and production-shaped proof. A formal contract remains revisable when new evidence exposes a material flaw; ratification cannot make an empirically wrong assumption true.

**Why A is the best next action here:** the current blocker is fragmentation and traceability across a large accepted decision set, not a newly demonstrated missing feature. Writing the contract can organize proof without falsely reporting it as passed. There is no need to create a new feature question or a prototype of every accepted clause to keep grooming moving. This is conditional on the integrated audit finding no unresolved material decision that must be settled first; actual defects cannot be concealed as later testing.

**When B should win instead:** the founder wants to judge the integrated interaction before reading the formal contract, or the audit reveals material visual/interaction ambiguity. The useful prototype is a research artifact, not a miniature production system. All applicable existing boundaries still hold.

**When C should win instead:** there is a concrete concern that the chosen model assumes the wrong staff work pattern, or actual intended-user access is available now and those assumptions could materially change scope. This is a credible alternative, not a slower or inferior route. Its result may narrow or correct the design; it must not automatically restart all forty decisions.

## Concrete task illustration

The following is an illustrative test scenario assembled from accepted journeys, not observed ministry behavior: a staff member opens a request, checks permitted CRM context, begins a Reply, saves a separate Internal note draft, leaves, resumes the right draft, handles a new incoming message, and returns to the correct work view. The outcome must keep private work private, preserve place and recipients, distinguish reading from handling, and maintain the owning domain's authority.

- Under **A**, the formal contract makes this complete journey and its required proof traceable to D2/D4/D9/D26/D33–D39.
- Under **B**, the research prototype makes the relevant transitions inspectable, including one representative interruption or failure instead of presenting only a happy-path animation.
- Under **C**, intended staff first show how a recent comparable request actually moved through their current tools, revealing whether this constructed scenario matches their work at all.

The D40 distinction between current Unwanted/Restore and future Hold/Stop is another justified candidate task if prototype evaluation is selected. These are examples of existing accepted work, not permission to add new requirements or a promise that every scenario must be prototyped.

## Evidence and permission boundaries

1. A documentary cross-check establishes consistency only. Source inspection establishes what checked source says. Synthetic probes establish their exact bounded result. A prototype review establishes only the reviewed artifact and interactions. Actual intended-user research establishes observed/reported outcomes within its stated sample/context. Runtime, database/RLS, concurrency and provider tests establish their own specific behaviors. None can be relabeled as another.
2. The workspace explicitly requires `/to-prd` before a formal PRD/specification and `/to-issues` before implementation issues. Q41 does not silently cross either boundary. A recommendation to begin `/to-prd` is not the command itself; a bare choice that omits a required stage instruction must be handled according to the user's explicit workflow.
3. No participant messages, recruiting or data access are authorized merely by this research. C's plan can be prepared, but real sessions require actual people and appropriate authorization. Do not promise that participant evidence has been or can be generated autonomously.
4. Synthetic B means synthetic records and simulated effects, not synthetic people presented as research participants. Keep production credentials, inboxes, provider sends, CRM writes and public publishing out of it. Use the existing Base UI/base-maia system if later authorized; no new design system is selected.
5. These are alternative next actions. They do not remove accepted user research or technical proof from A, prevent formalization after B/C, or authorize another broad feature discovery loop. Findings can require a targeted correction when supported by evidence.
