# D31 — Follow-up quick choices and Custom: independent UX research

**13 September 2026. A selected; exact amendments proposed for the root's adversarial synthesis.** This document supplies a concrete UX recommendation, verified product comparisons and explicit limitations. It does not independently ratify new defaults, implement a picker or create a formal specification. D3 remains the reminder owner; D24's saved patch still cannot store or apply reminder presets.

## Recommended exact menu

Use one small product-maintained chooser everywhere a qualified Support reminder can be selected:

| Choice                     | Exact intended meaning                                                                                                | Visible preview                                       |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **In 1 hour**              | Trusted server reference plus one elapsed hour, with the target rounded upward to the next whole minute.              | Actual date, time and zone.                           |
| **In 4 hours**             | The same trusted reference plus four elapsed hours, with the target rounded upward to the next whole minute.          | Actual date, time and zone.                           |
| **Tomorrow at this time**  | Actual reference local date plus one calendar date, at its displayed HH:mm with seconds zero, in the selected zone.   | Actual next date/time and any offset/DST consequence. |
| **In 1 week at this time** | Actual reference local date plus seven calendar dates, at that HH:mm with seconds zero. It does not mean next Monday. | Actual date/time and any offset/DST consequence.      |
| **Choose date and time…**  | Explicit Custom date/time in a visible named zone.                                                                    | The actual resolved instant before Save.              |

This four-choice menu is a **product judgment**, not a proven optimal set or an industry standard. The short elapsed choices support near-term review, the calendar choices support another day/week, and Custom covers a particular agreed time. There is no default selection or action triggered merely by opening the menu, no natural-language parser, no hidden keyboard duration, and no personal/tenant/inbox preset administration. Final root synthesis uses whole-minute targets: round the duration target (trusted server now plus one/four hours) upward to the next minute; for calendar choices use the actual server-local date plus one/seven dates and the current HH:mm with seconds zero. Never round the anchor into a new date before calendar addition. The exact target minute is visible; precision explanation belongs in timing details, not constant header clutter.

For example, a reference at 13 September 23:59:59 makes Tomorrow at this time **14 September 23:59**, not 15 September midnight. This is a semantic boundary example, not an executed timezone test. Its purpose is to prevent display-friendly minute precision from accidentally adding a second calendar day.

**Why not Tomorrow at 9 AM / next Monday at 9 AM?** Fixed morning choices are legitimate product patterns and could be useful to some staff. But they assume an appropriate hour and often an implicit working week. The same-time calendar alternative avoids declaring a ministry's start of work, handles a seven-day operation without special exceptions and needs no morning preference. Its cost is that someone wanting tomorrow morning must use Custom. That is an explicit tradeoff, not a claim that fixed morning presets are inherently unsafe.

Do not shorten the visible labels to bare Tomorrow or Next week if that hides the rule. The exact preview is primary confirmation, not a mandatory extra modal. A row can commit on deliberate activation once its current qualified preview is visible and valid; unusual adjusted/ambiguous times require the existing explicit review instead of silent acceptance.

## Timezone policy

The actual-source review found the tenant `default_timezone` and no qualified general personal scheduling-zone owner to reuse. **Final synthesis recommendation for new selection:** use the current tenant's qualified default timezone, with an explicit per-use IANA override available to all five choices. Missing or invalid required tenant-zone evidence needs visible qualification/correction, not a silently invented personal hierarchy or browser fallback. This replaces the exploratory idea of a user-first hierarchy. Do not borrow the requester/CRM person's location, D14's SLA calendar or D30's reporting timezone merely because a similarly named value is available.

Display **Time zone: [readable zone]** inside the chooser. Allow a one-use change through the shared searchable zone control; it changes the reviewed choice, not account preferences. Re-resolve the visible calendar choices after an explicit zone change before commitment. The control must distinguish selection-zone input from displaying a saved instant elsewhere.

For **editing an existing reminder**, initially show the saved absolute due instant in its retained input zone when that context remains qualified. Do not replace it with Tomorrow or the user's current zone preference. A display conversion alone preserves the instant. Only a deliberately edited wall-clock date/time or a newly selected quick choice proposes a changed instant; present that result before Save. Another viewer's timezone or travel cannot reschedule shared work.

D3-R07 already governs DST gaps/overlaps and invalid/past values. Preserve that rule: a non-existent local time is not silently moved; a repeated local time cannot be committed without an unambiguous choice. An affected calendar row becomes **Review time** and opens the same Custom control with its candidate and explicit correction/occurrence choice. A readable offset/occurrence explanation is needed only for that case, not around every normal time. Selection and display must support non-hour offsets. The exact minute target and its resolution remain bound through review/admission; rounding cannot silently change the selected date or business rule.

## Complete ordinary journey

### Set a quick reminder

1. Staff open the labelled **Follow-up reminder** control in a permitted conversation. Show the current reminder if one exists; do not silently replace it.
2. The compact chooser displays the named zone, four choices with actual resolved results, and Custom. Merely focusing a row, opening the picker, navigating dates or typing a filter performs no mutation.
3. A deliberate row activation proposes that exact reviewed result through D3's current authorized command. There is no arbitrary preview TTL for a still-valid explicit future candidate. As relative offers age, show anchored wording and the actual absolute date; if a calendar label would become false across a date rollover, use its absolute weekday/date. Keep the candidate instant fixed. **Refresh choices** is deliberate and optional when a person wants a new reference; it never happens under the pointer or rebases a retry. Past/invalid candidates need correction under D3.
4. Pending state prevents accidental double intent while remaining accessible. On admitted success, show the actual saved due time in the conversation and a concise confirmation. A lost response reconciles the same command; it never schedules another hour from the retry time.
5. If a concurrent new reply, reminder edit, expiry, ending or permission change invalidates review, retain safe input and show the current condition. Re-evaluate deliberately; do not overwrite a colleague's newer reminder or use a receipt to display a stale current state.

### Choose Custom

Custom opens a small date/time/zone form within the shared interaction pattern. Both typed entry and calendar selection are usable; no natural-language syntax, wheel-only time control or month-by-month navigation is mandatory. Use visible field labels, locale-appropriate formatting, unambiguous validation and an explicit **Set reminder** or **Save changes** action.

When setting a new reminder through Custom, prefill today's date in the selected zone and leave time unselected, unless an explicit candidate is carried from a reviewed choice. Do not quietly choose midnight or a default hour. When editing, prefill the actual saved date/time/zone. After a valid edit, show the resolved result. Invalid dates, an unintended past time and an unresolved DST choice keep input and focus available for correction. A valid future Custom candidate does not expire merely because the form has been open for a minute.

No extra reason, note or assignment is required merely to save. A particular support commitment can already be explained in authorized conversation context. A date picker cannot infer a promised external reply or change a Finance task's deadline.

### Inspect, edit and remove

The saved reminder control shows a readable actual time and provides zone detail without hover. Opening it displays the current saved value with **Change date and time** and **Remove reminder**, plus deliberate replacement quick choices. Cancel/Escape closes without changing anything and restores focus.

Remove is a narrowly named D3 action, not Delete conversation, Resolve or Cancel request. It uses the current reminder generation and current permission, confirms only the real result and follows D3's exact work/deferral semantics. It does not cancel a target or undo an already-admitted due review. A concurrent replacement must not be removed by a stale button. Retain a persistent path to inspect the current state/history instead of relying on a disappearing toast.

On Resolved work, do not quietly attach an invisible future timer. Use D3's explicit authorized reopen-then-remind or qualified Reopen-and-remind path, with its consequence clearly named. D31 does not expand that compound action or create a new send operation.

### Across Support, CRM, keyboard and existing selected-item actions

Support detail and the D26 CRM-entered detail use the same control and current source. Moving between authorized surfaces preserves the conversation/draft and return context. Viewing a reminder needs current source rights; editing requires the actual command right. Reading CRM does not grant Support or giving/care access.

A keyboard/command-palette action opens the chooser on the explicit current target; it cannot bypass the reviewed time by hardcoding 24 hours. Existing qualified bulk actions show the exact selected IDs/count and one explicit selection zone, with the reviewed intended time before admission; retain D3's per-item outcomes and conditional safety. Do not reinterpret one calendar preset in each destination inbox silently or expand to all matching conversations. Bulk recovery never recomputes a new time for failed items under the old intent.

While composing a reply, selecting a reminder is a separate deliberate existing D3/D4 control. **D24-R05 shortcuts cannot store or apply reminder presets.** Choosing a time never sends/prepares email or grows D24's saved patch. Existing P17/P6 send preparation, signatures, recipient review and outcomes remain separate. No Tiptap editor is needed in a time chooser.

### Due, offline and returning later

The due timer continues through D3's qualified Open-for-review effect, preserving cause and actual execution evidence. A reminder is neither proof of staff availability nor a guarantee of instantaneous worker execution. Owed replies, D14 targets, independent recovery and unfinished work remain discoverable under their existing rules.

Offline or unavailable-authority state permits safe inspection only under existing policy; do not offer a false Saved state or a silent offline write queue. Preserve permitted in-memory Custom input during recoverable connectivity errors. On reconnect or returning from CRM, recheck current target and authority before applying. Account/tenant changes clear inappropriate input/results; no browser-local reminder archive is needed.

## Mobile, accessibility and visual craft

Use Core's base-maia/Base UI components, semantic tokens and shared date/time controls. The chooser should be a quiet list of four readable rows, not five bright tiles or a miniature calendar product. Each row has a short choice label and exact result; long dates, zones and translations wrap. The saved control is one actual time, not repeated badges around the subject.

On narrow screens, preserve a single-column sequence: current value/zone, quick choices, Custom. If the existing pattern uses a sheet, ensure the on-screen keyboard cannot cover Save or the active field. Full set/edit/remove must work without desktop settings, hover, dragging or memorized shortcuts. Named controls and ordinary tab order remain available even if optional calendar-grid keys exist.

Focus moves into Custom predictably, initially to the appropriate field or selected date, and returns to the reminder control on close. Errors associate with their fields and provide a concise summary when needed. Screen readers announce the selected/saved/failed result without reading a live ticking preview repeatedly. No color-only saved/overdue meaning or animated countdown. Verify both themes, text zoom, 320-CSS-pixel reflow, touch targets, RTL/bidi, localized date/clock formats and unusual time-zone offsets with actual supported browsers and assistive technology.

## Maintenance and bounded scope

A has no preset Create/Edit/Delete settings, inheritance hierarchy or manager publication queue. Product maintainers own the four named semantic choices and their shared resolver/renderer conformance. Staff maintain only the actual reminder they intentionally set. A product change to a menu choice affects future selection, never existing accepted instants or pending command retries.

The source/read/command boundary must distinguish menu choice identity, input zone/calendar expression, preview reference, accepted instant and current reminder generation. Those are logical distinctions, not a mandate for a generic scheduling DSL or new database tables. Reuse qualified shared time and preference owners where sound; explicitly qualify a missing general timezone read rather than inventing browser assumptions or a second settings copy.

No office-hours skipping, public holiday rule, recurrence, schedule sync, personal preset library, AI time suggestion, automated follow-up email or new message family is required. A valid current target may fall outside office hours because it is a deliberate staff reminder; D14 promise accounting remains independent. New timezone settings or owner-policy changes are not smuggled in through this picker.

## Proof and final recommendation

Required proof includes both short elapsed choices with target-minute ceiling; +1/+7 calendar choices near midnight and DST without rounding the source date first; non-hour-offset zones; repeated/nonexistent local times; a long-open valid chooser without forced TTL refresh; aged wording/date rollover; deliberate Refresh choices; Custom typed/calendar use with blank initial time; due equality during expiry versus rejection of nonfuture scheduling input; invalid/past inputs; qualified tenant default and explicit per-use zone; viewing then editing an existing reminder after travel; concurrent replace/remove/due/new-input/Resolve; lost responses and old receipts; Support/CRM return with draft intact; exact selected bulk outcomes; keyboard/mobile/screen-reader flows; and no email, D24 patch, CRM task or personal-reminder side effects.

**Recommendation: retain A and adopt the four choices plus Custom above, with the qualified tenant default/per-use zone and exact-result review.** Their values are proportionate product judgments, not empirically optimal timing. The root/source synthesis's actual tenant-zone owner, duration-target minute ceiling, calendar-date/HH:mm rule, fixed valid future candidates without arbitrary expiry, explicit DST Review time and blank-time new Custom replace the earlier exploratory hierarchy/freshness suggestions. This research remains proposed synthesis support, not a competing independently accepted authority.

No prototype, live message, implementation, browser/assistive-technology test, tenant usability study or production timing/performance proof was performed. The sources support a careful design; they do not certify a perfect experience before the actual product is built and tested.
