# D22 practice evidence — financial and nonfinancial partnership requests

2026-10-10 UTC (2026-10-11 Asia/Bangkok). Research for the founder's selected A:
the same request-and-response capability may cover funding and significant
nonfinancial partnership invitations. Recommendations below are review proposals,
not a claim of implementation or separately ratified policy. Only this appendix is
changed; no agency was contacted and no live account was used.

## Finding

The nonfinancial ministry need is documented. OMF explicitly invites prayer,
practical support, church advocacy and hosting a travelling worker. The inspected
CRM products do not establish one universal record model: MPDX financial appeals,
Virtuous Gift Asks and grants, and volunteer opportunities have distinct purposes.
That is a credible narrow alternative, not evidence that prayer is less important.

**Product judgment:** A fits Core's continuing partnership model if “request” means
a specific invitation whose answer and follow-through matter. It should not turn
every call into a proposal, a positive answer into fulfillment, or a mixed invitation
into one all-or-nothing yes. Common ownership and response tracking can coexist
with separate authoritative records for money, participation, events and consent.

## Strongest narrow alternative

**B:** use structured proposals for fundraising, including appropriately qualified
noncash intentions; use shared interactions and tasks for other invitations. This
has published precedents, fewer request kinds to explain and focused funding reports.
It preserves nonfinancial participation and can work well when there are few such
invitations or a simple note and next task preserve their answer adequately.

Its potential weakness is losing the distinction between **staff completed the
follow-up call** and **the church accepted, declined or has not answered the hosting
invitation**. That is an inference, not a demonstrated vendor failure. A addresses
this without needing separate prayer, introduction and hospitality workflow engines.
The minimum common record should identify the request, who is accountable, the
reported response and its context, and relevant follow-through. Additional execution
belongs with its existing owner. Do not require a monetary value for practical or
prayer support, or rank their value using a fundraising forecast.

## Material review concerns and proposed clauses

Severity and likelihood are qualitative judgments; there are no measured incident
rates. Examples below are deliberately illustrative, not reported agency workflows.

### P1 — Partial responses become false blanket acceptance

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** “Will you pray, introduce us to your church
and provide monthly support?” can receive “yes to prayer; ask the committee about
money.” An undifferentiated Accepted outcome would misstate the answer.

**Proposed clause:** “Record a response against the request actually made, preserving
its scope, qualifications and unanswered parts. Acceptance of one independently
answerable request is not acceptance of another. Where distinct outcomes need
separate follow-through, prefer separate request records associated with the same
partnership and conversation rather than duplicate partnerships or a generic
multi-part proposal builder.”

**Proof:** Record that illustrative answer without a monetary commitment, a completed
introduction or a claimed church-wide decision. The financial response remains
unresolved. A later answer changes the relevant request without overwriting the
earlier response context. This does not prescribe a final response-status vocabulary.

### P2 — Agreement is mistaken for execution, giving or permission

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** A host's conditional willingness is not a
confirmed itinerary. A church member's response may be personal, rather than an
authorized decision for the church. Agreeing to pray does not establish consent to
every newsletter; providing a friend's name does not authorize outreach to that friend.

**Proposed clause:** “Keep the request, reported response, respondent/represented
party and scope, follow-up work, participation and actual fulfillment distinguishable.
A positive response alone creates no payment authority, gift, recurring agreement,
volunteer registration, event booking, third-party admission or communication consent.
Use the applicable source-owned process for those facts.”

**Proof:** A proposed visit can be accepted subject to dates without being marked
attended. A pending introduction remains pending until there is evidence of the
introduction. A funding answer can differ from requested amount or timing without
being relabeled as money received. The response records what was reported without
inventing authority on behalf of an organization.

### P3 — “Significant” creates unnecessary administration

**Medium severity; plausible likelihood.** A mandatory financial-size threshold or
manager approval would be unsuitable for prayer and practical partnership. Turning
each task into a request would duplicate ordinary work.

**Proposed clause:** “Use a request when staff need to track a specific supporter
invitation and its answer independently of the next task. Ordinary calls, reminders,
internal actions and routine logistics remain shared tasks/interactions. Give brief
examples; do not add a separate significance score or approval gate.”

**Proof:** “Ask the church to host a ministry visit” can have a request outcome;
“call to confirm parking” can remain a task. Completing the call neither accepts
nor closes the hosting request automatically. A routine prayer concern can remain
an interaction/task without creating a prayer-partnership invitation.

### P4 — Nonfinancial scope corrupts reporting or invents new modules

**High severity for financial misstatement; medium likelihood.** Counting hosting
acceptances as funded proposals, or recording them as zero-value gifts, destroys
the meaning of both financial and nonfinancial results. Treating all practical help
as a receiptable noncash contribution would also exceed the selected boundary.

**Proposed clause:** “Use a plain umbrella label such as Requests, with the actual
requested outcome visible. Money fields and financial aggregates apply only where
financially meaningful, under the financial owners' definitions. Nonfinancial
requests require no zero amount or invented valuation. Grant applications,
volunteer execution, event participation and qualifying noncash intentions retain
their source-owned requirements; a request may refer to them but does not replace them.”

**Proof:** A prayer invitation, introduction request and financial ask can appear in
the same permitted work view without combining their monetary totals or labeling
all successes as gifts. If practical help later qualifies for a contribution record,
the financial owner determines that treatment; the original request does not decide it.

## Evidence still needed

Use the real shared interface with staff and missionaries to test an ordinary money
ask, a prayer invitation, a conditional visit invitation and the mixed-response case.
Observe whether they can distinguish request response from task completion and
fulfillment, and choose a task when no separate response tracking is needed. Include
a church respondent acting personally. No interviews, usability results, financial
runtime checks or performance metrics are claimed here. No forced enum, currency
rule, universal agency terminology or new workflow subsystem follows from the sources.
