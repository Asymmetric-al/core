## Founder question — answered A

**Q20 — If staff no longer trust the recorded stage and do not yet know its
replacement, may they deliberately change it to “Not yet assessed” while keeping
the same stage list?**

For example, after a handover, staff are unsure whether a church's recorded
“Considering support” stage is still reliable. They want to keep using the same
list. This is an illustrative pressure test, not a claimed agency workflow.

**A — Allow the deliberate change (recommended).** Keep the same partnership and
stage list, preserve earlier assessments in history, and show no current stage
until staff choose an appropriate one.

**B — Keep the last recorded stage until another is chosen.** Preserve it as the
last recorded understanding; uncertainty alone does not let staff remove the
current stage. Staff may choose another eligible stage when ready.

**Recommendation:** A. It supports honest current uncertainty without making staff
choose an inaccurate replacement or change lists just to remove an assessment.
B's strongest benefit is keeping the last known classification available until a
replacement is recorded. That is a credible continuity choice, not an assertion
that staff should knowingly present false information. A's rationale is product
judgment, not a measured staff preference or error-rate claim.

## Consequences to review after the answer

- If A is selected, define a deliberate current-state change while preserving
  earlier assessments. It is not historical deletion, proof of never having been
  assessed, a supporter action or a reset of the relationship.
- If B is selected, keep last-recorded understanding intelligible without claiming
  it was recently reassessed. Do not silently invent a mandatory review workflow.
- Both options retain current source authority and ordinary accountability. Neither
  implies a new permission, approval, compulsory reason, acknowledgment, assessment
  task/deadline or donor communication.
- Do not infer clearing from inactivity, old age, a retired definition, failed
  lookup, inaccessible information, import omission or a generic nullable patch.
  A selected optional outcome must remain distinct from these states.
- Any permitted new action needs actual current-state/revision, atomic history and
  exact-retry safeguards; absence cannot erase a concurrently accepted assessment.
  A no-op must not fabricate history or another assessment occurrence.
- Keep the no-current-stage outcome distinguishable from a real stage with the
  same label. Staff need clear current/proposed/result states and an authorized
  Set stage action where applicable, without a second confirmation ritual.
- Paused/closed editing, bulk operations, historical corrections and cross-scope
  changes remain separate. This question does not settle them or expose those paths.
