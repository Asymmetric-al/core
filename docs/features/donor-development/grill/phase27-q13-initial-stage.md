## Founder question — A selected

**When staff start tracking a partnership but do not yet know which stage fits,
should they be able to begin anyway?**

Imagine a staff member is ready to take responsibility for work with a church,
but needs an introductory conversation to understand where that partnership stands.
This is an illustrative situation, not a claimed universal missions workflow.

**A — Allow “Not yet assessed” when needed (recommended).** Staff choose an
appropriate stage if they know it; otherwise they can start without a recorded
stage, clearly shown as not yet assessed. Responsibility and normal follow-up
still apply. An accurate introductory stage may already fit; this option does not
assume every new partnership needs to start unassessed.

**B — Require a stage before starting.** Staff must choose an appropriate stage
from the organization's approved list before the new partnership work is admitted.
This keeps every new journey classified from the outset, but staff must have enough
information to make that choice.

**Recommendation:** A avoids guessing or blocking useful work merely because the
stage is not yet understood. Missing assessment must remain visible and distinct
from a completed assessment, paused work or missing accountability. This is a
product judgment about truthful information and low friction, not a measured
agency preference. B is credible if each organization's definitions provide an
accurate starting choice even when little is known.

## Boundaries and consequences to review after the answer

- Either answer preserves authorized human admission, exact ministry/purpose,
  responsibility, current permissions and the existing due-work-or-owned-exception
  contract. An unassessed stage cannot make unowned work healthy.
- A would express missing assessment, not add a new organization-configured stage,
  automatic review date, task, approval queue or grace period by implication. Its
  display/storage/history/reporting treatment must be made precise after selection;
  no nullable schema or final UI control is selected now.
- B requires an actual appropriate assessment, not automatic first-stage assignment,
  intermediate clicks, an invented history or a claim of contact/giving/consent.
- Neither answer authorizes clearing an existing recorded stage, resets a reused
  journey, changes D9 reopening, or selects default/suggestion, preset-switch or
  correction policy. A reviewed group must preserve D4's actual per-member intent
  and truthful results; no blanket default or extra per-member ceremony is inferred.
- No answer to the next interview decision or product implementation is inferred
  from this choice.

## Answer and completed review

The founder selected **A — Allow “Not yet assessed” when needed**, emphasizing
appropriate tenant flexibility and clear, beautiful, understandable UX: users must
know their options, next action and how to change the stage later.

The [full D13 adversarial review](phase27-d13-adversarial-review.md) records the
exact decision, all 22 requested categories, R01–R13, AC01–AC18, a concrete UX/state
contract, five research appendices and 15 fresh isolated PostgreSQL checks.
**Disposition: Accept.** No material amendment or repeated ratification is needed.

Optional stage at a valid deliberate start is now settled. This is not a catalog
stage, missing responsibility, hidden-data fallback, new task/deadline or permission
to clear an existing assessment. Staff can select a known stage immediately or use
an obvious authorized Set stage action later. Reuse and reopening preserve existing
understanding; views and history distinguish absence accurately.

Initial suggestions/defaults, preset applicability/switching, correction and
clearing, detailed work policy and metric formulas remain separate. The review
neither chooses them nor claims live database, browser, migration, scale or staff
usability qualification. No formal specification, tickets, product changes,
activation or donor contact occurred.
