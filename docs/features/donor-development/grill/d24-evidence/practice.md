# D24 practice evidence — recorded appeal working lists

2026-10-10 UTC (2026-10-11 Asia/Bangkok). Research for selected A: an appeal keeps
a recorded working list, with deliberate additions, exclusions and removals.
This appendix supplies evidence and proposed safeguards, not new ratified policy.
No live accounts, agency interviews, implementation or outbound actions were used.

## Finding

Selected A has a close nonprofit comparator: Blackbaud documents managed constituent
lists whose membership staff control, with a change history and initial population
from current query results. MPDX documents appeal-specific additions, exclusions and
removals. Neither establishes that all Christian missions organizations prefer A.
HubSpot provides a credible live-segment alternative.

The principal risk is confusing the recorded plan with an actual request or current
permission to contact someone. MPDX's published guides themselves use “Asked”
inconsistently: one describes receipt of a letter/email; another places every added
contact there. Core should preserve its settled distinctions instead of adopting
that shortcut. Recorded membership also does not freeze contact restrictions.

## Strongest B, no-build alternative, and proportionate A

**B is credible:** a continuously refreshed list can suit an ongoing effort whose
staff intentionally want current filter matches. HubSpot documents that model.
It need not lose historical asks or responses; that loss is not inherent in B.
Its genuine tradeoff is that planned membership changes without an appeal-specific
staff decision. This is an alternative comparison, not a request to reopen A.

**No-build alternative:** existing saved views, shared tasks and individual Requests
could coordinate a small effort. This avoids another maintained population. It
does not, by itself, preserve an agreed appeal population and its intentional
exceptions as filters change. A spreadsheet could retain a snapshot but would
introduce a separate copy and would not become contact or financial authority.
These are product judgments, not measured limitations of actual agencies.

**Smallest useful A:** select permitted supporters individually or from current
filtered candidates, maintain the appeal's included/excluded population, and explain
material membership changes. Permit later additions without restarting the appeal.
Reuse existing Party, Request, shared-work and communication capabilities. No
separate approval ceremony, mandatory per-entry task, universal reason essay,
parallel general-purpose list builder or frozen duplicate contact database follows
from A. Routine interaction and recovery design remain delegated under D21.

## Material concerns and exact proposed clauses

Severity and likelihood are qualitative judgments, not incident statistics. All
examples below are illustrative acceptance scenarios, not practitioner reports.

### P1 — Planned inclusion becomes invented outreach or financial evidence

**High severity; plausible likelihood**, especially if MPDX's Asked default is copied.
The effect is false follow-up, response counts or financial interpretation.

**Proposed clause:** “Adding a supporter records intended appeal work only. It does
not assert that a Request was presented, a response received, a commitment made,
or a gift received. Membership changes do not create those facts or change their
source authority.”

**Proof:** Add a church before anyone contacts it: no Asked fact, Request response,
commitment or gift appears merely from inclusion. Later actual requests and
responses remain separately identifiable. Selecting the church does not select
every associated individual as a communication recipient or start their partnership
work. A prayer partnership alone establishes neither financial solicitation
permission nor ineligibility; applicable purpose/contact rules still decide.

### P2 — A saved plan preserves stale permission

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** Both HubSpot and Blackbaud document stale
snapshot exclusions. The effect could be prohibited contact or disclosure despite
an apparently valid old list.

**Proposed clause:** “Recorded membership is historical planning information,
not continuing authorization. Current restrictions and scope govern candidate
suggestions, actionable inclusion and every outward effect. Historical inclusion
may remain lawfully visible without making that supporter actionable.”

**Proof:** A supporter gains a relevant never-ask restriction after selection.
Authorized history remains understandable, while the prohibited next action is
unavailable. Removing an appeal-specific exclusion cannot remove the authoritative
restriction. A newly eligible supporter does not join the plan automatically.
This clause uses existing owners; it creates no appeal-specific consent engine.

### P3 — Exclusion, removal and re-addition erase or resurrect work

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** The inspected MPDX pages do not prove
history preservation; comparator list re-addition does not fully specify exclusion
precedence. The effect is lost context or renewed work that staff intentionally stopped.

**Proposed clause:** “Keep an intentional appeal exclusion distinguishable from
removal from the current working list. Neither deletes the supporter or rewrites
actual request/response history. Refreshing candidates, retrying an operation or
adding a matching group does not silently reverse a recorded exclusion. Deliberate
re-inclusion requires current authority and does not revive completed or withdrawn
work.”

**Proof:** Exclude a church from a special appeal while its ordinary partnership
continues. A later bulk addition that also matches the church preserves that
exclusion unless the authorized action deliberately includes its reversal.
Re-inclusion preserves earlier facts without a second copy or another automatic
request. Retained history remains subject to governing privacy/retention rules;
this is not unlimited retention or mandatory free-text justification.

### P4 — Changing membership quietly changes what the appeal's counts mean

**Medium severity; plausible likelihood.** A deliberate current list and historic
participation are different populations. The effect is misleading coordination or
claims of effectiveness even when individual facts remain correct.

**Proposed clause:** “Make current planned membership distinguishable from actual
requests and responses, including those involving supporters later removed or
excluded. Explain recorded membership changes without using them to rewrite past
activity or infer financial attribution.”

**Proof:** Begin with 40 illustrative churches, present some requests, remove one
contacted church, and add two others. Staff can identify the present plan and the
removed church's actual interaction without calling every planned church Asked or
counting re-added entries twice. Filter changes alone do not change the recorded
plan. This does not settle a conversion formula, financial denominator, P13
attribution rule or P16 fulfillment calculation.

## Evidence limits and minimum usability evidence

No inspected source establishes the frequency of group additions, excluded re-adds,
church contact changes, or nonfinancial appeal planning in the intended agencies.
No interviews, time savings, error rates or adoption metrics are asserted.

A later usability check should ask authorized staff to select an initial group,
add a late candidate, exclude one church, interpret a contact restriction, and
explain what removal did to an earlier request. Observe whether they distinguish
the plan, actual recipients and recorded responses without training them to treat
the list as consent. Use findings to simplify the interaction; no invented success
threshold or additional founder questionnaire is proposed.
