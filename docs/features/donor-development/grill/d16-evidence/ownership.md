# D16 — shared approved choices without a second applicability system

2026-10-10. Founder selected **A: staff choose the appropriate approved list**, with
excellent intuitive UX, and requested the full adversarial review. Research against
Core HEAD `225c02e9499bd6c0d493223cd4b1a4120ff787e3` and working-tree ratified D1–D15.
This appendix changes no product, formal specification, issue or live state.

**Recommendation: Accept A; no material amendment identified here.** D16 declines
additional configurable process-specific restrictions. It preserves mandatory source
validity, current authority and D15 availability. The draft-list/stage consistency
case follows already accepted identity and deliberate-intent rules; it does not
require another product vote or a saved-journey switching feature.

## Concerns, exact clauses and proof

Severity describes prospective impact; likelihood is conditional judgment rather
than an observed incident rate. Proofs are future acceptance obligations.

### O1 — “Source validity” conceals the rejected process restriction system

**High; plausible with hardcoded preset types or optional workflow filters.** E1/E6/E8.
**Clause:** “For new partnership work, offer the current organization-approved
new-use lists permitted by actual source and current authorization boundaries.
Do not add configurable or hardcoded process/Party-type/team/ministry/role/surface
mapping merely to decide which approved list staff may choose. Every mandatory
exclusion must trace to an accepted owning source invariant or permission rule.”

**Proof/effect:** differently named support/church/staff lists remain choices in
equivalent authorized scope without a new process-type field. List wording and
guidance help judgment; they do not execute predicates, demand reasons or install
another approval path. A genuinely required identity/scope constraint still wins.
DON-14 may restrict its own qualified operation; it cannot create an additional
native admission gate. Preserves A without reinstating B through implementation.

### O2 — “Shared” becomes unrestricted tenant-wide disclosure or authority

**Critical; plausible if the approved catalog is treated as public metadata.** E3/E7.
**Clause:** “Shared organization configuration does not imply identical permitted
projections for different authorization contexts. Independently authorize list
discovery/use, journey admission, configuration and result/history disclosure.
Selecting a list grants none of them. Derive trusted context and attribution;
validate selected references at acceptance.”

**Proof/effect:** configuration-only and journey-only actors retain their separate
powers; names, guidance, counts and errors expose no hidden configuration. Matching
authorized scope receives coherent source meaning across staff/missionary surfaces;
different legitimate P12 contexts may disclose different results. No per-list ACL
hierarchy, active-role-name shortcut or additional Legal Entity catalog partition
is invented. Caller-supplied ministry/list/owner IDs remain proposals, not authority.

### O3 — Changing an unsaved list carries an incompatible proposed stage

**High; likely with independent select fields or same-label mapping.** E2/E3/E5.
**Clause:** “Before admission, a proposed list change must make any incompatible
proposed stage visibly unresolved rather than carrying or mapping it silently.
The final deliberate intent contains either an eligible stage of the selected list
or clearly understood no-stage intent under D13. Validate that exact pair.”

**Recommended minimal design, not a new policy:** visibly clear only the incompatible
unsaved stage with a concise explanation, preserve other valid input, and let staff
choose a new stage or start unassessed. Keeping the incompatible draft value visibly
flagged until resolved is another possible design. Neither requires a compulsory
modal, acknowledgment, second save or a draft-history cache for every visited list.

**Proof/effect:** A/Stage A → B cannot submit Stage A under B even if B has the same
label; no B stage is inferred. Browsing/changing the draft records no stage visits,
assessment dates or task effects. Server-side retirement/denial/stale-input failure
rejects the submitted selected intent; it must not silently clear the stage and
claim successful admission. This is inherited draft coherence, not saved switching.

### O4 — A committed or uncertain admission is treated as an editable new draft

**High; plausible after lost responses or duplicate discovery.** E2/E4/E5.
**Clause:** “Distinguish unsubmitted draft, pending/unconfirmed command and accepted
source state. Recover the same intent/result before treating an uncertain start
as new. D4 reuse preserves the existing journey's binding, stage and obligations;
a newly proposed list does not authorize an upsert or switch.”

**Proof/effect:** admission accepts list A, response is lost, then the screen shows
B: receipt recovery exposes the permitted original result without replacing A.
A currently bound withdrawn list remains valid for qualified reuse/return under
D15; a fresh start cannot borrow that eligibility from another journey. Same-purpose
collisions do not create one journey per list. Cancellation before submission is
different from pretending an accepted or still-running command was undone.

### O5 — Availability races or group convenience change reviewed intent

**High; plausible with stale pickers or deferred group execution.** E2/E4.
**Clause:** “At each authoritative admission, validate the current exact selected
list and any stage, current authority, ordinary-journey identity and source work.
Do not replace an unavailable list, expand group membership or recompute members'
list choices from a later default or type guess. Keep atomic per-member acceptance
and truthful accepted/reused/unaccepted/unresolved outcomes.”

**Proof/effect:** withdrawal accepted before a queued new admission prevents that
admission; earlier accepted members remain existing work. A mixed finite reviewed
group retains actual member choices without forcing every member through a new
ceremony. Zero permitted new-use options leaves new work unavailable with permitted
recovery; it neither clears a list nor requires broader discovery rights. The sole
visible option is not evidence that the true permitted set has exactly one member.

### O6 — A list choice creates a default, assessment or workflow side effect

**High; plausible with first-option fallbacks or “smart” initialization.** E1/E4–E8.
**Clause:** “D16 resolves the choice set, not suggestions, defaults or automatic
selection when exactly one option exists. Preserve a genuinely established valid
source choice without asking again, but do not infer a choice from array order,
viewer, gift, score, related person or last-used preference without a separately
qualified source policy.”

**Proof/effect:** D4 deliberate admission and normal work remain; list discovery or
selection alone creates no journey, stage assessment, task, outreach, consent or
financial fact. D13 still permits no initial stage while requiring a valid list.
Any initial selected stage records only its actual accepted provenance. One-option,
zero-option and multi-option presentation must not be mistaken for a ratified
default policy. Stage and list names never become cross-list reporting equivalence.

## Recommendation and remaining release gates

B's strongest advantage is enforcing an organization's different prescribed
processes. It costs applicability configuration and maintenance; the founder chose
shared choices plus guidance. Appropriate staff judgment is not infallible, but no
accepted source requires an AI suitability score, process-type registry, mismatch
warning engine or extra approval as its correction. Preserve the narrower source
and current-authority safeguards, truthful history and separately qualified repair.

Accept A without an additional ratification loop. Its effect is specific: no extra
process-specific restriction layer. D14's applicability prerequisite is addressed
to that extent; D16 does not resolve all list acquisition behavior or qualify release.
Initial suggestions/defaults, exact single-option behavior and saved switching/
correction still require explicit treatment before offered paths depend on them.
Use the D4 admission command and shared UI/source model, not a new choice service,
permission engine or scheduler. Actual source/API, current-role, concurrency,
accessible browser, migration and staff-usability proof remains required.
