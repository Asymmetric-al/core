# Phase 27 Q22 — scope of partnership requests and responses

2026-10-11, Asia/Bangkok. **D21 A selected and accepted; reviewed D22 explicitly ratified; disposition: Accept.** Research
and interview only. Routine design details are delegated under the founder's
latest instruction; this question concerns a substantive product boundary.

## Why this question matters

Stage/list editing is no longer the interview focus. The next part is helping
staff prepare a specific request, record the supporter’s response and follow
through. An **ask** is that request, not the whole continuing partnership, a task,
a promise or money received. The product label can use plain “Requests.”

[D1](phase27-d1-review.md) and [D3](phase27-d3-adversarial-review.md) already preserve
prayer, financial and other admitted participation in one continuing partnership.
That is settled. They do not decide whether significant nonfinancial invitations
have the same structured request/response record as fundraising proposals.

The [P27 roadmap](../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/roadmap.md#phase-27-what-it-covers)
requires first-class asks/proposals with owner, response, requested/expected amounts
and source-confirmed funding. The [owner constraints](../../../prds/program-roadmap/owner-constraints.md)
apply financial context rules to every **monetary ask**. The original founder
request also includes nonfinancial church partnership and qualifying narrow noncash
intentions. These establish the owners and financial requirements, but leave the
nonfinancial request workflow open. A narrow fundraising option must still respect
that requested noncash-intention qualification; it is not cash-only.

## Founder question — answered A

**Q22 — Should the same request-and-response workflow cover both fundraising and
significant nonfinancial partnership requests?**

**A — Cover both (recommended).** Track requests such as financial support, joining
a prayer partnership, making introductions or hosting a missionary visit. Show
only the relevant details, with an owner, response and follow-through. Money fields
apply only to monetary requests; ordinary calls and to-dos remain simple tasks.

**B — Focus structured requests on fundraising.** Track fundraising proposals,
including narrowly qualified noncash intentions, here. Handle other partnership
invitations through existing interactions and tasks.

**Recommendation:** A. It fits the broader missions partnership model already
selected and gives significant invitations a clear response/follow-through record.
B is a credible narrower scope with focused proposal and funding reports; it does
not make prayer or practical participation less important. This is a product-scope
judgment, not a measured missions preference or a request to design individual fields.

Two independent owner/product reads confirmed the distinction is genuinely open
and more consequential than another stage/lifecycle detail. No new external search,
runtime evidence or practitioner feedback was needed or claimed to establish the
question. Existing financial/nonfinancial participation is not being put to a vote.

## Boundaries for the chosen direction

Whichever answer is selected, keep the continuing partnership, specific request,
shared next task, response, commitment and fulfillment distinguishable. A positive
response is not permission to charge, create a recurring agreement or fabricate a
gift. P13/P14/P16 retain financial authority; P6/P17 retain contact eligibility and
delivery. D3 prevents an extra journey merely because another request exists.

If A is selected, use one coherent request/response capability with relevant fields
and clear financial/nonfinancial reporting. Do not require an amount of zero for a
nonmonetary request or treat a requested introduction as a financial forecast. A
request tracks the invitation/response; it does not become a volunteer, event,
application, pastoral-care or participation system, or turn every routine task into
a proposal. Source-owned execution and actual participation remain with their owners.

If B is selected, keep nonfinancial partnership context and follow-through usable
through the existing tools; do not force a fake monetary proposal or duplicate
partnership to record it. Neither choice authorizes communication, record sharing
or a new approval process merely because a request exists.

The founder selected A, covering both funding and significant nonfinancial
partnership requests. The [full D22 review](phase27-d22-adversarial-review.md)
records the exact accepted decision, all 22 categories, coherent UX/edge cases,
source boundaries and proof obligations. **Disposition: Accept.** These safeguards
apply the stated boundaries and delegated routine judgment. The founder
subsequently confirmed, “Yes, ratify this amended D22.” The reviewed decision is
explicitly ratified on 2026-10-11, Asia/Bangkok, without substantive change. No
formal specification or product was built.

The options and conditional boundaries above retain the question as framed. The
