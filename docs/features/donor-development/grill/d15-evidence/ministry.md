# D15 — existing partnership work on a withdrawn stage list

Research appendix, **2026-10-10 UTC**. Founder choice A permits continued stage
use for qualifying existing work after an organization stops offering its list
for new work. This appendix reviews that choice; it does not ratify dependent
policies. No product execution, practitioner interview or live change occurred.

## Assessment and strongest alternative

**A is appropriate as a continuity policy, not as a promise that old lists will
soon disappear.** A relationship with a church or missionary's support team can
continue through changing personnel and years of ministry. Changing an internal
menu need not interrupt useful follow-up, require a new donor conversation, or
force a different assessment. The evidence establishes that continuity matters;
it does not prove that retaining an old list is universally preferable.

**B's strongest case:** requiring a deliberate replacement before the next stage
change can help an agency teach one current process and avoid incompatible live
definitions. This matters if an old process is unsuitable, rather than merely no
longer preferred for new work. B preserves history and does not require every
record to be migrated before stopping new use. Its cost is making a process
transition a prerequisite to an otherwise ordinary update; it also requires a
qualified replacement path.

Honor selected A without silently providing a tenant switch to B. Retirement
means stopping new use. Correcting unsafe definitions, withdrawing individual
stages, changing permissions or deliberately switching an effort's list retain
their own qualified rules. A must not override those rules or silently invent
the switching facility still reserved by the interview.

## Material concerns and minimal clauses

Severity describes potential effect; likelihood is qualitative judgment, not an
observed rate. Each proposed clause preserves the selected capability and existing
D1–D14 safeguards rather than adding an administrative workflow.

### M01 — “retired” is mistaken for closed or read-only partnership work

**High severity; plausible.** Staff may stop follow-up, think a missionary retired,
or copy the effort to recover editing. The external analogues explicitly separate
new selection from existing context; the ministry consequence is our inference.

**Clause:** “Withdrawing a list from new use does not close, pause, reassign or
duplicate existing partnership work, cancel its tasks, or alter gifts, prayer
participation, communication eligibility or history. Qualified existing work
continues under its normal accountability and current permissions.”

**Proof:** withdraw a list while an assessed church partnership has a due task.
Authorized staff and missionary views retain the same effort, task and stage.
The action sends no donor message and creates no migration obligation.

**Suggested explanatory copy:** “Unavailable for new partnership work. Existing
work can continue using this list.” This is proposed plain language, not a final
state name or new persistent banner on every task.

### M02 — preservation is implemented as display-only history

**High severity; plausible.** Retaining a stage label while blocking all selection
would implement B in practice. Still-unassessed existing work is particularly
vulnerable because there is no prior stage to display.

**Clause:** “For existing work qualified to continue on the list, authorized staff
can still set or change its stage using the list's currently eligible stages,
including its first assessment. Whole-list withdrawal does not itself retire
every stage; an independently retired stage remains unavailable for fresh choice.”

**Proof:** exercise direct forward, skipped and backward choices on qualified
existing work, then its first assessment where still missing. Separately retire
one stage: its historical value remains understandable but cannot be selected
anew. No compulsory note, reassessment, deadline or financial action is added.

### M03 — continuity becomes indefinite clutter or unmanaged work

**Medium severity; plausible over time.** Long-lived efforts may prevent older
lists from disappearing naturally. Hiding all their records would lose work;
showing all historical lists in every new-work selector would defeat retirement.

**Clause:** “Withdrawn lists are not offered for new work. Existing qualified work
remains findable and usable in its normal authorized work views with enough list
context to interpret its stage. Retaining referenced definitions does not require
displaying every obsolete list in ordinary selection controls.”

**Proof:** use several withdrawn lists with a mixture of active and closed work.
New admission offers valid current choices; ordinary work views retain applicable
existing work and its truthful totals. History remains available where authorized.
No forced sunset, cleanup task, annual reapproval, indefinite data-retention rule
or automatic closure follows. D6/D7 still govern whether the effort has useful work.

### M04 — “already used once” becomes a route for new use

**High severity; plausible.** Copying an old record or carrying its list into a
new admission could circumvent the selected boundary. Conversely, a new staff
assignee on the same effort must not be mistaken for a new partnership.

**Clause:** “Eligibility to continue belongs to qualified existing source work,
not to the actor's prior use, the donor's history, or a copied record. New-work
paths obey withdrawal even when their inputs come from older work. Retirement
does not authorize copied journeys or automatic list switching.”

**Proof:** attempt new admission using a stale form and old-list reference; it
does not create work under that list. Continue the same qualifying effort after
an authorized lead change without inventing a new journey. Do not introduce a
copy feature merely to test the source boundary.

### M05 — old definitions silently change reports or current authority

**High severity; plausible.** A combined report may drop retired-list work or
reinterpret it using a current list. A configurator may also infer access to all
affected donors from their authority to withdraw the list.

**Clause:** “Retirement preserves list/stage identities and historical meaning.
Counts and stage interpretation use their qualified source context; retirement
is not progress or a donor outcome. Configuration authority grants no additional
journey, history, usage-count or communication access.”

**Proof:** compare authorized totals and historical interpretation before and
after withdrawal, including equal labels with different meanings in two lists.
Revoked journey access remains revoked even when the actor can administer lists.
No universal stage equivalence or mandatory cross-list mapping is inferred.

## Reservations requiring explicit treatment

Define exactly which existing work qualifies: in-progress records without a stage
are within the offered continuity case; paused return and closed/reopened cases
must be addressed explicitly. D8/D9 preserve identity and last recorded stage,
but do not alone settle later selection on a withdrawn whole list. Neither assume
all historic records qualify forever nor force a new record to avoid deciding.

Defaults, if any, need safe treatment before withdrawal can leave new work without
a usable eligible list. Restoration, unused-list deletion/retention, later
switching and changing definitions remain separately qualified choices. No new
automatic expiry, archiving service, list-specific permission system, frozen
per-journey snapshot or migration engine is justified by these sources.

Usability evidence remains absent. With actual agency staff and missionaries,
test whether they can distinguish retired list from retired stage, closed effort,
permission loss and missionary retirement; then find the existing effort's next
action and make a permitted stage change. Include a prayer-only partnership and
an unassessed one. No invented participant feedback, threshold or preference is
claimed; these are proposed probes before product qualification.

2026-10-10. Earlier proposed labels and reservations here record the evidence-stage
scope, not a competing current recommendation. The full
review defines the owner-native dependency, naming-conflict and release-proof
requirements. No product implementation follows from publication of this appendix.
