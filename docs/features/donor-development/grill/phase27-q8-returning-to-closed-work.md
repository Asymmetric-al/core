## Adversarial check of D7 ratification before Q8

### What could go wrong with this answer?

Recording D7 could be mistaken for selecting permanent parent-record closure or
permission to undo it by toggling a flag. Preserve its exact boundary: the current
effort ends truthfully; later return and mistaken-close correction still need
their own source semantics. Ended child work and old attention do not revive.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

The next example assumes the earlier close was valid and the same church,
identified ministry and ordinary purpose remain appropriate. It does not assume
the former lead, access, stage or asks are still current. A changed ministry or
purpose invokes D2/D3's distinct rules rather than silently reusing old meaning.

### How does this affect the whole product?

D7 leaves identity, assignments, finance, consent and independent service with
their source owners. The next choice affects cultivation lifetime and historical
reporting, not those authorities. Either option must use existing deliberate
source admission, current permission and qualified shared work/attention.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

Staff can finish real work without pretending the relationship ended. When work
later returns, they should find prior context and understand what is current.
Both alternatives can preserve history and reuse known information; neither
inherently requires another church record or repeated data entry.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

The [D7 UX sources](d7-evidence/ux.md) and [ministry sources](d7-evidence/ministry.md)
support separating relationships, individual asks and task outcomes and retaining
findable history. They do not establish one universally correct parent-record
lifetime. Those official sources were checked earlier on 2026-10-10; this
ratification pass does not claim fresh vendor testing or agency-user feedback.

### Does this fit Asym’s existing repo and product direction?

Yes. [D1](phase27-d1-review.md) and [D3](phase27-d3-adversarial-review.md) favor a
continuing ordinary partnership, while D3 expressly leaves lifetime uniqueness
open. [ADR-0183](../../../adr/0183-source-owned-work-projects-into-one-shared-tasks-hub.md:93)
requires new identities for later child work; it does not decide whether the
parent cultivation journey may reopen. D7 explicitly reserves this question.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

No further D7 amendment is identified. Record the explicit ratification and ask

## Why this is the next meaningful choice

D7 R12 and AC15 reserve same-enrollment reopening versus a linked successor.
D3's one-ordinary-partnership rule explicitly concerns concurrent purposes rather
than lifetime uniqueness. Neither a database index nor the word Closed may
quietly settle the distinction. This is a product choice about whether a later
return belongs to the same journey or counts as a separately tracked effort;
physical columns, tables and mutation design follow that answer.

Two independent read-only reviews of D1/D3/D7, shared work and prior official
practice evidence recommended A. It keeps a continuing partnership's development
story together. B is a serious alternative: distinct efforts make separately
bounded starts/endings and effort-based reporting clearer, at the cost of multiple
historical efforts and their current/previous distinction. A also needs honest
closure/return history; B can present a unified history. Neither option inherently
has fewer typing steps, better retention or broader access.

This recommendation is a product judgment for Core's accepted relationship model,
not an observed agency preference or proof that one database design is universally
simpler. No new ADR, period/episode entity, permanent uniqueness constraint or
automatic stage rule is selected here.

## Original single founder question — answered A

Illustrative situation: staff correctly closed their development work with a
church. Months later, they deliberately decide to pursue partnership again with
**the same church, the same ministry and the same purpose**.

**Should they reopen the existing work, or start a new effort linked to it?**

**A — Reopen the same partnership work (recommended).** Keep one continuing
journey, preserving the earlier closure and recording this new start.

**B — Start a new linked effort.** Keep the earlier effort closed and track this
new period of work separately, with its previous context available.

Both preserve history and reuse the existing church/ministry records. Neither
revives completed tasks, old reminders or former permissions. Current
responsibility and what work is now needed must still be established.

**Recommendation:** A fits the continuing partnership model already chosen and
avoids dividing that journey merely because work stopped and later resumed.
B is preferable if separately tracking each return as its own effort is a core
product need. The founder's answer, not this recommendation, selects the policy.

## Boundaries and dependent questions

- This is a genuine return after a valid closure. Correcting an accidental or
  mistaken close is a distinct follow-up; do not bundle its policy into Q8.
- Same ministry/purpose does not bypass D2's handling of materially changed
  people, supporter intentions or future obligations. A genuinely different
  purpose must satisfy D3 rather than being disguised as reopening.
- D4/D7 deliberate current authorization still applies. A gift, referral,
  import, elapsed time or generic task checkbox does not start this work.
- Preserve every prior accepted closure and child outcome. Neither option
  permits erasing the closed period, reinstating old grant/engagement lineage,
  relabeling previous nonrequired work as completed, or replaying old effects.
- Do not automatically make an old stage, ask, owner or next task current.
  The exact stage/responsibility/next-action behavior requires its applicable
  current source rules; Q8 alone does not select those defaults.
- Existing ordinary work, multiple old efforts, hidden records, identity
  corrections and concurrent returns still need collision rules that respect
  D3 and current result authority. Neither option authorizes duplicates to
  bypass access or makes a hidden-history result observable.
- Ask one practical question at a time. After the answer, apply the required
  adversarial review and bring material amendments back before ratification.
