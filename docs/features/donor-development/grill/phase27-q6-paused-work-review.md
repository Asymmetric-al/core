## Why this question follows

The continuing relationship, ministry context, purpose boundary, deliberate entry
and default lead are settled. The original request explicitly leaves pause,
ending and reopening to resolve. The next question concerns accountability while
work is deliberately on hold, without introducing stage terminology.

Ordinary waiting for an agreed follow-up date is a weak founder question:
a dated shared task already represents the next action, and postponing one ask
does not establish that all partnership work should pause. Instead, ask about
the default when staff deliberately put further relationship-building on hold.
This does not decide all reasons for pausing or require a new state machine.

## Evidence and limits

- **Repository intent:** the [P27 roadmap](../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/roadmap.md:4091)

  native reminders. It does not resolve what paused work requires. The founder's

- **Accepted decisions:** [D3](phase27-d3-adversarial-review.md) preserves successive
  asks within one partnership and leaves exact lifecycle/collision rules open.
  [D5](phase27-q5-accountability-default.md) selects the partnership's lead without
  granting access or transferring every task. Neither settles Q6.
- **Published missions practice:** [TntConnect TNT Phase](https://www.tntware.com/tntconnect/help/en/pages/contacts_mpdphase.aspx),
  undated official desktop help, includes “Ask in Future” for requested later
  contact. [MPDX Status](https://help.mpdx.org/article/162-mpdx-status-mpd-phase),
  updated 2023-09-18, includes “Ask in Future” among active contacts.
  [MPDX Mobile Tasks](https://help.mpdx.org/article/500-tackle-mpdx-mobile-tasks),
  updated 2021-11-29, supports dated tasks and prompts for follow-up after a call.
  These support normal deferred follow-up, not a universal pause policy or a
  requirement to review every pause on a date. No vendor behavior was executed.
- **Product judgment:** a chosen internal review date is the recommended default
  for deliberately paused work, because it keeps responsibility visible without
  resuming outreach. The alternative reduces scheduled administrative work and
  suits pauses whose end depends on an external change. Neither advantage is a
  measured missions-user outcome; the example below is illustrative.

## Original single founder question — answered A

Imagine staff decide: “For now, we're putting work on developing this church's
partnership with this ministry on hold.”

**Should Core normally remind the responsible lead to review that pause?**

**A — Choose an internal review date (recommended).** On that date, remind the
lead to decide whether the work should stay on hold, resume or end. Reaching the
date does not itself change the partnership's state.

**B — Leave it on hold until someone deliberately returns.** Keep the lead and
history, but create no review reminder by default. This avoids a scheduled
review when staff are waiting for circumstances to change.

Either option concerns internal relationship-building work. It does not itself
contact the church, change contact permission, stop donations or commitments,
or discard already-agreed obligations. The reason for recommending A is to avoid
forgotten work, not to encourage more frequent solicitation.

## Boundaries for the eventual answer review

- This chooses a default, not a mandatory recurring review for every pause or a
  fixed duration. Do not infer exceptional indefinite-pause rules from silence.
- Distinguish a temporary pause from ending cultivation and from an authoritative
  contact restriction. An internal review never grants permission to contact.
- A deferred ask alone must not freeze unrelated promises or stewardship. Exact
  treatment of open tasks and asks must be resolved before a pause command ships.
- No automatic outreach, automatic resumption, new workflow platform, financial
  mutation, ownership transfer or increased disclosure follows from this choice.
- If A is selected, prefer existing appropriate shared work over a duplicate
  review task. Eligibility loss, changed review dates and concurrent actions must
  preserve responsibility and history under existing source boundaries.
- Physical states, indexes, unique constraints and the full pause/end/reopen
  transition contract remain unselected. This note is not evidence that Core has
  implemented or qualified pause behavior.
