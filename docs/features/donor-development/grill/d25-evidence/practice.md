# D25 practice evidence — gifts awaiting finance

2026-10-10 UTC (2026-10-11 Asia/Bangkok). Research for selected A: a source-qualified
appeal view of gifts awaiting finance, with responsibility and access to the existing
intake process. Recommendations below are review judgments, not additional ratified
policy. No accounts, staff interviews, product writes or external contacts were used.

## Finding

There is direct missions-agency evidence for the handoff, beyond MPDX's terminology.
Reliant's current Check Giving FAQ addresses a donor handing a check to a missionary
and forwarding it to the central office. It also encourages direct-to-office giving.
This supports an applicable handoff path, not a required stage for every gift.

The most consequential error would be creating an independently editable appeal
gift record beside finance intake. Selected A should make the existing source-owned
work visible and actionable. It does not establish another financial writer, receipt
trigger, universal custody system or proof that the current repository already has
the required source contract.

## Strongest B and minimum useful A

**B remains credible:** finance intake and ordinary shared tasks can track the check
without an appeal-specific pending view. Where finance already owns and sees the
entire handoff, another view could be redundant. It avoids maintaining yet another
status that might disagree with finance. No evidence here measures how frequently
appeal staff need the additional visibility.

**Minimum A:** show the same qualified intake/work item in appeal context, explain
why it awaits finance, identify the eligible person responsible for the next action,
and offer permitted source actions. On source resolution, retain an understandable
link to the outcome. A reporter should not retype the gift into a second system or
periodically certify physical possession merely to keep the item alive. Native
shared work handles follow-through; no parallel queue, mandatory second approver,
new shipping service or general custody-management product follows from A.

## Material concerns, exact clauses and proof

Grades are qualitative judgments, not measured failure rates. The scenarios are
illustrative tests, not reports of agency behavior.

### P1 — A report becomes financial truth or claims current possession

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** An unposted draft, a mailed check or a
donor promise could all be mislabeled as money received or currently in staff hands.

**Proposed clause:** “Awaiting finance identifies a qualified source-owned handoff,
not received funding, commitment fulfillment, acceptance or bank clearance. Preserve
what was reported or verified and its relevant time; do not infer present physical
possession from an old receipt report, received-date field or draft. Only source
authority establishes recording and financial outcomes.”

**Proof:** A promise that a check is coming does not create a gift-in-hand item.
Forwarding a previously received check does not leave a misleading assertion that
the reporter still holds it. Authorized staff can distinguish pending intake from
posted money. Posting a provisional check does not falsely imply bank clearance.

### P2 — The same check becomes two gifts, or the wrong gift resolves the handoff

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** Reporter and finance may act at different
times; a donor may have both recurring support and an appeal-specific gift.

**Proposed clause:** “The appeal consumes the same authoritative intake identity
and qualified outcome association. A repeated submission or another view does not
create duplicate intake or money. Amount, donor and date similarity alone do not
prove that a posted gift resolves this handoff.”

**Proof:** Finance records the check before the reporter returns to the appeal:
staff can resolve the existing handoff through the source rather than create another
gift. A monthly gift of the same amount does not automatically close a special-appeal
handoff. A supporter can have one recorded gift and another pending item; retries
and duplicate reports have an owner-qualified recovery path with preserved history.
This does not settle appeal attribution or P16 fulfillment matching.

### P3 — Moving the check or closing appeal work silently ends responsibility

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** Mailing, assigning or completing an
ordinary task does not establish that finance recorded the contribution.

**Proposed clause:** “Keep the next internal action and its responsible person
understandable until the source records the gift or authoritatively resolves the
exception. Assignment is responsibility, not proof of custody or financial access.
An appeal closure, audience removal or task completion does not certify recording
or discard unresolved finance work.”

**Proof:** A transfer to another staff member preserves eligible responsibility.
A lost, returned, mistaken or unacceptable check can reach an explicit qualified
exception outcome without fabricated money or an item that must remain pending
forever. One recorded item does not complete other pending items from the same
supporter. A contact restriction permits authorized internal handling while still
blocking prohibited solicitation; it does not force another donor reminder.

### P4 — Check evidence and acknowledgments escape their proper controls

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** Broad appeal access could expose images,
signatures or banking details; premature acknowledgment could misstate acceptance.

**Proposed clause:** “Expose only source-permitted handoff information needed for
the action. Do not require or copy a check image, bank details or signature into an
appeal note, notification, export or ordinary task. Supporting evidence, if needed,
uses its governed source and access rules. Recording a handoff does not itself send
an acknowledgment, create a tax receipt or grant financial authority.”

**Proof:** A staff member can understand their permitted next action without gaining
access to restricted payment documents. Shared-task previews and notifications do
not disclose protected details. Ordinary authorized personal thanks remains distinct
from a source-authorized official receipt; D25 does not introduce a new sending lane
or require staff to withhold human courtesy until bank clearance.

### P5 — Checks become an unjustified template for all support

**Medium severity; plausible likelihood.** A universal pending step would burden
online gifts and could turn donated equipment, hospitality or prayer into money.

**Proposed clause:** “Show handoff work only where qualified source facts make it
applicable. Preserve multiple items, partial outcomes and relevant tender/entity/
currency distinctions. Noncash acceptance and valuation remain source-owned;
nonfinancial partnership participation does not become financial intake.”

**Proof:** A directly processed online gift needs no invented staff-possession step.
Processing one of two checks leaves the other distinguishable. A proposed laptop
donation follows applicable acceptance/intake authority without a made-up cash value;
a promise to pray or host a visit stays within D22's nonfinancial request meaning.
No new asset-inventory or jurisdiction-policy engine is implied.

## Evidence limits and practical validation

The sources establish that handoffs and staged gift entry exist. They do not prove
target agencies' volumes, device preferences, acceptance policies, typical time to
finance, or a need for image capture. Core's P15 handoff/read contract must still be
qualified; external documentation is not implementation proof.

A later usability check should cover a check awaiting entry, an already recorded
check, two gifts from one supporter, an exception and restricted financial evidence.
Ask staff to identify what happened, what is still unknown, who acts next and whether
money is recorded. Observe unnecessary re-entry and mistaken assumptions; no invented
success rate, agency feedback or numerical performance claim is offered.
