## Exact corrected D4 — ratified 2026-10-09

> **An authorized person deliberately starts new cultivation, individually or
> for an explicitly reviewed finite group.** This records the organization's
> decision to undertake internal development work, not the supporter's agreement,
> prayer participation, financial commitment or communication consent. A gift,
> referral, score, import or other record arrival does not itself start a journey.
>
> Use one clear contextual action when the authorized Party, identified ministry,
> purpose and intended work are already understood. That action may also record
> the expressly included referral, ask or shared work through its qualified
> owners. Ask only for missing or materially changed information. Do not require
> another enrollment ceremony, manager approval, candidate queue, gift, financial
> account, email address or public Page. Ordinary Party/referral/task work remains
> available before the ministry is identified.
>
> A reviewed group binds the selected identities and intended ministry/purpose/
> work. It is not standing permission for future matches of a saved view. Show
> the actual selected scope and distinguish selected rows from all results; do
> not silently expand, retarget or substitute members during execution or retry.
> Existing durable execution may complete the same human-directed operation in
> the background. Preserve its human initiator and actual execution context;
> re-prove current source authority and eligibility at consequential boundaries.
> A recorded review is neither permanent permission nor a requirement to keep a
> browser session open.
>
> **Each member's admission is atomic; independent members of a group may
> complete separately.** Commit its accepted source change, required history and
> receipt, and appropriate existing due work or an owned actionable exception
> together. A queued request is not a completed admission. Preserve truthful,
> currently permitted results for accepted, reused, unaccepted and unresolved
> work. Resume the same intended operation without repeating confirmed effects;
> resolve lost responses from authoritative receipts. Cancellation stops work
> that has not been admitted; it does not undo completed admissions. Corrections
> use the owning sources and preserve reused work, later changes and history.
>
> D2/D3 govern identity, purpose and safe reuse. Reuse does not reset stage,
> ownership, instructions or obligations; an explicitly requested additional
> change needs its own authority. Deduplication grants no access, exposes no
> hidden journey and permits no workaround duplicate. Results, counts and
> recovery retain current disclosure rules. No new role or sharing grant follows.
>
> Admission itself sends no donor message and grants no financial or contact
> authority. Existing authorized source synchronization, receipts, restrictions
> and already accepted follow-up continue under their owners without enrollment
> clicks. Migration may preserve evidenced existing work and history, separately
> from admitting new work; imports remain outward-effect-dark. D4 adopts no
> standing automatic admission policy, extra rule builder, batch platform,
> permission system or donor-facing setup. Ordinary native cultivation remains
> independent of optional Workflow Studio.

**Ratified material tightening:** “reviewed group” means finite selected intent,
not a future-changing query; background execution retains current authority;
and independent member outcomes may commit separately, with atomic individual
admission, visible partial results and cancellation rather than fictitious undo.
These are consequential product semantics, so they are made explicit rather than
hidden in an engineering choice. Detailed labels/layout, numeric capacity,
initial stage, pause/end/reopen and merge collisions are not decided by D4.

## Adversarial check

### What could go wrong with this answer?

A “reviewed group” could become a live query, a blanket success toast or an old
permission token. A retry could duplicate tasks/asks even if the journey itself
is unique. The amended D4 binds finite intent and authoritative item outcomes.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

We have not established that every agency wants human-only admission, that
present batch code is safe to reuse unchanged, or that a configured source exists
for every future operation. A human click is intent, not donor assent or proof
that the software has met its authority and transaction obligations.

### How does this affect the whole product?

One P27 source admission serves individual/group operations and later P28
projections. P9, P12, finance, communications and durable execution retain their
owners. Restricting new admission does not disable their existing valid work.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

Use the action the user came to perform, with its start/reuse consequence clear.
Avoid repeated entry and manager ceremonies. Group progress survives interrupted
connections, and the user can distinguish completed work from remaining work.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

Explicit selection, captured scope, meaningful action labels, per-record results
and source-owned retry are supported by official product and technical guidance.
Vendor automatic membership, weak deduplication and no-undo limitations are
counterexamples to assess, not instructions to copy.

### Does this fit Asym’s existing repo and product direction?

Yes. CRM truth remains in Asym Postgres; compound writes remain server-command
owned; P12 governs current authority; P9 supplies shared work; P34 remains the
configurable automation owner. Existing coarse role gates and batch helpers are
implementation evidence to qualify, not substitutes for these contracts.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

Keep A with the explicit finite-group, per-member atomicity, partial-result and
recovery amendments above. No new approval layer, donor consent flow or optional
workflow dependency is justified.

## Alternatives and simplicity test

**Chosen A:** the person deliberately selects new work, individually or in a
reviewed group. It makes responsibility intelligible without assuming every
incoming record warrants a continuing journey. Ordinary entry should be one
useful action when its context is known, not a separate setup process.

**Strongest alternative B:** an organization qualifies a repeatable policy so
source events automatically choose new work. This can reduce repeated selection
and is a legitimate future product choice. P34 owns configurable trigger/filter/
action rules; fixed native behavior is a separate architectural category but
still needs an explicit P27 policy decision. Neither is already approved by A.
No source establishes that B is universally better for missions organizations.
Making A cumbersome would not be evidence for B.

**No-build alternative:** use existing Party, referral and shared-task records
until deliberate continuing cultivation is needed. D1–D3 justify a thin source
journey once that distinct responsibility exists. A donor database, private
candidate queue, approval engine, permanent portfolio list, purpose catalog or
new workflow system does not follow.

**Why independent members can complete separately:** selecting several unrelated
relationships expresses several intentions. One changed/ineligible target need
not erase other committed work. This needs reliable item outcomes, not a long
transaction held across a review screen or a provider call. A single item with
several linked writes remains atomic. A different coupled group operation would
need a separately explicit source contract; D4 does not promise group rollback.

## Database probe and limits

The [seven-check D4 probe](d4-evidence/selection-transaction-probe/README.md)
used only synthetic data in a network-disabled, disposable PostgreSQL 17.11
container. No application environment, source SQL, credentials, host mounts or
published ports were used. Cleanup removed the container and verified absence.
Scripts, results and immutable image/run metadata are preserved.

| Observation                                                                                                                   | Consequence                                                                               |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Review selected IDs `1,2`; after one arrival and one filter change, the live query selected `1,3`.                            | Rerunning a query can both add unreviewed work and lose reviewed work.                    |
| Fixed selected IDs remained `1,2`.                                                                                            | Membership can be preserved; source meaning and current authority are not thereby frozen. |
| Failure in item two of one transaction left zero parent/obligation rows.                                                      | Whole-transaction rollback includes earlier valid items.                                  |
| Separate item transactions retained the successful pair and removed the failed pair; a later independent item could complete. | Per-member atomicity and partial completion can coexist using ordinary SQL.               |
| Released savepoint work disappeared after outer rollback.                                                                     | Savepoint release or a loop iteration is not a durable success boundary.                  |

All seven assertions passed. These are sequential, deterministic counterexamples,
not a concurrent-worker or load test. The prior [D3 probe](d3-evidence/postgres-mechanics/README.md)
was not rerun; its 21 checks remain bounded evidence for constraints, nullable-key
pitfalls, RLS-hidden uniqueness and transaction/snapshot mechanics. Neither
experiment implements source authority, request receipts or actual bulk UX.

Current primary PostgreSQL and Supabase guidance confirms that separate HTTP requests do not
become one SQL transaction, `SKIP LOCKED` is not a complete population reader,
and serialization/lock failures need safe transaction retry. Installed client
retry behavior is not durable write idempotency. The Supabase changelog was
rechecked, including September 25's 15.19/17.11 extension-specific notices; D4
requires none of those optional features. Data API default-exposure changes do
not repair existing grants. No hosted upgrade or deployment safety is inferred.

## Category-by-category review

Severity describes impact if the named mistake occurs. Likelihood is qualitative
engineering judgment conditional on the shortcut, not an incident probability.
**Critical** concerns forbidden disclosure or authority; **High** concerns wrong,
lost or duplicated accepted work or substantial trust harm; **Medium** concerns
avoidable friction, operational cost or capacity failure. These are prevention
requirements in an emerging decision, not claims that an unimplemented P27
feature is currently exploitable.

### C01 — Problem validity, necessity, and alternatives

**Material concern: yes. Severity: Medium; likelihood: high if entry becomes a
separate administrative workflow.** A mandatory journey form after recording a
referral/ask duplicates work; enrolling every contact solves no established need.
Conversely, removing the journey entirely would discard D1–D3's distinct ongoing
responsibility. MPDX/Tnt batch entry and Salesforce quick actions support the
simpler contextual approach ([UX](d4-evidence/ux.md), [missions](d4-evidence/ministry.md)).
Neither source proves a universal missions policy.

**Effect on A:** retain and narrow it to deliberate new cultivation, not every
CRM action. **Permanent fix / exact carry-forward wording:** “Offer contextual
start/reuse with the explicitly included work when required meaning is known.
Collect only missing or materially changed information. Recording a contact,
referral, task or note without that expressed consequence does not enroll it.”
Do not freeze the screen layout, a separate wizard, a purpose taxonomy or bulk
job storage schema into this decision.

**Proof:** introduction-only, one-action deliberate start and reviewed group all
work without re-entering known data; existing Party/tasks remain usable without
a journey. Compare with B using actual intended-user work before claiming an
improvement, not invented performance or fundraising statistics.

### C02 — Brittleness

**Material concern: yes. Severity: High; likelihood: high if a filter or mutable
label represents reviewed intent.** Later matches, changed filters, page eviction,
renames, ministry retirement or duplicate repairs can change who is processed.
The new SQL probe reproduced membership drift; the current grid retains at most
eight 50-record pages, which is not a complete durable selection.

**Effect on A:** requires the finite-selection amendment. **Permanent fix / exact
wording:** “Bind the reviewed identities and action meaning before processing.
A changed saved view or newly matching record cannot enlarge that operation.
Revalidate material source eligibility/revisions without silently replacing the
selected Party, ministry, purpose or intended work. Routine display changes alone
do not demand a new review.” Capture references and needed provenance, not copied
full records or a long-lived database snapshot across human review.

**Proof:** add/remove matches after review; edit filters; evict pages; rename a
ministry; retire a target; repair a duplicate. Only the reviewed intent remains
eligible for consideration; material changes get a safe unaccepted/review result,
not an invented successor or false success.

### C03 — Technical debt

**Material concern: yes. Severity: High; likelihood: medium-high if existing
helpers are copied as finished infrastructure.** Current task/link/event and
batch/items use separate calls; contribution batch start lacks a durable
start-operation lookup, and initial client continuation depends on receiving the
first response. CRM notes' retry path does not fully bind the new input meaning;
Support retry status persistence has an unchecked returned error. These are
specific reuse gaps in [the code review](d4-evidence/database.md), not a reason to
build another task system or rewrite unrelated domains during grooming.

**Effect on A:** implementation qualification, not rejection. **Permanent fix /
exact wording:** “Qualify the existing owner command, receipt and executor seams
for D4; consolidate only the missing source guarantees. Do not copy multi-call
writes, UI seed state or a request correlation ID as atomic admission or durable
idempotency. Keep business mutations in `packages/api`.”

**Proof:** exercise actual owner calls with write/status failures and response
loss. Track exact upstream seams requiring correction; no broad platform rewrite
or optional-phase delivery gate substitutes for the narrow required guarantees.

### C04 — Edge cases

**Material concern: yes. Severity: High; likelihood: medium-high across ordinary
missions work.** Church versus contact, nonfinancial partner, unknown ministry,
existing journey, paused/ended work, duplicate Party, no eligible owner,
never-ask instructions, privacy aliases and a worker leaving mid-group can each
make a plausible default wrong. Ministry evidence supports these distinctions,
not a single universal process.

**Effect on A:** narrows defaults; preserves D1–D3. **Permanent fix / exact wording:**
“Select the actual Party and qualified ministry/purpose. A church contact, spouse,
shared mailbox or referrer is not a substitute subject or authority. Missing
ministry leaves ordinary authorized Party/task work available. Reuse preserves
existing progress and obligations; paused/ended/merge cases follow their separately
ratified lifecycle rather than silently reopening, resetting or duplicating.”
An existing owned needs-assignment exception may represent lost responsibility;
new active admission cannot invent an eligible queue or pretend missing work is
complete.

**Proof:** the scenario matrix below covers these cases, including no gift/email,
zero ministry participants and already-established partnerships. Do not fill an
unknown value with a dummy ministry, zero money or fabricated assent.

### C05 — Footguns

**Material concern: yes. Severity: High; likelihood: high for ambiguous bulk
controls and retry.** “Select all” can mean one page or all results; a slow
connection encourages another click; “Start” can reset existing work; “Undo” can
erase another person's task. Virtuous's page/all distinction and no-undo warning
are direct procedural evidence; Core's current export uses a report slice.

**Effect on A:** requires explicit scope and safe recovery. **Permanent fix /
exact wording:** “State the selected scope and action consequence. Preserve the
same operation identity across repeated submission and recovery. Start/reuse
never overwrites existing stage/owner/work as an incidental upsert. Cancellation
stops unadmitted work; correction cannot delete reused work or accepted effects.”
Use proportionate checked/reviewed controls rather than routine extra approval.

**Proof:** select page versus all explicitly supported results; double submit;
lose first response; start against existing work; cancel after one member commits.
No scope surprise, duplicated work or misleading global rollback.

### C06 — Tenant safety

**Material concern: yes. Severity: Critical; likelihood: medium if context or
shared workers are trusted implicitly.** A later active-Tenant switch, stale cache,
forged member ID or shared worker can process/read the wrong scope. Different
Legal Entities inside one Tenant are not interchangeable. P12's trusted-context
and deferred-action rules, plus workflow tenant boundaries, govern this risk.

**Effect on A:** inherited mandatory boundary. **Permanent fix / exact wording:**
“Bind each operation to its server-validated Tenant Authorization Context and
resolve each item's exact source scope. Preserve the original context reference;
do not retarget queued work to the person's later selected Tenant. Every item,
result, count, cache, work claim, export and retry remains context-qualified.”
Within-Tenant multi-entity work, if offered, needs each exact admitted scope; a
Tenant-wide grant or default is not presumed. Party remains its actual compatible
Tenant scope rather than gaining a fabricated Legal Entity column.

**Proof:** two Tenants, two same-Tenant entities, shared IDs, context switch during
processing and late old-context responses. Denied scope produces no mutation,
effect, result leak or cache contamination.

### C07 — Database, RLS, and authorization safety

**Material concern: yes. Severity: Critical; likelihood: medium-high under generic
CRUD/upsert or service-role reuse.** Human review does not enforce a foreign key,
prevent actor spoofing, authorize hidden reuse or stop an allowed update turning
into forbidden scope. Multiple permissive policies OR; integrity checks can
reveal RLS-hidden rows. No D4 source implementation was found in the inspected
code; no deployed catalog was qualified.

**Effect on A:** inherited architecture plus exact source proof. **Permanent fix /
exact wording:** “Use one qualified source mutation boundary and database-enforced
required identities, compatible composite references and semantic uniqueness
once lifecycle is settled. Resolve Tenant, actor and attribution from trusted
context; validate selected responsibility references against the existing owner.
Protect immutable scope/history; check both the existing and proposed row and
all independently changed fields. No browser or service credential bypass may
manufacture human admission.”

The future database design must explicitly account for:

- Non-null required scope; compatible `(tenant_id, party_id)` and exact Ministry
  Assignment Tenant/Legal Entity relationships; indexes for actual lookups and
  RLS paths; no redundant index merely because an FK exists. A nullable composite
  FK under `MATCH SIMPLE` can skip checks; a `CHECK` alone does not reject NULL.
- Unique keys for the separately settled journey identity and durable command
  meaning. A request UUID alone is not the same ordinary intent; a journey unique
  key alone does not deduplicate asks/tasks. No `active` predicate chooses
  pause/end/reopen rules. Identity/scope/purpose edits need source-controlled
  correction, not an unrestricted update.
- Source-governed delete/disposal: no arbitrary cascade deleting accepted work,
  required receipts or another owner's task; corrections retain attributable
  history while lawful retention can remove governed content. Trusted timestamps
  and explicit date-only versus instant types preserve temporal meaning.
- RLS for exposed tables and required internal protection; all applicable grants
  and policies, not one new restrictive-looking policy. Test `USING` and resulting
  `WITH CHECK` behavior. An omitted explicit `WITH CHECK` may inherit `USING`;
  absence of the keyword alone is not proof of a flaw.
- Invoker/unexposed views as appropriate; safe function owner, schema-qualified
  access/search path, EXECUTE and schema grants. `SECURITY DEFINER` uses its
  owner's privileges; it does not invariably bypass RLS. `FORCE RLS` does not
  constrain a `BYPASSRLS` role. Privileged service paths need the same source/P12
  checks. Inspect Data API/GraphQL, RPC, worker, import, export and Storage doors;
  D4 creates no attachment store or reason to broaden raw grants.
- No new financial amount store. Any expressly included ask keeps its own exact
  currency-aware values and source contract; money cannot be inferred from entry.
  Migration/backfill must preserve those meanings and reject bad references.

**Proof:** real isolated Supabase catalogs and authenticated public source commands;
SELECT/INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE/UPSERT/RETURNING and alternate doors; forged actor,
owner and scope, mixed policies, revoked privileges, malicious proposed-row changes,
NULL/reference/collision cases, failure injection and concurrent admission. RLS
alone does not qualify P12's source/field/purpose/epoch contract. The earlier SQL
mechanics counterexamples are evidence for this requirement, not its discharge.

### C08 — Overengineering

**Material concern: yes. Severity: Medium; likelihood: medium-high in response to
bulk and security requirements.** A preview service, candidate CRM, manager queue,
purpose catalog, workflow language or generic distributed saga would burden the
ordinary relationship action. A “reviewed selection” is an operation's minimum
intent/provenance, not permanent portfolio membership. Ordinary relational
mechanisms and existing owner commands are sufficient in principle.

**Effect on A:** narrows implementation and UX. **Permanent fix / exact wording:**
“Keep one thin source admission with individual and bounded group entry. Reuse
shared tasks, existing durable execution and approved views. Store only the
selection/meaning/receipts required for correctness; no new general batch or
approval platform. Do not hold a database transaction open while a person reviews.”
An ordinary start needs no free-text justification merely to imitate D3's
exceptional independent-purpose explanation.

**Proof:** the smallest useful single-item path works without Studio, AI, future
reporting, donor setup or manager action. Review every added data object/control
against a demonstrated invariant, not a speculative future workflow.

### C09 — UX/UI and user friction

**Material concern: yes. Severity: High for mistaken meaning or lost work;
Medium for friction. Likelihood: high if generic bulk/wizard UI is copied.** Entry
can be misunderstood as donor acceptance; extra forms, page loss, inaccessible
selection and disappearing results make routine field work difficult. Automatic
receipt/gift sync should not be held hostage to cultivation setup.

**Effect on A:** preserve contextual convenience and make outcomes understandable.
**Permanent fix / exact wording:** “Use clear action language for beginning
internal work; do not label a prospect an accepted partner. Show known context
and request only needed changes. Reuse Core's shared Base UI/base-maia controls,
keyboard-operable selection and announced progress/errors. Preserve selection,
focus and recoverable progress through navigation and weak connections without
claiming unacknowledged work succeeded.”

Use current permitted aliases and international display names; require neither a
Western first/last-name split nor email/address merely to enroll an existing
Party. Existing date/timezone owners govern due work; avoid UTC date shifts,
ambiguous locale dates and new timezone rules in the picker. Small screens need
usable counts, disambiguation and non-hover actions. Admission remains
server-authoritative; D4 adds no offline mutation permission or sensitive local
roster cache. Donor/public surfaces receive no new setup or checkout step.

**Proof:** actual contextual/referral/group flows with keyboard, screen reader,
zoom/narrow viewport, international names, date-only deadlines and interrupted
connections; currently permitted recovery after reauthentication. Representative
missionaries and staff must distinguish internal intent from supporter agreement
and recover a partial result without developer help. WCAG error prevention
permits reversible, checked or confirmed approaches; it does not mandate a modal
for every click. Source dates and evidence limits are in the UX appendix.

### C10 — Source of truth, ownership, and domain invariants

**Material concern: yes. Severity: High; likelihood: medium-high if the batch or
UI becomes authoritative.** A group summary, saved view, imported partner label
or worker result could overwrite source truth; selecting a new owner could be
mistaken for access. P27/P9/P12 and finance/communication contracts prohibit this
([ownership](d4-evidence/ownership.md), [financial review](d4-evidence/financial-effects.md)).

**Effect on A:** preserve distinct facts. **Permanent fix / exact wording:** “P27
owns admission and journey meaning; the source receipt establishes its accepted
outcome. Group progress is a projection of those outcomes. P9 owns Party/ministry/
assignment/shared-task facts, P12 authority, and financial/communication domains
their facts. A review snapshot, imported label or execution status cannot become
a competing writer.”

**Proof:** compare group result to source receipts after crash, correction and
restore; resource existence alone must not falsely prove this command succeeded.
Reuse cannot reset existing stage/owner/obligations. The invariant/ownership table
below is the minimum cross-artifact trace; it grants no new source powers.

### C11 — Hidden coupling

**Material concern: yes. Severity: High; likelihood: medium if “human only” or
“use workflows” is interpreted broadly.** A ban on new automatic admission could
stop ordinary synchronization or existing follow-up; a worker could require
optional Studio just to finish a reviewed group. Conversely, P34 rules or P42
web behavior might be used as an alternative admission authority. Governing
OpenSpec separates durable execution from configurable policy.

**Effect on A:** narrow its scope to selection of new work. **Permanent fix / exact
wording:** “Mechanical execution/retry of finite human intent and existing
source-native work remain possible without optional Studio. New automatic
selection is not admitted by D4. Consume only the exact qualified Party/ministry,
authority, shared-work and executor seams; do not require full P21/P22/P28/P30/
P34/P42 delivery or copy another product's policy.”

**Proof:** optional products off; gift recorded and independently authorized
receipt proceeds without a journey; accepted native follow-up still runs; a
future-matching event cannot enter new cultivation. A source outage blocks only
the action/fact requiring it, not unrelated permitted work or a false zero.

### C12 — Failure modes

**Material concern: yes. Severity: High; likelihood: high under normal network or
worker failure.** Request acceptance can be mistaken for individual admission;
task/history can fail after a journey write; commit can succeed before response
or progress storage fails. Current separate-call examples and the SQL probe make
these concrete hazards.

**Effect on A:** requires atomic member acceptance and recoverable partial outcomes.
**Permanent fix / exact wording:** “Persist the reviewed operation consistently
before processing. Each item commits its source change, required local work or
owned actionable exception, history and receipt atomically. Derived progress
failure cannot invalidate a source commit. Recover from source evidence before
repeating an unknown effect. Independent successes survive later item failure.”
Any necessary handoff intent uses existing transactional outbox/source patterns;
external providers are never inside the local transaction. Do not create a
journey with an unowned placeholder task to make an API look successful.

**Proof:** fail after every local write; drop first response; crash after source
commit but before group-result update; make executor unavailable; resume after
restart. The record, due work, history and receipts agree. Unknown stays visibly
unresolved under current permission until reconciled, rather than false failure
or invented success. Reused work survives failed additional changes.

### C13 — Lifecycle, temporal correctness, concurrency, and idempotency

**Material concern: yes. Severity: High, Critical for revocation races; likelihood:
high under retries and concurrent staff work.** Cancellation, stale workers,
expired claims and new request IDs can duplicate or erase work. A prior review
can outlive current authority. PostgreSQL snapshot/lock semantics and current
batch effect-before-status behavior establish the mechanisms.

**Effect on A:** explicit operation lifecycle, without choosing journey lifetime.
**Permanent fix / exact wording:** “Keep one scoped logical command identity and
immutable reviewed meaning across submission/reload/retry. Changed meaning under
the same key rejects. Each admission and cancellation has a source-defined
ordering with current authority and material revisions. Resolve timeouts from
receipts; expired claims are not proof of failure. Late workers cannot overwrite
a newer authoritative disposition or repeat accepted effects.”

Conceptual conditions, not a mandated database enum:

- Before submission, selection may be edited/discarded; nothing is admitted.
- A durably received finite request may have pending items; receipt is not full
  completion. Current context is re-proved at actual source acceptance.
- An item may be newly admitted, safely reused, not admitted with a permitted
  explanation, or still unresolved. Result visibility is separate from outcome:
  withheld details do not mean failure or absence.
- Cancellation prevents remaining unadmitted work when its fence wins; an item
  whose acceptance won remains committed. A later correction is a new qualified
  source operation, not rewriting the original outcome.
- Retry resolves the same command/effect and current allowed result. Changed
  intended membership/work requires a new deliberate action. Newly matching
  records never inherit the earlier review.
- Terminal confirmed outcomes do not become pending just because a worker lease
  expires. New review can resolve materially changed unaccepted work; it cannot
  replay completed actions. A genuinely new journey lifetime still awaits the
  separately ratified pause/end/reopen/merge rules.

Use trusted recorded-at instants; preserve source-reported historic/date-only
facts separately. Imports or backdating never create current intent. A same-user
Tenant switch cannot retarget work, and accepted source obligations do not vanish
because their initiating person later leaves. The applicable member/NHI/operator
context governs continuation; there is no new perpetual delegated authority.

**Proof:** independent connections with barriers for create/create, accept/cancel,
revoke/admit, source-retire/admit and stale-worker completion. Test same key/same
intent, same key/changed intent, distinct commands for the same ordinary work,
retry after lost response and interrupted workers. Verify both admissible causal
orders and exact source outcomes, not only HTTP status.
