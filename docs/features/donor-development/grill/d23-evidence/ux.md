# D23 UX evidence — built-in review only when needed

2026-10-10 UTC / 2026-10-11 Asia/Bangkok. The founder selected D23 A. This is research and a proposed coherent interaction contract, not implemented behavior, a formal specification or a claim of practitioner validation. Source HEAD: `225c02e9499bd6c0d493223cd4b1a4120ff787e3`.

## Recommendation and boundaries

Keep A with a small review section inside the existing Request. Routine authorized work stays quick. The proposed policy is an organization default, initially **Review not required**, and a qualified per-request choice: **Use organization default / Require review / Review not required**. Only actors with the current source-policy capability may change the policy or override it; ordinary authors cannot waive review by editing the Request. There is no amount-threshold detector, rule builder, mandatory chain, CMS lane or reviewer-invitation dependency.

Use readable effective-state text in daily work, such as “Review required · Organization default.” Expose the control to change that requirement only where authorized, with its effect apparent. Do not make every author choose an override or acknowledge the default. Organization settings need the two default options and their plain-language effect on governed Request actions, not a setup wizard. A changed policy does not rewrite past approvals, presentations or responses; current applicability must be resolved by the source.

The approval means **Approved to present these terms**. It is not a supporter response, commitment, gift, contact permission or proof that presentation occurred. [Q23](../phase27-q23-request-review-policy.md):54–76 explicitly separates prospective authorization from truthful historical recording. [Ratified D22](../phase27-d22-adversarial-review.md):255–279 preserves actual terms, late facts, financial truth and journey lifecycle. No review setting silently resumes a paused partnership, reopens closed work or activates optional Studio.

The strongest B alternative is coordination through existing tasks and conversations. It avoids building another decision boundary and works when teams accept responsibility for interpreting the conversation. Its cost is ambiguous applicability and weak assurance that the current terms were reviewed. A addresses those problems without reproducing a general approval platform. The recommendation is a product judgment, not a measured adoption or time-saving result.

## Daily experience and observable states

Keep Request terms, supporter response and internal review visibly separate. A compact **Internal review** area shows one clear state, the responsible next actor and the relevant action. The following are presentation meanings, not a mandated database enum or a requirement for six colored badges.

| Authoritative condition                                       | Plain presentation and next action                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Review not required                                           | “Review not required” with effective policy context; continue ordinary authorized work. Never label this “Approved.”                                                                                          |
| Review required, not submitted                                | “Needs review.” Show the eligible reviewer selection or established reviewer, the relevant terms and **Request review**. No extra confirmation or justification form.                                         |
| No eligible reviewer, lost eligibility, or routing unresolved | “Review needs a reviewer” or an appropriately limited unavailable state. Keep the work discoverable to authorized staff and offer source-owned responsibility recovery. Do not claim someone is reviewing it. |
| Submitted and routed                                          | “Waiting for Maria” with submission time and source-owned due information where applicable. The reviewer has **Review request**; the author can inspect submitted terms, edit or **Withdraw review request**. |
| Current terms approved                                        | “Approved to present · Maria · [decision time]” and **View reviewed terms**. Actual communication/contact and current action checks still apply. Approval is not a send operation.                            |
| Changes requested                                             | “Changes requested” with the actionable explanation and **Edit request** / **Request review again**. It is an internal proposal decision, not “Supporter declined.”                                           |
| Current approval cannot be established because reading failed | “Review status unavailable” and retry/recovery. Block only the dependent prospective action; do not infer approval or no review from an empty panel.                                                          |

Use permission-safe wording when the name or protected detail is unavailable. Loading, authoritative absence, restricted data and failed reads are different conditions. Filtered-out work is not closed work: after a successful decision removes a row from **My tasks**, retain understandable success and a permitted source link. A notification is only a route to the same review, not another decision form with a copied proposal.

## Exact proposed interaction clauses

**UX01 — One reviewable proposal, one deliberate submission.** The author uses the ordinary Request editor. When review is required, show the exact current terms and applicable purpose/action, then submit that version using **Request review**. Reuse already-entered data. Reviewer selection is limited to currently eligible people and does not grant access. Use one visible accountable reviewer; no default broad administrator audience. Do not advertise waiting until source acceptance and routing are confirmed.

**UX02 — An intelligible reviewer view.** Open from the Request or existing Tasks Hub into the same authorized source detail. Show who requested review, the supporter/ministry context, what is proposed and the actual relevant terms. For money, retain currency, cadence, term and owning financial scope; for prayer/hosting, show the practical invitation without invented monetary fields. Place **Approve to present** and **Request changes** beside the reviewed content. Request changes needs a brief actionable explanation; approval need not demand ceremonial prose. No mandatory second acknowledgment follows an already explicit decision.

**UX03 — Edits remain easy, but approved meaning cannot drift.** Before submission, edit normally. While a review is pending, show which version the reviewer is considering. Saving relevant new terms must visibly supersede the earlier review before the replacement is eligible for decision; submit the replacement deliberately rather than generating a review/notification on each keystroke. After approval, an edit that changes the approved invitation/action shows the consequence before Save: “These changes will need review before presentation,” when the effective policy still requires it. Preserve the earlier approved terms and decision in history. An internal note, staff forecast or unrelated task-date edit alone must not cause a new approval round. A free-text edit is not automatically cosmetic: changed outward promise, dates, scope, amount/currency/cadence or proposal content can change the approved meaning. The source owns the finite relevance rule and current policy evaluation; the browser cannot classify an edit to bypass it.

**UX04 — Reviewers decide the terms they actually saw.** Do not silently edit the proposal as part of approving it. If a reviewer proposes changes, make those changes visible and subject to the same submitted-version rule. Stale review tabs cannot approve unseen replacements. Comparing changed relevant values should be readable in ordinary language; expose the full current version as well as a useful change summary. Do not require raw JSON, hashes or UUIDs to understand the decision. A legitimate accepted approval remains historical and may remain valid when its reviewer later leaves; departure alone does not force reapproval. Current source/permission checks still govern future action.

**UX05 — Distinct controls for withdrawal, reassignment and correction.** **Withdraw review request** ends the pending review, not the partnership or Request. It preserves submitted terms/history and permits later resubmission if needed. **Change reviewer** uses a source-owned responsibility transition, verifies current eligibility and clearly identifies who now has the work. It is not editing a generic task assignee or transferring decision authority automatically. An erroneous accepted decision needs an authorized, traceable correction/withdrawal for future use; do not erase or backdate the original approval or imply past presentation never happened. These are contextual actions, not a mandatory management wizard.

**UX06 — Approval and fact recording stay separate.** Staff may directly record an already-made request and response with honest dates even when required review was absent. Show what is known about prior review separately; “No approval recorded” must not mean proven rejection, and an unknown historical policy must not be guessed. Do not fabricate a past approval to make the record save. Supported prospective actions enforce the current gate; Asym does not claim to prevent external conversation. A late response, correction or source-confirmed fulfillment does not need prospective presentation approval. None of these actions changes journey pause/review/closure or supporter response merely because internal review changed.

**UX07 — Due work has one owner and one place.** Admit only the actual current review or correction action through the registered source-work contract. The existing shared task opens the exact Request review; generic Complete, bulk completion, read notification, email delivery or task reassignment cannot approve it. Show the actual source-owned due date and timezone/aging meaning if present; do not import contribution corrections' 24-hour interval or invent an overdue date. Changes requested transfers the relevant next work to the author without leaving a stale reviewer task. Withdrawal/supersession ends the prior occurrence truthfully. A current no-recipient problem remains an owned exception; due reminders do not create permission, a fallback recipient or automatic approval. An old notification opens current authorized context and explains when its review is no longer actionable.

**UX08 — Honest save and decision recovery.** During submission, show an announced **Submitting review…** or **Saving decision…** state and prevent duplicate clicks. Keep entered feedback/terms within the open interaction when repairable errors occur. Dismissal must not claim to cancel a server operation already accepted. If the reply is lost, say the outcome could not be confirmed and offer checking/safe replay of the same submitted operation. Show accepted review state separately from notification delivery; a failed notification is not “Review submission failed” if the source accepted it. Stale decision, withdrawn review, changed reviewer and revoked access need current permitted recovery, not a blind retry against a new version. An old successful receipt cannot be presented as proof that the current proposal is approved.

Acceptance must exercise the actual product, not a toy approval form:

1. Complete a routine request under the initial no-review default; change the organization default and qualified per-request override. Confirm transparent applicability and no override grant from ordinary edit permission.
2. Submit and review both a monetary recurring proposal and a nonfinancial hosting invitation. Author and reviewer identify the exact terms, decision scope, responsible person and next action without interpreting status codes.
3. Submit with no eligible reviewer; remove eligibility while submission is open; reassign pending work. Never show false waiting, a stale actionable decision or unexplained lost work.
4. Edit before, during and after review. Compare a material term change against internal-note/forecast/task-date changes, concurrent decision, and old-link replay. Preserve history without unnecessary reapproval; reviewer departure alone does not revoke an otherwise valid accepted approval.
5. Request changes with usable feedback, correct/review again, withdraw pending review and correct an accepted decision. Distinguish each from canceling the Request, declining support and deleting history.
6. Record historical presentation/response without prior approval, including paused/closed partnerships. Save truthful facts without a fabricated decision or implicit reopening. Verify the prospective gate remains enforced separately.
7. Simulate dropped replies, a saved decision followed by failed notification, stale caches and access loss. Recover the exact accepted outcome without duplicate decision/task, stale approval or protected-detail exposure.
8. In keyboard/screen-reader/browser tests, verify persistent labels, named change controls, associated errors, pending/result announcements, overlay focus and source navigation. At 320 CSS pixels/equivalent zoom, long translated terms and RTL layouts remain readable, focus is not hidden by the mobile keyboard/footer, and essential actions need no hover. Preserve light/dark contrast and Core touch targets.
9. In a moderated staff pilot, participants correctly distinguish review not required, needs review, routed waiting, approved terms, changes requested and unavailable; identify who acts next; and record a past fact without inventing approval. Document observed confusion and revise the composition. No completion-time or aesthetic claim has yet been measured.

## Primary guidance and evidence limits

Fresh primary pages retrieved 2026-10-10 UTC:

- [WCAG 3.3.4 error prevention](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/error-prevention-legal-financial-data.html) supports correction/reversal or appropriate review safeguards. It explicitly does not require confirmation for every ordinary record save; it does not justify a second approval acknowledgment.
- [GOV.UK check answers](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/check-answers/) supports readable summaries, specific accessible Change controls and retained entered values. Adopt those qualities within the existing Request, not a mandatory separate page or GOV.UK component system.
- [W3C form notifications](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/notifications/) and [WCAG status messages](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html) support useful errors, repair and programmatically available async feedback. A disappearing row or a transient toast alone is insufficient recovery guidance.
- [Base UI Dialog](https://base-ui.com/react/components/dialog) documents focus entry/restoration and dialog composition. The real consumer must handle removed triggers, current destination, overflow and pending-operation dismissal; documentation does not prove those product choices.
- [WCAG reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html) and [focus not obscured](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html) supply measurable browser criteria with their stated scope and exceptions.

They were not rerun and do not qualify Request approvals. No new synthetic UI probe is justified before a P27 source/consumer exists. No application code, database or production state changed; no browser, delivery, accessibility conformance or practitioner test ran.
