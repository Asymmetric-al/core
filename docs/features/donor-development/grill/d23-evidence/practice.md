# D23 practice evidence — selective internal request review

2026-10-10 UTC (2026-10-11 Asia/Bangkok). Research for selected A: routine authorized
requests need no extra approval by default; an organization may require built-in
review for selected requests. Proposed safeguards below are review judgments, not
new ratified policy or implementation. No live accounts, integrations or agency
interviews were used.

## Finding

Current CRM and CMS documentation supports selective review, identifiable reviewers,
requested changes, withdrawal and decision history. It does **not** establish a
universal missionary approval practice, mandatory independent second person, or a
need for a general workflow engine. The strongest safeguard is to make approval
mean **permission for the identified proposed terms and prospective action**, while
keeping internal decisions separate from supporter responses and completed actions.

The strongest contrary evidence is architectural: vendors often tie approvals to
sales stages, quote publication or content publishing. Those mechanisms can be
useful in their domains and misleading here. An internal refusal is not a donor
decline; it must not close a continuing partnership or move its cultivation stage.

## Strongest B and smallest useful A

**B, tasks and conversations**, is credible where the team can readily establish
who reviewed what. It needs no separate review policy, queue or status model. A task
can ask a colleague to read a draft, and a conversation can explain changes. Its
limitation is that completing the task does not itself prove approval of the current
terms or enforce it before the application's supported outward action. This is a
product inference, not a measured failure of small teams.

**Minimum A:** reuse existing shared work for review assignments and reminders;
keep the actual decision and its applicable terms with the source-owned request.
Show whether review is needed, who can act, the decision and the next permissible
action. Submission, requested changes, a fresh decision and withdrawal should form
one understandable flow. Do not add universal stages, approval chains, automatic
escalation timers, new external tooling or a mandatory workflow designer.

## Material concerns and proposed safeguards

Severity/likelihood are qualitative review judgments, not incident rates. Scenarios
are illustrative tests rather than reports of agency behavior.

### P1 — A previous approval silently approves different terms

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** A funding request can change currency,
frequency, duration or conditions without changing its headline amount. A visit
invitation can add commitments for the agency without adding money.

**Proposed clause:** “Approval identifies the proposed terms and prospective action
reviewed. Any permitted material change makes the earlier decision inapplicable to
the changed proposal until the required current review is satisfied. Preserve the
earlier decision as history; do not relabel it as rejection. Unrelated notes, task
completion and factual response recording do not automatically invalidate approval.”

**Proof:** An illustrative monthly request changed to annual support, or a hosting
invitation changed to include agency-funded travel, cannot use the old approval.
Fixing an internal typo that changes no reviewed meaning does not require pointless
resubmission. The reviewer can understand the actual current proposal, not merely
see an Approved badge. This does not authorize otherwise prohibited identity changes.

### P2 — Internal review overwrites ministry or historical truth

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** Vendors' sales-stage coupling is not a
safe template for a continuing ministry relationship.

**Proposed clause:** “Review status, supporter response, cultivation stage and
partnership lifecycle are different facts. Approval does not send, publish, resume,
reopen, create a commitment or confer contact/payment authority. Rejection of a
proposal is not rejection by the supporter. Authorized staff may record an already
made request and its actual response without inventing earlier approval.”

**Proof:** Staff can truthfully record an off-platform conversation that occurred
without required approval; the system does not certify compliance retroactively.
A declined internal proposal leaves existing gifts, prayer participation and
relationship work intact. Prospective supported actions still enforce applicable
review and other owner requirements.

### P3 — Small-team convenience defeats policy, or review becomes universal

**High severity for an authority bypass; plausible likelihood.** Assigning an
unavailable reviewer must not mean automatic approval. Conversely, copying a
mandatory two-person rule would impose a product policy the founder did not choose.

**Proposed clause:** “Routine authorized requests remain review-free unless the
organization's applicable policy requires review. Apply that policy consistently
to both monetary and nonfinancial requests. Ownership or an administrator title
alone does not make someone an eligible reviewer. Make requester self-review
eligibility explicit in the applicable organizational policy; do not infer it as
fallback when another reviewer is unavailable. Enforce any required independence.”

**Recommendation:** permit an organization to reflect its real authority structure;
do not invent universal independent review. If all required authority rests with
one eligible person, display that honestly rather than simulating a second check.
Resolve actual owner-contract restrictions before exposing such an option.

**Proof:** Routine prayer invitations remain direct when no rule applies. A selected
nonfinancial request can still require review. Loss of reviewer eligibility produces
an actionable recovery path, not silent clearance or a second invented account.

### P4 — Withdrawal, revocation and stale decisions become interchangeable

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** Canceling a pending submission differs from
withdrawing permission already granted. Neither can erase an action already taken.

**Proposed clause:** “A permitted withdrawal ends the identified pending submission;
a later submission is a new review occurrence. A permitted revocation ends the
approval's future applicability while retaining its historical decision and scope.
Neither deletes prior facts or accepts later responses to the ended occurrence as
a current approval. Reviewer replacement must preserve who actually decided what.”

**Proof:** A reviewer following an old notification after withdrawal cannot approve
the replacement proposal. Revoking permission after a presentation records the
present restriction without pretending the presentation never happened. A policy
change cannot silently transform historical approval into current authority.

## Remaining empirical limits

No study establishes how frequently missionaries need this control or whether a
particular label is understood. Before release, test routine no-review work, one
required review, material revision, reviewer absence, withdrawn submission and
historical recording with real staff and missionaries in the shared interface.
Ask them to identify what was approved and what can happen next. Do not claim a
tested workflow, universal missions preference or measured reduction in errors.
