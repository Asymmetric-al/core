## Founder question — answered A

**Q18 — Once partnership work has been saved, should staff be able to change its
stage list when another approved list fits the ongoing work better?**

A stage list is the set of progress labels used to manage that partnership.
The supporter, identified ministry and continuing purpose stay the same here.
For illustration, an organization might introduce a list that better describes how
it now manages existing partnerships. This is a hypothetical decision example,
not a claim about observed missions-organization practice.

**A — Allow deliberate changes (recommended).** Authorized staff may choose
another eligible approved list on the same partnership work, keeping its history.
This supports both correcting mistakes and changing the working process later.

**B — Correct mistakes only.** Staff may fix a wrongly recorded list, but an
originally correct list stays attached to that partnership even if the organization
later prefers another process.

Both options preserve the partnership and its history. Neither requires deleting
or recreating the work. This question does not decide how its current stage should
be handled when a list is changed.

**Recommendation:** A. It fits the founder's preference for tenant flexibility and
allows processes to evolve without inventing another partnership or pretending a
previously correct choice was a mistake. B's strongest benefit is simpler continuity
and fewer process transitions to explain and report. These are product judgments;
no agency frequency, error rate or user-study result is asserted.

## Evidence and inherited boundaries

- [D3](phase27-d3-adversarial-review.md) keeps the continuing development purpose
  central to journey identity. A new stage list alone does not justify another
  ordinary journey. A genuinely independent purpose remains a different question.
- [D14](phase27-d14-adversarial-review.md) permits additional approved lists but
  does not silently adopt saved-work switching or stage mapping.
- [D15 R01/R02](phase27-d15-adversarial-review.md) lets currently attached work
  continue with a withdrawn list. A future switch away and back is a new attachment:
  previous use does not bypass current availability or authority. List withdrawal
  never forces migration, and restoration never silently moves saved work.
- [D16 R13](phase27-d16-adversarial-review.md) and
  [D17 R14](phase27-d17-adversarial-review.md) expressly reserve general switching
  and correction mechanics, while requiring safe recovery from a mistaken saved
  choice. Initial form choice, an organization's usual list and reuse of existing
  work do not grant saved-list mutation authority.
- [D13](phase27-d13-adversarial-review.md) permits an initially unassessed stage.
  It is not permission to clear an already recorded assessment during a switch.
  Stage order and matching labels do not establish equivalent meaning.
