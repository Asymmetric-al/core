# D29 practice evidence — coordinated financial and nonfinancial appeals

Fresh primary research retrieved **2026-10-11 UTC**. The founder selected A:
an appeal may coordinate financial, nonfinancial or mixed support. This appendix
assesses ministry fit, comparative CRM practice and proportionate safeguards.
It does not amend formal owners, create product behavior or assert that a reviewed
vendor implements Core's proposed model.

## Finding and strongest narrower alternative

**A fits documented ministry support categories, with a bounded coordination role.**
OMF and Cru explicitly describe support through prayer as well as finances; OMF
also names practical help, advocacy and hosting a travelling worker. This is real
published ministry need, not an invented list of possible CRM features. However,
these sources do **not** prove demand for a combined internal appeal view, reveal
those agencies' CRM workflow or establish how often mixed invitations occur.

The narrower **B remains credible**: MPDX explicitly limits appeals to financial
campaigns outside monthly goals. Core could retain financial appeals and coordinate
other invitations through D22 Requests and existing tasks. That preserves useful
nonfinancial work and simpler appeal totals. Its potential cost is separate views
for one coordinated purpose; that is a product inference, not a measured failure.

With A chosen, its value should be one understandable overview of a shared effort
and the actual invitations/answers, not additional event, prayer, volunteering,
communication or financial machinery. CiviCRM provides a relevant umbrella model,
but its broader component scope is not a template to import wholesale.

## Recommended safeguards and exact clarifications

These are proposals for the D29 review, grounded in accepted D22/D24–D28 boundaries.
Severity describes impact; likelihood is a qualitative implementation concern,
not an observed incident rate.

**P1 — One status hides several answers. High impact; plausible.** A church may
accept a hosting invitation, decline giving and leave an introduction unanswered.
Proposed clause: “An appeal coordinates existing Requests without replacing their
independent meaning, response or owner. A response to one invitation does not
answer another, create a gift or change the partnership's stage.” Retain partial,
conditional, later-revised and unanswered responses in their actual context.
Prefer the existing independent Request records to a new multi-part survey engine.

**P2 — Progress implies fulfillment or spiritual performance. High impact;
plausible.** Proposed clause: “Distinguish a recorded invitation, reported agreement
and source-supported follow-through. Agreement to pray is not measured prayer
performance; agreement to host is not proof the visit occurred.” Track the answer
and relevant coordination rather than requiring prayer logs or repeated proof of
faithfulness. Preserve D26 voluntary contribution and D27 actual-exchange meaning.
A declined invitation remains an understandable answer, not a failed relationship.

**P3 — Forced goals and incomparable units. High impact; plausible.** Proposed
clause: “An appeal may have a clear purpose without a numerical target. Financial
goals/totals appear only where applicable. Any nonfinancial count states what is
counted and whether it records invitations, affirmative responses or an independently
verified outcome; these are not combined with money into one completion percentage.”
Financial Committed/Received/Given buckets remain qualified financial views, not
mandatory columns for every supporter. An offered room is not a zero-value gift,
and a prayer partner is not a low financial contributor.

**P4 — Membership, requests and permissions get conflated. High impact; plausible.**
Proposed clause: “Retain D24's deliberate recorded membership and current eligibility.
Being on the list does not mean an invitation was made, answered or permitted.
Changing an appeal's purpose does not authorize a new contact purpose, audience or
disclosure.” A no-fundraising preference must be interpreted by its existing owner,
not bypassed by relabeling a funding solicitation as prayer. A referral does not
automatically enroll or contact the introduced person. Existing qualified party,
representative and ministry context governs each Request.

**P5 — Coordination expands into new delivery engines. Moderate/high impact;
plausible.** Proposed clause: “The appeal supplies shared purpose, recorded working
audience, linked Requests and source-qualified summaries. Execution stays with its
owner: money, commitments, gifts, communications, event participation, volunteering
and published prayer content remain separate.” Do not require a booking, volunteer
profile or new public page merely to record a hosting answer. Do not copy sensitive
prayer narratives, travel details or private correspondence into shared summaries.

**P6 — Counts exaggerate success or erase history. Moderate/high impact; plausible.**
Proposed clause: “Distinguish unique supporters from Request counts and current
responses from historical activity. State the relevant population and coverage;
missing or restricted information is not a confirmed negative response. Correction,
withdrawal and removal from the working list preserve source truth without
duplicating fulfillment or erasing accepted history.” A church and its representative
must not silently become two successes, nor must one gift be multiplied by linked
invitations. Optional giving ratings remain a financial lens, not a mixed-support score.

## Human-oriented presentation and proof still needed

An illustrative row can show a church's name with separate concise statements:
“Hosting: agreed, date to arrange”; “Prayer invitation: accepted”; “Funding: no
answer recorded.” Show the actual Request and its existing next action when opened.
Do not force the church into one exclusive progress column. Relevant financial
figures can remain available without dominating a nonfinancial appeal or showing
misleading zero-money progress. These labels illustrate meaning, not new fixed
states or a required layout.

Reuse the current recorded audience and Request controls. Creating an appeal should
not require duplicate supporter details, a new form designer, a separate status
taxonomy or confirmation that every supporter will receive every kind of invitation.
Where a count is useful, let readers distinguish “people invited” from “requests
answered.” A missing reply should not automatically create pressure to ask again;
existing tasks, restrictions and agreed follow-up control what happens next.

Before implementation claims, verify representative real-role tasks: coordinate a
mixed missionary-visit effort with partial answers; run a prayer-only appeal without
inventing money or a numerical target; revise a conditional hosting response;
remove a supporter while retaining qualified history; and review an appeal with
restricted financial information. Ask staff and missionaries to explain the summary
and locate the responsible next action. Measure actual confusion and workload in
that evaluation rather than inventing thresholds or assuming A is universally best.

The remaining empirical unknown is whether a shared appeal view improves these
users' coordination enough to justify its extra scope. The selected A can be honored
proportionately by reusing Requests and source facts while preserving the simple
fundraising-only use case. No production, formal specification or unrelated file
was changed for this research.
