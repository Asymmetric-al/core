# D2 — ministry target: alternatives, external practice and UX review

> Evidence appendix, researched 2026-10-09. Findings are inputs to the
> [consolidated D2 review](../phase27-d2-adversarial-review.md), which
> reconciles recommendations and controls their decision status. This is
> grooming research, not a ratified specification or runtime qualification.

Research-only adversarial input, 2026-10-09. Founder answer supplied by parent: **“A. Attach the journey to their identified ministry. make sure we think through this carefully and plan for edge cases.”** Treat the ministry anchor as selected, with review/ratification pending. D1's continuing purpose-specific journey is accepted. No implementation, formal specification, new source schema, production verification or remote write performed.

## Recommendation and answer effect

**Retain the ministry target. Narrow its interpretation.** An ordinary support-raising journey concerns a supporter Party's continuing relationship for an identified, organization-owned ministry. The existing Ministry Assignment identifies that ministry; it does not identify the supporter, replace financial records, or grant access. Personal, couple and team participation can change without pretending that all the participants, donors, permissions and gifts became interchangeable.

The evidence supports separate stable relationship context and specific asks. It does not prove an exact vendor equivalent for this enrollment, one universal ministry-support workflow, or a unique-record rule. D2 alone does not approve a generic purpose engine, initial setup journey, sharing policy, succession behavior, automatic audience formation, or a rule of one record per Party/ministry forever.

No discovered issue requires reversing the anchor. Three product matters must remain explicit, not be concealed as minor hardening: what staff can do before a ministry is identified; what legally/operationally counts as the same continuing ministry after a material change; and exactly what participants/missionaries/coaches may see or change. Other agents own the latter two's detailed domain review.

## 1. Product necessity and strongest alternatives

**Risk:** building an extra donor CRM with new ownership, task, history and configuration stores. **Severity high; likelihood medium-high** if “enrollment” is treated as a blank canvas. **Evidence:** P9 A12 already reserves development sockets and owns assignments/tasks; Salesforce action plans/opportunities and Virtuous My Portfolio aggregate existing records rather than requiring every concern to be recreated. **Inference:** the minimal additional business fact is continuing cultivation for a purpose, not another complete relationship record.

| No new enrollment; assignments + saved views + shared tasks + individual asks | Fewest new concepts; existing staff can maintain contacts, set tasks and track solicitations. Perfectly plausible for basic CRM use. | Cannot express independently governed continuing cultivation stages/next-action obligations for the same Party and different ministries without attaching that state to a task, each ask, global Party, or a new disguised relationship edge. **Exact corrective language:** “Use the existing Ministry Assignment to identify the ministry for ordinary missionary support. Add only the continuing cultivation facts Phase 27 needs; reuse Party, assignments, tasks, interactions, asks and financial services for their existing purposes. This decision does not introduce a general-purpose relationship engine.”

**Meaningful proof later:** run the same realistic scenarios through a prototype using only existing records and one using the proposed thin enrollment: one supporter supports two distinct ministries; one annual ask completes while stewardship continues; a prayer-only partner needs a visit; a worker leaves. Show which facts cannot be expressed without overloading another owner, and that unrelated data are referenced rather than copied. No artificial benchmark or marketing claim proves necessity.

## 2. Prospects and setup before a ministry is identified

**Risk:** a representative meets a church, prospective worker or supporter and cannot record the person/task without provisioning an official ministry, support account and public page. Staff work around it by creating dummy ministries, choosing a convenient wrong ministry, or recording the support journey in private notes. **Severity high; likelihood high** in initial adoption and mobilization. **Evidence:** P9 Ministry Assignment may have zero-to-many participants and optional finance binding, and is distinct from public pages; P9 already owns Party/tasks. Vendor procedural sources permit contact relationship work separate from financial asks. **Inference:** unknown purpose should remain honest unfinished work, not a fabricated identity.

**Answer effect:** preserve D2 for identified ministry support; leave the precise pre-enrollment product flow unratified. Existing Party/assignment/task work must remain usable. If founder needs a first-class journey before ministry identification, that is a meaningful additional target/lifecycle decision—not permission to silently use null, generic “unknown ministry”, current user or a household as substitute.

**Exact corrective language:** “Selecting an identified ministry is required when starting a ministry-specific support journey. It is not required merely to record a prospect, relationship or shared follow-up task. Missing ministry setup is visible and repairable through its owner; creating a cultivation journey must not provision finance, workspace or public publishing.”

**Meaningful proof later:** representative records a new church and next task with no Ministry Assignment; an authorized ministry is selected later without losing the encounter or duplicating the Party/task; unavailable ministry service produces an actionable state, not a local shadow object. Check whether this meets real staff practice in the pilot; do not presume that an internal task alone satisfies every early support-raising scenario.

## 3. Identity, same names and restricted ministry discovery

**Risk:** selecting the wrong “Smith Family Ministry”; disambiguation reveals a restricted country, real worker name, private participant list or support-account identifier; an empty authorized search causes duplicate creation; duplicate warnings reveal a hidden ministry. **Severity critical for sensitive-worker exposure; likelihood medium.** **Evidence:** P9 D19 separates stable identity from public display and membership; A15 makes search permission-bound; P3/P10/P12 own disclosure. Salesforce separately models person accounts, organizations, households and relationship records—it does not equate labels with identity. **Inference:** identity choice must be exact and privacy-safe before display convenience.

**Answer effect:** safeguard consistent with D2, not a new access grant. Exact ministry ID is selected through the owning authorized reader. Reuse only already authorized distinguishing context. No global-name uniqueness or fuzzy-name merge follows. A restricted record cannot become selectable just because a donor mentions its name or a participant knows it.

**Exact corrective language:** “Show the ministry's permitted name and enough permitted context to choose the exact record. Search and duplicate handling must not reveal restricted alternatives. ‘No accessible match’ is not proof that no ministry exists; staff can use the existing owner review path without creating a substitute.”

**Meaningful proof later:** test two accessible same-name ministries, one inaccessible same-name ministry, a renamed ministry, local-script names and a public alias; compare results, counts, suggestion text, error messages, notifications and exported labels across roles. Network responses must exclude hidden identity details, not merely hide UI rows. Authorized duplicate review must preserve the original enrollment target and historical identity; duplicate detection itself is not merge authority.

## 4. Role-specific work without parallel records

**Risk:** broad staff UI overwhelms missionaries; simplified missionary UI clones the source; church-relations staff see only donors; finance is presented as cultivation ownership; leaders' totals leak hidden ministries. **Severity high; likelihood medium-high.** **Evidence:** user boundary separates Mission Control, Missionary Workspace, Donor Portal and Public Website. P9 defines role-qualified assignments (`donor_rep`, `regional_rep`, `church_relations`, `mobilizer`), multiple assignees/one primary and assignment-derived portfolios. Virtuous aggregates relationship tasks and asks but its one Organization Group per user/access coupling is a product limitation, not Core precedent.

**Answer effect:** D2 does not pick responsibility or missionary sharing; use role-qualified views over the same source when independently authorized. “My ministries” cannot silently mean “my supporters”, “all participants can see all notes”, or “everyone with a public-page role can change cultivation”. A mobilizer can coordinate a relationship without converting this phase into an application/care product.

**Exact corrective language:** “Use the same journey through role-appropriate authorized views. Display the supporter, identified ministry, responsible people, current purpose and next action. Ministry participation and portfolio assignment do not grant record access or transfer responsibility.”

**Meaningful proof later:** staff, missionary, church-relations operator, finance user and coach examine the same source scenario under distinct grants; permissible actions and summaries differ without source duplication. Coaching aggregates must obey their separately approved disclosure contract; no inference of identifiable supporter access from a totals view.

## 5. Familiar language and bounded information architecture

**Risk:** staff face “Cultivation Enrollment / Target Type / Ministry Assignment Support Binding Version” before they can record a conversation; generic setup causes errors or spreadsheet fallback. **Severity medium; likelihood high.** **Evidence:** GOV.UK labels guidance calls for short, direct visible labels; P9 requires one shared shell and saved views, rather than a new donor application. **Inference:** domain precision belongs in the model; routine UI should use the user's work vocabulary.

**Answer effect:** presentation safeguard; final vocabulary still needs ministry-staff testing. “Partnership” or “support relationship” may work better than “enrollment”, but could also imply an already agreed relationship for prospects, so do not ratify that label blindly.

**Exact corrective language:** “Use plain labels such as ‘Ministry’, ‘Relationship purpose’, ‘Responsible person’ and ‘Next action’, with an explanation only where needed. Do not present internal source names as required decisions. Prospective and nonfinancial relationships must not look like failed donors.”

**Meaningful proof later:** ask representative, missionary and church-relations staff to identify who the relationship concerns, the ministry, who acts next and what a completed ask means without coaching. Include prayer-only partnership and a person who has not agreed to support. Preserve list filter/sort/scroll and focus when opening/closing a detail view; do not replace the accepted shared shell with a dedicated ministry CRM.

## 6. Keyboard, single-pointer, focus and touch

**Risk:** a screen-reader/keyboard user or worker on a small phone cannot select a ministry or change stage; a drag drops into an unintended column; focus is hidden behind sticky UI or lost on closing a drawer. **Severity high; likelihood high** if the board is the primary interface. **Evidence:** WCAG 2.2 2.5.7 requires a single-pointer alternative to dragging; keyboard equivalence alone is insufficient. 2.5.8 requires 24×24 CSS-pixel target size with specified exceptions; Core's touch craft guidance recommends 44px. 2.4.11 requires focused component not entirely obscured. Core accessibility skill requires manual interaction tests in addition to axe.

**Answer effect:** safeguards, not new scope. Do not infer an accessible cultivation board exists from the present CRM Kanban. Its current source groups loaded rows by **Party lifecycle** and only opens records; that pattern does not prove stage commands, complete authorized counts or reliable drag behavior.

**Exact corrective language:** “Every stage change offered by dragging must also be possible through a clearly labelled click/tap control and the keyboard. The same consequence review, permission check and conflict outcome applies to all input methods. Focus and context return predictably when a record closes.”

**Meaningful proof later:** complete ministry selection and stage change keyboard-only and touch-only without dragging; verify visible/unobscured focus with sticky headers, drawer, error summary and virtualized rows; verify focus return when the source row is removed or evicted; test target sizes in real rendered CSS. Use shared Base UI/base-maia controls; axe is supplemental, not complete conformance proof.

## 7. Mobile, long names and localization

**Risk:** target names truncate to indistinguishable labels, financial meaning loses currency/period, date-only review deadlines shift across timezones, RTL changes the reading order or icons imply reversed actions. **Severity high for wrong-target selection, otherwise medium; likelihood high** for international missions. **Evidence:** WCAG 1.4.10 reflow at equivalent 320 CSS px / 400% desktop zoom permits necessary 2-D regions but does not excuse unusable individual content; P9 search acceptance includes punctuation; current CRM card truncates names. **Inference:** a card/list alternative should make ordinary work possible without dragging a wide board; full names need an accessible viewing path, not hover-only tooltips.

**Answer effect:** safeguards; do not select date/locale/currency policy as a side effect of D2. User-facing label can change without changing ministry identity.

**Exact corrective language:** “Keep the exact ministry identity stable while displaying its authorized localized label. Make full names and distinguishing context available without hover. Ordinary support work must remain usable on a narrow screen; format dates, currency and periods explicitly without changing their source meaning.”

**Meaningful proof later:** narrow viewport and 400% zoom; representative long Thai/Arabic/Latin names, combining characters, punctuation and RTL; accessible lookup and confirmation preserve exact selection. Date-only next action remains on intended organization date when worker timezone differs. No silent FX, name-derived identifier or locale-specific string equality.

## 8. Errors, concurrent changes and poor connections

**Risk:** bad connection yields apparent successful enrollment, a double-click creates duplicate journeys/tasks, a stale form attaches to retired ministry, or replay after reconnect causes a consequential stage change/outreach. **Severity high; likelihood high** for field use. **Evidence:** GOV.UK error summary guidance pairs a focusable summary with field messages and links; WCAG 4.1.3 requires programmatic status messages without taking focus. User explicitly prohibits silent offline replay and requires trusted server commands and expected revisions. **Inference:** preserve safe user input when still authorized; never preserve cached restricted content after revoked context.

**Answer effect:** safeguards within the stated command/security standard. D2 does not approve offline queueing, automatic retry semantics or autosaving consequential changes. A network timeout means outcome may be unknown, not confirmed failure.

**Exact corrective language:** “Show whether the change is pending, confirmed, rejected or not yet confirmed. Recheck the exact ministry and current authority at save. A safe retry must resolve the same intended operation; reconnecting must not silently create another enrollment, task or message. Clear inaccessible content when authority changes.”

**Meaningful proof later:** disconnect before submission, after accepted server write but before response, and during ministry retirement/revocation; retry/double-submit creates one intended effect and displays server truth; stale selection never silently retargets. Test errors both visually and with a screen reader. A generic toast is insufficient for a field correction or unresolved outcome. Whether identity changes require dedicated review remains an explicit owner decision.

## 9. Shared encounters, briefs and privacy

**Risk:** the new ministry link is treated as permission to share staff notes, Support conversations or donor financial summaries with all ministry participants. A “shared summary” leaks the protected source in plain language even if original record remains hidden. **Severity critical; likelihood medium-high.** **Evidence:** current Salesforce training links interaction summaries to both account and opportunity, but distinguishes public/published from selected confidential sharing and says org privacy configuration matters. Core P9 missionary projection exclusion and v3 interaction direction remain under owner reconciliation (recorded E9); P26 owns Support context.

**Answer effect:** same encounter may be referenced in authorized contexts; no automatic shared note, privacy downgrade or duplicate editable transcript. Founder target choice does not resolve E9.

**Exact corrective language:** “A ministry reference identifies why the relationship is being developed; it does not open its notes, financial history, Support context or briefs to ministry participants. Shared encounter references remain subject to each source's permissions, including summaries, search, exports and notifications.”

**Meaningful proof later:** same meeting has a permitted development outcome and staff-private note; missionary sees only authorized projection and cannot infer hidden party/amount/note existence from counts or brief omissions. Revoke access while a brief and picker are open and reject late responses; cached output must no longer disclose protected data. These are future tests, not claims current code passes.

## 10. CMS and giving boundaries

**Risk:** the ministry is selected by public-page slug or giving link, so page republishing/suppression or finance rebind changes the internal target; editors gain access because they manage a page. **Severity high; likelihood medium.** **Evidence:** P9 D19 explicitly distinguishes Ministry Assignment, Page, Support Assignment and Designation; public release pins approved public-safe snapshot. Current Payload access documentation scopes access by operation and record/field, not by having any authenticated user or relationship; its default Local API bypass is documented. Core pins internal Payload `4.0.0-internal.1f9ae9a`, so public docs are conceptual guidance, not proof of that build's runtime behavior. Salesforce separates opportunity from commitment and transactions.

**Answer effect:** retain exact Ministry Assignment reference; no CMS/giving alias or synchronization authority. No further vendor CMS evaluation needed for this decision.

**Exact corrective language:** “A ministry may exist without a page, published profile, giving destination or support account. Cultivation never publishes its internal records, and publishing or finance changes do not silently change the cultivation target.”

**Meaningful proof later:** unpublished/restricted ministry with no finance binding supports authorized internal work; page rename/unpublish and finance-binding succession preserve historical target, grants and financial meanings. Public or editor queries cannot enumerate cultivation record identities, status, asks or briefs. Exact local API/security behavior requires source/runtime qualification separately.

## Consolidated proposed wording

“Ordinary missionary support journeys attach to the organization's identified ministry through the existing Ministry Assignment. The supporter remains a separate Party. This anchor survives ordinary participant and display changes without copying or transferring supporter identity, financial history, tasks or access. Material ministry succession still needs explicit owner decisions. Staff can record prospects and shared follow-up before identifying a ministry; no dummy ministry, finance setup or public page is created merely to satisfy cultivation. D2 establishes the target, not a generic purpose engine, automatic sharing, enrollment uniqueness or the later lifecycle rules.”

This wording is proposed for parent review. The prospect workflow sentence preserves existing CRM work but must not be taken as the founder's acceptance that every pre-ministry development need is satisfied. Keep that unresolved need visible.
