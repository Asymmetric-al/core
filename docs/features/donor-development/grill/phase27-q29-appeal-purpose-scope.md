## Founder question — A selected

**Q29 — Should an organization-level appeal coordinate nonfinancial support as
well as fundraising?**

For example, one effort supporting a missionary visit might invite churches to
give, host a gathering, make introductions or become prayer partners. This is an
illustrative scenario, not evidence of an observed tenant workflow. Each church
could respond differently to each invitation.

**A — Cover both within one coordinated appeal (recommended).** Let staff organize
financial, nonfinancial or mixed support around a shared purpose. Reuse the
recorded supporter list and existing Requests, showing each response and relevant
follow-up clearly. Show financial goals and gift totals only when applicable;
agreement to host or pray is recorded in its own terms.

**B — Keep appeals focused on fundraising.** Use appeals for financial goals and
gift progress. Continue tracking prayer, introductions and hosting through the
existing partnership Requests and tasks, outside the appeal. This keeps appeal
reporting simpler, but staff coordinate a mixed effort across separate views.

**Recommendation: A.** It extends the already selected common request model to
coordinated work without forcing nonfinancial participation into financial states.
B is a credible narrower product when coordinated fundraising is the only group
workflow that needs appeal-level oversight. Actual demand for mixed appeals has
not been measured. The recommendation is product judgment grounded in the selected
mission scope and comparable practice, not a claim of universal CRM consensus.

The founder selected A and requested the full adversarial review with exceptional
UX and current-practice research. The founder subsequently explicitly instructed,
“Yes, ratify this amended D29.” The reviewed amendment and safeguards are ratified
without substantive change.

## Original question boundaries — preserved history

- D22's independently answerable requests retain distinct responses. One shared
  appeal must not turn a yes to prayer into a yes to funding, duplicate the same
  request into new records, or create another partnership journey.
- A plan, a response, actual participation, a financial commitment and received
  money remain separate facts. Nonfinancial participation is not a zero-value gift.
  No mixed money/participation score measures relationship worth.
- D24's recorded working list and current source/permission/contact restrictions
  remain binding. Broadening appeal purpose never grants access, bypasses a
  restriction by renaming the invitation, or automatically sends anything.
- P13 owns money/designations/campaign attribution, P14 recognition and P16
  commitments/fulfillment. No source-confirmed amount is invented or double-counted
  through multiple requests, participants, appeals or staff responsibility.
  D25 reported gifts still do not become received money.
- A development appeal remains distinct from a Giving Campaign, public page,
  email campaign and designation. P6/P17 own communications; public content and
  publishing keep their existing owners. CiviCRM's broader campaign vocabulary
  does not change those Core boundaries.
- Neither answer introduces event booking, volunteer scheduling, prayer-content
  publishing, a general campaign engine, mandatory missionary logging or donor
  ratings. D26 voluntary participation and D28 genuine rating optionality remain.
