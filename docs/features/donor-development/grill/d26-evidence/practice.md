# D26 practice evidence — shared history with missionary autonomy

2026-10-10 UTC (2026-10-11 Asia/Bangkok). Research for selected A: newly recorded,
eligible routine partnership updates share a clearly understood working audience.
The founder additionally requires missionary autonomy and rejects turning the
experience into staff-style control. Proposed clauses below interpret that rider
for review; they do not establish new employment policy or a coaching product.

## Finding

Missions sources distinguish collaborative work, coaching and institutional
supervision. MPDX offers narrow, non-editing coaching access separately from broad
shared-account access. Reliant documents both participant-created goals in a
coaching cohort and mandatory reporting in other contexts. Sharing mechanics do
not establish whether an agency relationship is voluntary, supervisory or employed.

**Product judgment:** preserve A's shared routine context while making missionary
agency practical: clear audiences, concise updates, self-directed follow-through
within existing authority, and deliberately sought help. Sharing an update must
not itself create supervision, quotas, a duty to disclose personal conversations,
or an assessment of the missionary's faithfulness. Conversely, autonomy does not
erase accepted organizational work or source-owned legal/financial obligations.

## Strongest B and the smallest useful A

**B is strongest when an encounter mixes ordinary coordination with personal or
pastoral confidence.** Deliberately sharing an eligible update can reduce accidental
disclosure and give the author control over timing and content. It can also leave
collaborators unaware of relevant work. Those are plausible tradeoffs, not measured
staff outcomes. B does not require duplicate encounter records, and A must not
misrepresent B as inherently fragmented data.

**Minimum A:** an understandable shared-writing context for new, routine,
sharing-eligible updates, with its audience visible before saving and a clearly
separate permitted private path. A short coordination update can be enough; sharing
need not require a full conversation transcript. Reuse existing source references
and shared tasks. Do not add compulsory reports, universal coaching invitations,
quotas, supervisory dashboards or a second personal CRM. A normal shared update
does not need an extra sharing ceremony once its context and audience are clear.

## Material concerns and exact proposed safeguards

Severity/likelihood are qualitative judgments. Acceptance scenarios are illustrative,
not observed agency behavior or new founder questions.

### P1 — Shared context becomes control over a missionary's work

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** Existing vendor coaching reports could
tempt copying mandatory activity targets or treating every gap as poor performance.

**Proposed clause:** “Shared partnership history supports coordination; it does not
by itself enroll a missionary in supervision, require reporting of all personal
interactions, assign a coach, impose activity quotas or turn a suggestion into an
obligation. Follow-through remains with the accountable authorized person under
the already-governing source rules. D26 adds no staff appraisal or spiritual-effort
rating.”

**AC:** A missionary records an agreed church follow-up and selects an appropriate
next action without a coach approving routine choices merely because the update is
shared. Absence of new shared entries does not establish absence of ministry work.
Existing D4–D6 accountability and D25 gift-handling duties remain intact; the rider
does not make already accepted obligations optional.

### P2 — “Missionary” becomes a guessed legal status or permission

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** Missionaries may also be agency employees;
employment, collaboration, supervision and record visibility are different facts.

**Proposed clause:** “Do not infer employment status, managerial authority or data
access from the missionary label, partnership participation or a coaching title.
Shared history supplies only the explicitly qualified audience and actions. An
organizational reporting policy, if applicable, needs its own governing authority;
D26 does not create or nullify one.”

**AC:** A coach cannot edit donor details simply because coaching access exists.
A missionary with an independently authorized staff function receives only that
function's current scope, without unioning unrelated roles. A volunteer collaborator
does not inherit an employee's permissions. No blanket 'all missionaries are outside
staff policy' exception or new supervisory-policy engine is introduced.

### P3 — Routine coordination exposes personal or protected context

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** A church conversation can include both a
budget follow-up and a confidential family matter; an ordinary title proves little.

**Proposed clause:** “Share only eligible content deliberately authored for the
understood shared context. Do not require personal details or a full message body
to record the coordination fact. Existing private content, personal address-book
material, care/Support bodies and unrelated ministry context do not become shared
through a link, import, assignment or audience default.”

**AC:** Record 'follow up after the budget meeting' without exposing the pastoral
conversation. A private note's existence/content does not leak through a count,
brief or attention indicator. Staff and missionary can accurately identify the
shared audience before save. This permits neither laundering a restricted source
through a summary nor treating the author's willingness as donor disclosure authority.

### P4 — Invited help becomes broad or permanent access

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** MPDX's broad account sharing is materially
different from its narrow coaching view; neither is automatically Core's model.

**Proposed clause:** “A request for help or an invitation does not confer broad
account access. Any permitted coaching/collaboration access uses the existing
qualified invitation and permission owner, with understandable scope and revocation.
Shared history does not authorize overwriting another person's account of events
or silently reopening private material.”

**AC:** A departed helper loses current access through ordinary and derived surfaces.
Stopping optional help does not erase lawful institutional history or unfinished
source-owned work. Do not promise that every author can revoke all organizational
access to records already properly retained. No new coaching subsystem or universal
author veto follows from this review.

### P5 — Sharing is mistaken for contact, progress or staff diligence

**Medium severity; plausible likelihood.** Sharing and completeness can change
without any new interaction occurring.

**Proposed clause:** “Sharing, editing visibility or importing history does not
establish a new encounter, meaningful contact, consent, stage advance or completed
task. Explain coverage limitations without treating private or unrecorded activity
as zero work. Future attention measures remain advisory and separately qualified.”

**AC:** Sharing an older meeting preserves its occurrence date. A joint encounter
can serve permitted contexts without becoming two calls. Prayer/nonfinancial
partnership work is not treated as deficient for lacking financial activity. A
shared brief remains useful without revealing protected details or inventing them.

## Limits and practical validation

The evidence supports multiple collaboration/coaching arrangements; it does not
choose A for every agency. The founder's autonomy rider governs Core's experience.
The exact P9/P12/P28 shared projection still requires owner reconciliation; neither
MPDX nor Salesforce documentation proves that Core's existing notes can be exposed.

A later check with representative missionaries and agency collaborators should test
audience comprehension, a short shared update, protected context, self-chosen next
work, an invitation/removal where supported, and handoff of an existing obligation.
Ask each participant what they believe sharing permits another person to do. Observe
unnecessary reporting work or mistaken access expectations; no interview findings,
quantitative benefit or invented acceptance threshold is asserted here.
