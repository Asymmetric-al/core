The useful product is a small internal review inside the existing Request.
Staff can see **whether review is needed, who acts next, what was reviewed and
what they can do now**. Approval means permission to present identified terms;
it does not mean the supporter agreed or that anything was sent. The detailed

## Exact decision recorded

> **D23 — Provide built-in internal review when the organization requires it.**
> Routine authorized partnership requests proceed without additional review by
> default. The organization can require review through a simple organization
> default and an explicitly authorized request-specific choice. Show the effective
> requirement and its basis. Ordinary request editing cannot remove a required
> review; unavailable policy information never means review is unnecessary.
>
> When review is required, an authorized person deliberately submits the proposed
> terms to one currently eligible internal reviewer. Show the proposal, current
> review state, responsible next person and permitted next action together in the
> existing Request and shared work experience. Approval, requesting changes,
> withdrawal, reassignment and correction preserve understandable history.
> Missing reviewer authority is an actionable routing problem, never automatic
> approval or a guessed administrator recipient.
>
> Approval applies to the exact reviewed terms and prospective action. Changes to
> those terms require fresh review when review remains required; unrelated internal
> notes, forecasts or follow-up metadata do not restart it. Keep earlier proposals
> and decisions as lawful history. One person may prepare and review only where
> current qualified authority permits it and no governing separation rule forbids
> it; self-review is deliberate and honestly attributed, never an automatic fallback.
>
> Internal review is separate from supporter response, partnership stage or
> lifecycle, financial commitment, receipt, consent and publication. Approval never
> sends or executes automatically. Each future supported outward action checks
> the current terms, applicable review, acting person's authority and its own
> source-owner requirements. A valid earlier decision does not become false merely
> because its reviewer later leaves. Authorized staff can record actual past
> requests and responses without fabricating prior approval or reopening work.
>
> Keep daily work brief and clear: relevant terms, a readable review summary,
> explicit contextual actions, accessible shared controls and truthful saving,
> conflict and recovery feedback. Review decisions and their required local history
> and downstream intent commit coherently; retries and competing actions cannot
> approve unseen terms, duplicate a decision or overwrite newer truth. Tenant,
> field, purpose and source permissions apply equally to detail, tasks, history,
> notifications, exports and privileged paths. Assignment grants no access.

## One coherent policy and staff experience

### Small policy surface

Organization settings contain **Review not required** (initial default) and
**Review required**, with concise explanation. The qualified requirement owner can
set an individual Request to **Use organization default**, **Require review**, or
**Review not required**. Daily users see the effective result, such as “Review
required · Organization default.” They need not touch policy settings to prepare
or submit. Only currently authorized policy/exception actors see the change control;
read/edit/review/present powers do not imply it.

This is deliberate selection, **not an automatic amount detector**. It works for
monetary and nonfinancial invitations, including unknown amounts. Changing a type,
amount, owner or hidden field never waives the separately controlled requirement.
A proved no-review path does not load or depend on reviewer options: a directory
outage cannot block an action that needs no review. Other required action checks
still apply. An organization needing universal review can set that default; targeted exemptions
are attributed policy changes. Default changes preserve explicit request choices.
No condition language, currency conversion, hierarchy of entity/team defaults or
mandatory approval chain is introduced.

Current policy governs future supported presentation, including old unpresented
work. Tightening requires sufficient current approval; loosening never sends a
backlog. If a review is no longer required, its mandatory pending work ends as
**No longer required**, not Approved. An unchanged accepted decision can still
satisfy the current rule; an unrelated configuration revision alone is not a reason
to re-review. History is not rewritten. A later new review need creates a successor
work occurrence, not resurrection of an ended task. Independent owner restrictions
cannot be waived through these choices.

### Daily flow

1. **Prepare the Request.** Existing ministry/supporter context and responsibility
   remain visible. Funding shows its relevant currency, cadence and terms; prayer
   or hosting shows the invitation without fake money. A compact **Internal review**
   section explains the effective requirement. Routine no-review work adds no steps.
2. **Request review when required.** Show the proposed terms and one eligible
   internal reviewer. A qualified saved usual reviewer can initialize routing, but
   assignment must be proven current on submission. The author can change an
   eligible reviewer within their authority. One deliberate **Request review**
   action accepts the exact terms; no ceremonial confirmation or duplicate form.
3. **Review the proposal.** The Request or existing Tasks Hub opens the same source
   detail. Show proposed terms, the requested action, who submitted, and meaningful
   changes since earlier review. Primary **Approve to present**, secondary
   **Request changes**, and a contextual **Do not approve** distinguish permission,
   revision and internal refusal. Non-approval needs brief actionable explanation;
   approval needs no mandatory essay. Reviewers do not silently edit while approving.
4. **Continue with a clear next action.** The author sees the accepted result and
   permitted next action. Approval does not send. Requested changes lead to normal
   editing and deliberate resubmission. Staff may resubmit unchanged terms for
   genuine reconsideration with context; no fake text edit is required. A new round
   preserves the earlier outcome. The decision does not change the donor response,
   journey stage, pause, closure or existing commitments.
5. **Recover in context.** Withdraw a pending review, change an eligible reviewer,
   or correct/revoke an accepted decision through explicit source actions. Keep
   history and current status visible. A stale link explains why that review can no
   longer be acted on. A lost response offers **Check result** or safe retry of the
   same intent, not another blind approval.

| Condition                                       | Visible meaning and available recovery                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Known not required                              | “Review not required”; ordinary authorized next action. Never an Approved badge.                                                                                                                        |
| Required, unsubmitted                           | “Needs review”; review summary, eligible reviewer and Request review.                                                                                                                                   |
| No eligible reviewer                            | “Review needs a reviewer”; source-owned recovery for authorized responsible staff. Saving preparation remains possible; no claim of routed waiting.                                                     |
| Eligibility/policy cannot be established        | “Review status unavailable” or appropriate restricted wording; retry/repair the dependent action. Unknown is not an empty optional state; a reviewer-only outage does not block known review-free work. |
| Accepted and routed submission                  | “Waiting for Maria” with actual submission time; author can inspect, revise or withdraw; eligible reviewer can review.                                                                                  |
| Approved current terms                          | “Approved to present · Maria · [time]”; View reviewed terms and independently permitted next action.                                                                                                    |
| Changes requested                               | Show actionable feedback; Edit request / Request review again. No implication that the supporter declined.                                                                                              |
| Internally not approved                         | “Not approved internally”; explanation and permitted revision/reconsideration, not partnership closure.                                                                                                 |
| Changed terms / withdrawn / requirement removed | Explain the specific current disposition and next action; show prior decision in history, never as current clearance.                                                                                   |

Names and details appear only where permitted. These are presentation meanings,
not a requirement to add nine database statuses or nine simultaneous badges.
One concise status and next action should dominate; detail/history expand as needed.

### Reviewable terms, routing and clarity

The review target contains the proposed request and exact prospective presentation
scope: represented ministry/parties, requested outcome, outward wording, financial
terms, conditions and included qualified document versions. The source owns a
finite reviewed-field contract. Changing those fields creates new reviewed terms;
there is no client-supplied “cosmetic” bypass or AI semantic-equivalence guess.
A typo in **outward reviewed text** therefore changes its reviewed version; a typo
in an unrelated internal note does not. Financial amount alone is not the whole
proposal: currency, frequency, duration and conditions matter too.

An internal forecast, note, responsibility or follow-up date outside that review
basis does not restart approval. If a date or attachment is actually part of what
will be presented, it is reviewed content. Supporting references bind their qualified
version; arbitrary attachment mutation cannot alter approved meaning. The reviewer
needs a sufficient authorized view of the meaningful terms, not the entire private
dossier. Missing access to essential terms prevents decision and exposes permitted
routing/authority repair; assignment never supplies the missing access.

One current reviewer keeps responsibility understandable. A saved reviewer is
configuration, not a task. Submission with no eligible reviewer does not create
false Waiting or release an assignment; preserve preparation and show the source
routing problem. If eligibility is lost after submission, the review remains pending
but no stale recipient can act. Qualified reassignment changes responsibility,
retains the same unchanged review target and decision history, and re-proves all
current authority. Supersession or ending creates truthful shared-work outcomes
under ADR0183; a task checkbox cannot decide.

Self-review is available only to a person with qualified decision authority where
allowed; display that person honestly. No separate second-person setting or fallback
engine is needed merely to deliver D23. When a governing owner actually requires
independence, compare stable P12 Party identity and relevant authorship, not current
owner, profile or another login. Loss of eligible reviewers does not waive that floor.
A reviewer's later departure does not retroactively erase their valid decision;
a new explicit revocation or changed applicable rule may limit future use.

### Concrete visual and interaction quality

Use the existing shared record shell and `@asym/ui` components, **Base UI/base-maia
and Zinc semantic tokens**. Keep the review summary adjacent to Request terms,
with restrained hierarchy, useful spacing, readable contrast and text as well as
color. Expand long histories and secondary actions instead of covering the working
view with controls. Use clear labels—**Internal review**, **Supporter response**,
**Request review**, **Approve to present**, **Change reviewer**—rather than generic
“Status,” “Submit,” “Reject donor” or implementation vocabulary.

An illustrative review summary reads: “Internal review: Waiting for Maria.
Submitted 11 October at 09:15. View submitted terms. Change reviewer. Withdraw
review request.” After a material edit it reads: “Your changes are saved. These
terms need review before presentation.” This is proposed copy, not a ministry
workflow observed in research. It must localize without hard-coded names, date
formats, truncated international names or inferred location/time zone.

Use persistent labels, visible help, linked errors, keyboard order, screen-reader
status announcements and proper overlay focus return. Essential content/actions
must work at **320 CSS pixels**, equivalent zoom, RTL and light/dark themes without
hover dependence or a sticky footer hiding focus. On slow or interrupted connections,
show pending acceptance and retain repairable input in the current interaction;
do not persist sensitive drafts in browser storage by default. Browser dismissal
cannot cancel an already-accepted decision. Notification failure is separate from
successful review acceptance. A toast alone or disappearing task row is insufficient
confirmation. There is no new donor/public approval UI or added giving-checkout step.

No automatic expiry, escalation deadline or periodic reapproval is introduced.
Review time uses server-controlled instants; user-reported historical occurrence
uses qualified precision and timezone/unknown meaning. A backdated entry cannot
retroactively satisfy a prospective gate. Request follow-up and paused-journey
review dates retain their existing owner meanings.

### Database and mutation requirements

Use complete Tenant and source scope on requests, versions, reviews, decisions,
responsibility, receipts and outbox references. Non-null required parent keys and
composite unique/FK relationships must make cross-tenant or cross-request linkage
impossible; an optional child reference cannot bypass the required scoped parent.
Constrain finite decisions and choices, valid target references and terminal
cardinality. Separate append-only accepted evidence from mutable current heads.
Reject cascade deletion that erases decision provenance by ordinary edits; apply
lawful source retention/disposal explicitly without inventing permanent sensitive
snapshot retention. D23 introduces no new monetary calculation or amount storage.

All mutation paths use the same trusted source command semantics. Derive tenant,
actor, actual decision author, timestamps and allowed current context at the server;
validate a selected reviewer and target against current authority. Reject caller
changes to policy choice, approval status or provenance when not independently
authorized. Prove both the old row permission and allowed resulting state, including
changing Tenant, source, reviewer, target or terms. RLS is not field authorization or
a cross-row concurrency fence. Review grants, permissive/restrictive composition,
`USING`/`WITH CHECK`, views, function execution/search path, storage and service-role
paths using actual nonowner roles. Omitted `WITH CHECK` can inherit `USING`; omission
alone is not a vulnerability. Zero affected rows is not a successful decision.

Decision acceptance must serialize against relevant terms, pending-round,
responsibility, policy and authority changes. Use expected-head checks plus the
appropriate transactional locks/owner fences in one documented order; an application
precheck followed by an unguarded UPDATE is insufficient. Commit the decision,
mandatory local evidence, immutable receipt and required internal downstream intent
atomically. Remote task/notification projection retries from durable intent; its
failure cannot roll back or misreport an accepted decision. No externally approved
transaction or provider call is required inside the database transaction.

Idempotency is scoped to the durable business operation and exact review round,
actor/context and normalized intent, not solely an HTTP request. Concurrent same-intent
retries produce one result; competing distinct decisions cannot both win. Reuse of
an operation identity with changed content conflicts. Fresh current read authority
still governs a replay result; it never re-applies old state or exposes protected
payload because an idempotency key is known.

Supported outward action rechecks at the **owning action's authoritative start**, not
only when the UI displayed approval or a send was queued. Coordinate the relevant
current-policy/terms/revocation fence with that owner. Accepted action-start history
remains truthful if policy later changes; unreleased queued work is not grandfathered
merely by queue admission. Reconcile actual ambiguous provider acceptance under P6/17;
D23 does not claim a Postgres lock can undo an email already released. If the exact
owner boundary cannot be qualified, withhold that integration instead of bypassing
review. Recording what actually occurred remains separately available.

## Exact requirements to carry into the future specification

These are review requirements, not a prematurely published OpenSpec change.

- **R01 — Bounded policy.** The system SHALL implement the finite default and authorized
  per-request inherit/require/not-required choice above, with visible effective basis;
  it SHALL NOT infer optionality from missing policy, unknown money or ordinary edits.
- **R02 — Authority separation.** Preparation, submission, requirement administration,
  exception, decision, reassignment, correction and presentation SHALL resolve current
  exact P12 powers independently. Assignment and role labels SHALL grant none of them.
- **R03 — Reviewer eligibility.** Required review SHALL have one current qualified
  internal reviewer or a visible source-owned recovery condition. Allowed self-review
  SHALL be deliberate; actual separation floors SHALL use stable person identity.
- **R04 — Exact review target.** Decisions SHALL bind the exact source-owned business
  terms/version and prospective action. Source-defined reviewed-field changes SHALL
  supersede coverage; unrelated metadata SHALL NOT create another review.
- **R05 — Deliberate lifecycle.** Submission, decision, withdrawal, reassignment,
  resubmission and prospective correction/revocation SHALL follow the lifecycle table,
  preserve history and never fabricate donor refusal or revive ended work.
- **R06 — Current applicability.** Future supported actions SHALL use current required
  review coverage and source authority. Policy changes SHALL preserve explicit choices
  and historical facts, and SHALL never automatically send. Unrelated routing changes
  or reviewer departure alone SHALL NOT invalidate an accepted decision.
- **R07 — Domain boundaries.** Internal review SHALL NOT mutate supporter responses,
  stage/lifecycle, commitments, receipts, consent, publication or booking truth. Their
  qualified owner gates SHALL remain independently enforced.
- **R08 — Historical truth.** Authorized past presentation/response entry SHALL remain
  available without invented approval, with truthful occurrence/recording evidence and
  known/unknown approval basis. It SHALL NOT authorize future outward action.
- **R09 — Shared work.** Review and actionable recovery SHALL use registered source
  work and safe attention contracts. Generic task completion, notification read or
  timeout SHALL NOT decide; absent/indeterminate recipients SHALL release no fallback.
- **R10 — Minimal disclosure.** Reviewers SHALL receive a sufficient currently authorized
  view of relevant terms; protected bodies SHALL NOT be copied into general tasks,
  logs, messages, exports or caches without separately qualified exact projection.
- **R11 — Database invariants.** Complete scoped relationships, finite valid values,
  one pending target and one terminal decision per round SHALL be enforced by database
  constraints and one authoritative command boundary, including privileged paths.
- **R12 — Atomic acceptance and races.** Relevant current-head validation, terminal
  decision, mandatory local evidence, receipt and downstream intent SHALL commit
  atomically; conflicting edits/decisions/policy/authority changes SHALL have one
  coherent ordered result without stale clearance.
- **R13 — Durable retry.** Same business intent SHALL be idempotent across retries and
  deliveries; changed intent SHALL conflict; replay SHALL reauthorize its returned
  projection and SHALL NOT replace current truth with an old receipt.
- **R14 — Clear accessible UI.** The shared interface SHALL show meaningful current
  state, exact review scope, responsible next actor, permitted next action and change
  path; preserve relevant input, announce outcomes and distinguish failed/unknown/
  restricted states. It SHALL satisfy the concrete UX/browser cases above.
- **R15 — History and correction.** Accepted decision identity, actor, scope, terms
  reference, policy basis, recorded time and later dispositions SHALL remain traceable
  under lawful retention. Corrections SHALL NOT rewrite accepted history; retention,
  redaction and restore SHALL not revive expired access or erased sensitive content.
- **R16 — Independent native operation.** The complete native flow SHALL work without
  Studio, external approval vendors, a second inbox, a rule builder or an invented
  timer. Optional orchestration SHALL consume the same source fact and effect identity.
- **R17 — Bounded operational qualification.** Tenant-scoped current lists, authorized
  counts, paginated history and source recovery SHALL have measured workload/query
  evidence, bounded retries and explicit operations ownership before release.
- **R18 — Safe rollout.** Capability/schema/reader/worker/owner-binding compatibility
  SHALL precede activation; old clients/imports SHALL not create approvals. A kill
  switch SHALL stop unsafe new decisions/actions without converting required review
  to optional or destroying truthful history.
- **R19 — Proof and traceability.** Decision→glossary→owner mapping→future requirements/
  design/tasks→implementation/test/release evidence SHALL stay consistent. Actual
  authorization, concurrency, accessibility and rollout cases below SHALL be proven
  before claiming implementation readiness.

### Bounded experiment actually performed

The isolated [review-round probe](d23-evidence/review-round-probe/README.md) ran
**10/10 passing cases**, first attempt, on **PostgreSQL 17.11, READ COMMITTED**.
Its [results](d23-evidence/review-round-probe/results.json) and
[run metadata](d23-evidence/review-round-probe/run-metadata.json) retain exact
observations, input hashes, pinned image and cleanup. Six controlled sessions used
`pg_blocking_pids` to establish the intended blocked order: term revision/decision
both ways, duplicate retry, competing decisions and material-policy/action-start
both ways. Other cases establish metadata separation, late internal-event rollback,
reviewer departure and known-optional versus unknown/required-without-reviewer.

This proves those **synthetic database mechanics**, partially informing AC05–06,
AC13–16. The fixture is owner-only with synthetic eligibility, not real P12 or RLS.
Its action-start row is a local marker, not P6 dispatch or provider acceptance.
Its material-policy epoch models a relevant compatibility change, not a mandate to
invalidate approval whenever any configuration changes. It does not prove production
Supabase authorization, all ownership paths, load, accessibility, integrations or
practitioner understanding. No prior probe was rerun or relabeled as current proof.
No production, product code or live configuration changed.

## Adversarial check

### What could go wrong with this answer?

Approval could attach to changed terms, authorize the wrong person, leak private
ministry context or turn ordinary work into bureaucracy. The category review makes
those failures explicit and gives exact permanent R/AC safeguards. No known material
concern requires abandoning the selected bounded capability.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

We assume organizations can identify their actual requirement authority and an
eligible reviewer; some may give compatible powers to one person. That is not a
universal missions practice. No-review by default is Q23's product choice. Actual
capability population, scale and comprehension require release evidence; missing
reviewer/policy proof remains visible and safe rather than silently assumed.

### How does this affect the whole product?

P27 gains a native decision on requests and projects actual review work into shared
work. It does not gain financial, access, communication, publishing or booking
ownership. The recorded decision explicitly resolves conflicting adjacent/vendor
patterns without changing their governing scope.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

Ordinary requests remain quick. Required reviews have one readable state, one next
person and obvious actions in the existing Request/Tasks Hub. Material edits,
withdrawal, reviewer absence and lost responses have a clear recovery path. Donors
receive no new approval step or implied communication, and historical facts stay
recordable.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

It uses explicit state, exact submitted terms, current authority, transactional
invariants, recoverable outcomes, restrained conditional UI and accessible feedback.
Current primary CRM/CMS and W3C guidance informs those choices. Vendor-specific
sales restrictions and older case-specific ministry approval documents are not
misrepresented as universal proven practice.

### Does this fit Asym’s existing repo and product direction?

Yes, as a decision and qualification contract: Postgres owns truth, P12 owns access,
source-backed shared work owns presentation, and financial/communication owners keep
their effects. Current contribution-review code offers patterns and warnings, not
proof that the proposed P27 service already exists. Parked CMS governance and disabled
Studio contracts are not silently activated.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

**Accept A with the precise recorded interpretation above.** No material new policy
or owner expansion requires a separate amended choice. The proposal is now bounded,
coherent and testable; implementation must satisfy its safeguards before shipping.
The [document checks](d23-evidence/review-checks.json) establish this record's
structural consistency and preservation, separately from the limited SQL experiment
and the still-required product release evidence.
The first document-validation pass found formatting differences in four new evidence
Markdown files; those were corrected. Its retained [initial report](d23-evidence/review-checks-initial.json)
is separate from the first-attempt passing database experiment.
