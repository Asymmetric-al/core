## Why ask this next?

[D8](phase27-d8-reopening-review.md) selects reopening the same partnership work,
preserving its closure and later return. It does not establish that the last
recorded understanding of the partnership is still current. Q8 expressly reserved
stage behavior; the [P27 roadmap](../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/roadmap.md:4087)
requires stage history distinct from enrollment lifecycle.

For this question, **stage** means the short description of where the development
work stands, such as getting acquainted, discussing support or caring for an
ongoing partnership. These are explanatory examples, not newly approved stage
labels or a final sequence. Stage is not supporter value, consent, the outcome of
an individual ask, a gift or a command to contact someone.

The unresolved product choice is how much reassessment is required at reopening.
Whether to preselect an old stage in a mandatory form would prematurely assume
that such a form is required. Similarly, deciding whether an error-correction
button is separate from Reopen would ask about mechanics before a genuine new
product choice. D7 already requires qualified correction without erasing history;
its remaining intervening-event and collision rules remain design work.

## Original single founder question — answered A

Illustrative situation: staff reopen a church's partnership work after several
months. Asym knows where the work was last recorded, but circumstances may have
changed. Staff might need a conversation before they know where things stand now.

**Should staff have to review that recorded progress when reopening?**

**A — Keep the last recorded stage; let staff update it when ready (recommended).**
Clearly show that it is the last recorded understanding. Reopening does not claim
it was freshly checked, and staff can change it in context.

**B — Require a quick review as part of reopening.** Staff explicitly confirm
or change the stage, or acknowledge that it still needs assessment. Do not force
them to invent an answer when circumstances are unknown.

Both preserve prior stage history and avoid automatically resetting the church
to a new-prospect stage. Neither starts donor contact or revives old work.

**Recommendation:** A avoids an obligatory administrative step when staff may
be returning precisely to learn what has changed. B provides a deliberate
freshness check and may reduce overlooked stale assessments, but adds a required
interaction. Neither benefit has been measured with agency users. This remains
a founder choice, not a ratified default.

## Evidence, alternatives and safeguards

- [D3](phase27-d3-adversarial-review.md) keeps one ordinary partnership across
  asks and different participation forms; stage must not be inferred from a gift
  or the response to one ask. [D7](phase27-d7-adversarial-review.md), R02/R08/R12,
  preserves stage history and distinguishes source closure from other facts.
- [Q8](phase27-q8-returning-to-closed-work.md) did not authorize automatic use of
  an old stage as a newly assessed fact. A proposes retaining visibly dated prior
  knowledge; it is not implicit fresh confirmation. B proposes a required review,
  not a new assessment or approval platform.
- [ADR-0183](../../../adr/0183-source-owned-work-projects-into-one-shared-tasks-hub.md)
  still controls any actual required shared work. The existing next-action-or-owned-
  exception invariant applies under both options and is not being reopened.
  No task is created merely because a stage is uncertain or the journey returns;
  task admission requires its concrete source rule.
- D7's [UX](d7-evidence/ux.md) and [ministry](d7-evidence/ministry.md) evidence
  supports contextual information, preserved history and avoiding invented work.
  It does not establish that this particular confirmation step improves outcomes.
  Sources were checked earlier on 2026-10-10, not newly tested for Q9.
- Two independent reviewers agreed that stage handling is a meaningful next
  choice. They differed on whether explicit reassessment should be mandatory.
  Their feedback identifies the tradeoff; it is not ministry-user research or
  authority to choose for the founder.
- If a historical stage has been retired or is no longer valid in the applicable
  preset, do not silently coerce it into an active valid value. Show its permitted
  historical meaning and use the stage owner's qualified resolution path. Exact
  preset evolution and transition rules remain to be groomed.
- Do not invent a last-reviewed date from generic record modification time or
  reopening time. Use only authoritative recorded stage/history evidence; unknown
  dates remain unknown. Current and historical reports must state what their
  stage measures mean, without counting reopening as a new confirmed assessment.
- B's uncertain outcome does not by itself approve a new stage, mandatory task,
  queue, deadline or review cadence. A does not approve indefinite unowned work.
  Both must fit the already required current accountability and source contracts.
- Neither option supplies new permissions, new stage labels, automatic changes,
  a fixed stage order, reporting-duration rules or automated outreach. Those
  choices remain separate from this one required-versus-optional review decision.
