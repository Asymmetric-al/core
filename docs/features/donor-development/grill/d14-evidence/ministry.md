# D14 — additional stage lists: ministry fit and simplicity

Research appendix, **2026-10-10 UTC**. A was selected for review; this appendix
does not ratify it. Only additional organization-approved stage lists are in
scope. No product was executed, practitioner interviewed or live configuration
changed. Likelihood ratings are design judgments, not measured agency behavior.

## Assessment and strongest alternative

**Allowing additional lists is defensible as an optional organization capability.**
The evidence supports distinguishing genuinely different processes. It does not
show that every ministry, country, team, missionary or participation type needs a
list. Shared understanding matters more than the number of configurable lists.

**B's strongest case** is meaningful: supplied lists already support tenant-defined
stages, guidance and direct stage choice. That may accommodate local language,
different working styles and occasional extra steps without another catalog to
administer, choose or compare. MPDX even distinguishes presentation grouping from
underlying statuses. Keeping those capabilities and building no additional-list
facility would have lower configuration cost.

B's limit is a genuinely different set of stage meanings: combining incompatible
processes can leave people sorting through irrelevant choices or interpreting the
same label differently. Selected A makes room for that case. It does not establish
that any specific missions customer has demonstrated it; the best published
nonprofit examples below concern grants and gift opportunities, which must not be
mistaken for Phase 27's ongoing relationship work.

## Material concerns, exact amendments and proof

### M01 — list proliferation without a different process

**Severity: medium; likelihood: plausible.** Configuration grows while staff face
nearly identical choices and inconsistent training. HubSpot explicitly discourages
this team-based split; MPDX supplies the presentation counterexample. Neither
source establishes an optimal list count for missions.

**Decision effect:** retain A with shared-list guidance; no new approval ceremony.

**Proposed clause:** “Additional lists support meaningfully different cultivation
processes. Different personnel, team names, screens, participation modes or wording
do not by themselves require different lists. Existing approved lists remain
usable without additional setup.”

**Proof:** compare illustrative teams with the same stage meanings but different
terminology, then teams needing materially different meanings. Shared work must
remain straightforward in the first case; A must permit the second. Do not make a
justification essay, artificial numeric cap or administrator committee the remedy.

### M02 — separate lists become separate partnership truths

**Severity: high; likelihood: plausible.** A staff member and missionary might
expect their preferred lists to hold different stages for the same effort. A
church might also be assigned a list solely because it is a church, regardless
of the actual work. This follows from the local shared-source risk, not an observed
agency failure in the sources.

**Decision effect:** preserve D1–D3 and one source meaning; leave applicability open.

**Proposed clause:** “An additional list does not create another partnership or
stage authority. The same effort uses one applicable list and the same recorded
stage meaning in all authorized staff and missionary views. Creating a list does
not assign it to existing work or establish selection, default or switching rules.”

**Proof:** staff and a missionary view one shared effort; the church separately
supports another ministry. Creating another list neither duplicates the first
effort nor changes either record or grants access to the other ministry.

### M03 — financial and nonfinancial facts become process prerequisites

**Severity: high; likelihood: plausible.** Vendor opportunity examples can suggest
that each participation mode needs a pipeline or that meaningful progress requires
a gift. OMF's broader partnership model supplies the relevant ministry evidence.

**Decision effect:** preserve participation and source boundaries across all lists.

**Proposed clause:** “Additional lists retain the same separation of cultivation
assessment from participation, asks, tasks, communication eligibility, financial
facts and lifecycle. A prayer-only partnership does not require a financial action
or another list merely to remain meaningful. Changing participation does not
automatically create or switch a cultivation list.”

**Proof:** illustrate a church offering prayer and practical assistance, later also
giving. The changed contribution is recorded by its owner without a forced list
change, invented ask outcome, duplicate effort or permission to send messages.
This does not prohibit an independently justified process from using another list.

### M04 — familiar labels conceal incomparable assessments

**Severity: high; likelihood: plausible.** “Engaged” can mean different things in
two lists. Position three of four is not comparable to position three of seven.
The sources show process variation; the reporting consequence is our inference
and already follows from D10's meaning-preservation rule.

**Decision effect:** carry existing truthful reporting into new lists, without a
mandatory universal funnel or mapping project.

**Proposed clause:** “Reports and authorized views retain enough list context to
interpret each recorded stage. Shared labels or order do not establish equivalence,
progress percentages, giving probability or supporter value. Configuration changes
are distinct from actual partnership-stage changes; historical meaning is retained.”

**Proof:** two lists use the same label with different guidance; rename or reorder
one. No combined stage count silently claims equivalent progress, no journey moves,
and past history remains interpretable. Ordinary work totals may still be combined
under their actual shared definitions. D14 does not settle metric formulas.

### M05 — list metadata leaks information or becomes a new access system

**Severity: high where sensitive; likelihood: context-dependent.** An illustrative
name containing a restricted worker or ministry location may disclose information
even when records are hidden. Conversely, treating each list as a security boundary
creates avoidable permissions complexity. This is a source-boundary inference,
not a documented OMF or MPDX incident.

**Decision effect:** preserve P12 and minimize sensitive configuration content.

**Proposed clause:** “Names, guidance and configuration metadata are shown only
under their qualified visibility rules. Using or configuring a list grants no
record, ministry, financial or history access. Use process-oriented names and avoid
unnecessary personal or sensitive operational information in reusable definitions.”

**Proof:** test unauthorized cross-tenant metadata reads and an authorized user
with limited ministry access, including relevant selectors and reports. No new
list-specific permission system, universal catalog visibility or blanket secrecy
policy is inferred; exact applicability and metadata visibility need qualification.

## Appropriate current limits and unresolved evidence

A does not select list creation from scratch versus copying, defaults, assignment,
switching/mapping, list retirement/restoration, or limits on list count. Preserve
existing identity, history, usable-catalog and current-authorization requirements
when those dependent decisions are made. Additional lists cannot reintroduce
mandatory stage ladders, required tenant evidence fields or compulsory initial
assessment contrary to D11–D13. Organization approval uses existing configuration
authority, not a new approval queue or private missionary customization right.

Before final presets and entry UX, compare supplied lists with a small set of
actual agency process descriptions. Include staff, support-raising missionaries,
church relationships and nonfinancial partnership. Ask participants to explain
stage meanings, distinguish them from tasks/asks, and interpret another authorized
worker's record. Investigate whether each proposed extra list adds a useful
distinction that guidance cannot express. No participant feedback, measured
benefit, preferred labels, sample-size rule or success threshold exists yet.
