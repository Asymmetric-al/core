# D19 practice evidence — an optional stage when changing lists

2026-10-10 UTC. Research appendix for the founder's selected A; proposed safeguards
are not additional ratified decisions, implementation or a formal specification.
Scope: deliberately changing the approved stage list on the same partnership work.
The accepted D18 operation and D13's distinction between actual absence and failed
reads govern; vendor requirements do not override them.

## Finding and evidence limits

Allowing an honest absence of current assessment has direct nonprofit CRM precedent
and a narrower missions-tool analogue. It does not establish a universal missions
preference or prove demand for changing lists. The useful product judgment is to
allow the selected process change without manufacturing a destination assessment,
while keeping earlier assessments intelligible and the work visible.

The main hazard is treating **no current stage on the destination list** as **no
earlier knowledge of this partnership**. This also applies when returning to a
previously used list. Earlier assessment is historical evidence, not an automatically
restored current assessment. A list change is a recorded staff action, not proof of
new supporter progress, reduced commitment or a fresh relationship.

## Strongest alternative and minimum sufficient approach

**B, require a destination stage**, gives immediate categorization and makes a
stage-based board simpler. Staff may often know the appropriate stage while changing
lists. Salesforce illustrates a coherent model where stage is mandatory because it
drives financial forecasting. The strongest argument for B is interpretability and
complete current classification, not an unsupported claim that staff otherwise
neglect supporters.

For the selected Core model, B can block an appropriate process correction until
staff know a separate fact, or encourage a convenient but inaccurate stage. Those
are plausible risks, not measured behavior. A tenant-created “Needs assessment”
stage could explicitly mean real work in that process; it should not be required
merely to disguise absent assessment as completeness. Nor is a second partnership,
an explanatory note alone or a default first stage a truthful substitute.

**Recommended implementation boundary:** one clear stage consequence within D18's
existing deliberate save, with the permitted choice of an eligible destination stage
or qualified absence. No additional approval, compulsory reason, dedicated assessment
queue, deadline or mapping engine is justified by this research. Keep the familiar
Set stage action available later under the existing rules. D19 does not settle
clearing a saved stage while staying on the same list, automatic suggestions, bulk
changes, or operation while paused/closed.

## Material concerns and exact proposed safeguards

Severity and likelihood are qualitative review judgments, not measured incidence.

### P1 — The label wrongly erases earlier understanding

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** An established church can reasonably read
an unqualified “Not yet assessed” as “we do not know this supporter.” Returning to
an old list makes “never assessed on this list” especially misleading.

**Proposed clause:** “When used after changing lists, ‘Not yet assessed’ means no
current stage is recorded for the accepted destination binding. It makes no claim
that the partnership or that list has never been assessed. Preserve authorized
earlier assessments in history and distinguish them from the current result.”

Possible supporting copy: **“No current stage is recorded on this list. Earlier
assessments remain in history.”** This is proposed wording, not a final UI mandate.

**Proof:** Return from list B to previously used list A without choosing a stage.
The current record has no stage; A's old assessment and the intervening B history
remain historical. Staff can explain that the church relationship was not reset.

### P2 — A failed lookup becomes a supposedly deliberate absence

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** An empty control can be a loading state,
permission failure, incompatible old selection or the authorized outcome. Those
meanings must not be collapsed.

**Proposed clause:** “Show the destination list and intended current-stage outcome
clearly before the ordinary save. Accept absence only as the supported submitted
outcome; a failed or incomplete read, stale selection or missing field is not evidence
of that intent. No separate acknowledgment ritual is required.”

**Proof:** Compare an intentional unassessed destination, an already-unassessed
source, a failed stage load and cancellation. Only the accepted supported outcome
changes saved work. Already-unassessed staff do not have to manufacture a stage or
repeat an irrelevant declaration.

### P3 — Reports hide work or invent progress

**High severity; plausible likelihood.** Views grouped through configured stages
can omit unassessed records. Treating absence as stage zero invents ordering; using
the last historical stage as current hides the accepted change.

**Proposed clause:** “Authorized totals of current partnership work include records
without a current stage. Stage-specific views disclose their coverage and provide
an intelligible route to those records. Do not equate absence with a first stage,
failure, zero giving, no consent, or a stage reached. Keep historical stage intervals
distinct from current assessment and process-change history.”

**Proof:** Changing one existing partnership to an unassessed destination neither
changes the total number of partnerships nor creates a stage-entry achievement.
It remains findable. Any displayed current-stage duration is absent or explicitly
inapplicable; earlier durations are not relabeled as current. This does not require
a new duration report or aging mechanism.

### P4 — Optional assessment becomes permission to abandon real work

**Medium severity; plausible likelihood.** Conversely, adding mandatory assessment
tasks or repeated reminders would reintroduce a gate through another screen.

**Proposed clause:** “The assessment outcome changes neither existing accountability
nor the facts or obligations owned elsewhere. Keep the lead, legitimate next work,
asks, participation, contact restrictions and financial records intact. Do not
create or complete work, contact a supporter or infer financial participation solely
because the destination has no current stage.”

**Proof:** An illustrative prayer partner and an illustrative church supporting a
missionary team retain their existing facts and assigned work after the same change.
These examples are pressure tests, not claims about observed agency workflows.

## Empirical questions left open

No practitioner study, error rate or frequency of stage-list changes was established.
The current missions documentation does not show this exact multi-list operation.
Before release, use the real shared interface with staff and authorized missionaries
to test a known destination stage, honest uncertainty, a return to an earlier list
and finding the resulting record. Ask them to explain the current versus historical
assessment and identify the unchanged next work. Observe misunderstandings; do not
claim invented success thresholds, measured benefits or universal agency agreement.
