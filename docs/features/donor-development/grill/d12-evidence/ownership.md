## New consequences and precise inherited safeguards

Severity describes the prospective consequence; likelihood is conditional
engineering judgment, not an observed incident rate.

### O1 — A hidden allowed-moves graph reverses A

**High; plausible if a vendor pipeline abstraction is copied.** E1–E3.
**Clause:** “For an otherwise permitted ordinary update, any currently available
stage in the journey's applicable approved preset may be selected directly.
Neither rank nor the prior stage adds an adjacency, forward-only or tenant-defined
route restriction.” This adds no permission for retired/wrong-scope targets or
another preset. **Proof:** skip and backward moves succeed under the same source
safeguards; reorder changes neither availability nor movement rights. No special
backward-move reason, manager approval or hidden transition-policy builder.

### O2 — Repeated visits are collapsed, or stale writes survive a cycle

**High; likely with uniqueness or comparisons based only on target stage.** E3.
**Clause:** “A → C → A records two distinct accepted changes. A retry of either
change retains that occurrence's original outcome; it is not a new visit.”
Compare the expected enrollment revision, not only the visible current stage:
an old editor observing the first A remains stale after A → C → A. **Proof:**
both recurrence and retry, an old A-to-other-target request after the cycle,
lost response followed by newer changes, and same-key/different-intent conflict.
This requires ordinary source revisions/receipts, not an event-sourcing platform.

### O3 — Skips fabricate intermediate accomplishments

**High; plausible with funnel backfill or synthetic sequential events.** Q12 and
E2/E3. **Clause:** “Record the actual accepted from/to change only; stages not
visited acquire no transition, assessment, duration or completed-work evidence.”
**Proof:** A → D leaves B/C unvisited; no invented zero-duration visits, completed
appointments, consent, fulfilled asks or messages. Config order changes cannot
retroactively turn the same history into advancement or regression.

### O4 — Re-entry restarts old tasks, reminders or recipes

**High; plausible when effects are keyed by stage or replay an old workflow run.**
E5/E7. **Clause:** “Entering a previously visited stage does not revive ended work
or repeat prior effects. Any genuinely required new obligation/effect needs its
own qualified source occurrence and current authority; native and Studio callers
share its semantic identity.” **Proof:** return after an earlier task completed,
was canceled, reassigned or lost recipient eligibility; no old completion/read
state resets and no duplicate contact occurs. A new stage event is not itself
proof that every former destination-stage task should recur.

### O5 — Existing work is either blindly retained or discarded

**High; plausible with ‘cancel previous stage tasks’ or ‘all open tasks count’.**
E1/E3/E5. **Clause:** “Maintain appropriate dated work or an owned actionable
exception. Preserve applicable existing obligations; their owning source decides
any completion, cancellation, replacement or continuing applicability.” One open
task is not automatically the appropriate next action; a stage change is not
task completion. **Proof:** an appropriate existing task survives without a
duplicate; independently due work remains accountable; obsolete work cannot
make the journey falsely healthy. Detailed stage/work treatment remains open.
Do not import D7's closure-wide obligation census as a mandatory preflight for
every ordinary stage move or add a universal replacement-task wizard.

### O6 — A lifecycle-looking stage bypasses pause or closure

**High/Critical; plausible with P28's mixed legacy labels.** E4/E8.
**Clause:** “Direct stage movement changes recorded understanding only; it does
not pause, resume, end, reopen, archive or suppress a journey/Party, alter
participation, or remove an independently binding restriction.” Prayer and
financial partnership can coexist; moving ‘back’ is not evidence of lost support.
**Proof:** lifecycle/finance/consent-like labels produce none of those facts;
D6 review obligations and D7/D8 outcomes retain their source semantics. D12 does
not silently decide whether an ordinary stage edit is admissible while a journey
is closed, nor block separately qualified historical correction.

### O7 — Reporting turns recurrence into a continuous stay or false forecast

**High; likely with a single first-entry timestamp or ordinal funnel metric.**
E2/E3/E6. **Clause:** “Retain sufficient authoritative change/provenance evidence
to distinguish repeated visits and actual skipped stages. Each exposed metric
declares its meaning, source basis, time treatment and completeness.”
**Proof:** distinguish A → C → A from continuous A; do not infer money/probability,
relationship deterioration or completed work from rank. A no-op, list rename,
reorder or reopening creates no fresh stage/contact assessment. Unknown imported
dates remain unknown; historical closed periods remain closed.

This is a truthfulness requirement, **not** a selected first-entry age,
latest-visit duration, cumulative duration, pause-exclusion or funnel-conversion
formula. P27 supplies source facts; P33 owns qualified report definitions. Do not
introduce a separate analytics store or freeze a visit-table schema to answer D12.

### O8 — A current update becomes an unqualified historical correction

**High; plausible when ‘move back’ is labeled Undo.** E3/E4.
**Clause:** “A current reassessment adds its own accepted fact; it does not erase
or backdate earlier truth, reverse fulfilled work, or imply the earlier assessment
was mistaken.” **Proof:** a real return to a prior stage preserves the intermediate
history; actual erroneous-entry repair follows its separately qualified command.
Same-value selection remains a no-op under D11, not a new assessment or age reset.

## Permanent scope and remaining decisions

B's strongest benefit was organization process control, but the founder chose
accurate direct movement. Preserve useful approved definitions and normal source
accountability; do not retain B as a dormant route matrix or impose backward-move
ceremonies. Authorized users can still enter mistaken assessments; existing
attributable history and source correction are the bounded response, not an
invented AI truth judge, cooldown or percentage-complete model.

Still open: initial stage, preset assignment/switching, source-qualified correction
and backdating, exact work consequences for particular changes, and named stage
duration/reporting definitions. D12 settles skip/back movement only; it creates
no automatic stage transition, outreach, broad grant or donor-facing flow.

The root review should carry O1–O8 into later proof as inherited safeguards.
Choosing a new duration formula, automatic task replacement, broad closed-record
editing power or new mandatory review would be a material policy addition requiring
its own explicit treatment; none is necessary to accept A now.
