## Why this question is next

[D22](phase27-d22-adversarial-review.md) settles financial and significant
nonfinancial request scope, truthful responses and relevant fields. It deliberately
does not select the organization’s request-review policy. The [P27 roadmap's open
decisions](../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/roadmap.md#phase-27-open-decisions)
expressly reserve ask-review rules. The original founder brief asks us to resolve
practical approval scope and material changes after approval without inventing
financial authority.

[DON-15](../../../prds/workflow-studio/07-core-workflow-catalog.md#don-15--askproposal-and-appeal-coordination)
consumes required human review and exact proposal approval; it remains disabled
and requires exact source-owner qualification. That does not establish that every
request needs approval, who should approve it, or a mandatory workflow product.
Existing P12 access, financial-owner requirements and communication eligibility
remain governing in both options.

An illustrative situation: an organization might want a leader to review a
substantial church funding proposal before staff present it, while allowing
ordinary prayer or visit invitations to proceed within staff authority. This is
a product scenario, not a claim that all missions organizations follow that policy.
This choice concerns whether Asym manages that review, rather than the wording
of an individual field or status.

## Founder question — answered A

**Q23 — Should Asym let an organization require internal approval for selected
partnership requests before staff present them?**

**A — Built-in review when needed (recommended).** Routine authorized requests
proceed without extra approval by default. Organizations may require review for
selected requests, with a clear reviewer, decision and next action in Asym.

**B — Use existing tasks and conversations.** Staff handle internal review through
their normal tools and organizational process. Requests does not add a separate
enforced approval decision.

**Recommendation:** A, provided it remains a small native capability. It lets an
organization formalize the review it actually needs while leaving ordinary work
quick. B is a credible smaller product: teams can coordinate review through
existing work, but staff must establish compliance with that process themselves.
Neither option changes required upstream permissions or authorizes financial or
communication actions. The recommendation is product judgment, not a measured
missions-staff preference or evidence of an implemented review service.

## Boundaries and dependent design

If A is selected, approval concerns the identified proposed terms and prospective
action. It is not a donor response, financial promise, receipt, contact consent,
publication approval or access grant. Its meaning must survive relevant proposal
changes without automatically treating an old approval as approval of new terms.
Do not create a general approval designer, mandatory chain or review requirement
for every request merely to implement this choice. Exact policy applicability,
eligible review, revisions, replacement reviewers and recovery should be developed
as one coherent flow using the founder's delegated judgment, not a series of
micro-votes. A genuine unresolved authority conflict still needs an explicit ruling.

Whichever option is selected, authorized staff can truthfully record an already-made
request and response. The product must not force them to invent earlier approval
or block historical fact recording because an action occurred outside the expected
process. Record what happened separately from whether the action was approved.
Asym can govern its own supported actions; it cannot prevent an off-platform
conversation or retroactively approve it. Current never-ask/contact restrictions
remain binding for suggestions and prospective outward work.

Approval must not silently resume a paused partnership, reopen closed work, create
a commitment or activate optional Studio. Native ordinary request work remains
usable without Phase 34 or 42. This question neither designs a general volunteer,
event or grant system nor reopens D22's financial/nonfinancial scope.
