## Original single founder question — answered A

**Should staff be able to skip stages or move back when that better describes
the partnership?**

For example, after a conversation with a supporting church, staff may realize its
recorded stage no longer fits. This is an illustrative accuracy problem, not a
verified universal missions workflow or a requirement to record a conversation.

**A — Choose the appropriate stage directly (recommended).** Authorized staff
can select any currently available stage in the partnership's approved list,
including skipping stages or moving back. The earlier history stays intact.

**B — Let organizations restrict stage changes.** Organizations can define which
moves staff may make. This offers more process consistency but needs clear handling
when the permitted moves do not fit the actual relationship.

**Recommendation:** A lets staff keep the record accurate without unnecessary
steps. Organizations still customize their stages and explain what each means.
This is a product judgment about this continuing partnership model and usability,
not a measured claim that every missions organization prefers direct movement.

## Boundaries preserved under either answer

- Current authorization, tenant/source scope, eligible choices, privacy, truthful
  history, expected-version checks and next-task-or-owned-exception rules remain
  mandatory. A is not permission to select retired or otherwise ineligible stages.
- Reordering the list cannot silently change permitted moves. B would require an
  explicit, bounded policy and treatment of legitimate exceptions; it does not
  authorize arbitrary predicates, required fields or manager approval.
- A direct change records the accepted change, not fictional intermediate stages.
  Neither answer certifies contact, supporter agreement, consent, money or completed
  tasks. Neither authorizes automatic messages or stage changes.
- Keeping the recorded stage when reopening remains D9's decision. Initial-stage
  selection, preset switching, historical corrections/backdating and the detailed
  treatment of existing work remain separate questions. A current update must not
  be confused with correcting what was recorded about the past.
- No initial-stage, preset, correction or detailed existing-work answer is inferred
  from the movement choice.
