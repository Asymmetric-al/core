## 3. Accuracy and failure evidence

**Research on large language models (LLMs) for schema matching (VP).**

- **Parciak et al., VLDB 2024 workshop.** Inputs were names and descriptions only, with no data values, on health schemas. Mean F1: an n-gram string baseline scored **0.335**, and its threshold was tuned on the ground truth ("In practice, a data engineer cannot know which threshold to use"). GPT-4 scored **0.518–0.580** depending on prompt scope. GPT-3.5 in one-to-one mode scored 0.241, with the note "the high decisiveness indicates that using the 1-to-1 task scope makes the wrong decision most of the time". Findings:
  - "matching quality suffers from a lack of context information, but also from providing too much context information"
  - "the LLM sometimes jumps to conclusions as it overemphasizes similarity of attribute names", for example matching two time fields that describe different events
  - Run-to-run spread of F1 was 0.03–0.16; "LLMs are consistent enough to be used in practice"
  - Candidate lists stayed under 10% of the search space, which they "deem … acceptable for manual verification"
  - The authors flag that the benchmark is probably in the training data
  - https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11852v1
- **Steiner & Bizer 2026.** The LLM received "column names, a value summary (unique counts and example values per column)" plus sample rows. It "correctly identified all 67 attribute correspondences" (F1 1.00), against 0.32 for a label-based matcher and 0.19 for an instance-based matcher. The cost was about $10 per case study. Caveats: flat tables with one-to-one matches only, and public data that "may partly reflect memorized knowledge". Rule-based normalization failed on unknown formats ("2000" kept as-is). LLM taxonomy mapping "retains the original value when no suitable target taxonomy entry exists". https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10547v1
- **Buss et al. 2025.** LLM schema mapping suffers "inconsistent outputs due to sensitivity to input phrasing and structure"; the authors propose sampling and aggregation. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24716
- **SCHEMORA 2025.** Enriching metadata (names and descriptions, no sample values) gave "gains of 7.49% in HitRate@5". https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14376v1
- **Narayan et al. 2022.** On the Synthea benchmark, GPT-3 zero-shot F1 was about 0.5 (near useless), three examples gave about 45.2, and the prior best was 38.5 (S: the extracted table was garbled). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.09911
- **Dates.** DateLogicQA (2025) found LLM biases across seven date formats (S, https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13377v2). Canada mixes DD/MM and MM/DD; the official recommendation is YYYY-MM-DD but nothing enforces it (S, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_and_time_notation_in_Canada).

**Vendor claims (unaudited).** Flatfile's ML predicts "over 90% of matching actions" from "1.8 billion (and counting!) rows" (VV, https://support.flatfile.com/articles/8569925090-mapping-data). Dromo claims errors are cut "by up to 98%" (VV). No vendor published a headers-only versus headers-plus-samples comparison (U).

**Failure modes seen in the evidence:**

1. **Plausible but wrong:** a near-identical name with a different meaning (Parciak). For donors _(inference)_: "Date" (gift date vs. deposit date), "Amount" (gift vs. pledge total), "Fund" vs. "Campaign" vs. "Appeal", or "Name" holding a household name.
2. **Inconsistency:** the same input gives different output across runs or phrasings (Parciak; Buss).
3. **Date and locale errors:** ambiguous DD/MM vs. MM/DD. A model cannot settle this from a few samples if every day is ≤12 _(inference)_.
4. **Transformation errors:** unknown formats pass through silently, and values with no target are kept as-is (Steiner & Bizer). Value maps can also leak sensitive values (Ingestro).
5. **Prompt injection:** "Indirect prompt injections occur when an LLM accepts input from external sources, such as websites or files" (VV, https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/). A header or cell is such an input.
6. **Auto-execution on confidence:** Flatfile's automap plugin "Controls the minimum confidence level required for the plugin to automatically execute the mapping job" ("confident" means above 90%). That is the opposite of human-confirmed commit (VV, https://flatfile.com/docs/plugins/automap).

**How good products keep people in control:**

- Mark AI suggestions: OneSchema shows "A star … next to mappings created using AI"; Blackbaud shows a confidence level.
- Require review: OneSchema: "Users will then be prompted to review the suggested mappings". Dromo: "Every column is either confidently auto-matched or explicitly reviewed by the user". Insycle: "Always review these AI suggestions carefully".
- Let saved templates win: Insycle: "the template settings will take precedence over AI suggestions". Blackbaud updates "only unmapped fields".
- Make AI changes reversible: Flatfile AI Assist lets users "replay and undo".

**Are column headers personal data?** I found no ruling. _(inference)_ Generic labels ("Email", "Gift Date") identify no one and are normally not personal data under GDPR Art. 4(1) or PIPEDA. They can still carry personal or sensitive information in Asym's setting:

- Pivoted exports whose columns are funds or designations named after a worker, family or country.
- Custom-field labels such as a security tier or a ministry code for a creative-access field.
- A headerless file whose first data row is read as the header (a name, email and amount).
- Free-text or report titles inside header cells.

Because giving to a missions ministry can itself suggest religious belief (ICO inference rule; GDPR Art. 9(2)(d)), the ministry's own field and fund labels deserve the same care as the data. Q2 already treats headers as confidential (`decision-log.md:68`).

## 5. UX patterns users rate highly

- **Propose with sample values, side by side.** Show "their column, an arrow, your field, with a sample value" (S, https://www.saasui.design/blog/saas-data-import-mapping-ux-patterns, 2026-08-07). Flatfile previews on hover. _(inference)_ Sample values shown locally to the reviewer need no egress.
- **Show where a suggestion came from.** OneSchema's AI star, Blackbaud's confidence level, Ingestro's 0.6 match threshold. Dromo skips the mapping step only when every column matches a key, label or alternate name (S).
- **Never drop unmapped columns silently.** "Unmatched columns should be surfaced explicitly with an obvious choice" and not "silently dropped" (S). Contrast: Flatfile and the Salesforce wizard exclude unmapped columns by default. Brevo flags columns to skip (S).
- **Saved mappings per source.** Order of precedence: user history, then organization history (OneSchema, S); per-client memory (Dromo `autoMapHeaders`); exact-header templates (LGL, Bloomerang); saved templates beat AI (Insycle); fill only unmapped fields (Blackbaud). One-click "clear all mapping suggestions" (Flatfile).
- **Value-mapping tables.** Each source value maps to a target option, or "Keep this value as is" (import, then flag), or "Do not import" (blank that cell only) (Flatfile). Picklist suggestions need review (OneSchema).
- **Preview before applying transforms.** "Users can preview the data before applying changes" (CSVBox). Generated functions can be undone (Flatfile).
- **Bulk accept and keyboard flows.** No vendor documentation found (U).

## 6. Deterministic baselines

- **Standard layer order.** Ingestro's default is exact (ignoring case and punctuation), then previous mappings, then AI, then fuzzy. OneSchema uses exact match, "alternative column names" and history. CSVBox uses per-field aliases (VV/S). Bloomerang, Salesforce, Givebutter and Mailchimp use deterministic name matching with mandatory review.
- **Measured numbers.** No vendor publishes accuracy for deterministic-only matching (U). On hard cross-vocabulary schemas, string and label baselines reach only F1 0.32–0.34 (Parciak; Steiner & Bizer). Those benchmarks are adversarial, not repeated exports from a known vendor _(inference)_. Exact saved templates are 100% correct by construction but break on any header change, per LGL's warning about "even one column header name" being different.
- **Share of migrations from known exports.** No measured figure was found (U). The sector's practice points the same way: DonorPerfect names 13 source systems, Givebutter 8, and Bloomerang names Excel, Access, QuickBooks and the Blackbaud family, each handled through templates or a person. _(inference)_ Most Asym "other system" imports will come from a short list (Raiser's Edge, Bloomerang, DonorPerfect, LGL, Neon, Virtuous, Kindful, Mailchimp, spreadsheets). Per-source alias packs and saved mappings cover these. AI's marginal value lies in the long tail of hand-made spreadsheets.

## Common pattern

1. **Deterministic first, AI last.** Every product runs exact, alias and saved-mapping layers before any model, and saved templates take precedence over AI.
2. **Header mapping is sold as metadata-only.** OneSchema mapping, Insycle Meta, Ivandt and Flatfile all describe it that way. **Value work needs real values.** Samples go to OneSchema transforms and Ingestro pre-processing, and whole files go to OneSchema extraction.
3. **AI is on by default and turned off by the customer.** OneSchema (off only through the Customer Success Manager), Insycle ("all data sharing options are enabled" by default), Ingestro (`smart` in the default layers) and Brevo (S) all ship it on. Only CSVBox transforms are clearly opt-in.
4. **Disclosure is uneven.** Dromo's privacy page and blog conflict. Blackbaud discloses nothing for its feature. OneSchema reserves the right to use data to improve features for all customers.
5. **Suggestions are labelled and reviewed**, but some products let high confidence auto-execute (Flatfile automap). No donor-CRM vendor uses AI for migration; people and templates do that work.

AI suggestions from headers plus Asym's field catalog only, through Asym's own provider** (a D10 purpose, off by default)                                                           | Header strings the deterministic layers missed, plus catalog labels                                                                                                     | On hard schemas, names-only GPT-4 beat a tuned string baseline (F1 0.58 vs 0.335)                                       | Plausible but wrong matches; run variance; headers that carry worker, fund or place names; a data row read as the header; injection; provider retention (30 days on Covered Models) | Needs the unbuilt D10 foundation and a Phase 10 ruling on headers; barred for the D17 profile |

AI with sample values**                                                                                                                                                             | Cell values: personal data, possibly revealing religious affiliation or worker identity                                                                                 | Highest (F1 1.00 in three public case studies)                                                                          | Personal and special-category egress; Quebec PIA; donor notice                                                                                                                      | Conflicts with C17 ("merely to improve mapping")                                              |

Embedded importer vendor**                                                                                                                                                          | Vendor-dependent: OneSchema sends headers and template (mapping), samples (transforms) or whole files (extraction) to OpenAI, Anthropic or Google with 30-day retention | Comparable to B or C                                                                                                    | A second processor; conflicting or missing disclosures; a cross-customer "improve features" clause; non-`base-maia` UI; a separate custody of the bytes                             | Conflicts with ADR-0173 item 4, the UI invariant and Phase 29 custody                         |

**Recommendation (research view, not a decision).**

1. **Ship v1 deterministic-only (A)**, but build it to the market bar:
   - An alias pack and saved mapping per known source.
   - Saved mappings per source that win over everything else.
   - Fuzzy matches shown as "Suggested — review required".
   - Every unmapped column handled by an explicit choice.
   - Value-mapping tables for gift types, funds and payment methods.
   - Previews of transformed sample rows that never leave Asym.
2. **Never use AI for value transforms in v1.** Fund and designation names can name workers (Ingestro's warning applies doubly). Dates should be detected by scanning the whole column; if the column is ambiguous (no day above 12), the reviewer picks the format explicitly. Name parsing stays rule-based, and compound names ("John & Mary Smith") are flagged for review (see Q10).
3. **Measure before buying B.** During pilots, record inside Asym how many columns the deterministic layers left unmapped. Consider B only if that number is material for non-SiteStacker sources.
4. **If B is ever added**, these are the conditions:
   - Off by default, enabled per tenant by a named ministry admin, with the decision recorded.
   - Only unmatched header strings and catalog labels are sent. Headers that look like data (email or number patterns, very long strings) are filtered out deterministically first.
   - Nothing from restricted-tier fields or the D17 profile, and no value lists.
   - Output restricted to catalog field IDs or "none".
   - Each suggestion labelled with its reason, and never auto-accepted at any confidence.
   - A per-call egress record listing exactly what was sent.
   - A ZDR-capable, non-Covered model in a US or Canadian region (for example Bedrock in `none` mode, or Anthropic or OpenAI with approved ZDR).
   - Header text kept out of shared logs.
5. **Reject C and D for v1.** C breaches C17 as written. D adds a processor whose AI disclosures are inconsistent or allow cross-customer use.

## Open for counsel and security review

1. Whether ministry-specific header and catalog labels (fund or designation names, custom-field labels, pivoted columns) count as personal or sensitive information under PIPEDA, Quebec Law 25 and, for any EU donors, GDPR Art. 9 (religious-affiliation inference). Whether sending them to a US model provider triggers a Quebec s. 17 PIA or a GDPR 9(2)(d) consent question.
2. Whether Asym's model provider must be named as a subprocessor in each ministry's agreement before B is enabled (see the Q2 clause 5(a) subprocessor list), and whether tenant opt-in must be contractual or a product setting.
3. Whether ZDR (or Bedrock `none`) is required, or 30-day abuse-monitoring retention is acceptable for headers. If ZDR is required, which models are excluded (Covered Models).
4. Data residency: does a US or Canadian inference region matter for Canadian ministries' metadata?
5. Whether donor-facing privacy notices must mention AI-assisted import processing (Fundraising Regulator practice; OPC openness).
6. Security review of injection through headers, output allow-listing to catalog IDs, and logging and observability so header strings never reach shared telemetry.
7. Whether "headers are confidential" (Q2) extends to the alias and saved-mapping store. That store keeps source header text per tenant and must stay tenant-scoped and never be pooled across customers, unlike Flatfile's and OneSchema's cross-customer learning.
