## 1. United States

**CAN-SPAM (email)**

- The Act has no blanket nonprofit exemption. The FTC: "It is possible that a message from a nonprofit could meet the definition of 'commercial electronic mail message'". Messages from a trade association "to its members or donors are likely 'transactional or relationship messages'". The FTC's own jurisdiction excludes entities not "organized to carry on business for its own profit", but states and ISPs can still enforce. **VP**: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2005-01-19/html/05-974.htm (70 FR 3110). That preamble does not mention charitable solicitations.
- Pure donation appeals are generally not "commercial". Fundraising emails "generally need not meet the CAN-SPAM requirements". A charity selling branded goods can be covered. **S**: https://privacy.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2021-08/Email%20Compliance%20with%20Federal%20CAN-SPAM%20Act.pdf
- Opt-out deadline: "You must honor a recipient's opt-out request within 10 business days." The opt-out mechanism must work for "no less than 30 days" after sending (15 USC 7704(a)(3)). **VP**: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business and https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7704
- Carrying opt-outs to a new platform: it is unlawful to "transfer or release" an opted-out address "for any purpose other than compliance with this chapter or other provision of law" (7704(a)(4)(A)(iv)). The FTC adds "you may transfer the addresses to a company you've hired to help you comply" and "you can't contract away your legal responsibility to comply with the law." **VP**: same URLs. _Inference:_ the opt-out duty belongs to the sender, so changing software does not reset it, and moving the suppression list to Asym is the permitted compliance transfer. The FTC does not literally say "carry the list over".
- State anti-spam laws are preempted "except to the extent that any such statute… prohibits falsity or deception" (15 USC 7707(b)(1)). **VP**: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7707
- CAN-SPAM is opt-out only. It has no consent-record requirement.

**TCPA (SMS and calls)**

- Autodialed or prerecorded calls (texts count as calls) to wireless numbers need "the prior express consent of the called party" (47 CFR 64.1200(a)(1)). That paragraph has no nonprofit carve-out. "Telephone solicitation" excludes calls "By or on behalf of a tax-exempt nonprofit organization" ((f)(15)(iii)). Prior express _written_ consent ((f)(9)) is tied to telemarketing and advertisement, meaning "commercial availability… of any property, goods, or services" ((f)(1), (f)(13)). **VP**: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/64.1200 (eCFR redirected to a captcha). A secondary source says nonprofit texts are exempt only from the "written" part. **S**: https://blog.charitywatch.org/?p=1394
- Revocation: by any reasonable means, honored "within a reasonable time not to exceed ten business days" ((a)(10)). **VP**: same.
- "Revoke-all" rule: delayed to 2027-01-31. **S**: https://www.burr.com/telephone-consumer-protection-act/the-fcc-delays-effective-date-of-tcpa-revoke-all-rule-until-january-31-2027. On 2026-09-09 the FCC amended it: a marketing opt-out "acts as a revocation of consent for all marketing/advertising calls or texts from that caller", while an informational opt-out covers only "that specific category". The amendment takes effect 30 days after Federal Register publication, which I did not confirm. **S**: https://www.hunton.com/insights/legal/fcc-adopts-clarifying-changes-to-the-tcpa-revoke-all-rule-to-be-effective-30-days-after-publication
- Whether consent transfers: I found no primary source. Carrier rules (CTIA, as relayed by vendors) say an opt-in "should not be transferable or assignable", and "Carriers expect 1:1 consent records for all message types". **S**: https://help.dialpad.com/hc/en-us/articles/54907803504283-SMS-Messaging-Compliance-Principles-Best-Practices and https://www.bandwidth.com/support/en/articles/12823164. _Inference (U):_ consent given to the ministry probably still covers that ministry when a new vendor sends on its behalf. It does not cover a different sender. Counsel should confirm.
- Company do-not-call requests must be honored within 10 business days and kept for 5 years ((d)(3), (d)(6), telemarketing context). **VP**: same CFR URL.

**State privacy laws:** Colorado, New Jersey, Maryland, Minnesota, Delaware and possibly Oregon reach some nonprofits. The sources conflict, and thresholds apply. **S**: https://wiley.law/alert-New-State-Privacy-Laws-May-Apply-to-Nonprofit-Organizations. I did not research state "mini-TCPA" texting laws (for example Florida and Oklahoma) (**U**).

## 2. Canada

- **Scope:** a message is a CEM (commercial electronic message) if "one of its purposes, is to encourage participation in a commercial activity" (CASL s.1(2)). "Commercial activity" applies "whether or not… in the expectation of profit" (s.1(1)). **VP**: https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/page-1.html. A "Program update, volunteer recruitment or donation appeal with no commercial element" is "Not a CEM". **S** (Charity Law Group, updated 2026-10-07): https://www.charitylawgroup.ca/charity-law-questions/casl-for-charities
- **Charity exemption:** ECPR s.3(g) exempts a CEM "sent by or on behalf of a registered charity as defined in subsection 248(1) of the Income Tax Act" where "the message has as its primary purpose raising funds for the charity". **VP**: https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2013-221/page-1.html. The exemption covers "all of section 6". Event tickets whose proceeds go to the charity are exempt. Unrelated merchandise and sponsor promotion are not. **S**: Charity Law Group (above). The CRTC charity FAQ was blocked.
- **Implied consent from a donation:** implied consent requires "an existing business relationship or an existing non-business relationship" (s.10(9)). A non-business relationship includes a donation or gift to a registered charity "within the two-year period immediately before the day on which the message was sent" (s.10(13)). The window rolls forward with each gift. **VP**: https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/page-2.html. Express consent "has no expiry". **S**: Charity Law Group.
- **Consent is to the sender, not the software:** a request for consent must identify the person seeking it and the person on whose behalf it is sought (s.10(1)). Section 6 "does not apply to a telecommunications service provider merely because" it enables transmission (s.6(7)). A business buyer inherits _business_ relationships (s.10(12)). I found no equivalent rule for non-business (donor) relationships. **VP**: CASL pages 1–2. _Inference:_ a software change does not void consent. A ministry merger or a split between affiliated entities might.
- **Unsubscribe:** must take effect "without delay, and in any event no later than 10 business days" (s.11(3)). **VP**: page 2.
- **Burden of proof:** the person alleging consent "has the onus of proving it" (s.13). **VP**: page 2. The CRTC recommends keeping "all evidence of express and implied consent", "documented methods through which consent was collected", and "all unsubscribe requests and resulting actions". **VP**: https://www.canada.ca/en/radio-television-telecommunications/news/2016/07/enforcement-advisory-notice-for-businesses-and-individuals-on-how-to-keep-records-of-consent.html
- **Québec (Law 25 / P-39.1 s.22):** anyone using personal information "for commercial or philanthropic prospection purposes" must "identify himself" and "inform that person of his right to withdraw his consent". After withdrawal, the information "must cease to be used for those purposes". **S** (official text blocked): https://watchdogsecurity.io/law25/withdrawal-of-consent-for-prospection. Nonprofits count as "enterprises". **S**: https://www.dialoginsight.com/en/blog/law-25-en/impact-of-law-25-on-consents

## 3. GDPR / UK (brief)

- The controller "shall be able to demonstrate that the data subject has consented". It "shall be as easy to withdraw as to give consent" (Art. 7(1), 7(3)). **VP**: https://gdpr-info.eu/art-7-gdpr/
- Legacy consent survives a regime change only "if the manner in which the consent has been given is in line with the conditions of this Regulation" (Recital 171). **VP**: https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-171/. GDPR has no "consent portability" concept. Consent belongs to the controller, so changing processor does not by itself require new consent (_inference_).
- **UK PECR:** the commercial soft opt-in requires details obtained "in the course of the sale or negotiations for the sale of a product or service" (reg 22(3)), so it does not cover donations. The new reg 22(3A) charitable-purpose soft opt-in has been in force since **5 Feb 2026**. It applies where the charity obtained details while the recipient was "expressing an interest in" or "offering or providing support to further" its purposes, and requires a refusal option at collection and in every message. "Charity" means one registered under the Charities Acts of England and Wales or Northern Ireland, or the Scottish register (reg 22(5)). **VP**: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/22. Lists collected earlier get no benefit: "Pre-existing supporter lists will still require consent." **S** (ICO page blocked): https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/05/08/the-charitable-purposes-soft-opt-in-ico-issues-updated-direct-marketing-guidanc-102ms57. _Implication:_ US and Canadian ministries that are not UK-registered cannot use 22(3A) at all.

## 4. ESP and mailbox-provider practice

- **Mailchimp:** "If you import subscribed email contacts, be sure that you have permission". "You'll need to verify that you have permission to market to them." Import statuses are Subscribed, Unsubscribed and Non-subscribed. **VV**: https://mailchimp.com/help/import-contacts-mailchimp/. The AUP says "You must be able to point to an opt-in form or show other evidence of consent". Purchased, rented and third-party lists are banned. **VV**: https://mailchimp.com/legal/acceptable_use/. On reconfirmation, Mailchimp targets "subscribed contacts who haven't heard from you recently" and says "We generally consider such email addresses stale". It gives no fixed age. **VV**: https://mailchimp.com/help/reconfirm-contacts
- **Constant Contact:** for unsubscribes from another provider, "it's important to add them to your Constant Contact account". Once imported, you "won't be able to send emails to them" unless the person rejoins through a sign-up tool. **VV**: https://knowledgebase.constantcontact.com/articles/KnowledgeBase/33118-Import-a-List-of-Unsubscribed-Contacts-into-Constant-Contact-from-Another-Provider
- **HubSpot:** after an opt-out import, "The contacts' email addresses will be disqualified from receiving any future marketing emails". Those addresses are not billable. **VV**: https://knowledge.hubspot.com/marketing-email/import-an-opt-out-list
- **Virtuous:** the integration rule is that "the absence of a positive opt-in is treated as an opt-out, overriding any previous value". **VV** (Fundraise Up): https://fundraiseup.com/support/virtuous-opt-out/. Deceased individuals are "not automatically removed from Email Lists". **S** (Virtuous help blocked).
- **Salesforce Marketing Cloud:** uses auto-suppression lists, populated by import. **S**: https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/marketing/marketing-cloud/guide/interacting_with_auto_suppression_lists_via_the_soap_api.html (search snippet only).
- **Neon:** no field documentation found (**U**).
- **Mailbox providers:** Google says "Make sure recipients opt in", "Don't purchase email addresses", keep spam rate "below 0.30%" (target "below 0.10%"), and "Start with a low sending volume to engaged users, and slowly increase the volume over time." **VV**: https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126. Yahoo says "Honor unsubscribes within 2 days". **VV**: https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/
- **Warm-up:** start with "hyper-engaged" recipients, then recently engaged ones. Typical warm-up runs about 30 days and up to 60. **S** (snippet): https://twilio.com/en-us/resource-center/email-guide-ip-warm-up

## 6. Evidence regulators expect

- **CASL:** the onus is on the sender (s.13). Keep records of consent evidence, collection methods, policies and unsubscribes (CRTC 2016, above). CRTC bulletin 2012-549 reportedly says web-form consents need a record of "the date, time, purposes, and" manner. **S** (CRTC page blocked): https://webiis05.mondaq.com/canada/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/201566/casl-enforcement-bulletins-released-by-crtc-increased-compliance-burden-on-business. Implied consent from a donation is proven by the gift record and its date.
- **GDPR:** Art. 7(1) requires the controller to demonstrate consent. The ICO's "who, when, how, what told" guidance was blocked (**U**).
- **TCPA:** the 2015 order put the onus "almost entirely on the caller" to prove consent. **S**: https://www.classdefenseblog.com/2015/07/fcc-expands-potential-liability-under-the-telephone-consumer-protection-act-for-business-to-customer-calls-and-text-messages/. Carriers expect the acquisition method, the exact language shown, a timestamp, IP and phone number. **S**: Bandwidth (above).
- **CAN-SPAM:** no consent record is required. Opt-outs must be honored and must not be transferred except for compliance.
- **Migration attestation:** I found **no regulator text** that accepts an organization's attestation in place of underlying evidence. ESPs use attestation as an import gate ("verify that you have permission"), but Mailchimp's AUP still requires the ability to "show other evidence of consent". _Inference:_ an attestation records who vouched for the basis. It does not discharge CASL s.13 or GDPR Art. 7(1). Carry the original timestamp, source, wording and IP where they exist.

## Common pattern

1. **Suppression always travels and never weakens.** Every ESP provides an unsubscribe import, and US and Canadian law fix a 10-business-day ceiling. Yahoo's 2-day ceiling is stricter in practice.
2. **Consent travels only with its provenance.** It belongs to the sending organization, not the software. The burden of proof stays with the sender (CASL, GDPR, TCPA).
3. **Implied consent can be recomputed from imported gift history** (CASL's rolling 2-year window). It does not need to be imported as a flag.
4. **Re-permission is aimed at subsets** (stale, unprovable, UK/EU, SMS), not whole lists. Mailchimp and Google tie it to staleness and engagement, not to the act of migrating.
5. **Warm-up** starts with recently engaged recipients.

## Implications for Asym

1. **Import all suppressions first and unconditionally as hard floors:** email opt-out, do-not-contact, do-not-solicit, do-not-mail, do-not-call, deceased, hard bounce and complaint. Keep the source system, original date and reason where present. No imported value may lift a suppression, and an import may not re-subscribe anyone; only the donor can (as with Constant Contact). Treat a blank or absent field as "unknown", never as "opted in". Note that Virtuous-style syncs treat absence as opt-out.
2. **Store a legacy "subscribed" flag as a provenance claim,** not as consent. Capture the source system, original timestamp, capture source or form, wording and IP if exported, plus an org attestation (named staff member, date, stated basis). This matches the ESP norm and Asym's rule.
3. **Derive a per-jurisdiction sending basis instead of trusting flags:**
   - **US email:** non-commercial updates and appeals may legally go to donors who have not opted out (CAN-SPAM is opt-out based). The only gate is deliverability and stale-list hygiene. Emails with commercial content (merchandise, sponsors) need CAN-SPAM handling.
   - **Canada:** non-CEM updates and appeals, and fundraising-primary CEMs from _registered_ charities, need no consent. For other CEMs, compute implied consent from imported gift or volunteer dates (rolling 2 years). Express consent requires evidence. Non-registered ministries lose the s.3(g) exemption.
   - **UK/EU contacts:** require demonstrable consent. Non-UK ministries cannot use the 22(3A) soft opt-in. Unproven contacts go to a targeted re-permission campaign.
   - **SMS:** text only with per-number evidence of prior express consent. Re-collect consent where there is none.
4. **Without a mass re-permission campaign,** the main lever is gift and engagement recency. Send first to donors with recent gifts or opens (warm-up). Hold back long-dormant contacts or send them a reconfirmation message.
5. **Québec contacts:** every philanthropic-prospection message must identify the ministry and offer withdrawal.
6. **Open for counsel:** the TCPA vendor-change question, the effective date of the 2026 FCC revoke-all amendment, consent after a ministry merger or split, and the CRTC FAQ wording (blocked).
