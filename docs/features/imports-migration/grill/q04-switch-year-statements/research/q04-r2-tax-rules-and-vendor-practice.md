# Q04-R2 — Switch-year receipts and year-end statements: US/Canada rules and vendor practice

_Track: who may issue tax acknowledgments/receipts and year-end statements in the Switch Year, and whether one system's document may include gifts the other system received or already acknowledged._
_Research agent, read-only web research. Access date for every URL: 2026-11-02. Not legal advice; counsel should confirm before policy is ratified._

Labels: **verified-primary** (statute, regulation or tax-authority page fetched and quote checked) · **verified-vendor-doc** (vendor page fetched and quote checked) · **secondary** (law-firm, commentator or search-index snippet, not fetched in full) · **unverified/inferred** (my reasoning, no source states it). Quotes from canada.ca and vendor pages were captured through a fetch tool that caps each quote at ~125 characters, so long sentences appear in fragments. Pub 1771 quotes come verbatim from the PDF text.

## Summary

1. **US: annual statement optional.** A contemporaneous written acknowledgment (CWA) is needed per single gift ≥$250. It may be per gift or "one … such as an annual summary". Not acknowledging "incurs no penalty"; the burden is the donor's. (verified-primary)
2. **US: more than one acknowledgment is allowed.** Donors may use "one or more" CWAs. Nothing found forbids a summary that repeats gifts already acknowledged. (primary; the duplicate conclusion is inferred)
3. **US trap:** a year-end statement that is the _only_ CWA for a gift ≥$250 must carry the goods/services statement. _Durden_ lost the deduction without it; a later correction was not contemporaneous. (secondary)
4. **Canada:** for cash gifts, receipt "periodically through the year" or "one cumulative receipt for the year". Non-cash gifts are receipted separately. (verified-primary)
5. **Canada: no second official receipt for a gift.** No CRA page says so in words. But a re-issue must be a formal replacement citing the original serial number, with the original marked "cancelled" (Reg. 3501(4)–(5)). Incorrect or false receipts carry penalties. Canada-aware vendors exclude gifts already receipted. (primary mechanism; conclusion inferred)
6. **Canada: one serial-number space.** Each receipt needs "a unique serial number", so two systems in one year must not collide. LGL lets clients "pick up where you left off in your other system". (verified)
7. **Canada retention:** receipt copies are kept 2 years after the calendar year of the gift (cancelled/reissued ones, 2 years after the year of that action), in electronically readable form. The duty survives "conversions and upgrades … or migration to/from a third party". (verified-primary)
8. **Vendor pattern:** import old-system gifts without receipts so **one** statement covers the year (Planning Center, Tithe.ly→Breeze, Givebutter, Virtuous after validation). Fallback: "two end-of-year statements" (Planning Center). Canadian tools never re-receipt a receipted gift.
9. **Timing:** no vendor explicitly recommends a 1 January switch. Secondary sources say avoid a Q4 go-live.
10. **Donors:** churches warn "you may receive two separate statements … You'll need both". Tithe.ly says two statements "often confuses donors". No quantitative study found.

## 1. United States (IRS)

**Statute (IRC §170(f)(8)).** No deduction is allowed for a contribution of $250 or more unless it is substantiated by "a contemporaneous written acknowledgment of the contribution by the donee organization". The acknowledgment must state:

- the cash amount;
- a description, without a value, of any non-cash property;
- whether the donee provided goods or services, with a description and good-faith estimate if it did, or that they were "intangible religious benefits".

It is contemporaneous if the donor obtains it by the earlier of the return's filing date or its due date including extensions. — https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/170 (verified-primary)

**Regulation (Treas. Reg. §1.170A-13(f)(1)).** A taxpayer making "more than one contribution of $250 or more to a donee organization in a taxable year" may substantiate them "with one or more contemporaneous written acknowledgments". Also: "Separate contributions of less than $250 are not subject to the requirements of section 170(f)(8)". Paragraph (f)(11)(ii) treats each payroll withholding as a separate contribution. Paragraph (f)(12) treats a distributing organization "as a donee organization solely for purposes of section 170(f)(8)". — https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.170A-13 (verified-primary; eCFR redirected to a bot wall)

**Pub 1771 (Rev. 11-2023)**, https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p1771.pdf (verified-primary):

- Per gift or annual: "A separate contemporaneous written acknowledgment may be provided for each single contribution valued at $250 or more, or one contemporaneous written acknowledgment, such as an annual summary, may be used to substantiate several single contributions valued at $250 or more. There are no IRS forms for the contemporaneous written acknowledgment."
- Software and email: "Letters, postcards, or computer-generated forms with the above information are acceptable." The charity "can provide the acknowledgment electronically, such as via an email addressed to the donor."
- An annual statement is not mandatory: "An organization that does not acknowledge a contribution incurs no penalty; but, without a contemporaneous written acknowledgment, the donor cannot claim the federal income tax deduction. Although it's a donor's responsibility to obtain a contemporaneous written acknowledgment, a charitable organization must assist a donor…"
- "Separate contributions of less than $250 will not be aggregated."
- Practice, not law: "Charities typically send written acknowledgments to donors no later than January 31 of the year following the donation."
- Gifts under $250 still need "a record of the contribution or a timely written communication from the charitable organization showing the name of the organization, the date of the contribution, and the amount". A year-end statement serves this.
- Quid pro quo: a disclosure is required for "a payment exceeding $75 partly as a contribution and partly for goods and services", given "in connection with either the solicitation or the receipt". Penalty: "$10 per contribution, not to exceed $5,000 per fundraising event or mailing".

**The no-goods-or-services statement is load-bearing.** In _Durden v. Comr._, T.C. Memo. 2012-140, the donors gave their church $22,517. The deduction was denied because the church's letter "failed due to lack of required statement". A corrected letter came after the return was filed, so it was not contemporaneous. — https://www.calt.iastate.edu/annotation/durden-v-comr-tc-memo-2012-140 (secondary)

**Answers to the brief:**

- **Per gift or annual?** Either one, or both. (verified-primary)
- **Duplicate acknowledgments?** No IRS text forbids them; "one or more" are expressly allowed. The CWA does not fix the deduction; the donor deducts each gift once. Listing gifts acknowledged elsewhere on a year-end summary is therefore not a compliance problem if the donee is the same legal entity, the amounts and dates are right, and a goods/services statement covers every gift ≥$250. Marking those gifts is good practice, not law. (unverified/inferred)
- **Annual statement required?** No. (verified-primary)
- **Agent or software issuer?** The statute requires an acknowledgment "by the donee organization", and computer-generated forms and email are acceptable. No IRS text was found on third-party issuers. In practice, platforms issue acknowledgments in the charity's name. A gift legally received by a _different_ donee (a DAF sponsor, fiscal sponsor or intermediary charity) should not be acknowledged as the ministry's own. (unverified/inferred)
- **Deadline:** the earlier of the date the donor files or the due date with extensions. 31 January is custom, not law. (verified-primary)

## 2. Canada (CRA)

**Per gift or cumulative.** "There is no requirement in the Income Tax Act for a registered charity to issue an official donation receipt" "or that it issue a receipt within a certain timeframe." The CRA "suggests that registered charities issue receipts by February 28" of the following year. A charity "may issue receipts periodically through the year or, for cash donations," "issue one cumulative receipt for the year." "A separate receipt must be issued for each non-cash donation." — https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/operating-a-registered-charity/issuing-receipts/when-should-a-charity-issue-a-receipt.html (page date 2018-11-01; verified-primary)

Income Tax Regulations s. 3501(1)(e) requires, for a cash gift, "the date on which or the year during which the gift was received", which is the legal basis for cumulative receipts. — https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/c.r.c.,_c._945/section-3501.html (verified-primary)

**Required contents** (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/operating-a-registered-charity/issuing-receipts/what-information-must-on-official-donation-receipt-a-registered-charity.html, verified-primary):

- "a statement that it is an official receipt for income tax purposes"
- the charity's name and address "as on file with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA)"
- "a unique serial number"
- the registration number
- the location where the receipt was issued
- "the date or year the gift was received"
- "the date the receipt was issued"
- the donor's full name and address
- the amount of the gift, any advantage, and the eligible amount
- the signature of an authorized individual
- "the name and website address of the CRA"

The CRA checklist adds that a charity cannot issue receipts "on behalf of another organization or charity" or "in a name other than the name of the true donor". — https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/checklists-charities/issuing-complete-accurate-donation-receipts.html (verified-primary)

**Replacements, and no second receipt.**

- Reg. 3501(4): a replacement "shall show clearly that it replaces the original receipt" and "shall show the serial number of the receipt originally issued".
- Reg. 3501(5): spoiled forms are marked "cancelled" and kept together with the duplicate. (verified-primary)
- The CRA page requires "a statement that it replaces the original receipt" and says the charity "should keep its copy of the original receipt and mark "cancelled" on it" (page date 2017-06-22). — https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/operating-a-registered-charity/issuing-receipts/correcting-replacing-official-donation-receipts.html (verified-primary)
- The phrase "cancels and replaces" is vendor wording, not CRA wording:
  - CanadaHelps uses "cancels and replaces the lost receipt" (https://forcharities.canadahelps.org/charity-life-blog/how-to-issue-donation-receipts-as-a-charity; verified-vendor-doc).
  - Software4Nonprofits prints "This receipt cancels and replaces the original No. YYYY-NNNNN" and marks the old one "CANCELLED - it has been replaced by No. …" "to help make sure that the donor doesn't use both receipts" (https://www.software4nonprofits.com/DonationManual/duplicate_receipts.htm; verified-vendor-doc).
- Penalties: incorrect information carries "a penalty equal to 5% of the eligible amount stated on the receipt", rising to 10% for a repeat. A false statement carries "125% of the eligible amount". — https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/operating-a-registered-charity/issuing-receipts/consequences-improper-receipting.html (2025-09-08; verified-primary)
- **No CRA page found states "never issue two official receipts for the same gift."** It follows from the replacement-only mechanism and the penalties, and vendors build it in (§3). (inferred)

**Mid-year change of receipting system.** No CRA guidance specific to a mid-year software or provider change was found. Closest, IC05-1R1:

- ¶22: requirements must "continue to be met in the event of third party changes" "such as, software and/or hardware conversions and upgrades, bankruptcy or migration to/from a third party."
- ¶19: using "an application service provider" does not relieve responsibility.
- ¶21: "A good practice would be to have the third party provide the taxpayer with an acceptable copy" "in an electronically readable format."
- ¶37: conversion "must not result in a loss, destruction, or alteration of information".

— https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/ic05-1/electronic-record-keeping.html (verified-primary)

CPC-026 (2003) adds: "This tax-receipting privilege is not to be casually farmed out to third parties", and the charity must keep control "over the receipts that are issued". — https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/policies-guidance/policy-commentary-026-third-party-fundraisers.html (verified-primary)

Reading these together (inferred):

- A cumulative receipt from Asym for the Switch Year may cover **only gifts not already receipted**, whichever system received them.
- Re-receipting the old system's gifts is allowed only as a formal replacement: cite each original serial number and mark the originals cancelled.
- Old and new serial numbers must not collide.

**Gifts received by another legal donee.** Donations through CanadaHelps "are legally made to CanadaHelps CanaDon" "and CanadaHelps CanaDon issues the official tax receipt." — https://www.canadahelps.org/en/terms-of-use/ (verified-vendor-doc). The ministry must never receipt such gifts.

**Informational summary (not a receipt).** No CRA guidance on wording a giving summary was found. The CRA does recognise non-official acknowledgments: a charity may acknowledge gifts from other charities "by way of a letter or ordinary receipt" — "one that does not state that it is an official receipt for income tax purposes." — https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/operating-a-registered-charity/receiving-gifts/gifts-other-registered-charities.html (verified-primary)

Suggested approach (inferred):

- Omit the "official receipt" statement, the serial number and the signature.
- Show a prominent "This is not an official receipt for income tax purposes" line.
- Label already-receipted lines with the issuing system and receipt number.

**Retention.** "You must keep these receipts for two years from the end of the calendar year that you received the donations." Cancelled or reissued receipts: "two years after the end of the calendar year" "in which the receipts were cancelled or reissued." Electronic records "must be kept in an electronically readable format", at the Canadian address on file. With a third party, "you are still responsible for meeting all requirements."

— https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/operating-a-registered-charity/books-records.html (2025-09-08; verified-primary)

ITA s. 230(2) requires "a duplicate, of each official donation receipt". A stored soft copy can be that duplicate (CPS-014). Copies of email receipts "must be retained by the charity" (computer-generated receipts page). — https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/policies-guidance/policy-statement-014-computer-generated-official-donation-receipts.html (verified-primary)

Enforcement example: in a 2010 revocation the CRA cited "A large number of duplicate copies of receipts were either missing or not maintained." — https://www.canada.ca/en/news/archive/2010/08/canada-revenue-agency-revokes-charitable-status-christ-apostolic-church-international-canada.html (verified-primary)

**Does the migration have to carry the old receipt copies?** The duty sits with the charity, not with Asym. However, the old vendor's copies must stay retrievable for the retention window, and Asym needs the old receipt numbers to:

- avoid double receipting;
- reference originals in any replacement receipt.

(inferred)

**Common pattern:** import the year's prior-system gifts → suppress receipts on import → tag the batch by source → validate totals → issue **one** consolidated statement. Two statements is a documented fallback, not a recommendation. Canadian tools add a hard rule: a gift on one official receipt never appears on another, except through "cancels and replaces". No vendor reviewed tells clients to switch on 1 January. Secondary guidance says avoid a Q4 go-live (donordock.com/articles/nonprofit-crm-migration-checklist; search snippet, secondary).

## 4. Donor experience

- CyLife church notice: "Because CyLife switched giving platforms last year, you may receive two separate statements" "one from Pushpay (our previous platform) and one from Planning Center (our new platform)." "You'll need both statements for your tax records." — https://j2ns9q.nucleus.church/give (verified, primary church notice)
- Tithe.ly: two statements "often confuses donors". (secondary snippet, above)
- Planning Center frames the problem as churches' worry about "donors receiving split year-end statements". (verified-vendor-doc, blog above)
- No survey or quantitative evidence of donor confusion was found. No notice in the form "your Jan–Jun giving is on our previous system's statement" was found beyond the CyLife example. (unverified)
