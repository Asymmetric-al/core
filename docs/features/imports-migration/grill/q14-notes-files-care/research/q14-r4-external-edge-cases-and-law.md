# Q14 R4: Edge cases, law, retention and sealed-record patterns for bringing everything across

Researched 2026-11-03 for the founder's choice C. This extends `q14-r2-external-records.md` without repeating it. Labels: **[snippet]** means a search excerpt or secondary summary. **[unverified]** means no source was found. **[gap]** means nothing public was found. This is not legal advice.

## 2. Law affecting importing and holding

- **Washington MHMDA:**
  - No nonprofit carve-out (r2).
  - "Consumer" **excludes anyone "acting in an employment context"**, and "collect" includes retaining and receiving ([RCW 19.373.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373.010)).
  - Collection needs consent unless it is "necessary to provide a product or service" the consumer requested. Consent to share is separate. Disclosure to a processor is not "sharing" ([RCW 19.373.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373.030)).
  - **Implication:** the risk sits in donor health notes, which are unlikely to be "necessary", not in staff HR records. Leave-behind per category is the escape route. Asym acts only as processor.
- **Other US states:**
  - Colorado covers nonprofits above 100,000 consumers, and religious beliefs need opt-in consent ([BCLP](https://bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/the-colorado-privacy-act-applies-to-non-profits-is-your-non-profit-ready.html)) **[snippet]**.
  - New Jersey has no exemption. Minnesota, Maryland and Delaware have only narrow carve-outs ([Wagenmaker](https://wagenmakerlaw.com/blog/new-state-data-privacy-laws-affect-nonprofits)).
  - **Maryland** (35,000 consumers) allows sensitive data **only when "strictly necessary"**, and consent does not cure it ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2024/07/us-maryland-online-data-privacy-act-summary-and-comparative-analysis)) **[snippet]**.
  - **Implication:** most ministries fall below the thresholds, but minimisation and leave-behind are still product features.
- **FCRA and ADA:** the Disposal Rule requires reasonable disposal and no minimum retention. ADA medical information is kept in separate files. **Implication:** import background-check facts, not reports, and seal medical material.
- **Canada:**
  - Under PIPEDA, fundraising is generally not commercial activity, but donor-list trading is ([OPC](https://priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda-compliance-help/pipeda-interpretation-bulletins/interpretations_03_ca)).
  - BC PIPA covers all nonprofit activity. Alberta covers Societies Act nonprofits only for commercial activity ([Enkel](https://www.enkel.ca/blog/technology/privacy-acts-not-for-profits/)) **[snippet]**.
  - Quebec Law 25 covers nonprofits and requires a **privacy impact assessment before data leaves Québec** ([BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/insights/quebec-law-no-25-a-little-known-privacy-law-with-a-big-reach.html)) **[snippet]**.
  - **Implication:** Asym needs a one-page hosting and subprocessor fact sheet for ministries' assessments.
- **UK and EU:**
  - Article 28 requires a processor contract and authorisation for sub-processors ([Art 28](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/28)).
  - DPA 2018 Sch 1 para 18 allows safeguarding processing without consent ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12/schedule/1/paragraph/18/enacted)).
  - Subject access reaches imported staff notes, including opinions, with third parties redacted.
  - **Implication:** every tier must be findable by person for one privacy role.

## 3. Retention norms

- **Counselling:** BACP sets no single period, though it gives three years (its complaint window) as one example (above). Six years is commonly cited **[snippet]**. ACA sets no fixed period, while Ohio sets seven ([Ohio](https://regulations.justia.com/states/ohio/title-4757/chapter-4757-5/section-4757-5-09/)) **[snippet]**.
- **Safeguarding:** 70–75 years in the UK. Australia's Royal Commission recommends at least 45 years ([The Other Cheek](https://theothercheek.com.au/child-abuse-alert-churches-need-to-keep-their-records-for-100-years-or-forever/)) **[snippet]**. US churches are advised to keep screening records indefinitely ([Church Law & Tax](https://www.churchlawandtax.com/keep-safe/abuse-prevention/qa-how-long-should-we-keep-consent-forms-and-screening-records/)) **[snippet]**.
- **Applications:** the claim period (ICO), and one year under the EEOC (r2). **Background-check reports:** secure disposal.

**Implication for Asym:** the norms run from 1 to 75 years, so don't hard-code retention. Each family gets a review date set by the ministry. Safeguarding is never auto-deleted.

## 4. Sealed and restricted access patterns

- **Break the glass:** extra authentication plus a stated reason, with every use logged and reviewed. It usually warns rather than blocks ([AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/digital-health/are-break-glass-functions-required-employee-ehr-access)).
- **Existence disclosure:** the NHS planned a "sealed" level, whose existence was visible, and a "sealed and locked" level, whose existence was hidden. Making it "really sealed and not just a password protected file" proved difficult ([Digital Health](https://digitalhealth.net/2006/12/revised-plan-for-sealed-envelopes-published)) **[snippet]**.
- **MinistryPlatform Care Life:** three roles, Administrator, Manager (assigned cases) and Viewer ([ACST](https://help.acst.com/ministryplatform/help-topics/life-apps/care-life)) **[snippet]**.
- **Salesforce:** restriction rules don't protect child notes, "View All Data" bypasses them, and restricted records linger in search shortcuts ([Salesforce](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/atlas.en-us.restriction_rules.meta/restriction_rules/restriction_rules_about.htm)) **[snippet]**.
- **HR:** investigation files are kept apart and shared on need-to-know ([SHRM](https://shrm.org/topics-tools/tools/policies/access-to-employee-personnel-file-policy)) **[snippet]**.

**Implication for Asym:** use a separate sealed store, not a flag, because flags leak through child records and admin bypasses. By default ordinary staff can't see that a sealed record exists. No break-the-glass in v1, because small ministries won't staff the review.

## 5. File import safety

- **Supabase:**
  - Buckets are private by default. RLS on `storage.objects` governs every operation, and signed URLs expire ([buckets](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/storage/buckets/fundamentals)).
  - Uploads are denied without a policy, and **service keys bypass RLS** ([access control](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/storage/security/access-control)).
  - Size and MIME limits can be set per bucket.
  - No native malware scanning was found **[gap]**.
- **Scanners:** GuardDuty scans S3 objects up to 5 GB, tags them and quarantines ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2024/06/detect-malware-object-uploads-amazon-s3-guardduty)). **VirusTotal shares uploads with partners** ([VirusTotal](https://docs.virustotal.com/docs/historic-privacy-policy)), so it is forbidden.
- **Volumes:** typical ministry volumes are a **[gap]**, so a discovery sample is needed.

**Implication for Asym:** imports land in a quarantine bucket with no policies for authenticated users. A self-hosted ClamAV worker scans each file, re-encodes images, and moves it to a private Staff, Restricted or Sealed bucket. Imports never go into the current public buckets.

## 6. Simplicity

- Vendors use category privacy (Planning Center), a separate care module (MinistryPlatform), or private notes plus security per type (Rock).
- What proved too complex:
  - Rock removed note approvals because they add "a fair amount of complexity" ([Rock](https://community.rockrms.com/connect/technical-changes-coming-to-notes-in-rock)).
  - NHS sealed envelopes slipped by years.
  - Salesforce's layered sharing leaks child records.

## Simplest robust model

1. **Three tiers, set by source category and never by reading content:**
   - **Staff:** the default.
   - **Restricted:** named roles. Covers restricted workers and unknown categories.
   - **Sealed:** a separate store and bucket.
2. **Sealed has two holder groups:**
   - **Care:** care, counselling, safeguarding and crisis records.
   - **Personnel:** applications, references, medical records and check reports.
   - Sealed records are read-only, linked to one person and access-logged, and their existence is hidden.
3. **One row, many links.** The strictest linked person's visibility wins.
4. **Files:** quarantine, self-hosted scan, allowlist and cap, re-encoded images, opaque keys and download-only serving. Failures go to the archive with a count.
5. **Facts over documents** where a fact is enough.
6. **Review dates set by the ministry.** Safeguarding is never auto-deleted.
7. **A per-person finder** for subject access and deletion across tiers.

## What not to build

- Flags or ACLs on individual notes, or note approvals.
- Break-the-glass, or banners that say a sealed record exists.
- Content scanning, fuzzy duplicate removal, automatic encoding repair, or parsing of `.msg` and `.eml` files.
- Public or third-party scanners, inline PDF or Office previews, or video hosting.
- Retention timers that delete automatically.
