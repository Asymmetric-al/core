These are not two competing claims. They are two different axes:

- **Record ownership:** which domain owns the meaning and lifecycle of sessions, plans, staged-row records, source namespaces, runs and receipts. That is P30.
- **Byte custody:** which platform holds, protects, scans and disposes of private bytes and their derivatives. That is P29, on its one storage-object model. That model already carries an "owner-domain and owner-record reference" (`owner-constraints.md:171-172`; #2368 body line 7).

The r00 framing "Phase 30 database (ADR-0173) vs Phase 29 custody (#2368, REQ28-R26)" (r00-index:362, :857) misreads ADR-0173. ADR-0173 never says "database". It says the opposite: product rows "retain only bounded identifiers, dispositions, digests, counts, and revision references; staged content bodies ... stay in Phase 30 private staging/custody. No row-sized content ... ride database receipts" (`:260-263`). It also routes imported bytes into "Phase 29 private intake" (`:106-107`). ADR-0173 (2026-08-24) predates roadmap v3 (2026-09-22) and D17 (2026-10-16). The later texts make the custody half explicit as P29, with no second file store. A consistent reading of all sources is: **P30 owns the logical staging, plan, run and receipt records; P29 holds and disposes of the bytes and derivatives under P30 (or, for D17, the preparation purpose) as owner-domain.**

### 3.2 What is genuinely still open at owner level

1. **Physical representation of parsed row content.** It could be a P29 storage object referenced by P30 records, or row-sized data in a P30-owned private table that P29's copy inventory and disposal cover. No owner text decides this. The constraints are fixed: private, purpose-bound, counted in P29 copy inventory and capacity (`AC29-D10-07` at `custody-and-integration.md:153` on the PR head), the seven-day and 24-hour clocks (REQ28-R26), and not in receipts, events or step state (ADR-0173:262-263). This is an engineering design choice for the Phase 30 spec within those constraints. It is not a founder product choice.
2. **Who declares the D17 seven-day window and disposal duties.** The text is "P29/P30 own D17's already-ratified seven-day input window and 24-hour disposal duties" (`relationships.md:842`), with "Phase 29 executes the owning domain's retention decisions and doesn't set its own retention period" (#2368 body line 8). r05's handling (P30 profile declares, P29 executes, P28 stores neither; r05:187) is consistent with the texts. Phase 30 grooming should record it explicitly.
3. **Whether staff migration profiles reuse the D17 plan, run and receipt model.** Every owner text gives "plans, runs, receipts" to P30 as one owner. ADR-0173 says P30 supplies "the shared migration workbench rather than being duplicated" (`:336`). Roadmap D17 says "not ... a second importer" (`roadmap.md:4330-4331`). So **one shared P30 engine is already owner intent.** What remains open is its shape, not its existence.

### 3.3 The Phase 28 factory designs conflict with these owner texts (a fact, not a judgment call)

- **#2083 r2 (R09-a).** Once unblocked, it creates a P28-named private bucket `preparation-import-custody`, custody, holds, disposal-ledger and receipt tables, an hourly disposal runner "on custody's own tables", and plan and row storage (`preparation_import_review_plans`, `preparation_import_review_rows`) in migration `20261025210000` (r2 comment §1.1-1.3, §2, §6, §9). Its H2 pre-handback check expects "the P29/P30 owners' objects as merged, and that none of the new names exist". So the design creates these P28 objects **after** the owners exist; "no stand-in" only defers the build. This conflicts with:
  - `owner-constraints.md:162-170` ("existing private Upload Intake owns ... No second file store");
  - the Conrad-published #2083 body line 42 ("No ... new intake/custody engine");
  - D17 `:455` (P30 owns plans and receipts).
- **#2084 (R10-a).** It puts runs, leases, steps, receipts and the kill switch in `preparation_import_runs`, `preparation_import_run_receipts` and `preparation_import_controls` (migration `20261025220000`, comment 6393180524). D17 `:455` gives runs and row receipts to P30.
- **#2085 (R11).** It puts source bindings in `preparation_import_sources` (migration `20261025230000`). D17 `:455` gives source namespaces to P30. The approval itself concedes "Phase 30's profile may replace it later" (comment 6393310242).

The factory recorded none of these as departures from the P30 ownership texts. The departures it did record are structural-only scanning, scheduler off, XLSX off, and the source-instance label. All of these designs are blocked natively on #2369 or #2368, or transitively through #2083. **Nothing has been built**: no `preparation_import_*` migration exists in `supabase/migrations`, and `packages/lib/import` is absent on develop `629cb71dc` (= `origin/develop`, re-checked 2026-11-01). So the conflict is pre-implementation and fully correctable by re-scoping.

### 3.4 Answer on machinery ownership

- **Settled by owner intent:** one shared P30 import engine. P30 owns sessions, transport, parsing, mapping, staging records, plans, source namespaces, runs and row receipts. P29 owns byte and derivative custody, scanning and disposal. Target owners own validation and acceptance. D17 is a closed target profile on that engine, not a P28 engine.
- **Not settled, and not owner intent:** the factory's P28-named `preparation_import_*` table design and migration slots `20261025210000` / `20261025220000` / `20261025230000` / `20261025232000`. These are published factory build contracts that conflict with owner texts. Under `document-authority.md:13` ("A later consumer phase cannot acquire a producer's authority by shorthand") and `:37-38` ("preserve the last agreed behavior"), they cannot pre-empt P30 ownership. Phase 30 grooming must either generalize them into P30-owned objects or re-scope #2083/#2084/#2085 as P28 consumers of P30 objects.
- **Engineering choices left for the Phase 30 spec:** table names and physical layout, blob vs row storage of staged content, and migration numbering. These need no founder ruling.

The Samson approval rules "the in-repo ZIP reader plus saxes@6.0.0 as a direct dependency is OK", pinned exact and added only to `packages/lib/package.json` (comment 6393310925). r1 records that `saxes@6.0.0` is today only a transitive dependency through `jsdom` (r1 line 9).
- **The Conrad-published #2087 body** says "Extend existing R09/R10 job, not second parser/intake UI" (body line 37). It admits CSV, data-only XLSX, .tsv and .txt "through the same text parser" (body line 54).

### 4.2 Owner texts that govern the parser

- Build-versus-adopt is open and is "decided at grooming": `roadmap.md:4389` ("Choose an embedded importer or native implementation only after evaluating the required data boundary, source validation hooks and lifecycle"), `roadmap.md:4409` and `owner-constraints.md:362-363`.
- D17 C22 (ratified): "P28 SHALL reuse P30 intake ... Do not copy the parser ... No new vendor/package is selected by D17" (`phase28-d17-...review.md:370-373`).
- D17 limits (ratified): "explicit ratified **qualification limits**, not demonstrated capacity or universal P29 limits. Keep them centrally configured per qualified profile ... A larger migration belongs in qualified P30 staff tooling" (`review.md:395-399`).
- P30 owns "replaceable parsing" (`owner-constraints.md:396`).
- ADR-0173 clause 4: Payload's exact-pin plugin "may provide qualified parsing/serialization mechanics behind an adapter" for CMS imports (`:58-60`). So the P23 profile has its own parser candidate, governed by the same workbench.
- P29 AC29-D05-02 (ratified, PR head): "Candidate engines, dependencies, licenses, isolation and processing location require current qualification" (`custody-and-integration.md:73`).

### 4.3 Analysis

1. **#2347 is mostly compatible with P30 ownership.** It is a pure, replaceable parsing component with a caller-supplied profile and no storage. That is the shape `owner-constraints.md:396` calls "replaceable parsing". It lives in a shared package, not a P28 one. It explicitly defers the target profile to "the Phase 30 owner". Merging it does **not** by itself decide embedded vs native for the whole P30 product. An embedded importer would still need a server-side, profile-bound reader to re-validate bytes at commit (roadmap "Bind the plan to exact source bytes", `:4349`).
2. **Two parts of #2347 do pre-empt P30 decisions.**
   - **Hard-coded universal bounds.** The D17 qualification limits become module-level acceptance constants tested at limit and one over (body line 22), shared by the XLSX reader (#2087 r1 S1). The ratified D17 text says those limits are "not ... universal" and must be "configured per qualified profile". A staff or SiteStacker profile above 10,000 rows would have to change a shared constant that the D17 tests pin.
   - **The plan record is fixed by a P28 ticket.** `buildPlan`/`assertPlanCurrent` freeze plan fields and staleness rules (body lines 28-30), and R09-a stores the plan in P28 tables (line 65). D17 `:455` makes plans a P30 object.

   Both are fixable without discarding the module: make bounds a field of the caller-supplied profile, with the D17 profile carrying the ratified values, and have P30 own the plan schema.

3. **#2087 pre-empts more.** It selects a security-relevant dependency (`saxes`) and an in-repo ZIP reader for hostile XLSX. It did so by an AI coordinator's ruling in a P28 ticket, while:
   - the build-versus-adopt choice is still open at owner level;
   - D17 says "No new vendor/package is selected by D17";
   - P29 requires dependency qualification.

   r1 records `saxes@6.0.0` as already present transitively, so "new package" is arguable. But promoting it to a direct product dependency for hostile-input parsing is a selection. In practice R13-a cannot start until #2347 lands (native blocker), and R13-b XLSX activation "stays off until the envelope numbers are set" (approval). So the risk is that the decision gets made by default, not that hostile XLSX gets activated.

4. **The sequencing risk is real, though not immediate.** #2347 is `Ready`, unblocked and in the factory's queue. If it merges before Phase 30 grooming records the per-profile bounds and P30 plan ownership, the D17-only limits become the de facto engine bounds. #2087 then inherits them as "shared bound constants".

### 4.4 Answer on the parser

- **Settled (fact):** one parser, not a second one. This follows from ratified C22 ("Do not copy the parser"), the Conrad-published #2087 body line 37 and ADR-0173:336. The D17 numeric limits are ratified **for the D17 profile only**, and must be per-profile configuration.
- **Not settled (open decision):** whether Phase 30's product parser is the in-repo reader family (#2347 plus #2087), an embedded importer, or a hybrid. This is the roadmap's listed open decision, and it is decided at grooming. Neither the `Ready` label on #2347 nor the saxes ruling on #2087 settles it. The data-boundary half of build-versus-embed is a genuine founder product choice: does imported PII ever leave Asym's governed boundary to a vendor UI or service (cf. `owner-constraints.md:361-362`, "imported PII never leaves its governed boundary merely to improve mapping")? The library-level choice (saxes vs another tokenizer; in-repo ZIP reader) is engineering, subject to P29 AC29-D05-02 qualification.
- **What Phase 30 grooming can record without a founder ruling:** #2347 may proceed as the D17-profile reader behind P30's replaceable parsing seam, provided bounds move into the profile and P30 owns the plan schema. That needs a body amendment on #2347, of the same kind as the #2368 P29-058 amendment. #2087's dependency selection should be held (or explicitly scoped "D17 profile only, pending P30 build-versus-adopt") until grooming records the choice.

## Resolution

**Partially resolved.** The facts settle machinery ownership and the binding status of the rulings. One governance question is left for the founder, and it is optional.

1. **Who owns the shared machinery? Settled by owner intent.**
   - There is one shared Phase 30 engine.
   - P30 owns sessions, transport, replaceable parsing, mapping, staging records, plans, source namespaces, runs and row receipts.
   - P29 holds and disposes of the original bytes and all derivatives (including staged content, previews and error files) on its single storage-object model, and owns malware hygiene.
   - Target owners validate and accept.
   - The "P30 database vs P29 custody" dispute is a false dichotomy built on a misreading of ADR-0173. ADR-0173 never says "database", and puts row-sized content outside receipts (section 3.1).
   - What is left is engineering for the Phase 30 spec: whether staged rows are stored as blobs or as private rows, table names, and migration slots (section 3.2).
2. **The Phase 28 factory designs.** The `preparation_import_*` custody, plan, run, receipt and source tables (#2083 r2, #2084, #2085; reserved migrations `20261025210000`/`220000`/`230000`/`232000`) are published factory build contracts. They conflict with `owner-constraints.md:162-170`, D17 `:455`, the Conrad-published #2083 body line 42, and (since 2026-10-29) the `Ready` P29 owner ticket #2368. None is built. They must be re-scoped as consumers of P30 objects, or generalized into P30-owned objects, before #2369 is made `Ready` (sections 3.3, 6.3).
3. **The parser.** One parser is settled (C22, ADR-0173:336, #2087 body line 37). Build versus embed is **not** settled, and neither #2347's `Ready` nor #2087's saxes ruling settles it.
   - #2347 is largely compatible with P30 "replaceable parsing". It pre-empts P30 only in two places: hard-coded universal D17 bounds (body line 22, shared by #2087 r1 S1) against the ratified "configured per qualified profile" (`review.md:395-399`), and P28-owned plan storage.
   - An additive amendment can fix both.
   - #2087's dependency selection should be held, or scoped to the D17 profile, until Phase 30 grooming (section 4).
4. **Do the ticket rulings bind Phase 30? No, as an established fact.** None is founder-ratified or written into an owner document. Each is ticket-scoped factory authority delegated by Blake: the Oct 23 security grant, the Oct 22 standing authorization, and Chad relays. The repository's precedent (P24-01; ADR-0001 2026-10-24) treats such approvals as valid for their ticket scope and as not relaxing a phase contract.
   - Engineering defaults (`BATCH_ROWS` 50, 60 s lease, per-tenant cap 1 with refuse-not-queue, factory-measured envelope) are adoptable as **D17 profile defaults** without a founder ruling.
