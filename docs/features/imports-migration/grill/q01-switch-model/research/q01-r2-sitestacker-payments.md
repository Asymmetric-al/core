## 0. Bottom line (read this first)

1. **SiteStacker uses a token model.** Its FAQ says it "does not store payment methods and instead utilizes a token that references a payment method that is stored in the database of your merchant processor" ([151000183512](https://training.sitestacker.com/support/solutions/articles/151000183512-how-are-donor-information-handled-and-stored-in-site-stacker-for-financial-reviews-), modified 4 Apr 2024; verified-vendor-doc, accessed 2026-11-01). The card and bank credentials live in the **ministry's processor vault**. SiteStacker holds only references plus display data such as last four digits and expiry.
2. **SiteStacker itself runs online card and ACH recurring gifts.** Its own cron job ("processing recurring contributions") charges the stored tokens on each schedule's next run date. Evidence: [CRON glossary 151000097037](https://training.sitestacker.com/support/solutions/articles/151000097037-cron) (29 Sep 2023); [Add or Edit a Recurring Donation 151000115212](https://training.sitestacker.com/support/solutions/articles/151000115212-add-or-edit-a-recurring-donation-on-behalf-of-a-donor) (26 Apr 2024): "Recurring will run in the early morning hours on the next run date … the charge will happen on save since all the recurring has already run". Both are verified-vendor-doc. **Consequence:** for card and ACH schedules held in a processor vault, turning SiteStacker's scheduler off stops those charges. The donor's payment credentials stay usable in the vault until the ministry deletes them or the processor relationship ends (inferred). This is the reverse of a gateway-native subscription model.
3. **PayPal is the exception (inferred from vendor-served code).** SiteStacker's public checkout script (`https://sitestacker.com/Contributions/js/frontend/form/Payment.js`, fetched 2026-11-01) builds PayPal Payments Standard forms with `cmd=_xclick-subscriptions`, `a3/p3/t3`, `src=1` and `srt` (default 52, clamped to 2–52) for recurring gifts. PayPal executes those subscriptions, so they **keep charging after SiteStacker is switched off** until they are cancelled in PayPal or reach their `srt` count (§2, §3.4, §5).
4. **Several processors, configured per Site.** "Payment Processor Gateways" are "exclusively connected to a Site". A new Site is recommended when you want "an entirely different payment processor" ([Sites vs. Site Channels 151000118930](https://training.sitestacker.com/support/solutions/articles/151000118930-sites-vs-site-channels), 8 Sep 2023; verified-vendor-doc). The checkout client code keys gateway configuration by **Site × currency × payment method** (vendor-served code, §2 C1; §3.1). A single ministry may therefore have more than one merchant account or processor, split by Site, by currency (USD/CAD/EUR) and by card versus bank.
5. **Named processors.**
   - **Vendor-documented:** iATS, STI, Payeezy, Braintree and PaymentSpring are the processors that "work with the Reverse/Refund tool" ([151000115214](https://training.sitestacker.com/support/solutions/articles/151000115214-refund-a-credit-card-or-ach-gift), 28 Sep 2023). PaymentSpring also has its own articles.
   - **Vendor-served code only, never mentioned in any public SiteStacker article:** client tokenization wrappers for **Stripe**, **Braintree**, **Authorize.net (Accept)** and **Nelnet** (which calls `paymentspring.generateToken`, i.e. the PaymentSpring library), plus **PayPal Payments Standard** and **Apple Pay** (§2).
   - **The list is not exhaustive.** It names only refund-tool processors as of 2023, and the code shows only gateways that tokenize in the browser. No public SiteStacker article mentions Stripe, PayPal, Apple Pay, Authorize.net, Vanco, Cybersource, NMI, BluePay, Bambora, Moneris or Paysafe (Freshdesk public search for each term, 2026-11-01, returned no article).
   - **Correction to sibling q01-r3 §1 S8:** the refund article 151000115214 is live (HTTP 200, re-fetched 2026-11-01), not 404.
6. **Stripe tenants: either the ministry's own account or Connect.** The Stripe wrapper calls `Stripe(publicKey, {stripeAccount: accountId})` when an `accountId` is configured, and `Stripe(publicKey)` otherwise. SiteStacker therefore supports both:
   - direct keys on the ministry's own Stripe account; and
   - acting on a connected account through Stripe Connect.
     The connected-account type (Standard, Express or Custom) and the platform's identity could not be verified. Either way, Customers and PaymentMethods are created on the account that processed the charge (inferred from `customer_id` → `person_profile_code_override`, SetupIntents and Financial Connections, §3.2). There is **no evidence that SiteStacker uses Stripe Subscriptions**; the evidence points to SiteStacker-scheduled off-session charges.
7. **Asym must never import or receive raw card or bank credentials.** This is repo-ratified: Phase 13 `:743-746` says "Asym never handles raw PAN, CVV/CVC, or full bank credentials". Every processor migration path is therefore one of four options:
   - (a) the **same account** is reused. _Corrected 2026-11-01 (agrees with sibling q01-r4 G18):_ this is **not** a Phase 16 "reconnect". "Apparent same binding restored → Reconciling" applies only to a binding Asym itself held (`phase-16-…md:2083`). Two rows of the same table block it here: "another application/operator owns executor → Externally controlled/read-only" (`:2085`), and "replacement executor" is formal cutover (`:1314`). Moving a schedule from SiteStacker's cron to Asym is therefore a formal cutover even on the same Stripe account. Asym's onboarding also always creates a new account (Account Links, "Do **not** use OAuth", `phase-13-…md:659`), so same-account reuse is not available without a Phase 13 owner amendment;
   - (b) a **processor-to-processor PCI token migration** into the Stripe account Asym will use;
   - (c) the **donor re-authorizes**;
   - (d) the old executor stays **external/read-only**.
     Phase 16 `:1314` makes (a), (b), (c) and any new account a formal cutover. It needs proof that the old executor stopped plus fresh IDs and authorization.

## 3. How SiteStacker money flows work (synthesis of §1 and §2)

### 3.1 Configuration: who owns the merchant account

- **The processor is a per-Site component.** A ministry adds "a Payment Processor" in the Processors component and adds currencies on its Currencies tab (P13). Gateways are "exclusively connected to a Site" (P12). The checkout code selects a wrapper per **Site × currency × method** (C1).
  - So one SiteStacker tenant can hold several gateway configurations: a card processor per currency, a different processor for bank debits, and a different Site with "an entirely different payment processor".
  - This is inferred from P12 and C1. No document states a maximum number of processors.
- **The merchant account is the ministry's own**, not a SiteStacker pooled account:
  - P1 says the token references a method "stored in the database of **your** merchant processor".
  - P16 tells ministries to put "{Name on merchant account}" in the ACH authorization.
  - P18 tells PaymentSpring clients to change settings "in your Payment Spring dashboard", and to set minimums with "your processor".
  - All four are verified-vendor-doc. The reading that the ministry is merchant of record is **inferred**.
- **Stripe can run in either of two modes:**
  - direct keys (`Stripe(publicKey)`), where the ministry's own account and keys are configured in SiteStacker;
  - Connect (`Stripe(publicKey, {stripeAccount: accountId})`, C4).
  - Under Connect, a platform's publishable key is used _on behalf of_ the ministry's connected account. The platform may be WMTEK's or another party's, and the connected-account type (Standard, Express or Custom) is unknown. Both are **unverified**. Neither mode is documented in any article.
  - Under Connect direct charges, Stripe documents that "Transaction objects such as `PaymentIntents` and `Charges` exist on the connected account, not on the platform" and that "The connected account's balance increases with every charge" ([docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges.md?platform=web&ui=elements); verified-vendor-doc, 2026-11-01).
  - Initialising Stripe.js with `stripeAccount` is the documented direct-charge pattern, so under either mode the donor Customers and PaymentMethods are on **the ministry's own Stripe account**. That is inferred from C4 plus the Stripe documentation. A SiteStacker _destination-charge_ design that stores objects on a platform account cannot be excluded without server code (unverified).
- **WMTEK sets up bank debits, not the ministry.** EFT/ACH is configured by WMTEK support on request (P14).

### 3.2 Tokenization and what SiteStacker stores

- **Browser tokenization** (the "transparent redirect" wrappers):
  - Stripe → PaymentMethod `pm_…` (card) or SetupIntent-confirmed `pm_…` (US bank via Financial Connections; SEPA via IBAN).
  - Braintree → nonce.
  - Authorize.net Accept → opaque data (card only).
  - Nelnet/PaymentSpring → token, for card and bank (C4–C7).
- **The SiteStacker server then exchanges the single-use token** for a stored gateway reference: a Stripe Customer plus PaymentMethod, a Braintree vault token, an Authorize.net CIM profile, or a PaymentSpring customer method. That stored reference is the "token" of P1.
  - The exchange is **inferred**, because no server code is public.
  - The Stripe US-bank flow is the one place where the server visibly returns a gateway `customer_id`. SiteStacker stores it in a field called `person_profile_code_override` (C4). That name suggests a per-person "profile code" column holds the gateway customer reference (inferred).
- **What SiteStacker's own database holds** (C8, P9, P10):
  - per method: currency, card last four, name on card, expiry month and year;
  - for bank methods: account last four, **full routing number**, bank name, account holder name, personal/corporate and checking/savings;
  - the address, and the CRM person the method belongs to.
- **Raw card data can still pass through SiteStacker.** Where no wrapper is enabled for a Site × currency × method, or where the legacy ExtJS form (C8) is used, raw card and bank numbers **transit** SiteStacker's server on their way to the gateway.
  - P1 only says SiteStacker does not _store_ them.
  - The SiteStacker PCI scope is unverified (P28 states no level).
  - Asym never touches this path (Phase 13 `:743-746`, repo-ratified).
- **Saved methods without a schedule are common.** Recurring methods are always saved. One-time donors may be offered a "store" preference (C10). Staff can add methods on a donor's behalf (P4, P5). The vault therefore holds methods with no active schedule, and SiteStacker's Payment Methods tab lists them (P9).

### 3.3 One-time gifts, capture, fees and currencies

- **Settings exist; behaviour is undocumented.** Checkout settings include `authorize_only`, `auto_capture` and `auto_process` (C9). Whether any tenant runs authorize-then-capture is unknown. No article documents it.
- **Fee coverage is a percentage added to the cart.** It is designated to a "Transaction Fee Campaign" (P15). The `sitestacker.com` example is 3% (C9).
  - **Inferred:** the processed amount includes the fee, and the fee is a **separate designation** line in SiteStacker.
  - A migrated gift therefore has two designation lines, and reconciliation must expect the processor amount to equal the gift plus fee.
- **Currency.** Currency is chosen per checkout (a "currency" panel, C9) and per batch (P23). A method row carries a currency (C8).
  - Stripe bank debits are coded only for USD (ACH) and EUR (SEPA), not CAD (C4).
  - Apple Pay is coded for USD/US only (C3).
  - Historic Giving `currency` is "(USD,CAD)" (r07 V10).
  - Marketing claims "150+"/"135+" currencies (marketing only).
- **PayPal one-time gifts go through PayPal Standard** (`_xclick`). The cart id travels in `custom` (C2). Recording the gift therefore depends on PayPal notifying SiteStacker. That notification is presumably Instant Payment Notification (IPN), which is inferred because no IPN handler is visible.

### 3.4 Recurring gifts: who is the executor

- **Card and bank recurring on Stripe, Braintree, Authorize.net, PaymentSpring and the other vaulting gateways: SiteStacker's cron is the executor.**
  - Evidence: P6 (the cron is "processing recurring contributions"); P5 (charges run "early morning … on the next run date", or "on save" when the run date is today, "since all the recurring has already run"); P8 (SiteStacker re-processes a failed recurring gift after the donor updates the method); P7 (cancelling is a SiteStacker status change, not a gateway call); C4 (the Stripe client uses a SetupIntent when a recurring gift starts in the future, i.e. "save now, charge later").
  - Label: **inferred (strong)**. No document names the gateway call.
  - Consequence: if the SiteStacker cron or the whole installation stops, these schedules stop charging. The saved credentials stay valid in the ministry's vault.
  - **Not excluded:** some gateways also offer native recurring that SiteStacker _could_ use: Stripe Subscriptions, Braintree Recurring Billing, Authorize.net ARB, PaymentSpring Plans/Subscriptions/scheduled charges (§4), and iATS Customer Code schedules. No SiteStacker source shows that it does, and whether it does for any tenant is unverified.
  - Separately, a ministry may have created gateway-native schedules **outside** SiteStacker, directly in its gateway dashboard. Those are invisible to a SiteStacker export.
- **PayPal recurring is executed by PayPal.**
  - `_xclick-subscriptions` with `src=1` and `srt` up to 52 (C2).
  - PayPal documents that such a subscription "ends only when the merchant or subscriber cancels it", and that `srt` stops it "after N billing cycles" ([PayPal Subscribe sample](https://developer.paypal.com/archive/paypal-payments-standard/integration-guide/html-example-subscribe); verified-vendor-doc, 2026-11-01). The page also says Payments Standard "is deprecated and should not be used for new integrations".
  - SiteStacker caps `srt` at 52 cycles even for open-ended gifts (C2). So a monthly PayPal gift silently ends after 52 payments, about 4 years and 4 months (inferred from code).
- **Offline Recurring (pledges) has no executor.** It is a schedule of expected offline gifts; batch gifts can be linked to it (P22, P23).
- **SiteStacker treats a schedule edit as a new schedule.** Changing a schedule leaves an archived clone and creates a new one (q01-r1 V9, `Is Original Schedule`). Run dates are tenant-editable (P5). Cancelled schedules still show a next run date (P7). The `next_run` column is therefore not proof of future intent; status is.

### 3.5 Failures, retries, dunning, card expiry

- **Decline notice and self-service retry: documented.**
  - A declined gift triggers "Contributions – Contributions Declined". Its `{$recurring.updatePaymentURL}` link lets the donor replace the method, and SiteStacker then re-runs the gift automatically (P8).
  - `remove_failed_payment_methods` is a checkout setting (C9). It is undocumented; it probably removes methods that failed at checkout (inferred).
  - "Delinquent Sponsors" are sponsors whose recurring payment "has been failing" (P26), so repeated failures stay visible.
- **Retries: undocumented.** No article documents an automatic retry schedule, a maximum attempt count, or an automatic suspension rule. All are unverified.
- **Card expiry: documented manual process; automatic updating unverified.**
  - The documented process is manual: filter Payment Methods by expiry, then contact the donor (P9).
  - Gateway account updaters exist: PaymentSpring has a `POST /tokens/update` "Account Updater" endpoint ([docs.paymentspring.com](https://docs.paymentspring.com/); verified-vendor-doc). Stripe applies card-network updates to saved cards automatically (general knowledge; not re-verified here).
  - Whether SiteStacker consumes updater results, which the marketing site calls "Never-expiring card updates", is unverified. So SiteStacker's last-four/expiry columns **can disagree with the vault** (inferred).

### 3.6 Refunds, reversals, voids, chargebacks

- **Refunds are full refunds, built-in for five processors only.** A refund is a full amount, issued through the gateway for iATS, STI, Payeezy, Braintree and PaymentSpring. It creates a separate negative "refund" transaction (P2).
  - For other gateways, which presumably include Stripe, Authorize.net and PayPal, refunds are done in the gateway dashboard (inferred). Whether SiteStacker then records anything is unverified.
- **Offline gifts are reversed with a "reversal" transaction** (P3).
- **No public documentation on chargebacks, disputes, ACH returns (R-codes) or voids.** All are unverified.
  - **Consequence:** SiteStacker's gift history may show money as received that the processor later returned or lost in a dispute (inferred).
  - Phase 30 reconciliation must therefore compare against **processor settlement reports**, not SiteStacker totals alone (r07 checklist item 19).

### 3.7 ACH / eCheck and authorization records

- **The authorization record is a checkbox setting plus text, with no documented per-donor timestamp or export.**
  - The "Enable ACH Authorization field" shows a configurable authorization text at checkout (P16). The `sitestacker.com` default is "I authorize payment processor to debit my bank account on behalf of site" (C9).
  - No article says SiteStacker stores a per-donor authorization timestamp, IP or text version, or that it can export one (unverified).
- **Where mandate evidence may exist, by gateway:**
  - Stripe US bank: a SetupIntent is confirmed through Financial Connections or micro-deposits (C4), so Stripe holds a Mandate object on the account (inferred from the Stripe API).
  - SEPA: Stripe holds a SEPA mandate.
  - Braintree US bank: the mandate text sent to Braintree is **hard-coded** as "I authorize Braintree to debit my bank account on behalf of My Online Store." (C5). Braintree requires that `mandateText` be the exact text "you displayed to the customer in your UI" ([Braintree ACH client-side guide](https://developer.paypal.com/braintree/docs/guides/ach/client-side/javascript/v3/); verified-vendor-doc).
    - So Braintree-stored mandate text for SiteStacker donors may not match what they saw (inferred from code).
    - This weakens any claim that an existing Braintree ACH authorization can be carried forward.
  - PaymentSpring and Authorize.net eCheck: the record is whatever the gateway retains (unverified).

### 3.8 Receipts and messages on each charge

- **SiteStacker sends its own receipts.** "Contribution Receipt", "Recurring Scheduling Confirmation" and "Recurring Scheduling Updated" are SiteStacker system messages, and Mastercard rules require them on (P17).
- **The pre-billing reminder is a Spark automation** (P17).
- **The gateway may send a second receipt.** Some gateways can also send receipts themselves: PaymentSpring has receipt templates and a `send_receipt` flag ([docs.paymentspring.com](https://docs.paymentspring.com/); verified-vendor-doc). Stripe can email receipts per account setting (general knowledge).
  - Whether a ministry's gateway also emails donors is a per-tenant fact (unverified).
  - It matters for the switch: Asym must not duplicate it, and turning SiteStacker off does not stop it.
- **Year-end statements are a separate SiteStacker send** with a testing mode (P27).

### 3.9 Text-to-give, Apple Pay, Google Pay

- **Text-to-give** is an SMS vendor linking to the ordinary checkout (P19). It adds no new payment rail or token. The SMS vendor's keyword reply still points at the SiteStacker URL after the switch (inferred), so it is a cut-over item.
- **Apple Pay** is supported via the Braintree wrapper and a SiteStacker merchant-validation route (C3, C5).
  - The Apple Pay domain verification belongs to the SiteStacker host (inferred).
  - Whether Apple Pay tokens can back recurring gifts is unverified.
- **Google Pay**: no evidence in code or docs (unverified absence).

### 6.2 Per-tenant processor census (read-only, run by the ministry before the first practice run)

This turns every inferred cell in §5 into evidence. Each item is a ministry action plus a file. No Asym access to the gateway is implied.

1. **List every processor.** From the SiteStacker Processors component, list every configured processor and every currency, per Site. Screenshot or export them. Also record which ACH flow the installation runs (Stripe Financial Connections, Braintree `usBankAccount`, PaymentSpring bank token or server-side). The installations differ by code version (§2).
2. **Inventory native recurring per gateway.** For each gateway, export:
   - all active **gateway-native** recurring objects (Stripe subscriptions/subscription schedules, Braintree subscriptions, ARB subscriptions, PaymentSpring subscriptions and `scheduled_transactions`, iATS recurring customer codes, PayPal subscription profiles);
   - the count of saved customers/methods.
   - A non-zero native count means some executor is not SiteStacker's cron.
3. **Count PayPal subscriptions** still active, with their `srt` cycle counts (C2).
4. **Check gateway-sent emails.** Record whether each gateway sends its own donor receipts or emails (PaymentSpring `send_receipt`; Stripe customer emails).
5. **Check card updating.** Record whether an account updater is active (PaymentSpring `/tokens/update`; Stripe automatic card updates). If so, SiteStacker's expiry and last-four columns may be stale.
6. **Identify the Stripe topology**, if Stripe is used:
   - direct keys or Connect;
   - if Connect, whose platform and whether the ministry can log in to its own Dashboard;
   - whether any other application charges the same account.
   - This decides whether self-serve copy is possible (Stripe copy doc: Connect accounts without Dashboard access need the platform owner to act).
7. **Identify "STI".** If "STI", Payeezy or iATS is in use, have the processor confirm in writing:
   - whether it can transfer the vault to Stripe directly, processor-to-processor;
   - the cost and lead time.
   - Refuse any path where full card numbers are exported to the ministry or to Asym (for example iATS "unmask and export"; §4).
8. **Pull processor reports** for every month in the import window and for the freeze window. Item M3 depends on them.

## 10. Summary

1. **Merchant accounts belong to the ministry; SiteStacker keeps only tokens.** SiteStacker charges through the ministry's own merchant accounts and keeps only gateway tokens plus display data (P1; C8). Card and bank credentials live in the ministry's processor vault, so no SiteStacker export can carry them.
2. **Processors are per Site, and possibly per currency and method** (P12, P13, C1). Named processors:
   - **Vendor-documented:** iATS, STI, Payeezy, Braintree and PaymentSpring (refund tool, P2).
   - **Vendor-served code:** Stripe (direct keys or Connect `stripeAccount`), Braintree (card, US bank, Apple Pay), Authorize.net (card), Nelnet/PaymentSpring (card, bank), and PayPal Payments Standard.
   - **No public SiteStacker article mentions Stripe or PayPal.** The full gateway list is unverified.
3. **Stripe tenants: the objects are on the ministry's account.** Customers and PaymentMethods sit on the ministry's account, whether it was used directly or as a connected account (inferred from C4 plus Stripe's direct-charge documentation). There is no evidence of Stripe Subscriptions. SiteStacker's own cron charges saved methods off-session (P5, P6, P8; inferred, strong).
   - **Turning SiteStacker off stops those charges.** It does not delete the vault.
   - **Charges that keep going after SiteStacker is off:** PayPal subscriptions (PayPal-executed, with `srt` capped at 52 cycles) and any gateway-native schedules a ministry created.
4. **Every move of a recurring gift onto Asym is a Phase 16 formal cutover,** whichever gateway the ministry used. It needs proof that the old executor stopped, plus fresh IDs and authorization (`phase-16-…md:1314`, `:2085`). Asym never receives raw credentials (`phase-13-…md:743-746`).
5. **Gateway-specific migration constraints:**
   - Stripe can copy cards, US ACH and SEPA between Stripe accounts, keeping Customer IDs. It cannot copy Canadian PADs or subscriptions, and ACH copy requires mandate acknowledgement.
   - Braintree exports cards only to a PCI-attested receiver, in at most two exports.
   - Authorize.net, PaymentSpring, iATS, Payeezy and STI have no official export procedure found.
6. **Changes to Option A's refinements** (§6.3):
   - Widen "gifts-only catch-up" to all money facts, sourced from SiteStacker _and_ processor reports.
   - Split the "automations off" attestation so receipts, decline/update-card messages, recurring reminders and the donor dashboard keep running while SiteStacker still charges.
   - Record gateway, merchant account and executor type in lineage.
7. **Edge cases:** twenty-seven payment edge cases are listed in §7.
