## 0. Bottom line (read first)

1. **Recurring donors cannot be moved by a SiteStacker export.** SiteStacker says it stores only a token that points at the payment method held by the ministry's merchant processor (training.sitestacker.com article 151000183512, modified 4 Apr 2024; verified-vendor-doc, paraphrased by fetch tool). The card and bank numbers sit in the processor's (gateway's) vault, so any move of saved payment methods is a **processor-to-Stripe transfer the ministry requests**. Asym only receives the mapping file of old IDs to new Stripe IDs. A SiteStacker CSV can never carry that transfer. This fits the Phase 13 rule that Asym never handles raw PAN, CVV/CVC or full bank credentials (`phase-13-…md:743-746`; repo-ratified).
2. **Stripe documents three channels, and each one creates new Stripe IDs:**
   - (a) _PAN import_: from a non-Stripe processor into a Stripe account, run by Stripe's Data Migrations team over PGP-encrypted SFTP. It **can target Connect connected accounts**.
   - (b) _Self-serve copy_: from one Stripe account to another, including into and out of Connect accounts. Customer IDs stay the same; payment-method IDs change.
   - (c) _Clone_: platform-to-connected-account cloning of payment methods.
   - None of the three moves subscriptions, invoices or charges.
   - Stripe warns that the old subscriptions must be cancelled "as soon as your Stripe subscriptions become active or you'll bill your customers twice" (docs.stripe.com/get-started/data-migrations/pan-import; verified-vendor-doc).
3. **If SiteStacker or its gateway created the recurring schedules, they keep charging until someone stops them at the source.** Stripe states this for Connect: "Subscriptions aren't automatically canceled when you disconnect from the platform", and "Your platform can't update or cancel a subscription that another platform created". Direct-charge subscriptions "continue to run on the connected account" after disconnect (docs.stripe.com/connect/subscriptions; verified-vendor-doc). This is the external fact behind Phase 16 K.2 and K.5 (`phase-16-…md:1274-1282`, `:1314`; repo-ratified). Proof that the old executor stopped is therefore a real, separate step for each source, not a formality.
4. **The conflict with repo rules is direct, not narrow.** _Correction (resumed run): an earlier draft called this conflict "narrow" and said the repo "never says" whether an authorization survives a processor change. That understated the ratified text._ Phase 16 is explicit in four places:
   - **REC-23:** "Different-account cutover requires old-stop and fresh authorization" (`phase-16-…md:2696`).
   - **`:1236`:** "changing entity or merchant/account scope creates a separate group under fresh authorization", and "No bulk action may widen exposure, redirect, **bind methods**, remove limits, or restart donors".
   - **`:1314`:** formal cutover needs "applicable fresh IDs and collection authorization".
   - **`:1172`:** a collection authorization binds "exact terms, rail, provider/merchant, method token".

   Asym's onboarding always creates a new connected account. That is q01-r4 G18, from Stripe's own text: OAuth "isn't recommended for new Connect platforms", and OAuth `read_write` cannot connect "Standard accounts that are controlled by another platform" (docs.stripe.com/connect/oauth-standard-accounts; verified-vendor-doc). So **every move of a SiteStacker recurring gift onto an Asym executor needs fresh donor authorization under today's ratified text**, even when the vault moves without anyone handling PAN. This agrees with q01-r4 §5.2. The industry default, token migration plus a dated handoff with no donor action (§4 P-A), is therefore **ruled out unless the Phase 16 owner amends P16**, with counsel/processor sign-off (P16 production gate `:18`). §6 sets out two designs:
   - one that complies with today's text: vault staged on the new account plus a per-donor hosted confirmation, with no re-keying;
   - one that needs an amendment: "proved continuation", plus the evidence set industry practice relies on.

   Neither is a Phase 30 decision (C1).

**What S11 means for ACH (inferred).** Stripe's ACH import requires the merchant to certify that it holds Nacha-compliant mandates and can produce pre-migration authorization evidence on request (T13). The quality of a SiteStacker ministry's ACH authorizations therefore depends on whether that ministry turned the field on, on what text it configured and when, and on whether the authorization named the same merchant. None of this can be read from a SiteStacker export according to any public document. Before certifying, the ministry has to produce or reconstruct this evidence itself. It is a readiness question for the ministry and its counsel, not a Phase 30 data field.

**What follows.** No SiteStacker export can carry card or bank credentials (S1). Any move that keeps payment methods is therefore a processor-to-Stripe transfer. What it needs from the SiteStacker side is (a) the recurring _schedule_ rows and (b) a join key that links each schedule to the processor's vault record. The Recurring View CSV columns are undocumented (r07 checklist #14, "gateway profile reference" is an open question). The join key is the single most important unknown for keeping recurring donors without re-authorization (inferred).

Virtuous's token-migration article states the same dependency outright: "Tokens hold no details on frequency or amounts, only payment method data. A unique ID is vital for connecting to schedules and amounts." (support.virtuous.org article 34009891677197, updated 2025-09-26; verified-vendor-doc via the Zendesk public API).

Because SiteStacker runs the schedule itself (S2), **turning off SiteStacker's recurring engine is the "old executor stopped" event**, not anything at the gateway. The exception is a gateway that also holds its own recurring schedules (for example Authorize.net ARB, §3), which would have to be stopped as well (inferred).

**Repo fit, Stripe side.**

- Phase 13 puts saved Customers and PaymentMethods "on the connected account" (`phase-13-…md:667`; repo-ratified). It uses direct charges on controller-properties accounts with a full Stripe Dashboard and no OAuth (`:659`; repo-ratified).
- T16 and T18 therefore let a PAN import or a self-serve copy land **directly in the ministry's own connected account**. Because the account has a full Dashboard, the ministry as owner can probably file the intake form and accept copies itself (inferred from T2 and T18). No Stripe page confirms this for controller-properties accounts specifically, so it is unverified (q01-r4 matrix row C agrees).
- Asym never receives PAN or bank numbers; it receives only the mapping file (T4, T18). That keeps Phase 13 stories 172 and `:743-746` intact (inferred).
- T17, the manual ACH path, would make Asym or the ministry submit raw account and routing numbers. For Asym-run tooling this is **forbidden** by P13 `:745` and P16 `:225`, `:1494` (repo-ratified).

**Patterns across X1–X13 (inferred from the rows above):**

1. **Card export is a regulated hand-off from one processor to another.** The receiver must be PCI DSS Level 1 or show a current AoC. The **account owner**, sometimes an officer, makes the request. Files are PGP/GPG-encrypted. **Extractions are rationed**: Braintree allows two exports, and Authorize.net allows two a year at least 30 days apart. That rationing is the hard technical limit on any "delta" or "catch-up" token export after the switch. The norm is one bulk export plus at most one top-up, which matches Option A's single final export.
2. **No source exports recurring schedules as objects that can run on their own.** Schedules travel as data (CSV rows) and must be rebuilt by the receiver. Stripe (T6, T19), Braintree (X1) and Bloomerang (X5, through its download format) all say so.
3. **Some sources run their own recurring executor alongside the vault.** Authorize.net ARB (X2), PayPal subscriptions (X3) and SiteStacker's scheduler (S2) all do. Each must be stopped separately. A vault export stops none of them.
4. **Fees and lead times are real but modest.** Fees run $250–$500 and delivery 2–4 weeks, plus Stripe's "few days to several months" for a processor to produce the file (T3). Some processors refuse or stall: q01-r6 P4 names Click & Pledge, SafeSave and NeonPay as past examples. Stripe notes a processor "might be contractually obligated to release your data on request" (T9).
5. **Church platforms mostly ask donors to re-enroll** (Pushpay, Tithe.ly, Ministry Brands) and leave cancelling the old schedule to the donor. In practice that is the main source of double charges (§5).

### 4.3 Attrition evidence: still no measured number

- **No primary or peer-reviewed measurement of recurring-donor loss caused by forced re-authorization was found.** This confirms q01-r6 `:98`.
- **Vendor and consultant figures are not comparable:**
  - Tithe.ly claims "75%+" of recurring givers move within 4–6 weeks with weekly emails, which implies up to about 25% do not move (X10; vendor claim).
  - A vendor guide says 70–80% are lost without tokens (q01-r6 P7; unverified).
  - A NonprofitPro consultant reports hearing "20 to 30% of sustainers drop" after a processor transition (search summary only; the page redirected; snippet-only).
  - Engaging Networks cites 2–3% monthly payment failure compounding to 25–30% a year. That measures general decline churn, not migration loss (snippet-only).
- **For planning (inferred):** treat re-authorization as a material loss risk with an unknown size, and treat token migration plus a dated handoff as the low-friction default wherever a processor will release data. ### 6.2 Design 1: complies with today's text (vault staged, per-donor confirmation, no re-keying)

**Inferred design. No verified vendor ships exactly this.**

1. The ministry, as account owner, has the old processor send the vault to Stripe, or runs a Stripe self-serve copy, into its **new** Asym connected account. Asym never sees PAN (T2, T18; P13 `:745`).
2. Asym receives only the mapping file: old ID → new `cus_`/`pm_` IDs plus fingerprint, last4, expiry and brand (T4). It is held as restricted provider evidence (Phase 29 custody, C11) and bound only to the exact tenant, account and mode (P13 stories 169–171). Schedules from the SiteStacker Recurring View are imported as P16 review records that never backfill authority (`:2581`).
3. Each donor gets a hosted **"confirm your recurring gift"** flow (P13 story 173). It shows the exact terms and the _masked migrated method_. The donor's confirmation is the fresh, human, versioned authorization bound to the new method token and the exact terms (P16 `:1172`). **Open P16 ruling:** whether confirming a migrated method, rather than re-entering it, satisfies "fresh authorization". q01-r4 §5.2 item 2 flags this as open.
4. On confirmation, that donor's SiteStacker schedule is stopped with evidence. The first Asym date is set after the last legacy charge, never immediately (T19; q01-r5 K7). Release happens cohort by cohort (`:1312`).
5. Unconfirmed donors stay external read-only on SiteStacker until a dated deadline. Then SiteStacker's scheduler is switched off: one global stop with evidence. The ministry phones the remainder, as Tithe.ly recommends (X10).
6. Wallet, PayPal and ACH-without-evidence donors skip step 3's masked method and re-enter a method.

**Cost:** SiteStacker stays on for the confirmation window, and gifts-only catch-up imports run during it. This is the late tail q01-r5 K1/K2 describe. Attrition should be lower than with re-keying because the donor only clicks, but no source measures it (§4.3).

### 6.4 What stays with Phase 30 in either design (C1)

## 8. Summary

- **What must come over from the processor side, and what cannot.** A SiteStacker export carries recurring _schedule data_ only. Card and bank credentials live in the ministry's processor vault (S1). Five processors are vendor-named (S8); Stripe, Authorize.net and PayPal Standard appear only in vendor code (S9). Moving a vault is always a regulated hand-off from one processor to another, requested by the account owner:
  - Stripe imports from another processor (T1–T4, T16);
  - Stripe copies between Stripe accounts (T18);
  - Braintree, Square, Authorize.net and Bloomerang export only to PCI-compliant receivers (X1, X2, X4, X5).

  Asym receives only a mapping file of old IDs to new ones. No source moves schedules, subscriptions or history as live objects (T6, X1). Every executor must be stopped separately: SiteStacker's scheduler, any gateway-native schedule such as ARB, and PayPal subscriptions (X2, X3).

- **Industry practice comes in three paths** (§4.1):
  - P-A, token migration plus a dated handoff with no donor action: Donorbox, Fundraise Up, GoFundMe Pro, Bloomerang, Stripe's own guidance.
  - P-B, re-enrollment campaigns: church platforms, PayPal everywhere, small organizations.
  - P-C, leaving the old executor running.

  Timelines run from 1–6 weeks (Fundraise Up) to "several months" when a processor is slow (T3). Sender fees run $195–$3,000 (N2). **No measured attrition figure exists.** The only numbers are vendor claims and hearsay (§4.3).

- **Fit with Asym's rules.** Asym's ratified text rules out P-A as practised. Asym always onboards a _new_ connected account (q01-r4 G18; Stripe OAuth doc verified). REC-23, `:1236` and `:1314` then require fresh authorization and forbid bulk method binding. This is a direct conflict, corrected in §0 item 4. The rest fits:
  - P-B fits but is costly.
  - P-C fits, but keeps SiteStacker running as the executor, which strains the literal Option A promise.
  - Manual ACH entry (T17) and active-active schedules are forbidden.
  - PayPal-through-Stripe is unavailable (T23).
- **Two designs remain.** They are owner decisions, not Phase 30 decisions.
  - **Design 1** fits today's text. The vault is staged on the new account, then each donor gives a hosted one-step confirmation of the masked migrated method, with no re-keying. It needs a P16 ruling that confirming a migrated method counts as fresh authorization.
  - **Design 2** amends P16 to admit "proved continuation" under the evidence conditions a–h in §6.3, with counsel and processor sign-off.
- **Phase 30's part either way** (§6.4):
  - transport and staging of schedule exports and mapping files;
  - PAN-upload quarantine;
  - per-executor, per-rail and per-currency run evidence;
  - catch-up profiles for PayPal and processor reports, beyond SiteStacker;
  - closing forms before the final token export.
- **Corrections made in this file.**
  - §1 S8/S9: the refund article is live and names five processors; Stripe appears in vendor code.
  - §0 item 4: the conflict is direct.
  - §2: who can submit the intake form for a controller-properties account is unverified.
