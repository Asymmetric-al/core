## Verdict and exact candidate decision

**Accept with required amendments.** Include a bounded direct-correction
capability in the missionary workspace and let the organization govern its
permitted scope/fields through the existing permission system. This solves the
selected job without a new correction queue, independent ACL or routine
approval requirement for each contact edit.

> **D5 — Direct ordinary contact correction under tenant permissions.** The
> missionary workspace supports direct correction of eligible ordinary shared
> contact details. The organization controls the permitted missionary-purpose
> editing through its existing tenant permission configuration. A correction
> succeeds only when the current actor, exact contact, field, purpose and source
> restrictions permit it; being a missionary, viewing or sharing the contact,
> originally entering it, or holding a setting alone is not sufficient.
>
> Save changes the organization's authoritative eligible contact value through
> its source owner, with truthful history and conflict/recovery behavior. It
> does not maintain a competing private override or require routine staff
> approval of an already qualified ordinary edit. Explain the shared effect
> in the short editor and show only the current permitted editing choices.
>
> Tenant configuration uses the existing P3/P12 permission and field-policy
> controls, current administration authority and governance for the actual
> change. It cannot override protected-record/field, purpose or source floors,
> create unsupported field semantics, or turn ordinary correction into broad
> CRM browsing. Permission administration and contact-value editing are
> separate powers. A pending policy change is not an effective permission.
>
> D5 does not itself select universal enablement, a new installation preset,
> a permanent all-tenants-off product mode, per-contact collaborator settings,
> a suggestion inbox or a new approval engine. Missing or unknown authority
> remains denied under the existing fail-closed rules; independently valid
> permissions retain their own scope. Exact initial mapping and new field/source
> qualification must be resolved before activation, without guessing access.
>
> Correction does not verify possession, change login or represented/legal/
> billing/public identity, merge people, alter giving, subscribe someone,
> change contact restrictions or disclose private notes. Each field uses its
> actual source contract; a phone example does not make email, postal and
> account fields equivalent. D1–D4's audience, continuity and archive boundaries
> remain unchanged.

This is a product capability plus tenant control, not a claim that the current
application, P9 producer or P12 configuration already implements it. Exact
capability keys, registry classification, field mappings and source transaction
design belong to later authorized owner reconciliation and implementation.

## Necessity, strongest alternative and smallest viable product

The chosen job is correcting an ordinary shared contact detail when the
missionary learns the new fact. Direct qualified correction can avoid a staff
handoff and improve currency, but no representative impact measurement has been
obtained. The stronger data-quality claim is falsifiable: the user must update
the intended source once, understand that staff see the result, and avoid
overwriting a newer correction or changing another domain's facts.

The strongest alternative remains staff-applied correction through existing
CRM authority. It centralizes stewardship and avoids adding missionary write
scope, at the cost of coordination and delay. It does not imply a new proposal
queue, reviewer assignment, notification or SLA. The founder chose A, so this
alternative is not carried forward as a hidden compulsory approval layer.

The smallest product is the existing contact editor plus the existing tenant
permission administration surface, extended only for the exact qualified
missionary-purpose capability/fields. No-build is appropriate for a separate
permission engine, contact override store, correction inbox and new workflow
service. It is not enough to show an editable field while leaving the backend
generic, unqualified or permanently unable to accept any legitimate correction.

## Concern register and exact proposed clauses

Likelihood is conditional on the stated shortcut, not measured incidence.
Category numbers follow the founder's 22-category scheme. These are crosscutting
findings for the root review, not independent all-category runtime qualification.

### D5-PS01 — Tenant settings become a second permission system

**Categories:** 2, 3, 5–8, 10, 11, 15, 19. **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:**
Plausible if a new `missionary_can_edit_contacts` preference bypasses source policy.

**What/why:** A convenient setting can drift from capability grants, field
permissions, protected identities and record scope. A field hidden in one
surface may remain editable through another route, or a setting may wrongly
grant an entire CRM record rather than the allowed purpose.

**Evidence:** P3 Modules 1–3; P12 A/B/D; Q5 limits on broad CRM editing.

**Answer impact:** Preserve tenant control; require integration with the existing
authority instead of an independent Boolean or duplicate matrix.

**Permanent fix:** Express the qualified capability/scope/fields in the existing
registry, policy and resolver. UI renders and changes that same source through
its current administration path; all writers consume the same effective result.

**Exact clause:** “Tenant control of missionary contact editing belongs to the
existing permission and field-policy system. No Phase 28 preference, local
allowlist or secondary ACL can independently grant or bypass the exact current
actor/contact/field/purpose and protected-source checks.”

### D5-PS02 — An invented default silently broadens or removes rights

**Categories:** 1, 5–7, 10, 13, 15, 20. **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:**
Plausible if configuration is translated into all-on, all-off or a new seed.

**What/why:** The founder selected availability and tenant control, not automatic
granting to every missionary or revocation of an independently valid existing
edit grant. Conversely, “defaults unresolved” must not become pressure to invent
a broad preset or ask another unnecessary permission question before useful
design can proceed.

**Evidence:** P12 A/C/D registry, seeded staff defaults and exact active context;
P3 fail-closed policy; current Q5 says existing exact grants remain independent.

**Answer impact:** Accept A. Do not ratify an installation preset by implication.

**Permanent fix:** Follow exact existing declared mappings where applicable;
qualify any new mapping with its owner. In the absence of a qualified permission,
deny that action normally. This is an authority rule, not a newly selected
permanent product mode or a reason for a special tenant onboarding wizard.

**Exact clause:** “D5 selects no universal grant or new installation default.
Preserve exact independently valid permissions; missing/unknown authority grants
nothing. Qualify new registry/module/field mappings through existing owners
before activation, without inferring rights from role names or inventing a
separate default-setting product.”

### D5-PS03 — Ordinary corrections include other domains by accident

**Categories:** 4–7, 10, 11, 14, 15, 19. **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:**
Plausible if the editor takes a generic profile/contact payload.

**What/why:** Phone, login email, household destination, legal name, billing
identity and communication preference have different meanings. A routine edit
could change identity claims, private communication access, subscription state
or source-authoritative facts the missionary cannot correct.

**Evidence:** P9 B17; P12 personal-contact paragraph; Q5 phone example expressly
does not equate email/postal/account fields; D2 source/consent separation.

**Answer impact:** Retain direct correction; qualify each eligible field through
its exact owner. No need to ask the founder to design normalization algorithms.

**Permanent fix:** Use a finite owner-qualified correction contract and existing
field catalog. Unsupported field semantics are not made safe by an admin toggle.
Validate the complete selected request before committing; preserve raw meaning,
source provenance and separately governed verification/identity facts.

**Exact clause:** “Only owner-qualified ordinary contact fields are selectable
for this capability. Tenant configuration cannot authorize unsupported or
independent Auth, legal, billing, public, consent, verification, merge or financial
effects. Validate every selected field and current permission before one accepted
source correction.”

### D5-PS04 — Configuration power and value-edit power are conflated

**Categories:** 4–7, 10, 15, 18. **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:** Plausible
if anyone who can edit a contact can also expand their permitted fields.

**What/why:** An administrator's ability to configure a purpose need not include
access to actual private contact values; a missionary editing a value must not
gain policy administration. A generic “admin” label or ownership of the contact
is not sufficient current authority for either operation.

**Evidence:** P12 code-owned capabilities/current Tenant context and separate
module/entity/field permissions; P3 subtractive resolver; ADR-0211 exact governance.

**Answer impact:** Preserve tenant choice without inventing a special custodian,
support bypass or self-service permission escalator.

**Permanent fix:** Reuse separately qualified administrative commands and
current actor/target ceilings. Show safe field/scope labels and effective-state
explanations without needing to enumerate protected people or private values.

**Exact clause:** “Managing missionary correction permissions and correcting a
contact value are separate authorized actions. Configuration grants no contact
read/write right by itself; ordinary editing grants no configuration power.
Administration uses existing current scope, ceilings and safe disclosure rules.”

### D5-PS05 — Policy governance becomes either bypassed or per-edit approval

**Categories:** 1, 2, 5, 8, 9, 12, 13, 15, 17, 19. **Severity:** High for an
unauthorized widening; Medium for repeated delay. **Likelihood:** Plausible if
every “Save” is treated alike.

**What/why:** Applying a pending exposure expansion immediately violates the
accepted policy. Requiring another person to approve every ordinary contact
value correction rejects the selected direct-edit outcome and creates a queue.
Not every capability assignment is itself a projection-policy change, either.

**Evidence:** ADR-0211 lines 16–37; P12 D26 ordinary qualified updates and existing
grant/field-policy administration; Q5 explicitly rejects a universal edit queue.

**Answer impact:** Accept A, with governance applied to the actual operation.

**Permanent fix:** Distinguish current field policy, proposed policy change,
capability assignment and contact value mutation. Use existing governance for
each. Show effective versus pending configuration; routine value Save uses the
already effective policy without a new review object.

**Exact clause:** “Apply ADR-0211 to actual projection-policy widening and the
existing P12 rules to capability/grant administration; pending changes grant
nothing. Ordinary contact corrections under qualified effective authority need
no new supervisory approval. D5 creates no correction queue or approval engine.”

### D5-PS06 — A private copy competes with the shared source

**Categories:** 2–5, 10–14, 17, 19. **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:** Plausible
if the new editor writes the old preparation payload or updates both masters.

**What/why:** The missionary could see “saved” while staff retain another phone,
or a later private save could overwrite a better staff/donor correction. A
shared-contact edit must not accidentally publish the preparation's notes,
change its archive state or reopen work.

**Evidence:** D2 authoritative contact admission; D3 private/history boundaries;
D4 orthogonal working state and no extra canonical edit right; P9 source ownership.

**Answer impact:** A is a direct authoritative correction, not an ongoing private
override/synchronization feature. D1–D4 remain intact.

**Permanent fix:** Read/write the permitted shared contact through its qualified
owner; keep original preparation provenance and private material governed
separately. Any source echo is a projection, not another editable master.

**Exact clause:** “A shared-contact correction updates its authoritative owner,
not a competing private override. It does not disclose preparation notes, change
D2 sharing/notification, restore D4 preparation, or create/close independent work.
Original assertions remain provenance only under their own retention/audience.”

### D5-PS07 — Old settings or concurrent corrections defeat current intent

**Categories:** 2, 4, 7, 12–15, 20, 21. **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:**
Plausible under normal network delay, open tabs or concurrent staff correction.

**What/why:** A field enabled when the editor opened may be forbidden at Save;
another actor may correct the value; a lost-response retry may overwrite their
newer update. Conversely, unrelated permission changes must not deny a still
valid correction or make an accepted value change appear undone.

**Evidence:** P12 current authority/epochs; P9 B17 atomic revision/recovery;
D2–D4 distinguish acceptance, current state and historical receipts.

**Answer impact:** Add exact current-policy/source proof, not new user choices
about locking, retries or per-field conflict algorithms.

**Permanent fix:** At source acceptance, reprove the exact current ability and
relevant source revision. Preserve all-or-nothing selected-field validation and
durable outcome recovery; current read permission still governs returned values.

**Exact clause:** “Correction acceptance uses current effective authority and
the relevant source revision. Lost responses recover the same accepted outcome
without overwriting newer facts. Permission narrowing applies through its owner;
unrelated grant changes do not defeat an independently valid path, and later
revocation does not rewrite a correction that was validly accepted.”

### D5-PS08 — Settings complexity overwhelms the small contact-editing job

**Categories:** 1, 3, 8, 9, 16–19, 22. **Severity:** Medium. **Likelihood:**
Plausible if the feature adds its own role editor, field matrix or per-contact setup.

**What/why:** Tenant control does not require arbitrary rules, separate
missionary roles, per-record collaborator controls, recurring review or another
admin product. Too much configuration encourages broad permission workarounds;
a false-simple master toggle conceals effective restrictions.

**Evidence:** Founder tenant-settings and simplicity goals; P12 existing
friendly module/entity/field controls; Q5 narrow capability rather than broad CRM.

**Answer impact:** Keep meaningful tenant control within P12, not an ornamental
setting or a new configuration framework.

**Permanent fix:** Reuse the current permission surface with a plainly labeled
bounded contact-correction entry and only qualified scope/field controls. Show
effective state and pending changes; deeper source restrictions remain enforced.

**Exact clause:** “Expose tenant control in existing permission administration,
using its qualified scope/field model and clear effective/pending state. Add no
per-contact configuration, second role builder, arbitrary rule language, review
cadence or separate missionary-permissions engine for D5.”

### D5-PS09 — Direct editing stays unusable while every boundary is defended

**Categories:** 1, 4, 9, 12, 17, 19, 21. **Severity:** Medium–High. **Likelihood:**
Plausible if deny-only tests, hidden controls or a blanket predecessor gate count
as completion.

**What/why:** A feature that always says “ask staff” despite valid tenant policy
does not deliver A. A positive save could still be hard to understand, silently
partial, or require navigation through the full admin CRM. Missing one optional
integration cannot block an independently qualified native correction.

**Evidence:** Q5 chosen direct capability; P9 A17 no provider gate; P27 clear
composed UX inherited through prior reviews; brief complete-journey proof.

**Answer impact:** Preserve affirmative utility and inherited authority together.

**Permanent fix:** Qualify a real permitted missionary journey and corresponding
admin configuration, including narrow enabled/denied cases and honest recovery.
The exact P9/P12 source seam is required; unrelated providers/future phases are
not blanket prerequisites. Existing staff correction remains separately usable.

**Exact clause:** “Completion requires a real tenant-authorized missionary to
correct an eligible shared value once and understand the result, alongside
negative permission cases. No placeholder, permanently deny-only surface or
unrelated provider gate substitutes for the exact qualified native path.”

### D5-PS10 — Permission and correction claims outrun actual evidence

**Categories:** 6, 7, 12, 14–21. **Severity:** High for access/data failures;
Medium for cost/performance. **Likelihood:** Plausible if target P12 text or
legacy mutation code is presented as current qualification.

**What/why:** The existing shared-row update is not proof of the selected
field/purpose settings. A new audit may expose values; old writers may ignore
narrowed policy; policy edits may cause unbounded invalidation. Source intent and
past generic probes cannot establish these actual D5 results.

**Evidence:** Q5 current-runtime limitations; document-authority vocabulary;
P12 registry/current-context contracts; P9 B17 convergence of reached paths.

**Answer impact:** Record the selected product outcome and exact pending seams,
not a declaration that they are deployed or that new budgets have been measured.

**Permanent fix:** Trace selected capability/configuration to owner contracts,
actual producers/readers, migration and outcome evidence. Use minimal protected
business receipts, bounded queries and representative workloads. Scope delivery
gates to the exact needed seams, with no vague “monitor later” waiver.

**Exact clause:** “Qualify D5 configuration, current authorization, source save,
safe audit/recovery and all reached legacy paths together before activation.
Preserve intent/implementation/qualification distinctions, prove compatible
cutover, and derive capacity/user-success budgets from actual evidence.”

## Smallest meaningful tenant and missionary experience

The following is design judgment within the selected capability and tenant
control, not additional access policy or an independent configuration product.

**Tenant administrator:** Within existing permissions, use a plain label such
as **Missionary contact editing**. Present the bounded purpose/scope and the
eligible fields using existing field-policy controls. Distinguish effective
permissions from a pending governed change. Reuse existing safe permission
explanation/preview where qualified; do not require opening actual private
contact records to understand the setting. Unsupported fields are unavailable,
not switches that appear to authorize them. A role's friendly name is not the
authority. No per-contact setup, copied policy matrix or mandatory new wizard.

**Missionary:** Use the existing contact detail and a short editor containing
only the fields currently qualified for correction. A concise explanation can
say **“Updates the contact details shared with your organization.”** One Save
performs the source-owned correction. Show pending, validation, changed-since-
opened, accepted-but-refresh-failed and unavailable outcomes honestly. Preserve
permitted input during recovery without exposing protected retained drafts.
An action unavailable under current policy does not silently create a proposal,
notify staff or ask for a new permission grant. Explain the actual limitation
and use an existing separately qualified next step only where one exists.

The contact-detail editor is not newsletter preferences, a profile/security
form, a global CRM editor or the source of an activity score. Editing from an
archived preparation's permitted shared-contact link need not restore the
preparation; its current source authority determines whether Save is allowed.

## Engineering/owner work versus genuine remaining product choices

**Resolve without another founder fork:** exact capability registration and
module/explicit-only classification; field catalog/source mapping; using P12
configuration instead of a new preference table; enforcing read/edit/source
floors; revision/conflict and retry mechanics; migration/writer census; policy
change classification; safe audit; accessible editor and current-state feedback.
Ordinary value edits need no new approval question. Field-specific technical
validation is not a reason to ask the founder to design data handling.

**Do not invent a default:** the selected requirement can progress using
existing declared permission mappings and normal fail-closed absence. Exact
new mappings must be qualified before release. If the owners later identify a
real competing product expectation about pre-enabled scope for new tenants,
bring that concrete material access choice back; do not ask an abstract global
On/Off question merely because a new capability has not yet been named.

**A separate product choice is required only if necessary to change meaning:**
automatically granting additional rights; allowing a field/purpose the current
source deliberately excludes; enabling a broader population/record scope;
creating a correction proposal/approval workflow; or allowing private corrections
that diverge permanently from shared truth. None follows from D5. Do not add
these options speculatively to prolong the contact-permissions branch.

The existing platform's administration rules remain applicable. ADR-0211 is a
real constraint when the change is projection-policy widening; it is neither a
new permission request for this research note nor a reason to stop ordinary
qualified value editing. No implementation or actual permission change occurs
in this research-only session.
