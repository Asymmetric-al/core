## Three identities, with exact limitations

**Reviewed plan.** A plan records exact session/workspace, a byte-identity marker, mapping version, source-instance namespace and applicable authority evidence. Its rows record the reviewed normalized assertions, source ID, selected action and any authorized target revision. Plan and row UPDATE/DELETE are rejected. Changing a mapping produces another plan; an obsolete session/plan revision cannot approve. These markers stand in for qualified P29 bytes and P30 parsing/mapping; no parser, actual upload-byte hash or content-security process is executed.

**Stable source record.** Acceptance identity is scoped by tenant, private workspace, source-instance namespace and stable external record ID. In production, that namespace must distinguish the actual source instance/account/list where IDs are unique, not just a vendor name. Upload digest/row number locate evidence; they are not person or cross-import identity. Exact reimport at another row position returns **Already imported**, retaining the same target and new run lineage. This means a prior admitted effect exists, not that today's target remains identical to the old file.

The changed-payload case is **Needs review**, with no overwrite. A new-source candidate is staged for review rather than merged by its shared email. Those review decisions are supplied fixture input, not a working identity classifier or proof that all duplicates can be found. No canonical Party matching, hidden-match branching, household inference or automatic merge occurs. A later source-qualified ordinary correction remains a distinct action. This matches the proposed initial add/reuse profile, not P30's broader potential update capabilities.

**Worker attempt.** A job has a monotonically incremented claim generation, worker and expiry. Unclaimed, wrong-worker, old-generation and expired attempts cannot advance the row. A final live-clock check after locks prevents an attempt whose lease expired while waiting from committing a late outcome; its SQL error rolls back that row transaction. The five-minute lease and two-second race deadline are illustrative test values, not selected product TTLs. The real execution substrate, claim renewal, heartbeat, backpressure and scheduling remain unqualified.

**Files without stable IDs are outside this proof.** Same immutable bytes/same meaning/scope should return or resume the already identified run under the qualified P30 contract. The test does not implement that run-reuse mechanism. Changed/reordered no-ID files require conservative authorized candidate review; neither filename, row number, content fingerprint nor global email uniqueness may be invented as durable person identity. The negative control shows four file/row keys for two stable records and two wrong row-position attachments after reordering.

## Acceptance, partial work and current access

Approval changes only the staged run's admission state. The row command rechecks current exact authority and approved plan, locks its current lease and any reviewed target, then reserves/locks the scoped source identity. Two different runs for the same source record serialize on that identity. A new accepted preparation row, its source linkage, provenance and outcome commit together. Failure of required provenance rolls all current-row records back; previously accepted rows remain. Sequence allocation may leave harmless gaps on rollback and is not treated as business success.

One deliberately review-only row does not undo other accepted rows. A source exception on one row does not stop an independently eligible next row. No batch-wide all-or-nothing or universal Undo is claimed. There is no watermark or scheduler in this fixture: the existing P30/Inngest implementation must preserve late commits, enumerate unresolved rows and avoid advancing completion past unprocessed lower-order work. `done` is set only in the same transaction as a durable final row outcome.

Cancellation serializes with row acceptance. Cancel-first prevents new acceptance; row-first preserves that accepted row and then stops the remaining work. Cancellation never deletes accepted preparation, source lineage or later independent edits. Its fixture command is a narrowly scoped owner reduction; the actual cancel/read powers still need current P12 qualification. It is not a general permission bypass after someone loses access.

Policy-off first blocks the waiting row and protected preview readback. Row-first can lawfully commit before the later reduction. Re-enabling the gate does not revive a plan carrying stale exact authority evidence. The real source may freshly requalify unchanged original intent under current applicable authority; unrelated tenant epoch changes do not automatically mandate a new upload or repetitive review. No restored snapshot or old worker can grant itself authority.

An existing restricted target's revision is rechecked under lock. The synthetic independent target-edit command increments that revision only; it does not model a real contact-field correction. Edit-first produces a conflict. Verify-first may record Already imported before a later edit, without overwriting it. The receipt's original-acceptance meaning remains distinct from current contact contents.

## Actual role and effect evidence

The real login roles are `missionary_a`, `staff_a`, `worker_a`, `worker_b` and `policy_manager`, all nonsuperuser/non-BYPASSRLS. `command_executor` is NOLOGIN, nonsuperuser/non-BYPASSRLS and not table owner. All ten tables have enabled and forced coarse tenant RLS. Ordinary missionary/staff/worker roles have no raw table access. Staff's bounded preview is unavailable and raw staging SELECT is denied. After the fixture gate is revoked, the missionary's preview is also unavailable.

The executor has limited command rights and no permission on the dark-effect table. Default PUBLIC function execution is revoked and search paths are fixed. [roles.json](postgres-probe/roles.json) and [privileges.json](postgres-probe/privileges.json) retain the observed grants. Fixed SQL-login-to-tenant and worker-to-originator mappings are harness trust, not actual Auth, signed context, service-owner ceiling or another P12 resolver.

The accepted `preparation` payload stands in for already field-qualified restricted assertions. Its JSON shape is not a production allowlist, parser, contact validator or classification policy. The empty dark-effect table is a negative-effect witness only: no actual send, newsletter, grant, relationship enrollment, public page, task, gift or commitment producer is connected. In particular, this test does **not** prove that imported adverse contact evidence is accepted atomically by the real consent owner and enforced by the recipient/send gate. Such rows must wait for that qualified adapter where required; a quiet toy table is insufficient.

Preview admission and raw grants are tested, but a complete lease-independent result reader, protected error download, audit reader, current classification floor and hidden-match/timing non-disclosure are not. Same-row retries within the current lease return the recorded outcome; an expired worker must use the separately qualified result/recovery path. Retained outcomes are evidence, not a standing right to read them.

## Proposed technical clauses

**DB17-01 — Immutable reviewed meaning.** P30 records a private, no-business-write plan pinned to P29 byte identity, parser/mapping/source profile, source-instance namespace, exact target scope, row selections and relevant target/authority evidence. Changes produce a new reviewable plan. Final acceptance uses that exact plan plus current source authority; filename or browser state is never the commit contract.

**DB17-02 — Identity is source-qualified.** Distinguish run/row location from stable source record and canonical person identity. Scope durable source IDs to tenant and source instance plus the actual target context. Reimport resolves the same admitted effect without new target creation. Changed payload is an authorized discrepancy/correction decision, not implicit overwrite. No-ID changed files need bounded candidate review; neither email, file hash nor row ordinal becomes a global person key.

**DB17-03 — Per-row durable acceptance and honest partial status.** Commit the accepted target action, lineage, required audit/provenance and authoritative row outcome atomically. A failed row leaves no partial target action; other accepted rows remain. Run progress derives from durable row outcomes and unresolved work, preserves late commits and does not jump a cursor over unfinished rows. Completion with issues, cancellation and failure must not relabel accepted work as absent or promise universal rollback.

**DB17-04 — Fenced workers, source revisions and reductions.** Reprove current P12/target authority at each consequential source acceptance. Use the existing source/execution owner's durable semantic identity and current claim generation; stale or expired workers cannot mutate source or advance result/cursor state. Lease checks account for waits. Serialize cancellation/policy reduction and expected target revision against acceptance. Preserve a lawful earlier commit; do not let resume/re-enable restore revoked authority or overwrite later edits.

**DB17-05 — Restricted inputs and results throughout.** Protect original bytes, staging, preview, accepted preparation, lineage, errors, logs, status counts and downloads under their actual source purpose and current audience. Staff/service roles do not create ordinary access. Qualify result disclosure before hidden-match branches and keep raw browser/worker table grants closed. Current result-read authority is distinct from a prior accepted operation or active worker lease.

**DB17-06 — Owner effects only, with no outward replay.** Invoke the exact P9 restricted target and P4 identity rules; initial intake adds/reuses and does not bulk-correct shared/preparation fields. No positive consent, send, account, grant, canonical Party, financial supporter, enrollment or public effect follows from a file. Where adverse contact evidence requires a real consent-owner restriction receipt, accept it atomically with the row through that owner or block that row; do not substitute staging metadata or an unused suppression table. Reversal, cleanup, retention and restore remain owner-controlled and cannot erase later independent facts.
