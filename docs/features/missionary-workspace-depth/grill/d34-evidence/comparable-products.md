# D34 — comparable products and a small expired-referral history

Research retrieved **October 18, 2026 UTC**. The founder selected **limited expired-referral history** and challenged unnecessary complexity. This note supports the subsequent review; it does not claim ratification of amendments, implementation, an authenticated competitor test, or observed missionary preference. Only this research file is authored here.

## Selected scope and useful conclusion

The selected scenario is an expired referral with no other active supporter or referral relationship. Maya may recognize an eligible expired referral through a minimal, currently authorized history projection. The entry does not itself disclose contact details or private referral context. Continuing the referral remains a separate authorized decision. The [Q34 snapshot](q34-before-answer.txt) records the preceding unanswered question; the current founder answer supersedes that question status for this review, without selecting downstream details.

**Recommendation:** keep current work prominent and put eligible expired entries behind one clear filter or history choice in the existing referral surface. Show **Expired**, approved recognition information and an actual date only where known and permitted. Do not make a new archive application, renewal wizard, dashboard, request queue or permission editor. Browsing an expired entry should require no acknowledgment, reason, save or confirmation. This is a design judgment informed by the comparisons below, not a demonstrated best-performing design.

The evidence supports separating active work from retained history. It does **not** establish one common privacy model: several products retain much richer contact records, while another excludes archived contacts from ordinary queries. Those contrary examples matter when explaining what Core is deliberately preserving and withholding.

## Strongest alternatives and tradeoffs

**Staff-assisted recovery** was the strongest simpler alternative to selected A. It keeps the ordinary active-relationship boundary and avoids an additional missionary history disclosure purpose. The cost is that Maya cannot recognize the old referral herself and needs an authorized human handoff. This is a plausible product tradeoff, not a measured frequency or burden. Selecting A should buy recognition and orientation; it should not quietly acquire all of a general archive system's capabilities.

**Rich hidden or closed records with deliberate reactivation** are the strongest contrary product examples (S01, S03, S04). They reduce re-entry and may provide more context for future work. They also assume access or actions that D34 did not select. Rejecting those extra rights is an owner-boundary decision, not proof that those products have poor UX. If the minimal approved information cannot distinguish referrals usefully, that is a real usability limit to test, not a reason to silently reveal private details.

The recommended middle path is a discoverable, limited source projection in an existing surface. It adds no new action merely because an item is visible. It does not require importing a competitor's entire contact lifecycle, customizable archive rules, bulk tools, permissions taxonomy, notifications or reopen flow.

## Owner reconciliation and unresolved limits

The [founder referral journey](../phase28-founder-brief.md:137) and [P9 ownership constraints](../../../../prds/program-roadmap/owner-constraints.md:81) retain source ownership of provenance, expiry and reporting. The [ordinary active-relationship boundary](../../../../prds/program-roadmap/owner-constraints.md:143) requires an explicit bounded source-purpose amendment for selected A; an archive filter alone cannot change it. [P12 current context](../../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-12-full-role-permission-configuration.md:1552) and [purpose/contactability](../../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-12-full-role-permission-configuration.md:1654) continue to distinguish reading from contacting. [P27 genuine return](../../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-27-donor-development/partnerships.md:377) is not a referral renewal or restored-access shortcut.

This note does not select a continuation actor, new organization setting, default, referral timeout, retention duration, export facility, indefinite history, historical-count entitlement or restore command. It also does not establish a qualified referral producer or runtime permission path. Detailed source safeguards belong in their owners and qualification evidence; they need not become extra questions or ceremonies in Maya's ordinary browsing flow.

Only public primary pages and local documentation were read. No competitor accounts, signups, real contacts, provider mutations or external communications were used.
