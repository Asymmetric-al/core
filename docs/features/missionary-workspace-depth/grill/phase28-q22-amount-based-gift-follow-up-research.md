# Q22 frontier — may a fixed amount independently prompt personal follow-up?

Research date: **October 17, 2026 UTC**. **Founder answered A; corrected decision and amendments explicitly ratified October 17, 2026 Asia/Bangkok.** D21's
corrected decision and C01–C20/P01–P28 have been explicitly ratified. This note
prepared one distinct product choice. The original pre-answer research is retained
below; the completed D22 review records the answer and subsequently ratified amendments.
No new financial implementation or formal owner amendment is made.

## The single choice

Grace makes a received gift to Maya's ministry. It is not her first gift, and
there is no separately qualified special-purpose or above-commitment reason.

**Should the organization be able to choose an amount that, by itself, creates
a personal follow-up task for Maya?**

- **A — Allow an optional organization-set amount rule (recommended).** When the
  organization enables it and selects an amount for the relevant currency, an
  eligible gift meeting that rule can create follow-up. Explain the amount-based
  reason without calling the gift extra support or rating the donor.
- **B — Require another qualifying reason.** Amount alone creates no native
  missionary task; other qualified automatic follow-up and Maya's manual actions
  remain available.

This is an illustrative scenario, not an interviewed donor or missionary. The
question concerns a fixed organization-selected amount, **not** whether this gift
is unusually large for Grace. D21 already excludes automatic attention whose
deciding basis is the latter comparison. An amount-based reason could be truthful
without claiming generosity, additional support or a new financial classification.
Neither option sends a message, changes money or grants access.

**Recommendation:** A permits a simple, explicit organization policy without
historical guessing. It can express a real donor-care preference while keeping
ordinary missionary work unchanged when the organization has not enabled such a
rule. B is the strongest simpler alternative: ordinary large installments do not
create extra work merely because of their size, and no amount-setting surface is
needed for native follow-up. The documentation supports the feasibility of an
explicit rule, not measured net benefit, optimal amounts or acceptable task volume
for Core tenants. This is a product judgment for the founder, not a fact to assume.

## Counterexamples checked before presenting the choice

- An ordinary large installment can meet a fixed amount rule without being extra.
  A may intentionally create a task for that reason; B would require another
  qualified reason. This is the core tradeoff, not a financial data error.
- An explicitly additional gift may be smaller than the routine installment.
  Choosing A must not silently make its threshold a minimum for other independently
  qualified special/above-commitment purposes.
- A contribution split across ministries may exceed the chosen amount in total
  while the permitted relevant allocation does not. Source basis must be named;
  no hidden allocation can leak or qualify the wrong subject.
- Two currencies cannot be compared to one untyped scalar. No implicit FX or
  dollar default is authorized.
- A later refund or source correction may change eligible effective amount.
  Current evidence and D20 correction preserve human history and exact coverage.
- Editing a rule or importing old gifts cannot turn historical evidence into new
  live follow-up. Originally live unconsumed repair remains distinct.
- The same gift could qualify under more than one reason. A does not authorize
  duplicate intended work, automatic donor communication or a second task engine.

These are reasoned cases checked against owner contracts, not a new executable
experiment. D21's source inspection remains explicitly inherited; no current
amount-rule implementation, hosted configuration, user interview or performance
proof is claimed. No app/schema, formal owner/ADR/OpenSpec, dependency, provider
operation, ticket or external contact changed.

## Founder answer and completed review

The founder selected **A — Allow an optional organization-set amount rule**, with
optional tenant customization and clean simple UX. The subsequent clarification
explicitly selected **one organization-wide policy**, without workspace overrides.

The [completed D22 review](phase28-d22-amount-based-gift-follow-up-adversarial-review.md)
returns **Accept with required amendments**. It proposes Off by default, one
positive inclusive threshold per supported currency, one exact source-certified
per-gift/Party/ministry amount, prospective policy edits and Off, retained admitted
work, current source correction and existing task/coverage ownership. The setting
has inert preview/test, current management authority, truthful persisted state and
usable Off even with invalid draft edits. No automatic message, extra-support
label, history-only inference, retrospective task sweep or scope override follows.

A and organization-wide scope were selected at review; the founder subsequently
explicitly ratified the corrected decision and C01–C24/P01–P34 as future
obligations on October 17, 2026 Asia/Bangkok. They are not implemented by ratification. New evidence consists of
25 finite counterexamples and 29 isolated PostgreSQL outcomes/seven actual races,
including an expected unsafe rounding example. Actual source/P12/task/UI/user/load
qualification remains explicitly required. No Q23 was asked in the initial review; the session records subsequent continuation.
