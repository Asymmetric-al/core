Page-Off or retention choices. The latest user ratification accepts the

Suggested next single question:

> **Before a new reader joins Maya's newsletter, should they confirm by email
> even when they are signed in with that same verified address?**
>
> **A — Confirm each new signup or rejoin (recommended).** The reader deliberately
> confirms the email for that newsletter before joining. Existing active
> subscribers are not asked again.
>
> **B — Reuse a signed-in reader's verified email.** A signed-in reader joins
> immediately after choosing Join for that newsletter using their current verified
> address. Other readers confirm by email.
>
> Both choices keep signup separate from giving and require no new account.

Why A: it retains one comprehensible public admission journey, fresh exact-
newsletter/address evidence and the reviewed D6 design without adding a
cross-purpose account-proof branch. That is a product judgment, not a claim
that B cannot lawfully express consent or that mailbox confirmation proves a
natural person's identity. B's explicit Join would still be new purpose-specific
consent; a verified account alone would never enroll anyone. B is therefore a
real, defensible lower-friction alternative, subject to current exact-address
proof and source qualification if selected.

Do not say confirmation is required by universal law, that competitor evidence
proves conversion improvement, or that this choice establishes a default period.
Do not describe merely opening an email link as the confirming action: the
reviewed safeguard keeps GET/scanner/prefetch effects inert.
