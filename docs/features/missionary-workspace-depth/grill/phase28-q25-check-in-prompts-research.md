# Q25 — optional organization-provided coaching check-in questions (answered A)

Research date: **October 17, 2026, Asia/Bangkok**. **Answered A.**
The founder selected optional organization-provided questions. The completed
[D25 review](phase28-d25-check-in-questions-adversarial-review.md) accepts A with
required amendments, explicitly ratified on October 17, 2026 UTC. D24 remains
ratified. This is a research decision, not a formal specification, new live
permission or implemented questionnaire.

## The one choice

Maya wants to give Jordan context that numbers cannot explain before their next
coaching conversation. She can write a voluntary check-in. Her organization might
offer questions such as **“What went well?”** and **“What would you like help
thinking through?”** These are illustrative prompts, not a prescribed ministry
method, care assessment or evidence of an observed interview.

**Should organizations be able to provide optional reusable questions for these
written coaching check-ins?**

- **A — Optional organization-provided questions (recommended).** Authorized staff
  can maintain a short reusable set of written questions. Maya can use whichever
  help, skip questions and add her own context before deliberately sharing. A
  plain written check-in still works when no questions are configured.
- **B — Written check-ins without organization templates.** Maya writes in her own
  words. The first release has no staff-managed reusable question sets, reducing
  administration and configuration.

**Recommendation: A.** It gives organizations useful guidance without imposing
one agency's method or making a blank page the only starting point. Keep the
capability small: optional written questions, no required answers, scoring,
conditional survey builder or compulsory weekly submission. B remains a complete
native qualitative check-in; it removes template administration, not coaching.

Both options preserve the missionary's deliberate sharing, current organization
permissions and Jordan's read-only role. Maintaining questions does not grant
staff access to responses. Numeric target sharing does not share written
check-ins automatically.

The question is **not** whether check-ins exist, whether participation is optional,
whether every answer must be submitted, or whether coaches can edit missionary
records. Those boundaries are already settled. It is also not a request to choose
field wording or a layout that the design team can determine from evidence.

The strongest alternative is B: ordinary authored context with no template
management. It is simpler and may suit organizations that want entirely personal
preparation. A adds a small real staff workflow and version/meaning obligations;
it should be chosen for reusable guidance, not because another product has forms.

An existing [FORM-04 reference blueprint](../../../prds/program-roadmap/source-2026-09-22/assets/ws/form-blueprints/FORM-04.json:5)
is disabled and explicitly a reference blueprint. Its illustrative required
next-action field is not a mandate for this native voluntary check-in. The
[delivery contract](../../../prds/program-roadmap/delivery-contract.md:188)
preserves blueprint status versus buildable/current behavior. A does not require
the later Workflow Studio form builder or inherit its assignment/validation model.

## Consequences identified before the answer

A is now selected. The original review frontier called for the planned native
coaching entry and a small organization
configuration surface, within current tenant capabilities. Preserve a useful
no-template path. Keep questions visually optional, allow personal context, and
make the intended audience clear before sharing. Draft/save and disclosure are
different effects; staff's permission to maintain questions must not become
permission to read responses.

Template edits must not silently rewrite the question attached to an older answer
or replace text in an existing draft. Removing a template must not delete lawful
check-in history. Exact source ownership, current grants, revision rules, draft
recovery, rendering, retention and the meaningful positive/negative tests need
the subsequent answer's bounded review. Neither A nor B adds coach writing,
automatic tasks, new notifications, AI, care assessments or imported private notes.

Written content deserves its own disclosure qualification. D24's numeric
continuation cannot act as a standing permission for future narratives. This
question does not choose that separate publication/history behavior, invite a new
coach, or change existing episode duration.

The existing [D24 source census](d24-evidence/current-coaching-source.md) is reused
as same-day bounded static evidence: it did not find a qualified coaching-summary
runtime in the inspected paths. It is not proof of a check-in/template service.
No new source scan, database experiment or probe rerun was needed to ask this
product question. Actual service/UI/permission/user qualification remains future
work after the decision, not a competitor-derived implementation claim.

The subsequent [D25 review](phase28-d25-check-in-questions-adversarial-review.md)
completes fresh source/primary research, all 22 review categories, exact draft and
publication safeguards, 24 future proofs and 17 synthetic counterexamples. It
proposes one bounded question set, preserved answer meaning, private drafts and
deliberate exact Share/Update, independently controlled withdrawal and current
P12/source permissions. These amendments are now explicitly ratified as future
requirements, not implementation or unlimited narrative-history authority.

No application/schema, formal ADR/OpenSpec, ticket, dependency, provider operation,
