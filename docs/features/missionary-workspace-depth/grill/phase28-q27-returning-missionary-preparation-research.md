# Q27 — resuming own preparation after returning (answered A)

Research date: **October 17, 2026 UTC**. **Answered A.** The founder selected
resumption of own saved preparation, with useful flexibility and simplicity.
The [completed D27 review](phase28-d27-resume-preparation-adversarial-review.md)
returns **Accept with required amendments**; the founder explicitly ratified its
corrected decision and amendments on October 17, 2026 UTC. This note preserves the
original question and preparatory research; it is not a formal specification,
source-owner amendment or implemented permission.

## The one choice

Maya returns to the **same organization and ministry** and regains access to her
missionary workspace. Some prospect preparation she saved before leaving,
without sharing it, is still lawfully retained. This is an illustrative scenario,
not an observed ministry interview or a promise that preparation is always kept.

**Should Maya be able to resume her own eligible preparation from before she left?**

- **A — Resume her own saved preparation (recommended).** When the organization
  permits it and current access rules allow, Maya can find and deliberately
  resume selected preparation she previously saved. Returning alone restores no
  old permissions or sharing.
- **B — No resumption of earlier private preparation in this release.** Maya can
  make new preparation and use currently authorized shared CRM records. There
  is no workflow for resuming private preparation from her earlier membership.

**Recommendation: A.** It can save re-entry and preserve useful personal context
without transferring it to staff or successors. This is a product judgment,
conditional on exact current historical-access qualification. Authorship or a
matching email alone cannot authorize discovery. B avoids that new workflow and
its historical-access obligations; its cost is losing convenient use of retained
work. It does not require duplicate canonical contacts, delete retained material
or revoke an independently valid current source permission.

Both options preserve D3's no-new-continuity-access rule for other people,
ordinary currently authorized CRM access, D4's archive semantics and source-owned
retention. Neither revives old sharing, coaching access, reminders, scheduled
sends, subscriptions, connectors or completed/cancelled work.

## Original consequences identified for the answer review

If A is chosen, keep the journey small: after current access is established,
offer an unobtrusive way to find eligible **Earlier preparation**, review it and
continue on the same source entry. Do not force a return wizard or bring every
old prospect into the active list. Previously archived entries keep their state;
the existing deliberate **Return to active prospects** action applies when
allowed. Wording/layout here is a design direction, not a frozen screen design.

The review must qualify the canonical original-author binding, exact current
Tenant/assignment/ministry/source-history admission and revocation behavior
before any discovery or preview. Returning to another ministry or organization
does not imply permission. Same identity is necessary evidence but insufficient
authority; a reused email must not expose another person's history. Staff may
administer eligible access only within their authority, without acquiring a new
right to inspect private bodies. Missing history authority fails closed without
disclosing whether protected preparation exists.

Resumption must preserve the same source record and lawful provenance, detect
later corrections and mark earlier contact assertions as historical rather than
newly verified. It must not recreate a canonical person, promote private facts to
shared CRM, reset retention or recover purged content. Retained evidence alone
need not include a readable body. Ordinary source eligibility, restrictions and
retention must remain current throughout discovery and use, including retries,
membership changes and multiple tabs.

The answer review must distinguish resume/continue from archive return, restore
from backup, permission reinstatement and reconnecting a provider. No background
effect restarts merely because Maya reads or resumes an entry. Explain unavailable
content without promising permanent retention or exposing forbidden details.
Keep any required current-access decision separate from routine permitted edits;
do not invent a per-entry approval queue.

If B is chosen, do not show an inert recovery control or imply past preparation
was deleted. Current authorized shared CRM and ordinary work remain available.
New preparation uses existing identity/matching rules rather than duplicating
contacts to work around the absent workflow.

The [D3 bounded current-source census](d3-evidence/current-runtime.md) is reused
