# D25 evidence — optional coaching check-in questions and writing UX

Research date: **October 17, 2026, Asia/Bangkok**. Fresh public primary-document
research; bounded repository inspection. The founder selected **optional
organization-provided check-in questions**. The detailed profile below is the
main review's **proposed amendment**, not a claim that the founder separately
ratified every limit or interaction. No authenticated vendor testing, interviews,
prototype evaluation, app changes, accounts, submissions or notifications occurred.

## Decision and source boundaries

The useful job is small: authorized staff offer reusable questions; Maya writes
what helps, can skip every organization question, and deliberately shares her
chosen check-in with a permitted coach. Jordan reads that admitted context to
prepare for a conversation. Guidance must not become a reporting obligation.

The [Q25 frontier](../phase28-q25-check-in-prompts-research.md), now answered A,
distinguishes this from introducing qualitative check-ins themselves. The
[founder brief §10](../phase28-founder-brief.md:223) and
[D8-C09–C12](../phase28-d8-adversarial-review.md:486) already preserve read-only
coaching, voluntary narratives, declared audiences and source restrictions.
[Retained owner constraints](../../../../prds/program-roadmap/owner-constraints.md:118)
do not make weekly self-report compulsory. Numeric sharing under
[D24](../phase28-d24-coaching-activity-summary-adversarial-review.md) does not
publish present or future narrative answers. A new coach receives no historical
check-in by implication. Prompt-management authority grants no response access.

The reference
[FORM-04 blueprint](../../../../prds/program-roadmap/source-2026-09-22/assets/ws/form-blueprints/FORM-04.json:5)
is disabled/reference material; its illustrative required field is not authority
for a compulsory response. There is no dependency on a general form builder,
Workflow Studio, external coaching software or a new editor package.

Aligning with the main synthesis, the **proposed initial profile** is one
organization question set containing **zero to five plain written prompts**, all
optional, plus freeform writing. New drafts capture the exact question wording
and order. Existing drafts/history do not change when staff edit the set. Private
autosave is separate from deliberate sharing. Each check-in has one current
shared revision and recipient set; a private correction is deliberately
published to all listed current recipients. Stop sharing remains independent of
an invalid or unsaved correction. The five-question ceiling is bounded product
judgment, not a vendor recommendation, accessibility requirement or measured
optimal length. Three example prompts would be illustrations, never automatic
organization configuration or an agency-wide method.

## Small complete journeys

### Staff: maintain guidance without acquiring response access

Use the existing organization settings context and exact management capability.
Title **Check-in questions**; explain **Offer optional questions to help people
prepare for coaching. They can skip questions or write their own update.** Show
the saved set, with an ordinary **Edit questions** action if authorized. Unknown
permission/loading is not an empty editable set. A read-only staff member must
not receive a misleading working Save control.

In the editor, use a short ordered list of plain written questions, **Add
question**, clearly named **Move up**, **Move down**, **Remove question**, and one
**Save questions**. Show **Up to 5 optional questions** beside Add under the
proposed bounded profile. Removing the final question is valid: **People can
still write a check-in in their own words.** Do not silently discard an extra
question or save a blank “question” that creates an unexplained empty input.
An unsaved removal can simply be cancelled/reverted; no modal for every row.

A compact preview uses the same question labels and optional markers Maya will
see, with no real answers. This is preview of guidance, not access to missionary
drafts. Explain the consequential rule once: **Changes apply to new check-ins.
Existing drafts and shared check-ins keep their original questions.** A source
version conflict preserves staff's still-authorized text and offers comparison
with the current set instead of overwriting another administrator's changes.

Do not add required flags, question types, conditional branching, answer scoring,
templates per coach, schedules, reminders, response completion dashboards or an
assignment system. Exact proposed limits and supported text should be qualified
against real use. No arbitrary difficulty rating or “recommended weekly effort”
belongs in this configuration.

### Maya: write now, share deliberately

From the existing permitted coaching context, **Write check-in** opens a focused
page. Show the actual organization/subject context without exposing restricted
locations. Mark **Draft — not shared with coaches**. Avoid **Only you can see
this** unless the actual owner access model proves that stronger promise.

With prompts configured, show **Questions from [organization] (optional)** and
**Answer any that help. You can leave the rest blank.** Each input still has its
own persistent visible question label and **(optional)**. Provide **Anything
else you'd like to share? (optional)** as ordinary freeform writing. Without
prompts, use a direct **What would you like to share?** textarea and explain that
sharing is optional. No error, disabled entry, organization-setup request, minimum
answer count or checkbox to enable every field is needed.

Use brief, neutral questions that invite useful context; illustrative examples
include what went well and what the missionary wants help thinking through.
Do not introduce a compulsory care assessment, religious compliance questionnaire
or imported private history. A short contextual reminder can explain the actual
sharing boundary without placing a legalistic warning above every answer.
Browser copy/paste remains usable; it does not confer permission to copy protected
CRM/member-care content into a new audience. No automated inference or new AI
content policing is implied.

The proposed private autosave should provide durable source-confirmed recovery.
**Saving draft…** becomes **Draft saved** only when the current revision is
accepted. A later edit immediately makes any earlier saved receipt visibly
outdated. Preserve permitted in-progress text through recoverable errors and
reconcile an ambiguous save through the original operation; do not claim device
memory guarantees survival of tab closure. Do not invent a protected offline
cache. A native page editor is preferable to trapping long writing in a tiny
modal. Preview/back must preserve selection and text rather than restart entry.

**Review check-in** presents the exact proposed shared content, original question
wording for answered prompts, freeform text and the named permitted audience.
Omit skipped questions from the recipient view rather than exposing a completion
score or a list of blanks. Keep **Edit** and the deliberate **Share check-in**
action obvious. Review is not publication; no additional generic “Are you sure?”
dialog is needed when this screen already explains the action and audience.
Sharing a completely empty check-in can be prevented with **Write an answer or
add your own context before sharing**, while saving/skipping remains valid. This
is a content-existence recommendation, not a requirement to answer an
organization question.

The review must be bounded by both Maya's rights and the actual recipient
projection. A named recipient is not a new collaborator/ACL shortcut. Do not
silently add a newly invited coach, share all past check-ins or apply D24's future
numeric-period continuation to this narrative. If current permission changes
before publication, explain the safe known result; do not claim an update shared
with everyone if only some recipients remain eligible. Reconcile/review the
actual eligible set according to the owner contract before consequential retry.

### Maya: correct or stop without a new workflow

The proposed simple model keeps one current shared revision for the listed
current recipient set. **Edit check-in** starts private draft changes while the
previous authorized shared revision remains clearly labelled. Autosave does not
replace what Jordan reads. Review identifies all currently eligible recipients;
**Update shared check-in** deliberately publishes the reviewed revision to that
set. Avoid recipient-specific answer branches or automatic publication on blur.
Retention of older evidence is not a promise of an end-user revision browser.

**Stop sharing** remains available independently of an invalid correction,
unsaved field or failed autosave. It acts on the currently accepted share, not an
unsaved candidate. Explain **This removes current coach access to this check-in**
where that exact effect is qualified. Do not say it deletes every copy, undoes
past reading or erases retention evidence. Stop/permission loss must fence a late
Share/update response from restoring disclosure. An unknown Stop result remains
unknown until reconciled; a lost network response is not success or failure proof.

### Jordan: read the actual shared context

Use the existing authorized missionary detail and a small **Check-ins** section.
Show admitted entries and a clear authored/shared date meaning; a date label must
not imply a calendar week or reporting deadline Maya did not choose. Open the
current permitted shared revision as readable question/answer blocks and freeform
paragraphs. Preserve line breaks and international text; distinguish authored
words from navigation and controls. No supporter drilldown, answer editing,
coach comments, acknowledgement task, response scores or follow-up assignments
follow from this module.

Missing sharing says **No check-ins shared with you** only where that absence is
itself authorized and established. It does not say Maya failed to check in or has
no drafts. Do not show unanswered prompts, draft counts, private edit timestamps,
unshared titles or stale snippets to make the page feel populated. Multiple
missionary/project contexts remain explicit; the portfolio does not combine
answers into a rank or engagement score. Independently shared numeric summaries
stay independent rather than being injected into the narrative preview.

substitute a reassuring sentence for a qualified save/share protocol.

## Mobile, accessibility and bounded complexity

The repository already supplies a native
[Textarea](../../../../../packages/ui/components/shadcn/textarea.tsx),
[Field primitives](../../../../../packages/ui/components/shadcn/field.tsx), and
[base-maia configuration](../../../../../packages/ui/components.json). Reuse
these inside the existing settings/coaching surfaces. Their presence is a
bounded static fact, not proof of a check-in service or accessible composed UI.

Use a one-column writing flow, persistent labels/help, logical headings, readable
paragraph width, visible keyboard focus and explicit names on repeated row
actions. Keep focus associated with the moved question after reordering and in
a predictable nearby control after removal. If drag is offered, retain click/tap
move controls and keyboard operation. Avoid sticky actions that obscure the
focused field or the last lines above the phone keyboard. Reflow non-excepted
content at equivalent 320 CSS pixels and test zoom/text enlargement. Evaluate
target size/spacing using SC2.5.8 with its exceptions; larger comfortable controls
are sensible, not a false universal AA measurement.

Input errors must name the field/problem in text, retain the answer and provide
a usable correction route. Blank optional answers are not errors. Associate
labels/help/errors programmatically; color alone and placeholders do not suffice.
Autosave/status feedback should be persistent enough to inspect and announced
without focus theft when it is a qualifying status message. Avoid announcing
every character or a rapid save loop. A toast alone is not a recovery location.

Support ordinary Unicode, multiline text, input-method composition, mobile
dictation, copy/paste, long translated questions and bidirectional text safely.
If text lengths are bounded, specify the counting rule consistently in client
and source validation. UTF-16 units, Unicode code points, grapheme clusters and
encoded bytes are different; do not label one as another. Test combining marks
and emoji, and preserve over-limit text for correction. Do not silently truncate
or force ASCII to simplify limits. Root's precise finite resource limits remain
owner qualification, not a usability study result.

The strongest simpler alternative is freeform-only with no staff configuration.
It removes a real administration/versioning cost but was not the selected product
choice. Preserve its strength through the zero-question path and the freeform
field. A general form builder adds many unneeded decisions; a one-question-per-
page wizard adds navigation for this small related task. Both would need evidence
of an actual problem before replacing the proposed simple page.

## Material concerns — eight dimensions, clauses and proofs

Likelihood is qualitative review judgment, not incident measurement. These
clauses are proposed for synthesis and owner qualification, not implemented or
ratified formal requirements. Each proof below is a test to perform, not a result.

### U01 — Optional questions become compulsory reporting

1. **Failure:** A required answer, blank-response badge, weekly deadline or score prevents freeform use or pressures participation.
2. **Importance:** It contradicts the chosen voluntary coaching purpose.
3. **Severity:** High.
4. **Likelihood:** Plausible when borrowing a survey/assignment engine.
5. **Evidence:** D8-C11; Q25 A; S01's configuration-dependent path and S02/S04's broader assignment model are counterexamples to import cautiously.
6. **Decision impact:** Keep A and preserve a complete zero-prompt/freeform path.
7. **Permanent fix:** Optional labels/validation, no mandatory cadence or reporting derivatives; empty authored draft can remain unshared.
8. **Exact clause UX25-C01:** “Every organization check-in prompt SHALL be optional. A sharing-eligible freeform check-in SHALL remain possible with no configured prompts or with every prompt skipped. Missing answers or absent shared check-ins SHALL NOT create a score, overdue state, compulsory duty or inferred performance.”

**Proof:** No template, all prompts skipped with freeform, one answer only, blank
draft, and a missionary with no shared entries. Confirm useful writing works and
no staff/coach projection reveals private completion status.

### U02 — Template changes rewrite an older answer's meaning

1. **Failure:** Reorder/remove/edit associates an existing answer with a different question or deletes authored text.
2. **Importance:** A coach may read words under a meaning Maya never approved.
3. **Severity:** High; potentially critical for sensitive disclosure.
4. **Likelihood:** Likely if answers reference only mutable list position.
5. **Evidence:** Q25 consequences; S03 distinguishes template changes from existing work.
6. **Decision impact:** Keep the short template capability with captured question identity/wording.
7. **Permanent fix:** Bind draft/shared revision to exact prompt wording/order; apply configuration changes only to new drafts.
8. **Exact clause UX25-C02:** “A new check-in draft SHALL capture the exact question identities, wording and order it uses. Later question-set edits, reordering or removal SHALL NOT rewrite existing drafts, shared revisions or retained authored history, nor remap answers by position.”

**Proof:** Two drafts straddle a template edit; reorder identical-looking prompts,
remove all prompts and edit an answered prompt. Original meanings and text remain;
new draft receives the new set. No coach edit permission appears.

### U03 — Autosave, review and sharing are confused

1. **Failure:** Draft typing becomes coach-readable, preview publishes, or a stale save receipt appears to cover newer text.
2. **Importance:** Consequential disclosure occurs without informed action.
3. **Severity:** Critical for unintended disclosure; high for lost writing.
4. **Likelihood:** Plausible with a generic shared-document editor.
5. **Evidence:** D8 declared audience; S04's save/submission distinction; S10 review guidance.
6. **Decision impact:** Use private autosave plus one deliberate publish action.
7. **Permanent fix:** Separate current draft and accepted shared revision; exact projection/audience review; source-confirmed receipts.
8. **Exact clause UX25-C03:** “Draft saves and previews SHALL NOT publish check-in content. Share and Update shared check-in SHALL deliberately admit only the reviewed revision to the exact qualified current recipient set. Save/share labels SHALL report the established state of the exact revision and SHALL NOT imply reading, notification or delivery.”

**Proof:** Type without sharing, preview/back, type again after save, switch device,
and edit an already shared entry. Coach continues to receive only admitted content;
both users can explain which version is visible.

### U04 — Prompt administration or numeric sharing expands narrative access

1. **Failure:** Staff see responses because they maintain questions; D24 continuation or new coach membership exposes old/new narratives automatically.
2. **Importance:** Configuration, relationship and content admission are different authority.
3. **Severity:** Critical.
4. **Likelihood:** Plausible with reused organization dashboards or broad grants.
5. **Evidence:** D8-C08–C12, Q25 scope, D24 numeric-only continuation.
6. **Decision impact:** Retain prompts without general response analytics or hidden data payloads.
7. **Permanent fix:** Exact current source projection for drafts, preview, shared body and metadata; no implicit history.
8. **Exact clause UX25-C04:** “Question-set management SHALL grant no check-in response, draft or response-metadata access. Numeric-summary sharing, coaching duration and new relationship membership SHALL NOT publish any written check-in or history. Protected source text SHALL NOT be copied, quoted or summarized to bypass its audience.”

**Proof:** Prompt-only staff inspect endpoints/search/snippets; new coach joins;
numeric periods roll over; hidden draft exists. None receives narrative content,
private metadata or history absent a qualified deliberate admission.

### U05 — Correction or stop has a misleading effect

1. **Failure:** Autosaved corrections publish immediately, recipients receive inconsistent branches, or invalid draft text blocks Stop.
2. **Importance:** Maya loses understandable control over a continuing disclosure.
3. **Severity:** High; critical if revoked disclosure resumes.
4. **Likelihood:** Plausible without separate draft and share operations.
5. **Evidence:** Main proposed one-current-revision model; D8 current rights; S03/S04 illustrate distinct work states, not Core stop guarantees.
6. **Decision impact:** Keep one current shared revision/set and independent Stop.
7. **Permanent fix:** Explicit update to listed current recipients; Stop operates on accepted scope and fences later stale changes.
8. **Exact clause UX25-C05:** “Private corrections SHALL leave the current authorized shared revision unchanged until deliberate qualified update. Stop sharing SHALL remain independently operable despite invalid or unsaved draft changes and SHALL prevent stale operations from restoring access. Its receipt SHALL describe current access removal without claiming to recall prior reading.”

**Proof:** Invalid correction then Stop, concurrent Share/Stop, recipient revocation
during review, stale tab update and lost Stop response. No late resurrection or
false success; no per-coach text branches are invented.

### U06 — Weak connectivity destroys text or asserts unknown truth

1. **Failure:** Timeout means “not shared,” duplicate publication follows blind retry, or refresh destroys unsaved writing.
2. **Importance:** Mobile users need reliable writing and honest disclosure status.
3. **Severity:** High.
4. **Likelihood:** Plausible; no frequency asserted.
5. **Evidence:** Source admission/revision qualification; S12/S13 concern communication, not network guarantees.
6. **Decision impact:** Keep autosave with explicit recovery rather than claim offline operation.
7. **Permanent fix:** Durable accepted revision, operation reconciliation, safe retained in-progress text and conflict handling under current rights.
8. **Exact clause UX25-C06:** “The UI SHALL distinguish pending, accepted, rejected and indeterminate save/share results. Recovery SHALL reconcile the original operation and preserve still-authorized text without blind duplicate publication or late overwrite. No offline completion or persistence claim SHALL exceed the qualified storage behavior.”

**Proof:** Lose request/response, reload after accepted save, conflict from a second
tab, revoke while typing and recover a late response. Inspect source and UI truth;
do not claim a kept browser buffer is durable after tab closure.

### U07 — Coach absence states reveal drafts or imply poor effort

1. **Failure:** Empty report displays unanswered prompts, private draft count, overdue state or a stale revoked preview.
2. **Importance:** It leaks preparation and turns voluntary context into monitoring.
3. **Severity:** High.
4. **Likelihood:** Plausible with generic submission dashboards.
5. **Evidence:** D8-C11/C12; S06's variable cadence is a service example, not policy.
6. **Decision impact:** Simple admitted-entry reading, no completion dashboard.
7. **Permanent fix:** Distinct loading/unavailable/authorized absence states; current content projection; no unshared existence inference.
8. **Exact clause UX25-C07:** “The coach view SHALL expose only currently admitted check-in content and eligible metadata. Missing, loading, unavailable and access-ended states SHALL remain distinct. It SHALL NOT enumerate drafts, skipped answers or unshared entries, or infer effort, health, compliance or zero activity.”

**Proof:** Same coach-visible state with no draft versus multiple private drafts;
timeout versus truly no shares; revoked entry cached in a list; multiple contexts.
No payload or accessible label discloses the hidden distinction.

### U08 — Inaccessible controls and arbitrary limits make writing burdensome

1. **Failure:** Drag-only ordering, disappearing labels, focus-hidden controls, chatty autosave or Unicode truncation defeats the simple flow.
2. **Importance:** The feature must work on phones and with assistive technology.
3. **Severity:** High for excluded users; medium for unnecessary friction.
4. **Likelihood:** Plausible without composed-UI testing.
5. **Evidence:** S07–S15 and inspected shared primitives; none proves the future UI passes.
6. **Decision impact:** Reuse simple components and qualify finite limits; avoid a new builder/wizard.
7. **Permanent fix:** Persistent associated labels/errors, non-drag reorder, retained input, deliberate status feedback and international mobile testing.
8. **Exact clause UX25-C08:** “Question editing, writing, review, sharing and stopping SHALL be operable by keyboard and applicable single-pointer input with meaningful labels, visible focus, readable reflow and textual errors. Qualified length/count limits SHALL be explained consistently and SHALL NOT silently truncate authored text or invalidate skipped optional answers.”

**Proof:** Keyboard and screen-reader full journeys; tap-only reorder; zoom/320 CSS
pixels; phone keyboard; translated long labels; Thai/Arabic/combining marks/emoji;

## Qualification still required

The main owner review must qualify source storage/admission, revision/current
recipient semantics, retention, role changes and privacy projection. This note
does not establish them from vendor docs. Finite resource bounds, autosave
recovery and plain-text rendering need actual implementation proof. Stopping
access does not automatically delete lawful history. No notification, reply,
export, attachment, automatic prior-answer fill or new coach-history expansion
is added to complete this job.

Before calling the experience excellent, evaluate realistic tasks with intended
staff, missionary and coach users, including mobile and assistive-technology
use. The critical qualitative questions are whether people can distinguish
optional guidance from obligation, draft from shared content, and private edits
from published updates; whether staff can edit/remove questions without rewriting
answers; and whether Stop works without completing a broken draft. Any study
sample is a proposed usability gate, not statistical proof of universal need,
privacy safety or effectiveness. This research supplies documented patterns and
falsifiable tests, not invented participants or results.

Only this research note was authored. No app, schema, formal specification,
dependency, tenant setting or external system was changed.
