# D25 evidence — optional questions and truthful written check-ins

Research date: **2026-10-17, Asia/Bangkok**. The founder selected A, optional
organization-provided questions for voluntary written coaching check-ins. The
bounded source/lifecycle design below is a review recommendation, not a separate
ratification, formal owner amendment or implemented service. Only this note is
authored; no app, schema, live data, dependency, ticket or provider operation changes.

## Smallest complete domain contract

Use one small organization-managed ordered set of optional written questions and
an ordinary native check-in composer that also works without questions. The
missionary may answer any helpful questions, skip the rest and add original context.
No required answer, score, branching form, custom field type, survey campaign,
assignment, submission deadline or workflow engine is necessary. The example
questions in Q25 are not automatically activated organization policy.

Recommend a bounded **P28 coaching source** for question configuration and newly
authored check-in content, drafts, accepted shared revisions and disclosure intent.
This must be explicitly bound in the later owner/specification work. P9 continues
to own genuine partnership encounters and the existing shared task source; P12
owns current purpose/audience/field/history authorization. P27 D26 is an exact
partnership-content exception, not permission to put a generic personal reflection
on an arbitrary donor Party. A check-in is not an encounter, CRM note, financial
record or coaching workflow. Later optional Studio automation must invoke the
same qualified source, not create another answer ledger.

The reserved `support-raising.checkin.submit@1` name in FORM-04 supports this
planned source boundary; it is **not proof that the command or retention profile
exists**. Ordinary native check-ins must not wait for the later form builder.

## Questions, identity and revisions

The source must be able to recover **which question, with which exact wording,
in which reviewed order, received this answer**. A stable question identity plus
an immutable question-set revision is sufficient conceptually; a separate general
schema/versioning framework is unnecessary. Do not key answers by question index,
display text or a mutable label. Do not recycle a removed question identity for an
unrelated question. The check-in's reference/snapshot must be tenant-consistent
and authoritative; arbitrary client-supplied IDs or text are not proof of a real
organization question revision.

At draft creation/use of questions, bind the exact offered revision. A normal
staff edit, reorder or removal affects future offers, **not** text already written,
the questions an existing draft is answering, or the meaning of an accepted answer.
There is no need to interrupt an ordinary valid draft whenever staff fixes a typo.
The writer can continue with its truthful captured questions; current content and
sharing restrictions still apply. New questions are not silently inserted into an
open draft, answers are not rebased by matching labels and existing text is not
discarded because a question disappeared from the current set.

Render a shared answer with its original exact question, in its accepted order.
Keep the necessary lawful question provenance when staff retires a set; ordinary
configuration removal must not cascade-delete responses. A later safety restriction
or lawful disposal follows the source's real content/access rules. It does not
authorize falling back to today's question text, retaining forbidden prose forever,
or silently describing an unavailable answer as unanswered.

Share only answered questions and original context in the initial reader view.
Do not publish unanswered fields, a completion percentage or “Maya skipped these
three questions.” The template itself may have an independently permitted reader,
but that is not response/completion metadata. If all answers and original context
are empty, there is nothing to share: keep writing optional and avoid creating an
empty “submitted weekly report” or a required-answer error. Questions being absent
or temporarily unavailable must not block independently authorized freeform entry;
an outage is not proof the organization configured no questions.

## Draft, sharing and correction lifecycle

1. **Write / save draft.** The source records the current authorized preparer,
   tenant and exact missionary/ministry coaching context. Draft save/autosave
   never shares. Show an accurate unshared state and the audience before disclosure;
   do not infer author-only custody from a generic `restricted` note flag. No new
   exceptional administrator reading right is proposed by this feature.
2. **Review and share this check-in.** One deliberate source command accepts the
   exact content revision, captured questions and current intended audience. Recheck
   the existing coach relationship/episode, source classification and actual authority
   at acceptance. Preview is additionally bounded by the preparer's own rights.
   A stale audience or content revision conflicts without sharing another version.
3. **Edit a shared check-in.** Keep the currently shared accepted revision distinct
   from a new unshared working revision. Saving edits does not expose unfinished
   wording. A deliberate **Update shared check-in** accepts the reviewed successor
   under current authority. A permitted reader sees the applicable shared version,
   not a draft or an implicitly complete edit-history browser. Older revision
   access and lawful retention remain independently governed.
4. **Stop/restrict sharing.** Use the existing source-owned disclosure boundary;
   ended or narrowed access applies to subsequent reads and recovered results.
   It does not claim to recall what someone already saw or erase lawful source
   history. Draft disposal, shared correction, access withdrawal and legal erasure
   are different actions; do not implement all of them as unrestricted DELETE.
5. **Recover interruption.** Accepted source revision, required evidence and durable
   result commit together. A lost Save/Share reply recovers the same intended result
   under current rights; it does not create another check-in, share a new draft or
   silently adopt new template/audience state. Late responses cannot overwrite newer
   writing or content from a different organization/context.

These are proposed minimal narrative semantics, not a standing grant for future
written content. D24's numeric same-target continuation is inapplicable. Each
check-in/revised shared content is deliberately disclosed within its actual source
profile. Exact older-check-in access, episode continuity and retention need their
own qualified scope; a new coach, invitation or staff role does not acquire earlier
answers automatically. No answer cadence, retention duration, recurring reporting
obligation or reminder schedule is selected here.

Current source restrictions always outrank saved draft/share evidence. Ordinary
question retirement is not automatically a response revocation; ordinary response
sharing is not automatically organization-wide reading. Different facts must not
be collapsed into one “template enabled” permission switch.

## Narrative eligibility and next actions

The new source is for **newly authored, sharing-eligible coaching context**. D8-C11
and the founder brief forbid relabeling, copying, quoting or summarizing protected
CRM/Support/member-care/private history into a coach answer, whether manually or
automatically. Do not prefill from earlier private notes, sent email bodies,
care records or prior check-ins merely because the missionary can read them.
Editing the missionary's own still-permitted draft/shared revision of this same
check-in is distinct from copying another protected record into a new audience.

This is not a prohibition on all ordinary newly authored personal context or a
promise that software can recognize every sensitive sentence. Source classification,
audience guidance, bounded review and current field/purpose policy must be real;
no AI sentiment classifier, care assessment or new surveillance pipeline is needed.
Do not introduce a “safe to share” checkbox as a substitute for source eligibility.
Organization questions cannot confer permission to disclose what the source forbids.
Use ordinary safe text rendering; no prompt can supply executable markup, authority,
dynamic source lookup or a request to fetch protected records.

“I'll call Grace on Friday” remains an attributed statement. It does not create a
task, assign it to Jordan, send a message, change a target or mark an encounter
complete. If Maya deliberately wants a task, use the existing independently
authorized shared-task command/composer with its normal audience and current source
rules. Creating/linking it is separate from Share check-in; checking/retiring it
does not rewrite the check-in. Do not silently copy a protected answer into a more
widely readable task title/body. A task reference reveals only what its own current
projection permits; the coaching check-in grant alone does not expose its history.
Reading an “agreed” sentence is not an electronic acknowledgment by the coach, whose
role remains read-only.

## Material concerns — exact clauses and falsifiable counterexamples

### CI25-01 — Template edits reinterpret answers

1. **Harm:** an answer is shown under a different question or lost when staff changes order.
2. **Severity:** high integrity and possible privacy harm.
3. **Likelihood:** high with array-index keys or a live current-template join.
4. **Cause:** mutable display text/order is used as question identity.
5. **Evidence:** S01 consequences; S08 version-dependent answers; source reasoning above.
6. **Effect on A:** minimal stable question identity plus exact captured wording/order revision.
7. **Clause:** “Every prompted answer SHALL bind its same-tenant stable question identity and exact wording/order revision. Reorder/edit/retire SHALL not relabel, remap or discard existing draft/shared answers. Ordinary retirement SHALL preserve lawful provenance without reusing the identity for another question.”
8. **Proof:** answer question A then reorder A/B; rename A to a materially different question; remove/re-add a question; repeated labels and cross-tenant ID collisions; old answer remains attached to the original meaning.

### CI25-02 — Prompt administrator becomes response reader

1. **Harm:** staff or a coach reads private drafts/answers merely by maintaining organization questions.
2. **Severity:** critical for protected narrative disclosure.
3. **Likelihood:** plausible with shared admin endpoints or generic question/response joins.
4. **Cause:** configuration authority is treated as content/audience authority.
5. **Evidence:** S01; S03 C09/C11; S05 current purpose and field scopes.
6. **Effect on A:** separate management, preparer and sharing-qualified response actions; no new role/ACL engine.
7. **Clause:** “Question create/edit/order/retire capability SHALL grant no response or draft read, export or coaching mutation. Each response projection SHALL independently prove its current source/purpose/audience/history rights. A staff or coach title, question authorship, group management or template reference SHALL not substitute.”
8. **Proof:** question editor reads its configuration but cannot enumerate answer existence/body/count; independent eligible coach can read the selected shared check-in without template-admin rights; current field/purpose revocation reaches caches and receipts.

### CI25-03 — Draft or ordinary edit publishes unfinished content

1. **Harm:** autosave reveals private preparation, or current readers see a half-written correction.
2. **Severity:** critical for disclosure; high for trust.
3. **Likelihood:** high with one mutable response body and a shared flag.
4. **Cause:** working content and accepted shared revision are the same live object.
5. **Evidence:** S03 deliberate audience/read-only; S05 draft capability; S01 lifecycle requirement.
6. **Effect on A:** one check-in identity with separate working and shared revision semantics, not separate copied products.
7. **Clause:** “Saving working content SHALL not publish it. Sharing and updating shared content SHALL deliberately accept the exact reviewed revision under current audience and source authority. A still-permitted accepted revision remains reader truth until a qualified successor or restriction; older revision access is not automatically granted.”
8. **Proof:** first autosave before Share; edit a shared response then autosave; old reader versus draft reader; share/save race; correction after coach revocation; the reader never sees working prose or an unreviewed successor.

### CI25-04 — Live configuration destroys or blocks valid preparation

1. **Harm:** ordinary staff changes delete answers, continuously block writing or make freeform unavailable.
2. **Severity:** high for data loss; medium for avoidable user burden.
3. **Likelihood:** plausible with mandatory current-template version or cascade delete.
4. **Cause:** configuration revision is coupled to draft validity and source content lifecycle.
5. **Evidence:** S01 consequences/no-template path; S08 immutable meaning; S04 correction/retention separation.
6. **Effect on A:** normal changes apply prospectively; existing truthful snapshot continues, current restrictions still govern.
7. **Clause:** “An ordinary question-set edit/retirement SHALL affect new offers without overwriting or silently rebasing existing preparation. Missing/unavailable optional questions SHALL not block independently authorized freeform writing. Safety restriction and lawful disposal SHALL follow their own source rules, not current-template substitution or unrestricted cascade deletion.”
8. **Proof:** staff fixes typo during typing, adds/removes questions, empties set, fetch fails, question is later genuinely restricted; draft text survives where permitted and unsafe text is not resurrected from a copy.

### CI25-05 — Voluntary prompts become a required questionnaire

1. **Harm:** skipped questions trigger penalties, mandatory next action, reminders or invented reporting duties.
2. **Severity:** high autonomy/product-scope harm.
3. **Likelihood:** high if FORM-04 required fields or MPD-04 automation are copied literally.
4. **Cause:** reference blueprint/configuration flexibility is mistaken for accepted native policy.
5. **Evidence:** S02/S03 voluntary terms; S07 disabled recipe; S08 inert required next_action.
6. **Effect on A:** only optional text guidance; no form engine or obligation/backlog.
7. **Clause:** “All offered questions SHALL remain optional. The missionary MAY skip every question and share only original context or share nothing. No completion percentage, skipped-question disclosure, compulsory next action, due/missed report or workflow dependency SHALL follow. Empty content SHALL not fabricate a submitted check-in.”
8. **Proof:** no configured questions, all skipped with freeform text, all empty, removed question, blueprint required flag and missing weekly report; normal work remains usable and no task/score/reminder appears.

### CI25-06 — A check-in launders private source history

1. **Harm:** CRM, care or personal-mail content is disclosed through a nominally new answer.
2. **Severity:** critical for protected narrative/third-party privacy.
3. **Likelihood:** plausible with autofill, “summarize my week,” reuse buttons or broad read-to-share assumptions.
4. **Cause:** readable content is treated as eligible for a new coaching purpose.
5. **Evidence:** S02:189; S03 C11; S04 P9 floor; S05 source classification.
6. **Effect on A:** newly authored eligible context, actual policy/audience guidance, no imported private-history route or AI dependency.
7. **Clause:** “Question text or response authorship SHALL not authorize copying, quoting, summarizing or reclassifying protected source content into coaching. Existing private notes, care, messages and other check-ins SHALL not be automatically prefilled. Newly authored sharing-eligible context and same-check-in authorized editing remain distinct from protected-history import.”
8. **Proof:** private note/mail/care selection cannot populate shared answers; a question asking for forbidden data grants nothing; new eligible reflection works; editing the same still-permitted check-in retains correct provenance; no claim of perfect detection of arbitrary private prose.

### CI25-07 — Numeric continuation or a new coach opens old/new writing

1. **Harm:** future drafts or earlier answers are exposed without deliberate narrative disclosure.
2. **Severity:** critical privacy harm.
3. **Likelihood:** plausible if all coaching modules share a broad episode or current-template flag.
4. **Cause:** D24 continuation, invitation or response retention is reused as written-content authority.
5. **Evidence:** S03 C08/C11; S10; S04 explicit historical-access rule.
6. **Effect on A:** each accepted check-in/revision and current audience are explicit; no automatic old/future narrative enrollment.
7. **Clause:** “Numeric target sharing SHALL not authorize written check-ins. The response source SHALL enforce the deliberately accepted content/audience scope and current history rights; new invitations, reassignment, re-enable, retained content or a question-set revision SHALL not add older/future responses or revive ended access.”
8. **Proof:** numeric sharing active but draft private; new weekly check-in; replace coach; same coach re-invited; staff becomes prompt editor; old grant restored from backup; no unauthorized body or existence leak.

### CI25-08 — Written next action becomes shadow task truth

1. **Harm:** text creates/assigns/finishes work, or copied prose widens task audience without review.
2. **Severity:** high for operational integrity and disclosure.
3. **Likelihood:** plausible from “agreed next action” blueprint language or automatic parsing.
4. **Cause:** narrative intention is treated as a structured source command or coach confirmation.
5. **Evidence:** S03 C09/C11; S04 R02/R10; S06; S07/S08.
6. **Effect on A:** narrative stays an attributed account; optional existing task commands remain separate and permission-qualified.
7. **Clause:** “A check-in answer SHALL not create, assign, complete or duplicate a task, establish coach approval or count an encounter. Any deliberate task creation/link SHALL use its existing source command/current audience. Check-in read does not grant task history, and task lifecycle does not rewrite narrative truth.”
8. **Proof:** ‘Call Grace Friday’ produces no task; explicit task command yields one independently owned task; coach read/Done attempts cannot mutate it; private answer is not silently copied to broader task fields; closing task leaves check-in unchanged.

### CI25-09 — Retry or concurrent publication changes what was shared

1. **Harm:** lost-response retry creates duplicate check-ins or shares changed wording/audience; stale callbacks overwrite a newer draft.
2. **Severity:** high integrity/privacy harm.
3. **Likelihood:** plausible during normal multi-tab and weak-network use.
4. **Cause:** transport identity replaces business intent; latest template/content is resolved again during retry.
5. **Evidence:** S04 R08/R09; S05 current authority; S06 durable source intent.
6. **Effect on A:** bounded source commands with expected revisions, atomic evidence/result and current-authority recovery; no workflow orchestrator required.
7. **Clause:** “Save/Share/Update SHALL bind the exact source/context, intended revision and current audience. Required local content/evidence/result acceptance SHALL be atomic. Replaying the same accepted intent SHALL recover it under current rights, not create another check-in or adopt latest text; changed intent conflicts. Late responses SHALL not replace newer or different-context preparation.”
8. **Proof:** simultaneous staff question edits and Share, missionary dual-tab edits, lost reply, same intent/different payload, revoke before/after acceptance, cross-tenant target spoof; no partial shared source or duplicate effect.

### CI25-10 — Historical meaning is kept by retaining every private body forever

1. **Harm:** old drafts/shared revisions leak through audit, backups, export or a supposedly deleted question set.
2. **Severity:** critical for unauthorized retained narrative; high for misleading disposal promises.
3. **Likelihood:** plausible with generic full-JSON audit or cascaded delete/restore.
4. **Cause:** provenance, body retention and current reader access are conflated.
5. **Evidence:** S03 C17/C18; S04 R14; S05 source/history floors; S08 retention owner reserved, not implemented proof.
6. **Effect on A:** qualified source retention/disposal and minimized audit; no new duration or archive entitlement.
7. **Clause:** “Question/answer provenance, retained bodies, revision access, withdrawal and lawful erasure SHALL have explicit source-owned semantics. Retirement SHALL not destroy lawful answers, and retained evidence SHALL not grant current reading. Ordinary audit/telemetry/notifications SHALL not duplicate private prose; restore SHALL reconcile current restrictions/disposal before release.”
8. **Proof:** ordinary template retirement versus actual erasure; response body expiry with permitted minimal provenance; old-revision endpoint/export denial; restore predating revocation; logs and notification payloads contain no hidden answer or discarded draft.
