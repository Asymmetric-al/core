## Scope and evidence quality

Inspected the current public DonorElf site and its published dashboard image;
Sola Features, FAQ and Onboarding; Epistle Tour, MPD, Security, Privacy Policy
and Giving Impact; TntConnect Task Types; MPDX coaching and mobile-task help;
and Mailchimp's bot/privacy explanations. No authenticated product account,
vendor demo workflow, delivery, private record, or mutation was exercised.
Screenshots demonstrate a published example, not functioning controls or
authorization. Marketing and tutorials do not establish independent outcomes.

The DonorElf HelpScout home and article URLs returned **404** to both web-open
and a normal HTTPS request during this pass. Search supplied indexed first-party
article text, generally labeled crawled two weeks earlier. Those are explicitly
**indexed historical documentation**, not successful current-page retrieval or
proof that the described application behavior still exists. The group article's
title was found, but its body was not obtained.

## Opening decision: a missionary's new prospecting work

**The inspected priority benchmarks do not settle who may see a new prospect
from the moment it is entered.** DonorElf advertises prospect/prayer contacts;
Sola describes personal notes and configurable oversight; Epistle describes
profiles and history. None of the inspected pages documents the exact initial
staff audience, private admission boundary, deduplication disclosure, promotion
rule, or access after a worker leaves. It would be an unsupported inference to
call any of them a model for either private-by-default or staff-visible-by-default
prospecting.

MPDX's coaching documentation supplies a narrower useful precedent: an invited
coach receives a documented view without editing or partner identities. It does
not establish prospect-entry privacy. Its Cru initial-MPD reporting obligations
are also agency-specific, not Asym policy.

The genuine product choice therefore remains: when a missionary records a new
personal contact for possible ministry partnership in the organization's native
CRM, does ordinary authorized staff visibility begin immediately, or only after
a deliberate sharing step? Follow-on choices include lawful exceptional access,
what sharing reveals, correction authority, safe duplicate reconciliation,
household/team collaboration, and departure continuity. Neither option requires
a second CRM or makes a relationship a permission grant. No answer is selected
here.

## Implications to carry into grooming

1. The strongest common precedent is understandable, contextual work: explain
   the source, its currentness, the responsible person and the useful permitted
   action. It is not evidence for universal late-gift thresholds or quotas.
2. Private prospect admission and staff sharing remain a genuine founder choice.
   Absence of vendor documentation is not evidence that either policy is safer
   or easier in practice. Test expectations with missionaries and authorized
   staff using synthetic scenarios after the intended policy is chosen.
3. Reuse of approved story content is distinct from audience eligibility and
   delivery. No competitor feature changes the Phase 25 request-only newsletter
   doorway or silently subscribes existing contacts.
4. Coaching authorization is independent of partner access, money access and
   shared interaction participation. Voluntary context must not become mandatory
   surveillance through reports or missing-log interpretations.
5. None of these sources qualifies Asym runtime behavior, pilot outcomes,
   performance budgets, source completeness or exact predecessor availability.
