## Why this is the next meaningful choice

The [founder brief](phase28-founder-brief.md), sections 7 and 9, already calls
for useful daily work and complete native newsletter preparation/sending.
Source-owned task completion and voluntary new shared partnership context are
inherited constraints, not new product votes. P27 D21 delegates ordinary
interface judgment. D1–D5 resolve the preceding contact questions without
settling newsletter subscription.

The brief explicitly reserves a native signup or preference-center amendment.
The visitor's job is materially different from the currently accepted request
handoff: joining a newsletter versus asking someone to arrange joining. This
changes a public journey, consent and ongoing preference ownership. It merits a
founder decision before investigating the dependent admission design.

Other genuine choices remain in the coverage frontier: deliberate approved
Update reuse versus prepared digests; external-coach assurance and modules; and
P14's expressly reserved, independently eligible mediated thank-you. They are
not silently rejected or approved by choosing this next question. Gift-task
existence, generic completion semantics and Goal/Support Plan independence are
already constrained and need no repeat product vote.

## Original founder question (now answered)

**Q6 — Should visitors be able to join a missionary's native newsletter
directly from their ministry page?**

Illustrative scenario, not an observed ministry workflow: Beth visits Maya's
page and wants Maya's newsletter. The existing accepted **Request newsletter**
journey passes along Beth's request; it does not subscribe her.

**A — Direct native signup (recommended).** For ministries using Asym's
qualified native newsletter, Beth can deliberately join that newsletter through
a clear signup flow, with the required consent and address checks. Routine
signup needs no missionary handoff. Preserve request-only behavior where the
newsletter is externally managed.

**B — Keep the request-only public doorway.** Beth asks Maya for the newsletter;
joining is completed separately through the appropriate authorized newsletter
process. This preserves the existing public contract and reduces new signup
scope, but leaves an extra handoff. The request itself still authorizes no
manual or automatic enrollment.

**Recommendation:** A. It completes the native newsletter journey already
requested and reduces routine handling. It requires a narrow, explicit owner
amendment; it is not a relabeling of Request received or proof that the change is
already available. No single/double opt-in default is selected by this question.

## Boundaries and dependent work in either option

Keep the public choice understandable: join the named newsletter or request it,
according to the actual qualified capability. Do not require two competing
buttons, a transport chooser, an account, donation or broader relationship as a
speculative convenience. Any necessary native proof must be truthful rather
than inherited from the deliberately unverified request contract.

Subscription, address proof, current suppression, recipient eligibility,
provider acceptance and inbox delivery remain distinct. Signup must not grant
CRM/supporter/finance/protected-reader access, enroll cultivation or coaching,
subscribe unrelated topics, alter donor identity or convert old requests into
consent. The exact minimum contact/consent representation belongs to its source
owners; no public Party creation or separate subscriber database is approved.

If A is selected, the subsequent adversarial review must resolve the explicit
P25/P23/P6/P17/P12/P28 owner amendments and applicable P3/P4/P22 seams before a
formal specification. It must cover exact purpose, address proof/admission,
withdrawal, duplicate interest, existing suppression, changed addresses,
restricted-worker disclosure, abuse, retries, expired links, user-visible
pending/accepted states, retention, migration and rollout. Missing producers
block activation of that capability; they do not authorize a parallel provider
or permission path. This is dependent qualification, not additional choices
silently ratified here.
