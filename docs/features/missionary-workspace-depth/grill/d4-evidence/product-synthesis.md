# D4 product synthesis: archive from active prospect preparation

Research date: 2026-10-16 UTC. Research-only input to the Phase 28 grill, not a
formal specification, source-owner amendment, ticket plan or implementation.
No application, GitHub, provider, live-data or supporter changes were made.

The parent relayed the founder's choice of Q4 **A — Set aside / archive from
active prospects**, with a full adversarial review and simple, clear UX. The
recorded Q4 concerns voluntary, still-unshared preparation, not the closure of
managed partnerships or accepted tasks. The session still showed Q4 pending
when inspected; this note treats the later relayed answer as the current choice.

## Verdict and exact candidate decision

**Accept with required amendments.** Archive is a useful, modest way to remove
unused preparation from the active list without requiring deletion. Keep its
meaning narrow: a working-list disposition, not canonical CRM lifecycle,
relationship status, permission, task closure or a permanent retention promise.

> **D4 — Archive unused prospect preparation.** A currently authorized
> missionary may deliberately take their still-unshared prospect preparation
> out of their active prospect list. The retained preparation remains findable
> through a clear archived view or equivalent authorized filter, and may be
> returned to active prospects while current source authority and retention
> permit. Archiving does not delete the preparation or promise that it is
> retained indefinitely.
>
> This changes only that preparation's participation in active prospect work.
> It does not mark the canonical person inactive, change another missionary's
> work, withdraw shared CRM facts, change contact restrictions or consent, stop
> or restart tasks/reminders, close or reopen a partnership/Request, create a
> conversation or giving event, notify staff, or publish private material.
> Any actual personal reminder or organizational obligation keeps its own
> source-owned status and authorized work path.
>
> Restoring means returning this retained preparation to the active list. It
> does not restore expired access, undo later corrections, revive completed or
> cancelled work, restart communication, or bypass retention/disposition.
> Current authority and source truth govern the operation and its result.
>
> Use a small, clear action and a findable archived view. Explain any existing
> open work that remains under its own rules without forcing cancellation or
> adding a cleanup ceremony. Separate contact availability to staff from the
> private preparation's active/archived state. D2 sharing remains a distinct,
> deliberately reviewed and source-qualified action.

This selects an archive/restore product outcome, not a physical archive table,
new service, custom ACL, recurring review task, timer, automatic departure rule,
generic Undo history or additional edit/sharing right. The exact current source
producer remains an owner qualification; D1's bounded preparation model is
still an engineering recommendation rather than implementation proof.

## Necessity, no-build and strongest simpler alternative

The chosen job is modest: “I am not working on this prospect now, but I may need
this preparation later.” Forcing deletion or keeping every entry in active work
would not satisfy it. There is no evidence that the user needs archive reasons,
categories, scheduled reactivation, staff review, bulk processing, automatic
inactivity detection or a separate archive administration screen.

The strongest simpler implementation is an existing qualified source disposition
plus the existing list/filter controls, **if they already mean exactly this**.
Use that instead of a new archive subsystem. A transient browser filter that
forgets which preparation the user set aside, or a source status with wider
canonical/contact/work effects, does not deliver the selected outcome. A saved
view can present the result; it does not by itself establish a particular
preparation's current disposition. Storage representation is engineering work.

No-build is appropriate for the archive service, cleanup workflow and global CRM
archive extensions. It is not appropriate for the selected durable user outcome
unless an equivalent qualified operation already supplies it. The inspected
legacy admin CRM archive is a not-available message, not evidence of such a
producer. Deletion remains a separate source-governed capability, not a second
mandatory step or a substitute for A.

## Material concerns with exact proposed clauses

Likelihood is conditional on the named shortcut, not a measured incident rate.
Category numbers match the founder's 22-category scheme. These crosscutting
findings feed the root's complete review; they are not independent runtime
qualification or a claim that every implementation concern has been tested.

### D4-PS01 — Preparation archive becomes global contact inactivity

**Categories:** 4–7, 10–15, 19. **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:** Plausible if
the existing Party/donor lifecycle status is reused by name alone.

**What/why:** Marking a person archived globally can suppress staff discovery,
other ministries, matching, valid communication or unrelated work. D4 expresses
one missionary's preparation-list choice, not a fact about the person.

**Evidence:** Q4 scope; P9-A10/§D Party lifecycle; D2 ordinary staff availability;
D3 independent-fact preservation.

**Answer impact:** Preserve A; reject the wider status substitution.

**Permanent fix:** Keep the disposition scoped to the exact preparation and
its qualified current actor/source. Reuse existing storage only if its semantics
and permissions match, not simply because it has an `archived` field.

**Exact clause:** “Archiving prospect preparation changes only that preparation's
active-list participation. It does not set canonical Party/donor lifecycle,
affect another worker's preparation, hide independent shared facts or change
source contact eligibility.”

### D4-PS02 — Personal reminders are silently cancelled or hidden

**Categories:** 4, 5, 9–14, 17–19, 21. **Severity:** High for lost promised work;
Medium for ordinary reminder confusion. **Likelihood:** Plausible if the task
query excludes all archived preparation or archive cascades into linked work.

**What/why:** A personal reminder can be a deliberate real task even when it is
not an organizational obligation. Its status must not change merely because the
prospect leaves the active list. Hiding it while leaving a timer running is also
misleading. Conversely, an unshared idea with no actual work must not gain a new
“review archive” task or compulsory follow-up obligation.

**Evidence:** Q4 explicitly excludes accepted-task closure; brief §7 real outcome
semantics; ADR-0183 source-owned work; P27-P05; scoped legacy task divergence.

**Answer impact:** Accept A. Preserve both personal work and accepted duties,
without claiming they have identical responsibility or permission rules.

**Permanent fix:** Keep each existing task/reminder's own source applicability,
status and authorized work path. Contextual UI may explain and link permitted
open work; it must not require cancellation or invent a cleanup workflow.

**Exact clause:** “Archiving or restoring preparation does not cancel, complete,
suppress, reschedule or restart any existing personal reminder or source-owned
obligation. Their current source rules continue independently. Explain known
authorized open work without compulsory cancellation, guessed reassignment or
a new archive-review task.”

### D4-PS03 — Restore becomes recovery of old rights or past business state

**Categories:** 4, 5, 7, 10–15, 20–21. **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:**
Plausible if Restore copies a snapshot or treats authorship as authority.

**What/why:** A previously valid author may lose access; a contact restriction
may change; work may finish while preparation is archived. Restoring an old
snapshot can resurrect cancelled sends/tasks, overwrite corrections or revive
revoked access. Denying every legitimate return by an old author is the opposite
unsupported policy.

**Evidence:** D3 current/history distinction; D1 no grant/effect revival; P27-P06
and Q10/Q11; ADR-0183 predecessor work is not reopened by projection controls.

**Answer impact:** Restore is selected only as active-list restoration subject
to current source and retention rules, not generic historical Undo.

**Permanent fix:** Return the same retained preparation to active view using its
current qualified revision/permissions. Preserve later source truths. If the
record or its readable body has lawfully ended, show the actual unavailable
outcome without fabricated reconstruction or a new record as silent fallback.

**Exact clause:** “Restore changes only the retained preparation's active-list
state under current source/history authority. It restores no expired grant,
deleted body, superseded assertion, ended work, consent or outward effect and
does not automatically create a replacement preparation.”

### D4-PS04 — Shared-contact availability gets coupled to archive state

**Categories:** 3–5, 8–13, 15, 19. **Severity:** High for disclosure or removal;
Medium for needless friction. **Likelihood:** Plausible if one global status
encodes both sharing and active-list participation.

**What/why:** Archive could silently Unshare, Share could unexpectedly restore
the preparation, or Restore could publish private notes. Forcing another founder
choice about every combination would obscure the already chosen distinct
meanings. Yet a broad “all archived contacts” capability is not authorized.

**Evidence:** D2 exact contact-only sharing and independent action; Q4 excludes
sharing and unrelated effects; D3 separates contact availability/private history.

**Answer impact:** A does not amend D2. Allowing a currently authorized D2 action
from an archived detail is a reasonable composition judgment, not a new reader
or permission. Its exact source path still needs qualification.

**Permanent fix:** Treat the two meanings independently. A deliberate source-
qualified Share keeps its ratified review and effects; archive/restore creates
no Share or Unshare. Do not automatically restore simply to run Share.

**Exact clause:** “Contact availability to staff and preparation active-list
state are separate. D4 does not grant or remove D2 authority. Any deliberately
invoked qualified Share retains its exact contact-only review and outcome;
sharing does not itself restore preparation, and archive/restore does not
share, unshare or notify.”

### D4-PS05 — Archived becomes undiscoverable, or discoverability leaks it

**Categories:** 2, 4–7, 9, 12, 14–16, 21. **Severity:** High for disclosure;
Medium–High for duplicate/lost work. **Likelihood:** Plausible if every query
uses `active only`, or an archive panel receives broader access.

**What/why:** An archive that cannot be found again fails the chosen reversible
outcome and encourages duplicate preparation. Excluding archived identities from
safe matching can create duplicate canonical people. Counting/listing hidden
archived records before permission checks leaks their existence.

**Evidence:** Q4 promises authorized find/reuse; P9 saved views/search and D2
qualified identity reuse; D3 source-safe matching and derivative protection.

**Answer impact:** Keep an obvious authorized archived view/filter and correct
identity handling. Do not force users to search a separate archive product.

**Permanent fix:** Apply the same current audience and source safety before
archive counts/list/detail/search. Safe duplicate resolution considers relevant
retained source records without exposing hidden matches; incomplete/unavailable
results are not “no archived records.”

**Exact clause:** “Provide an understandable, source-authorized path to find
retained archived preparation. Archive changes the active-list default, not
identity truth or privacy. Counts, searches, matching and details retain current
source authority and truthful unavailable states.”

### D4-PS06 — A small reversible action grows into a cleanup platform

**Categories:** 1–3, 8, 9, 16–19, 22. **Severity:** Medium. **Likelihood:**
Plausible if every conceivable cleanup use case is included in D4.

**What/why:** Archive reasons, scheduled return, automatic stale detection,
bulk approval, new status builders, owner dashboards and retention configuration
are not needed for the chosen job. They increase decisions, implementation and
support burden. A browser-only hide/filter, however, may merely simulate a
durable outcome and lose it on the next device.

**Evidence:** Q4's small reversible operation; founder simplicity instruction;
P27-Q09 existing shell/list controls; current archive-not-available evidence.

**Answer impact:** A warrants the smallest qualified persistent outcome, not a
new archive service or rejection as “just a filter.”

**Permanent fix:** Reuse an equivalent qualified state and existing views if
available. Otherwise add only the bounded source outcome and its necessary
read/write/recovery semantics at the existing owner. Storage remains engineering.

**Exact clause:** “Use the smallest existing qualified source state and list
controls that deliver durable archive and qualified restoration. D4 adds no
archive service, status builder, required reason, review queue, automatic stale
detection, scheduled reactivation or retention configuration.”

### D4-PS07 — Archive/restore resets time, retention or activity meaning

**Categories:** 4, 5, 10–15, 18. **Severity:** High for retention or false source
history; Medium for misleading freshness. **Likelihood:** Plausible if a shared
`updated_at` drives every business rule.

**What/why:** A lifecycle edit is not a personal exchange, verified contact
detail, donor response or proof that relationship work resumed. Updating a row
must not refresh these facts or extend retention by accident. “Archived” also
does not establish an entitlement to store private content forever.

**Evidence:** Brief §§7/9; D1/D3 retention boundaries; P27-Q10; source-controlled
business outcome requirements.

**Answer impact:** Accept A with truthful source event/history and qualified
retention; do not add a new period or automatic timer.

**Permanent fix:** Record the archive/restore fact as itself under its audience;
keep business occurrence/currentness and retention calculations source-owned.
Apply an archive-triggered retention effect only if its exact owner rule exists.

**Exact clause:** “Archive/restore is a preparation-list action, not an exchange,
contact verification, resumed obligation or giving event. It does not reset
source freshness or extend/bypass retention absent an explicit qualified owner
rule. Retained archive and its derivatives remain subject to lawful disposition.”

### D4-PS08 — Retry or Undo overwrites a later valid action

**Categories:** 2, 4, 5, 12–14, 20–21. **Severity:** High for an unintended source
effect; Medium for a list that lies. **Likelihood:** Plausible on poor networks,
multiple tabs or delayed responses.

**What/why:** A late Archive response can remove an entry already restored;
retrying an old operation can archive a later restored revision; an old Undo can
restore after a new deliberate archive or access loss. Treating every repeated
desired state as one lifelong operation can also collapse legitimate later cycles.

**Evidence:** P27-Q09/Q11; D1/D2 durable source operation/revision principles;
no D4 concurrency or application proof obtained.

**Answer impact:** Preserve the simple action. Correct recovery belongs in the
source/UI contract, not a generic Undo engine or repeated confirmations.

**Permanent fix:** Bind each intended transition to its exact scope/revision and
durable operation. Recover that accepted result under current authority; reject
stale opposing transitions instead of overwriting. Any Undo uses the ordinary
qualified restore path and current conditions, not a historical snapshot.

**Exact clause:** “Repeat delivery recovers the same intended archive/restore
transition; separate legitimate later transitions remain distinct. Stale retries,
late responses and optional Undo cannot overwrite newer source state or bypass
current authority. Unknown acceptance reconciles the original operation.”

### D4-PS09 — Simple-looking UI fails the actual reversible journey

**Categories:** 1, 4, 5, 9, 12, 21. **Severity:** Medium. **Likelihood:**
Unmeasured; plausible until users complete the real journey.

**What/why:** An unlabeled box icon, disappearing card, inaccessible archive
filter or vague “restored” toast can confuse archiving with deleting a person or
reopening work. Too many confirmation/reason screens defeat ordinary reversible
cleanup. A beautiful isolated component is not the same as a usable phone flow.

**Evidence:** Founder clarity/simplicity instruction; Q4 actual consequence;
P27-Q09 composed WCAG 2.2 AA and recoverable input/list position.

**Answer impact:** Keep A. The minimal journey below is a design recommendation
to validate, not a claim of measured adoption or an additional product right.

**Permanent fix:** Use clear text in the existing design system, one ordinary
action, truthful pending/result state, stable focus/list position and a findable
archived filter with a clear restore action. Explain remaining work contextually.

**Exact clause:** “Use clear labels for removal from and restoration to active
prospects, with an accessible archived view and honest pending/error/unknown
states. Do not require a reason, routine confirmation or drag/icon-only control.
Validate the whole phone, keyboard and recovery journey with representative users.”

### D4-PS10 — A demo archive or broad migration passes for qualification

**Categories:** 6, 7, 12, 14–21. **Severity:** High for access or migration loss;
Medium for capacity/operations. **Likelihood:** Plausible if legacy statuses or
prior synthetic proofs are treated as equivalent.

**What/why:** Unknown legacy donor status is not evidence of this preparation
disposition. Unbounded archive listings, copied private history, direct SQL
repair or incompatible older clients can defeat a small correct UI. Existing
probes do not prove this source or its actual task/share integration.

**Evidence:** Scoped workspace/current-runtime gaps; P27-Q10/Q11/Q12; session
qualification limits. No measured performance or retained-data census in this pass.

**Answer impact:** A requires actual producer/readers/migration proof; no blanket
later-phase dependency or new operations dashboard is justified.

**Permanent fix:** Inventory reached records/writers, preserve source IDs and
history, qualify exact transitions/readers and bounded workloads, fence old
writers and use safe source receipts for recovery. Do not reinterpret global
inactive/archived CRM status as D4 without proved provenance.

**Exact clause:** “Qualify the exact archive/restore source and reached API,
authorization, tasks, sharing, search, migration and UI paths before activation.
Map retained records only from proved provenance, preserve current restrictions
and effect identity, and establish capacity budgets from measured workloads.”

## Small recommended journey

These are engineering/design judgments within the selected product meaning;
none is a new access or retention choice.

1. In the existing prospect-preparation list/detail, offer a plainly labeled
   **Remove from active prospects** or **Archive** action within the clearly
   scoped preparation context. Use the existing shared base-maia controls; one
   deliberate action, no mandatory reason or repeated confirmation.
2. If currently authorized open work is known, show a short contextual statement
   such as **“Your open reminder will stay active.”** Provide the existing
   qualified task link where useful. Do not invent a zero count, expose hidden
   work, block archive merely to force cancellation, or build a cleanup wizard.
3. After accepted archive, remove this preparation from the active list, retain
   position/focus and announce **“Moved to Archived.”** An optional immediate
   **Undo** may use the same current-authority restore command; a permanent
   archived view remains the recovery route when that transient control is gone.
   Unknown acceptance stays unknown until reconciled; it is not fake success.
4. Offer **Active / Archived** using existing filter conventions. Archived
   details keep their correct private audience, current contact facts and any
   independent source-linked work. The two categories need no new full screen
   or visible lifecycle-management settings.
5. **Restore to active prospects** returns the same permitted preparation to
   active view. A current restriction, disposition or revision conflict produces
   a clear scoped outcome. Restore does not replay another domain's effects.

If D2 Share is currently qualified from archived detail, keep that explicit
action and its accepted review/notification semantics. It need not automatically
restore preparation or require a redundant restore-then-share ritual. If an
exact source precondition truly prevents that composition, explain the actual
limitation and repair it within the selected source contract; do not silently
broaden rights, invent a product prohibition or ask the founder to design states.

## Required proof and limits

- A current authorized user can archive, find and restore the same preparation;
  another tenant/staff member gains no new list/count/detail access.
- Canonical identity, other workers, staff contact availability, private notes,
  restrictions/consent, finance and independent source history remain correct.
- Existing personal reminders and accepted duties keep their source status and
  authorized work paths; no new archive review task, cancellation or restart.
- Archive/restore and deliberate Share produce only their intended effects,
  including when concurrent; no share-to-restore or restore-to-share side effect.
- Stale revisions, repeat operations, unknown responses, multiple tabs, optional
  Undo and access loss cannot overwrite newer truth or revive old permissions.
- Safe search/matching includes relevant retained records without existence
  leaks; an archived view is not a new broad CRM lookup permission.
- Lawful disposition, restore/import, migration and compatible disablement keep
  their exact owner rules. No claim that all bodies are forever recoverable.
- Representative users understand the archive/work/sharing distinctions and can
  complete the accessible phone/desktop failure journeys. Bounded query and
  network budgets come from measured workloads, not invented targets.

These are activation proofs, not unresolved safety gaps assigned to monitoring.
After qualification, an unauthorized disclosure, changed independent record,
silently ended/restarted task or duplicate transition is an actionable source
regression. This note prescribes no new monitoring product, fixed retention
duration or unmeasured performance threshold.

## Remaining genuine choice

No extra founder fork is needed to decide whether Archive cancels tasks, Restore
revives permissions, or Share changes list state: the selected narrow meanings
and inherited owners already exclude those effects. Do not extend the privacy
branch by asking the founder to choose schema fields or every command ordering.

Exact retained-content disposition and historical return eligibility still need
their owner qualification; ask only if that work identifies a genuine missing
product policy. Do not invent a number of months merely to close a register row.

The strongest next independently meaningful frontier is **correction of contact
facts after D2 sharing**, explicitly required by founder brief §7. A useful
situation is a missionary learning that the shared phone/email is wrong: should
their permitted correction update the organization contact directly, or should
it remain a proposal for the responsible staff to resolve? First inspect the
exact P9/P12 contact-purpose authority so the question does not reask an already
settled role capability or grant broad CRM editing by implication. Recommend
direct simple correction only within a proved existing bounded source authority;
any new correction right is material and must be explicitly selected. This note
does not ratify either path or require a proposal/approval subsystem.
