# Q13 frontier: first-release regional discovery

Research date: October 16, 2026 UTC. **Answered B with tenant control; direction
recorded.** The founder selected regional browsing plus a world map, with the
tenant able to turn the feature on/off, emphasizing clear simple UX. The
[D13 decision and design](phase28-d13-regional-map-decision.md) records this choice
and reviewed recommendations; detailed design is not separately ratified. D12
resource publication remains a separate selected scope.

## The original product choice (now answered)

**How much regional discovery should the first release include?**

- **A — Simple region browsing (recommended).** Missionaries can browse the
  organization's approved public ministry listings by broad region in a clear
  list, without an interactive map initially.
- **B — Region browsing plus a world map.** Add a map of the same approved,
  generalized public information, while retaining an equally useful list.
- **C — No dedicated regional discovery initially.** Keep approved public-directory
  and ministry-page entry points; add no new region-browsing or map experience here.

The original recommendation was A as a bounded way to satisfy the inherited regional-discovery
direction with a readable, phone-friendly, low-bandwidth experience. B makes the
geographic overview more visual but requires additional safety, accessible-map
and rendering qualification. C is the simplest initial scope and must be recorded
as deliberate narrowing of the roadmap, not accidental omission. This is product
judgment, not measured missionary demand or proof that maps perform worse.

## Governing evidence and alternatives

- [Founder brief](phase28-founder-brief.md:235) requires qualified resource and
  public-page/Ministry Update entry points, while explicitly leaving first-release
  library/region scope open. Geography needs independent approval, generalized
  disclosure and an accessible list; sensitive coordinates must not be sent to
  third-party maps.
- [P28 field resources](../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/roadmap.md:4181) retains
  regional/world-map entry-point direction only through qualified projections;
  [open decisions](../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/roadmap.md#phase-28-open-decisions)
  expressly leaves precise scope to this session. The strongest no-build alternative
  is C, using qualified directory/page entry points without new regional UI.
- [P22 D13](../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-22-public-ministry-pages.md:524)
  owns discovery from currently Listed public releases under an exact Tenant,
  Legal Entity, environment, Site and locale. Its safe aliases and generalized
  geography do not expose hidden entries, counts, coordinates or inferred
  sensitivity. Do not enumerate internal Ministry Assignments to populate a view.
- [P22 D20](../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-22-public-ministry-pages.md:624)
  and [out-of-scope boundary](../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-22-public-ministry-pages.md:872)
  exclude maps from that launch catalog. B is not inherited P22 map functionality;
  it requires an explicit bounded P28 geography/map consumer contract reconciled
  with P22/P10/P12. The existing exclusion does not categorically prohibit a later
  independently qualified P28 map. A must also qualify its exact region grouping
  and source projection, rather than infer a new filter from a display label.

All options preserve the exact public scope and current release/safety rules.
Missionary login does not reveal private workers or locations, Shared-by-link
entries do not become discoverable, and no cross-Site/locale fallback or hidden
count follows. Public information elsewhere is not automatic admission here.
Neither A nor B is a live location, nearby-people, distance or travel-tracking
feature. Broad geography must not imply an exact residence or deployment.

No provider, dependency, coordinate pipeline, private directory or automatic
location capture is selected by this question. B needs a safe complete map/list
journey, not a new map dependency imported from the outdated attachment. Exact
source implementation and runtime qualification remain later work under every
option; no existing directory deployment is claimed.

This is one scope question. Do not bundle region taxonomy, maps provider,
geolocation permissions, notification preferences or connection choices into it.
The evidence is scoped repository inspection and independent owner review; no
new geography data, map experiment, field-user interview or performance result
was obtained. Those facts cannot be substituted with plausible ministry stories.

The founder subsequently selected B and added tenant On/Off control. D13 supersedes
the list-only recommendation, requires a functioning map plus useful list, and
records a single combined workspace control with current public-source safety.
The next independent question is calendar connection scope; no answer is inferred.
