## The single product choice

**Should every newsletter require the missionary's deliberate decision to send,
or should Phase 28 also offer automatic update digests?**

- **A — Missionary decides for each newsletter (recommended).** The missionary
  prepares the newsletter, reviews its content and recipients, and deliberately
  authorizes sending that newsletter. Publishing a Ministry Update does not by
  itself authorize a newsletter send.
- **B — Also offer optional automatic digests.** A missionary can deliberately
  enable automatic emails combining eligible Ministry Updates, with delivery
  at each recipient's chosen frequency, without reviewing each resulting email.
  Ordinary manually prepared newsletters remain available.

These are mutually exclusive first-release scope choices, not competing models
that could never coexist. B adds standing, bounded automatic-send authority;
it does not replace ordinary newsletter writing. Both preserve applicable
organization permissions, recipient consent and source safety. Neither adds
another sending provider, subscription authority or optional-sync dependency.

**Recommendation: A.** It delivers the already-requested native newsletter job
with a visible decision about each issue's content and audience. B's real
advantage is reduced recurring effort and reader-controlled frequency. That
advantage has not yet been established as necessary for this release by actual
ministry feedback. Choosing B would be a valid scope decision requiring explicit
digest/cadence contracts; it is not rejected as inherently unsafe or impossible.

Content reuse, preview conveniences and one-off scheduling do not decide this
fork. Q7 does not silently ratify them. An automatically prepared draft that
still needs an explicit send decision fits A's authority model, but automatic
draft generation is an independent composition proposal, not included merely
by choosing A. This preserves the useful middle alternative without adding
another bundled decision or asking the founder about engineering details.

## Evidence and boundaries

- **Explicitly open scope:** the [founder brief](phase28-founder-brief.md),
  “Ministry Updates and low-effort communication,” calls short-update reuse,
  digest preparation and recipient cadence proposals requiring decisions. The
  [D6 review](phase28-d6-adversarial-review.md) does not authorize automatic
  newsletter sends, digests or cadence through public signup. The
  [old-document reconciliation](phase28-legacy-dashboard-context.md) reinforces
  the interest in low-effort communication without resolving these questions.
- **Real comparison:** [Epistle Tour](https://www.epistle.org/tour/), re-read
  October 16, 2026, documents combining updates into emails and recipient-selected
  daily, weekly or monthly delivery. This is vendor documentation, not a Core
  implementation, independently measured user benefit or a commitment to those
  same frequency choices in Asym.
- **Disclosure caution:** [Epistle Security](https://www.epistle.org/security/),
  re-read on the same date, distinguishes restricted posts from emails containing
  only a notification. That distinction is relevant; its other security claims
  are not adopted or independently verified here.
- **Content owner:** [P22 D11](../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-22-public-ministry-pages.md)
  retains one canonical Ministry Update and independently admitted audience
  releases. A public/protected-reader approval does not automatically authorize
  every newsletter recipient, body, image, title, link or attachment. Reuse
  needs exact eligible content and separate newsletter preparation authority.
- **Existing preferences:** [P25 EX04](../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-25-donor-dashboard-depth/contracts/experience.md)
  separates Show in my Updates from Email me about new posts. Neither Q7 option
  disables those existing choices, treats them as native newsletter consent or
  silently changes their behavior.
- **Preparation and sending owners:** [P17](../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-17-system-messages-template-management.md)
  keeps human-authored newsletters outside the fixed system-message catalog;
  [P6](../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-06-shared-communication-event-model.md)
  retains qualified preparation, recipient eligibility, dispatch and delivery
  evidence. Existing source ownership and proposed D6 native membership scope
  remain distinct; this question creates no second pipeline.
- **No invented duplicate rule:** retry deduplication does not determine whether
  a post notification and a separately authorized newsletter should both exist.
  Their overlap, including recipient expectations, needs an explicit product
  rule in the relevant design rather than silently suppressing either purpose.

## What follows from the answer

If A is selected, review exact per-newsletter authorization and the preparation
journey. If B is selected, additionally settle deliberate activation, permitted
content, recipient cadence, catch-up/backlog, pause/withdrawal, configuration
changes and overlap with existing notifications. Source safety and current
recipient eligibility are required in either case; they are not optional
questions for the founder.

No new glossary term, ADR, specification, ticket, code or dependency is created.
This is scoped pre-question research, not a post-answer adversarial review or
production qualification. No real messages were sent and no current user study
or runtime experiment is claimed.
