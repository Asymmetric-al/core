# D10 — Earlier-period authority, range evolution and privacy

**Subsequent status — October 16, 2026 UTC:** the founder explicitly ratified
the [D10 corrected decision and amendment](../phase28-d10-adversarial-review.md),
including C01–C20/P01–P28. The research below preserves its pre-ratification
reasoning and candidate language; only the reconciled main review defines the
ratified amendment. No runtime or source qualification is implied.

Research only, October 16, 2026 UTC. The founder selected Q10 A: allow selected
earlier support-summary periods, within organization limits and with dates shown
before saving. D8/D9 and their amendments are ratified. This document supplies
D10 review recommendations; it does not ratify them, amend formal owners,
implement access, select an expiry or activate historical disclosure.

## Recommendation

**Accept with required amendments.** Add a bounded historical-period selection
to the existing D8/D9 sharing purpose. At the authorized save, capture the exact
finite earlier periods being shared. Keep those period bounds fixed until an
authorized person deliberately changes the selection. Continue to re-evaluate
the financial values and current disclosure rules for those periods through the
existing owners.

This freezes **which dates are authorized**, not a copy of money, the then-current
privacy rules, the source's known facts or the duration of access. An admitted
late gift or corrected amount may change a currently authorized earlier-period
summary; a new import date cannot bring an outside-period gift into scope.

Treat this as a recommended concrete amendment consistent with “selected earlier
periods,” not a range-evolution policy already chosen by the founder. D10 does
not select a standing fixed-start accumulation, rolling lookback, automatic
retention of every period once viewed, a number of months, or indefinite access.

The historical selection and any separately qualified current-period behavior
remain separate. If a current period later becomes a past period, that fact alone
must not add it to the selected historical range. Conversely, a fixed historical
range does not disable an independently authorized current-period module. The
current-period rule itself must be explicitly qualified; neither D9's example
month nor a source cache's retention length supplies that authority.

## Range-evolution alternatives

| Alternative                         | What the user would authorize                                                                                                      | Assessment                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Captured finite earlier periods** | The displayed exact earlier dates, attached to this share/profile. No scheduled permission job is necessary.              |
| Fixed start, accumulating end       | Everything from a selected date through successive new reporting periods, continuously growing while access lasts.                 | Potentially useful, but adds standing future historical disclosure. | Rolling lookback                    | A window that moves forward, removes old dates and adds new ones automatically.                                                    | Adds both ongoing expansion and narrowing, plus a window length and temporal semantics. Do not turn this comparison into three new founder questions inside D10. The
review can recommend the bounded first option explicitly, expose its concrete
consequence and leave any future recurring-range product unselected. A routine
authorized extension can use the same simple Edit sharing → review dates → Save
flow. No new approval queue or report builder is needed.

The UI can present a small set of source-qualified period choices or a simple
bounded range whose endpoints are valid source periods. This research does not
select months rather than another owner-qualified grain, an arbitrary maximum
lookback, a discontiguous-period editor or a new date library. Persisted authority
must resolve to exact finite boundaries in the source's date/calendar model;
copying a relative label such as “last few periods” is insufficient if it changes
meaning after save.

## Three meanings the implementation must keep separate

1. **Captured sharing scope:** the exact historical periods, subject, modules,
   profile, currency/entity scope and source sharing episode deliberately admitted.
2. **Current effective disclosure:** that captured scope intersected with current
   principal/context, live source authority, current organizational/security
   floors and qualified source availability/privacy. It may be narrower or absent.
3. **A query or displayed page:** a requested subset of effective scope. A URL,
   date picker, chart viewport or saved filter neither creates nor changes a grant.

User narrowing updates the authoritative sharing scope. The old broader version
cannot be replayed by Back, a lost-response retry, policy re-enable, restore or a
stale cache. A later deliberate re-expansion is a new currently authorized source
change; if the episode ended, it needs the proper new activation rather than
mutation of a terminal old record. Preserve otherwise valid independent grants.

A temporary source outage or a withheld result does **not itself** erase or end
the source sharing scope. A later permitted result may return within that still-
valid captured scope after current reproof. This is different from lifting a
fence on a revoked/expired/ended grant or a D8-disabled sharing episode, which
requires the explicit owner transition. The system must never use a successful
refresh as evidence that a terminal or safety-fenced basis became valid again.

## Proposed corrected decision clause

> A missionary with current source authority may deliberately include selected,
> organization-permitted earlier support-summary periods in an existing authorized
> coaching share. Review the exact dates, subject and available summary measures
> before one Save. The recommended implementation records a finite historical
> selection whose date bounds do not move with the calendar or silently accumulate
> additional periods. Adding or restoring earlier-period access requires a new
> deliberate authorized sharing change; current restrictions may narrow what can
> be shown at any time.
>
> These bounds describe the financial periods the coach may view, not a frozen
> copy of the figures or the lifespan of the grant. Within the selected dates,
> source-qualified corrections and newly known facts may update the summaries
> under current authority and privacy checks. Source gift/expectation dates,
> not creation/import/correction timestamps, determine the represented period.
> The selection gives no raw donor/gift/commitment access, historical check-ins,
> conversations, task history, old goal versions, exports or new writing rights.
> Existing separately qualified current-period access remains independent; a
> current period does not automatically become authorized browsable history.
> No rolling range, default lookback, access duration or universal privacy
> threshold is selected by this answer.

## Material concern register

Likelihood is an engineering judgment conditional on the described design
shortcut, not an observed incident rate. These are qualification blockers for
the affected historical-summary path.

### H01 — A date filter is mistaken for a historical grant

- **What:** The UI sends start/end dates and the service treats the query as
  authorization, or a single `can_view_history` flag exposes all retained periods.
- **Why it matters:** A coach can widen history through a URL, client request or
  another subject's filter without a missionary-authorized sharing change.
- **Severity:** Critical. **Likelihood:** High with generic reporting APIs.
- **Evidence:** S01–S03, S08, S10–S12.
- **Answer impact:** Preserve optional history, but make the approved range a
  source fact independent of query input.
- **Permanent fix:** Capture finite scope with exact subject/profile and verify
  requested periods against current effective scope at every protected boundary.
- **Exact proposed clause — HC01:** “Historical-summary authority SHALL be
  recorded as exact finite source-period bounds and admitted subject/module/
  profile scope in the existing coaching share. Query dates, saved filters,
  chart viewports, role labels and retained data SHALL NOT authorize or widen
  it. Every read and aggregate applies current exact scope before enumeration
  and arithmetic; outside-scope requests disclose no protected result.”

### H02 — Selected history silently grows or rolls forward

- **What:** Saving a relative label causes next month's old/current periods to
  join the historical view, or a fixed start implies an open-ended upper bound.
- **Why it matters:** A one-time historical selection becomes standing future
  disclosure that the eligibility answer did not select.
- **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:** High with convenient relative-date helpers.
- **Evidence:** S01:83–89,102–104; S02:480–485; S03:440–444.
- **Answer impact:** Recommend a captured finite selection as an explicit D10
  amendment; do not pretend recurrence/duration was already authorized.
- **Permanent fix:** Resolve selected source periods at save. Keep current-period
  authority and its eventual movement separate from historical grant scope.
- **Exact proposed clause — HC02:** “The recommended D10 historical selection
  SHALL retain its saved finite date bounds until a new deliberate authorized
  change. Clock movement, a newly completed period or a source cache rebuild
  SHALL NOT add historical periods. An independently qualified current-period
  view remains separate; fixed-start accumulation and rolling lookback require
  their own explicit product/source qualification and are not inferred here.”

### H03 — Fixed date bounds are mistaken for frozen financial facts

- **What:** A source correction is ignored because the original number was
  shared, or an imported old gift is treated as current because it arrived today.
- **Why it matters:** The first preserves false money; the second bypasses the
  historical boundary. Today's commitments cannot reconstruct past plans safely.
- **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:** High with cached or created-at reporting.
- **Evidence:** S01:72–82; S03:455–479,556–566; S13–S14.
- **Answer impact:** Current truthful values for selected earlier periods are
  allowed only through the qualified source metric, not a new archival ledger.
- **Permanent fix:** Separate represented period from source evaluation cursor,
  retention and observed-at time. Use source-corrected dates and historical metrics.
- **Exact proposed clause — HC03:** “Fixed historical scope SHALL not freeze
  amounts, input membership or disclosure policy. The qualified source may update
  a selected period with current effective corrections and late facts, subject
  to current admission/privacy. P7 gift dates and P16's qualified expectation
  periods govern inclusion; import, creation, refresh or correction times are
  not substitutes. Outside-period facts remain outside, and unavailable historical
  expectation evidence SHALL not be reconstructed from today's terms or goals.”

### H04 — Earlier summaries become raw or unrelated historical access

- **What:** A producer requires raw gift/commitment access, or the history
  selection also exposes old conversations, private notes, tasks or goals.
- **Why it matters:** D10 concerns only represented financial summary periods;
  those other sources have different audiences, fields and historical grants.
- **Severity:** Critical. **Likelihood:** Medium–High if a whole detail view is reused.
- **Evidence:** S01:39–42; S03:493–517; S04, S07–S08, S11, S15.
- **Answer impact:** Keep the selected summary-only purpose and all earlier D8/D9
  boundaries; no extra historical modules by association.
- **Permanent fix:** Exact source input admission for the history-summary purpose,
  classified derived output and independent authority for every other module.
- **Exact proposed clause — HC04:** “The trusted producer SHALL admit inputs
  for the exact historical-summary purpose before arithmetic, then independently
  admit the coach's derived output under P3/P10/P12. This neither requires nor
  creates raw donor/gift/commitment access. Earlier check-ins, interactions,
  private/care context, task history, old goal versions and exports retain their
  own authority and SHALL NOT follow from D10.”

### H05 — Saved old authority bypasses current restrictions

- **What:** A historical grant or materialized series preserves a former tenant,
  entity, participant, identity, audience or safety state at read time.
- **Why it matters:** “They could see it then” is not authority to disclose it
  now, and a successor ministry or new account is not the old subject/principal.
- **Severity:** Critical. **Likelihood:** High for precomputed history snapshots.
- **Evidence:** S02, S08–S12, S15.
- **Answer impact:** History eligibility cannot revive a past identity or grant.
- **Permanent fix:** Apply current context/source/floor and exact identity bindings
  to every historical read, release, preview and recovered result.
- **Exact proposed clause — HC05:** “Historical dates SHALL always be read
  under current exact principal, Active Tenant Assignment, Tenant, entity,
  subject, purpose, profile, fields and governance/source state. Captured history
  limits are a ceiling, not an authorization snapshot. Merge, relink, rebinding,
  rejoining, former membership or a formerly permitted cache SHALL not transfer
  or resurrect access.”

### H06 — Narrowing, temporary withholding and terminal revocation collapse

- **What:** A narrow save is undone by an old retry, or an outage permanently
  destroys selection; alternatively, a later successful refresh revives revoked
  history because the source now has data again.
- **Why it matters:** One behavior leaks previously removed periods; the other
  creates unnecessary repeated configuration and false ended-access claims.
- **Severity:** Critical. **Likelihood:** High in multi-tab/retry recovery.
- **Evidence:** S02:443–477; S03:548–566; S10:1586–1587; S11:2409–2413.
- **Answer impact:** Preserve clear reversible editing under current authority
  without replaying superseded intent or inventing permanent outage effects.
- **Permanent fix:** Versioned source scope, atomic evidence and idempotency;
  retain distinct captured scope, live effective availability and terminal fences.
- **Exact proposed clause — HC06:** “An accepted narrowing SHALL replace the
  effective historical selection and fence removed-period results. Old Save,
  retry, restore or policy-enable intent cannot restore it; later expansion needs
  a new deliberate currently authorized command. A temporary withheld/unavailable
  result does not itself end or erase the selection. It may return only within
  a still-valid basis after fresh reproof. Revoked/expired/ended or D8-disabled
  bases require their explicit owner activation; independent valid grants remain
  intact and outcomes explain the exact affected scope.”

### H07 — More historical periods defeat otherwise safe summary privacy

- **What:** Adjacent/overlapping intervals, corrections, goals, current totals
  and shared projects allow subtraction to isolate a protected gift or supporter.
- **Why it matters:** Each individual point can pass a small-cell test while the
  offered series or a repeated release exposes a prohibited fact.
- **Severity:** Critical. **Likelihood:** Medium–High for small ministries.
- **Evidence:** S03:501–517; S04:231; S09–S10:1664; S13:1152–1154; S14:1264.
- **Answer impact:** Earlier-period availability requires finite combined-output
  qualification, not automatic permission to draw every historical chart.
- **Permanent fix:** Extend the existing qualified disclosure profile to the
  supported series, intervals, complements and correction transitions.
- **Exact proposed clause — HC07:** “The offered history profile SHALL be
  qualified as a combined series with its permitted interval choices, current
  summaries, overlapping subjects, relevant published complements and repeated
  source corrections. Apply existing P12 small-cell rules and D9 inference floors;
  no new universal N or anonymity guarantee is introduced. Withhold or source-
  qualify coarsening of unsafe values and reconstructing complements. A new
  sharing version or narrower query does not erase prior disclosure risk.”

### H08 — Unavailable older periods reveal protected existence or fake zeros

- **What:** A missing month is displayed as zero, a gap reveals a hidden donor,
  or the calendar lists periods based on protected gift/commitment existence.
- **Why it matters:** Both numerical meaning and privacy can fail without a raw
  record being returned. Exact suppression reasons are themselves sensitive.
- **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:** High with chart defaults/history pickers.
- **Evidence:** S08:650–667; S09:441–449; S10:1649–1650; S13:1154; D9 S03.
- **Answer impact:** Keep history useful with safe dates and explicit truthful
  states; do not equate unavailable with absent fundraising activity.
- **Permanent fix:** Derive selectable period shapes from qualified scope/profile,
  not unapproved fact existence; use existing uniformity and coverage rules.
- **Exact proposed clause — HC08:** “Period selection, labels, counts, gaps
  and error states SHALL not enumerate protected history. Source-complete admitted
  zero is distinct from absent, incomplete, withheld or unqualified evidence.
  Safe unavailable states SHALL reveal no hidden contributor count, protected
  interval or denial reason; charts SHALL not fill gaps with zero, interpolate
  invented values or connect them as proved observations.”

### H09 — Another representation retains old broader periods

- **What:** Cache, Back navigation, chart payload, accessibility table, preview,
  export, stored result or backup serves dates removed from the current share.
- **Why it matters:** Restricting the visible selector is insufficient when raw
  historical series or stale authorized bodies remain reachable elsewhere.
- **Severity:** Critical. **Likelihood:** High without exact shared result scope.
- **Evidence:** S03:511–517,548–566; S07–S12.
- **Answer impact:** Add no export, archive or offline authority; preserve already
  selected current read semantics at every historical representation.
- **Permanent fix:** Same bounded projection and current release checks, exact
  scope/version cache identity, source-safe preview and late-result rejection.
- **Exact proposed clause — HC09:** “Every historical-summary representation
  SHALL honor the current exact range/profile/source/grant context. Caches,
  recovered outcomes, charts, accessible text, preview and secondary transports
  cannot carry broader dates or fields. Preview also obeys its requester's own
  current authority. Restore SHALL reapply current fences; screen history grants
  no export/print, archive, notification content or persistent offline access.
  Previously observed values cannot be recalled, but future release must stop.”

### H10 — A small history feature becomes a retention or analytics platform

- **What:** Implementation clones financial snapshots, builds arbitrary date
  queries, imports source retention as permission, or requires every future owner
  phase before a useful bounded view can ship.
- **Why it matters:** This creates duplicated authority, unnecessary dependencies
  and impressive documentation without a qualified positive user journey.
- **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:** Medium.
- **Evidence:** S01:95–104; S05–S08; S13:2527–2539; S14; S04:311–325.
- **Answer impact:** Keep source-owned history, bounded scope and measured proof;
  no table-count mandate, archived-money product or invented retention policy.
- **Permanent fix:** Reuse the existing finite metric/profile registry and source
  commands; qualify exact seams, lawful audit and actual supported workloads.
- **Exact proposed clause — HC10:** “D10 SHALL reuse the existing source
  metric/profile, grant, projection and correction owners. Financial retention
  and cache duration confer no access; no new authoritative historical-money
  snapshot, report builder, policy engine or global lock is required. Retain
  minimum lawful scope-change/source-version evidence under its owner policy.
  Qualify useful positive historical access plus denial, race, correction,
  inference, migration and UX cases at actual boundaries; synthetic examples
  and documentation do not establish production qualification.”
