# D8 — Support-raising coaching practice and useful bounded views

Research date: **2026-10-16 UTC**. Research-only evidence for selected D8: an
organization may permit outside coaches; the missionary deliberately invites
one, the coach uses their own identity, and coaching access is read-only and
separately authorized. This note does not decide the remaining module, duration,
historical-access or project-scope questions, implement a feature, or reopen D7.

**Recommendation:** make coaching useful through a clear view of the agreed
goal, qualified progress, the missionary's chosen discussion topics and agreed
next actions. Support one coach working with several separately authorized
people or ministry/project contexts. Do not equate a comprehensive coaching
experience with unlimited CRM access, a dense surveillance dashboard, compulsory
weekly reports, a new chat/LMS/calendar system or an AI dependency.

The primary evidence supports these jobs and varied coaching arrangements. It
does not prove that a particular dashboard improves funding, that every coach
needs every data module, or that users prefer one fixed cadence. Exact Asym
modules remain product proposals requiring source qualification.

permission grants or a decision to ship every module now. Source references

## Current owner constraints and unanswered scope

The [founder brief §10](../phase28-founder-brief.md) requires deliberately chosen
coaching, targets, qualitative check-ins and agreed next actions; source-approved
redaction; and no default donor finances, private notes, care records or Field
Account access. These remain effective even when a missionary wants an external
coach to see more.

[P12's capability separation](../../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/phase-12-full-role-permission-configuration.md)
independently names a bounded coach-progress projection, project/team summary,
participant roster, source access grants and invitation/access-state inspection.
The coach need not be a Support Assignment participant. Conversely, participation
or staff status does not grant coaching. This directly supports a multi-context
experience without merging permissions or inventing a separate coach ACL store.

The [Phase 28 roadmap](../../../../prds/sitestacker-parity/roadmap.md) retains
goals/trends/check-ins while forbidding missing-log penalties and automatic
coaching enrollment under P27 D26. P9/P27 retain actual interaction/task meaning;
P13/P14/P16 retain giving/recognition/commitment truth; P21 supplies only exact
authorized finance projections; P22 and P17/P6 retain content and communication.
This is targeted owner reading, not a claim those integrations are implemented.

The [Q8 frontier](../phase28-q8-coach-eligibility-research.md) presented eligibility
alone. D8's answer therefore does **not** by itself decide:

- the exact data modules a missionary may choose to share;
- mandatory or default grant duration, past-history reach or export permission;
- coach-authored comments, shared notes, task edits or direct messaging;
- a peer cohort, coach marketplace, certification registry or staff appraisal;
- automatic calendar/reminder/report-email behavior;
- a project-wide grant from an invitation about one missionary.

These are not excuses for a skeletal experience. They are boundaries for a
complete, coherent implementation of the selected modules once defined. Do not
ship many empty or permanently disabled dashboard cards as “full featured.”

## Recommended lean experience

The following is a concrete **design recommendation**, not additional founder
policy. Use the existing shared [base-maia UI](../../../../../packages/ui/components.json)
and ordinary qualified source paths. Read-only access still needs a useful view,
honest state, navigation and revocation; it does not need a second application.

### Missionary: understand and manage the relationship

1. Enter **Coaching** in the correct ministry/organization context. Explain the
   actual permitted purpose in one sentence. Offer **Invite coach** only when
   current policy permits the operation; explain a policy block without exposing
   private account facts or inviting an impossible action.
2. Identify the intended person and show the exact qualified information that
   would be visible before invitation. The invitation recipient is not silently
   a staff account, donor, participant or verified coach. Keep identity assurance
   and acceptance in their existing owners.
3. Show the actual state: **Invitation pending**, **Active** or **Access ended**,
   with the current source-defined scope/history/expiry once those are decided.
   An email accepted by a provider is not an accepted coaching relationship.
4. Let the missionary inspect a truthful explanation or qualified preview of
   **What your coach can see**. This must reflect the coach projection; it cannot
   impersonate the coach or reuse the missionary's broader dashboard unchanged.
5. When separately adopted, offer a brief optional check-in centered on the
   missionary's question, progress/context they wish to share and agreed next
   actions. Do not require repeating available source facts or disclosing private
   third-party information to submit it.

### Coach: find the right person or project and prepare to help

1. Accept as the intended identity and use the appropriate tenant context.
   A multi-organization coach must never see a union of tenant data merely
   because the same login has several accepted invitations.
2. Open a simple **Coaching** list of authorized contexts. A row/card can show
   safe subject label, purpose and access state, plus a currently authorized
   freshness/check-in fact if that module exists. Use search/filter only within
   the permitted corpus; no exposed count of people the coach cannot view.
3. Open one detail headed by its actual person/joint-ministry/project subject,
   organization and purpose. Show selected, qualified modules in a small number
   of understandable sections: goal/progress, shared discussion/check-in and
   agreed next actions, as applicable. Do not fill the screen with every possible
   financial/activity chart.
4. Represent lack of data precisely: **No check-in shared for this period**,
   **Not shared with you**, **Progress data unavailable**, or a dated source
   value when that is true. “No check-in” must not become **Overdue** or **At
   risk** unless a separately qualified duty actually exists. Avoid revealing
   the existence of a protected record through an overly specific explanation.
5. Help through an existing permitted conversation channel. The coach's D8
   read-only grant does not allow editing the missionary's goals, tasks, donor
   records, newsletter, or check-in. Any later own-feedback action needs a
   distinct authored-content owner and explicit scope; it is not smuggled in as
   a harmless note button.
6. Provide a clear **End my coaching access** path if qualified, alongside
   ordinary navigation. Do not make a person impersonate the missionary or ask
   an administrator to remove a voluntarily accepted coach relationship when
   the governing source permits self-removal.

### Multi-person and project handling without excess complexity

The useful comparable is M2's list-to-detail navigation, not an organization-wide
leaderboard. Reuse the same layout for independently authorized contexts, but
keep subject type and purpose legible. A project target, an individual recurring
support target and a joint ministry's budget are different source facts even if
they share visual components. Never add their progress bars or currencies into
one “coach success” total by default.

No fixed coach caseload, program length, weekly form or progress threshold is
supported as universal. Ordinary list scalability and phone usability need
representative testing, not arbitrary limits copied from a vendor training
package. A calendar integration, conference-call provider, document editor or
resource library may aid coaching, but none is required merely to read and
discuss a qualified native coaching view.

## Strongest alternative and what should not be built

The strongest simpler alternative is a **deliberately shared, narrow meeting
brief**: current agreed goal, permitted progress and the missionary's questions,
with coaching conversations occurring through existing channels. It reduces
disclosure and reporting burden. Its weakness is less continuity and more
preparation work if every meeting requires assembling the same facts manually.
Recommend a persistent source-qualified view of the selected information, with
the ability to prepare a concise discussion, rather than treating a snapshot or
downloaded spreadsheet as the whole product. Exact history and export policy
remain undecided.

The strongest broader alternative is an **agency supervisory program** with
weekly reports, quotas, escalation and leader visibility. R1/R2/M1 establish that
such programs really exist. It can support a particular organization's operating
model, but D8 does not create that model in Core. Importing it would conflict
with the founder's established autonomy boundaries and would mix optional coach
help with separately governed operational duties.

An **external-call-only/no native view** avoids software work but fails the
already-requested invited coaching view. A **new chat, LMS, coach marketplace,
AI coach or spreadsheet/reporting engine** adds scope without evidence it is
necessary for the chosen job. Tailored's use of Zoom/Drive/email and Via's use
of an online learning platform are service-provider choices, not Core dependencies.

## Testable questions for later validation

These are proposed usability/qualification exercises, not research results or
new questions sent to the founder. They should use representative permitted
data and actual integrated behavior once the relevant modules are specified.

1. Can a missionary correctly explain what an invited coach can see and cannot
   change, including the difference between a pending invitation and accepted
   access? Can an outside coach enter without being misclassified as staff?
2. Can a coach with several invitations find the intended person/project and
   identify the organization and subject before reading details? Does a switch
   avoid carrying over another context's filters, cached facts or draft input?
3. Can both parties prepare for a conversation using the same authorized
   goal/progress meaning without duplicating data entry? Can they distinguish
   missing, stale, not shared and genuinely zero values?
4. Can a participant decline an optional target/check-in without receiving a
   failure label, automatic task or staff escalation? Does a low count explain
   recorded coverage rather than claim no ministry work occurred?
5. Can a missionary ask for practical help without exposing donor identities,
   private pastoral information or an unapproved newsletter draft? Are lawful
   safe alternatives understandable rather than a generic permission error?
6. Can the coach identify agreed next actions without acquiring edit powers?
   Does the missionary remain the actor for separately authorized source work?
7. Do personal, joint and project subjects remain distinct, with clear periods,
   currencies and permitted histories? Can small-group or differencing attacks
   recover identities from a supposedly redacted aggregate?
8. Does ending access remove current ordinary and derived disclosures, including
   search/counts, notification targets and refetched charts? Does the interface
   avoid promising deletion/recall of every already disclosed copy?
9. Can phone/keyboard/assistive-technology users navigate list/detail, read a
   trend in an alternative form and recover from loading/unavailable states?
   Shared UI primitives alone do not prove this journey works.
10. Can the native coaching view work with optional calendar, external storage,
    AI, SMS and workflow integrations absent? Does an actual missing source
    qualification remain honestly unavailable instead of using a shadow source?
