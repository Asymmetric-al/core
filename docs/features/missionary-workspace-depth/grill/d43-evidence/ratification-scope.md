The [founder brief](../phase28-founder-brief.md) requires **one meaningful

The next adjacent unresolved product branch is **how the reader's finite
confirmation window is governed**. D43 settles the start, strict expiry boundary,
no-extension rule and current restrictions; it deliberately leaves the duration
and whether organizations choose it unresolved. No implemented or already
qualified native purpose profile was identified in the recorded source audit
that would answer the missing policy as a fact.

The strongest simple option is one standard Asym window, clearly explained to
readers, with no duration field for missionaries or staff. The strongest
alternative is an organization-set finite window within supported limits.
That is a real tenant-control/product tradeoff: consistency and fewer settings
versus locally chosen completion time. Neither option permits an indefinite
request, an individual-reader override or extending an already accepted request.

**Recommended single next question:**

> **Q44 — Who sets the newsletter confirmation window?** A reader may need to
> return later to finish signup. Should all organizations use one standard
> confirmation window, or should authorized staff be able to choose their
> organization's finite window?
>
> **A — One standard window (recommended).** Asym supplies one finite window.
> Readers see the deadline; staff have no extra duration setting.
>
> **B — Organization-set window.** Authorized staff choose a finite window
> within supported limits. Readers still see their resulting deadline.
>
> Either choice preserves each accepted request's original deadline. Signup
> can still be disabled, and current safety rules may stop confirmation earlier.

This question chooses the policy's ownership, **not the numeric duration**.
Its recommendation is a product judgment favoring the founder's simplicity
requirement, not an external standard or a claim that organizations never need
different periods. A duration recommendation must be researched and made
explicit in the subsequent decision; the older 72-hour suggestion cannot become
a hidden default under either answer. If current owner research establishes an
already accepted applicable duration policy, omit this question and apply that
policy rather than invent a founder choice.

Do not ask about lifetime, purge deadlines and retained consent evidence in the
same question. They have different consequences and owners. The reader's
completion window is product policy. Disposition of redundant pending material,
minimal consent/withdrawal and anti-replay evidence, delivery evidence and backups
requires the existing source/privacy contract and concrete engineering proof.
The founder should be asked only a genuine unresolved privacy/product tradeoff,
not to choose a deletion-job interval or database implementation.

The six earlier packets remain a real reconciliation frontier. Their selected
directions are not candidates for reopening: restricted preparation, no new
departure-continuity right, native newsletter signup, optional ongoing coaching,
curated resources and regional list/map discovery are already selected. Their
remaining amendments should be reconciled against later ratified decisions and
owners; ask another question only where a material product choice survives.
For example, D13's initial activation/default recommendation is separately
reviewed, whereas resource publication custody is primarily an owner-contract

window branch or declare the entire phase complete.
