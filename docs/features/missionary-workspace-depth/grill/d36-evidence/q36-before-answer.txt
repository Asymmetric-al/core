## The single choice

Maya deliberately chooses **Continue referral** for an eligible expired referral.
She needs to understand when its new active follow-up period will end. This is
an illustrative decision scenario, not an observed ministry incident.

**How should the new follow-up period be set?**

- **A — Use the organization's configured period (recommended).** Authorized
  staff set the finite period. Maya sees the resulting end date and continues
  without choosing another date.
- **B — Allow a permitted missionary to adjust the end date.** The organization
  supplies a default and supported limits; Maya may keep the proposed date or
  adjust it for that continuation.

Both preserve D35's optional tenant control, current source/action/privacy rules,
independent staff authority and deliberate acceptance. Applying the chosen terms
is part of that one continuation; neither option automatically renews a referral
in the background or treats activity/editing as an extension. Nothing is sent.

A is recommended because it keeps the ordinary action short and predictable.
B is the strongest flexibility alternative: a case-specific end date can fit a
particular piece of work, but it adds an editable date control and bounded date-setting
power. B need not force a date choice: the organization's proposed date can be kept. These are product judgments, not measured ministry preferences or claims
that missionaries need different periods. The organization remains in control
under both options.

## Simple journey under either answer

Maya discovers eligible work in the existing Expired view. Current authorization
admits the action; permitted source terms are explained before acceptance.
Under A, the source supplies the resulting period with no routine date form.
Under B, the proposed date can be kept; an optional adjustment control must communicate the organization's limits
and reject unsupported terms without exposing hidden source data. Shared Core
controls, current source precision/timezone and accessible error/focus behavior
remain governed by D35; no separate timer builder or permission system is implied.

An accepted action still says **Referral continued**. Current Active state remains
a separate current permitted fact. A rejected or unknown result follows the
ratified recovery journey; changing a date cannot repurpose the original intent.
Neither answer reopens completed tasks, rewrites past cycles or revives private
referral context. No amount, gift, coaching or donor-facing step is introduced.

## Scope deliberately left unselected

This question selects only how the **renewed** period is set. It does not choose:

- Numerical days, minimum/maximum bounds or a universal default.
- The initial-referral period or separate versus reused configuration for it.
- Retrospective changes to existing cycles or tenant-policy changes mid-cycle.
- An indefinite window, automatic renewal, recurring extension or bulk action.
- Personal-data retention, history access, grant duration or contact consent.
- Mandatory fresh-referrer evidence or a new approval/request queue.

Reusing an appropriate ordinary referral policy may later avoid duplicate
settings, but it is not a settled source fact and is not silently bundled into A.
The exact calendar/time semantics, policy revision behavior, supported bounds and
resulting source qualification belong in the answer review after this choice.
No engineering-storage decision needs a founder vote.
