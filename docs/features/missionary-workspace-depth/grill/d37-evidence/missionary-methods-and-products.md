# D37 evidence — missionary methods and product guidance

Research date: **October 18, 2026, Asia/Bangkok**. Research-only evidence for the
optional organization starting guide. The founder selected A and subsequently
confirmed: **keep it optional; tenants can offer, edit or disable it; no mandatory
completion or gates**. The suggestions below are recommendations for the answer
review, not ratified implementation, curriculum adoption or a new owner contract.

Read alongside the [founder brief](../phase28-founder-brief.md:77),
[resource and guidance boundaries](../phase28-founder-brief.md:235) and
[Q37 research](q37-before-answer.txt). Q37's original
unanswered text is a historical question artifact; the answer and clarification
above are the current research context. Existing D12 amendment status is not
changed by this note.

## Finding

Build a small, optional organization-authored guide that connects useful advice
to permitted resources and ordinary workspace actions. Do not install a single
fundraising method as the application's operating model. The named methods differ
meaningfully about invitations, follow-up and spiritual framing. A missionary
should be able to use the advice that helps, continue ordinary work and return
later without a course, completion score, permanent beginner classification or
additional CRM.

This is a product recommendation supported by public descriptions and instructions,
not a demonstrated increase in fundraising, adoption or missionary wellbeing.
No users were interviewed, paid courses accessed, whole books read, videos watched
or authenticated products tested in this research. Public source availability
does not grant republication rights.

| Shadrach / The God Ask public teaching        | Prepare a clear invitation while recognizing the prospective supporter's independent response.                   | Theology, scripts, amount suggestions and exact appointment practice are not universal application rules.                       |

| Cru's structured program                      | Organizations may have useful approved material and people who can help.                                         | Its assigned-coach and staff-training model conflicts with automatically importing those practices into optional Core guidance. |
| MPDX / TntConnect help                        | Make a useful next action discoverable; explain only the settings relevant now.                                  | No need for another dashboard, full software tour or prerequisite setup wizard.                                                 |
| Sola / Epistle / DonorElf product positioning | Keep advice close to relationship and communication work.                                                        | Marketing alone does not prove learning effectiveness, safe AI coaching or a specific product interaction.                      |

The evidence does **not** establish one superior support-raising method. Shared
topics do not make the methods interchangeable. A library of approved resources
alone remains the strongest simpler alternative: it requires less presentation
maintenance and leaves all sequencing to the reader. A's benefit is a short,
findable bridge from advice to existing actions. Its value disappears if it
becomes another administrative checklist.

## Small original starter: content recommendations

The following is **original generic example content**, not an extract, condensed
replacement for a book or automatic tenant configuration. Topics can be offered,
edited, reordered or omitted by authorized staff. Six topics here illustrate
coverage; they are not a selected count, required sequence or engineering limit.
Short headings should describe jobs, not rank a missionary's maturity.

| Suggested topic                         | Brief example guidance                                                                                    | Destination and limits                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Understand your organization's approach | “Find your organization's support-raising guidance and giving instructions.”                              | An approved resource; optional method/theology resources are clearly attributed. Explain where to ask a question. Do not require a financial goal or ministry assignment merely to read or begin otherwise authorized early relationship work. |
| Organize the people you know            | “Start with the relationships you want to care for. Add or import permitted contact details when useful.” | Existing authorized contact/import flow; respect restricted preparation and explicit sharing. No minimum contact count, wealth inference, automatic contact scraping or assumption that a friend wants fundraising messages.                   |
| Prepare your invitation                 | “Explain the work, why it matters and the ways someone may choose to participate.”                        | Approved examples and existing draft tools where qualified. No automatic publication/send, invented promise of impact, universal ask amount or suggestion that prayer support equals financial or newsletter consent.                          |
| Choose a next step                      | “When appropriate, arrange a conversation or leave yourself a reminder.”                                  | Ordinary permitted task/appointment action. Opening a guide item creates neither. A person can decline; the guide supplies no forced cadence, overdue pressure or new reporting duty.                                                          |
| Care for existing partners              | “Use approved updates and personal follow-up to maintain relationships.”                                  | Existing communication and follow-up flows with their actual eligibility, audience and consent. Reading guidance does not subscribe someone, add a coach or mark a gift acknowledged.                                                          |
| Get help when you want it               | “Return to these resources or use your organization's available support.”                                 | Existing approved resources and separately permitted coaching/support options. No auto-invitation, task sharing, completion report or private data transfer.                                                                                   |

An organization can supply its own approved script or suggest a licensed external
course as a resource, but the guide cannot require attendance or purchase before
using the workspace. If a resource is paid, external or requires another account,
identify that before the user leaves. Do not embed a paid lesson or branded
worksheet merely because its public syllabus is visible.

**Missionary journey recommendation:** a modest **Getting started** entry opens
the organization's brief guide. Suggested introduction: “Optional guidance from
your organization. Use what helps, skip any step and return whenever you need.”
Each item has a short explanation and a descriptive link such as **Read giving
instructions** or **Open contacts**. No forced tour, checklist completion,
achievement percentage or shame-producing empty state. Ordinary navigation stays
available. Reopening the guide is navigation, not proof of adoption or consent.

**Staff journey recommendation:** authorized staff maintain a short introduction
and ordered items using existing content/resource capabilities. Prefer one
straightforward edit/preview/save experience over a course builder. Show what
missionaries will actually see, including destination availability; do not make
preview execute an action. Offering, editing and disabling are the selected
controls. Exact publication and disable semantics belong to the main review;
no optimistic saved state, permission grant or new completion ledger is implied.
Disabling the guide should not silently withdraw independently available source
resources or erase existing work.

Keep language readable on a phone and make written alternatives available for
essential instructions instead of relying on video alone. Use the existing
[shared UI constraints](../../../../../packages/ui/AGENTS.md:19), ordinary
semantic headings, descriptive links and keyboard-operable controls. Source
examples are design evidence, not an accessibility certification. The separate
UX/implementation review must verify actual focus, reflow, target sizing and
screen-reader behavior.

## Material concerns and review clauses

Each proposed clause below is research advice, not a formal owner amendment.
Likelihood is a qualitative design-risk estimate, not measured incidence.

### R1 — Method quietly becomes mandatory policy

- **Severity:** high. **Likelihood:** plausible when importing a course outline.
- **Evidence:** M1–M8 differ in method and program structure; the founder explicitly retained optionality.
- **Decision impact:** a required sequence or completion gate contradicts D37.
- **Fix:** allow organization-authored guidance without an enforced curriculum.
- **Exact proposed clause:** “The guide is optional; no item, course, purchase, reading state or completion status gates otherwise permitted work.”
- **Proof:** skip every item and still complete an independently permitted ordinary action; disable the guide without disabling source work.
- **Disconfirming result:** the UI or backend refuses work until a guide item is completed, or an administrator can silently make the guide mandatory.

### R2 — Advice performs an action or widens audience

- **Severity:** high. **Likelihood:** plausible with deep links or automatic setup.
- **Evidence:** the founder's existing resource/action boundaries, plus S3–S5's distinction between instructions and work.
- **Decision impact:** a guide must not bypass deliberate contact sharing, consent, source authority or coaching choices.
- **Fix:** navigate to the existing authorized flow; preserve its own review and acceptance.
- **Exact proposed clause:** “Opening guide content or following its navigation does not create contacts/tasks, send messages, subscribe supporters, publish content or share information.”
- **Proof:** trace every guide destination with the viewer's current permissions; no mutation occurs on opening, previewing or returning.
- **Disconfirming result:** a step opens a resource outside its audience or completes a source action merely through navigation.

### R3 — A helpful introduction turns into pressure or surveillance

- **Severity:** high. **Likelihood:** plausible given program/accountability comparators.
- **Evidence:** M8/S1 show stronger organizational programs; founder boundaries reject missing-log penalties and automatic coaching enrollment.
- **Decision impact:** completion reports, quotas and funding promises undermine the selected voluntary journey.
- **Fix:** provide practical suggestions without a progress score, wealth ranking, guilt language or automatic reporting.
- **Exact proposed clause:** “Guide use is not a performance, spiritual-maturity or fundraising-success measure and grants no new staff or coach access.”
- **Proof:** an unused guide produces no overdue state, missed-goal penalty, shared reading status or coach assignment.
- **Disconfirming result:** staff can rank missionaries by guide use, or skipped content generates a follow-up duty.

### R4 — Copyrighted material or vendor marketing becomes the product

- **Severity:** high. **Likelihood:** plausible when aiming for a comprehensive starter.
- **Evidence:** paid M4 courses, published M1/M6/M7 works, and untested S1/S7 advertising.
- **Decision impact:** copying training or adding an AI coach/new CRM exceeds the useful scope.
- **Fix:** original generic instructions plus attributed approved links or separately licensed resources.
- **Exact proposed clause:** “A public outline is research evidence, not permission to reproduce a curriculum; no third-party course, CRM or AI-coaching dependency is required by this guide.”
- **Proof:** content review identifies ownership/permission for hosted material; the core journey works without registering with an external provider.
- **Disconfirming result:** copied lessons or branded worksheets appear without rights, or the guide depends on a new software subscription.

### R5 — A resource is unavailable but the interface claims success

- **Severity:** medium, high if private information is disclosed. **Likelihood:** plausible during edits, permission changes or external link failures.
- **Evidence:** actual loading-only/failed source retrievals in this pass and existing current-access boundaries.
- **Decision impact:** optional guidance must not become a dead end or leak hidden titles/details.
- **Fix:** show only currently permitted information, keep ordinary work usable and provide truthful failure/retry navigation.
- **Exact proposed clause:** “A missing or inaccessible destination is not completion; guide presentation does not override the destination's current access decision.”
- **Proof:** test missing, revoked, slow and externally unavailable destinations; ordinary navigation remains operable and no inaccessible details are exposed.
- **Disconfirming result:** a broken link marks success, a loading failure becomes an empty configured guide, or stale preview content reveals restricted material.

### R6 — Global and financial meanings are oversimplified

- **Severity:** high for incorrect financial instructions, medium for usability. **Likelihood:** plausible if a US-centric training resource is treated as universal.
- **Evidence:** M4's advertised language variants, M6's global scope, M9's expressly US-context institutional guidance and S5's international settings.
- **Decision impact:** a universal fundraising script, culture, currency or receipting instruction can mislead.
- **Fix:** approved organization-specific instructions and short localized text; leave actual financial and communication states to their source owners.
- **Exact proposed clause:** “Guidance never turns an intention into received support, an offline report into a posted gift, an invitation into consent, or a missionary into an official receipt issuer.”
- **Proof:** review examples for different currencies, cultures, ministry stages and permitted early work; test a phone and keyboard-only path with no required video.
- **Disconfirming result:** advice promises deductibility universally, invents money states, requires an unselected assignment, or excludes a user who cannot consume the chosen media.

## Remaining evidence limits

No inspected source establishes the optimum guide length, default step count,
fundraising timeline, contact quantity or universal training order. No source
proves that a guide will outperform a well-maintained resource library for Core
users. Qualitative validation should therefore check whether a new user can find
one useful permitted action, an experienced user can ignore the guide, and both
can return to help without misunderstanding audience or completion. Those are
future tests, not results obtained here.

Additional full-book or licensed-course study could help a tenant author its own
material, but is not necessary to justify a small optional product capability.
Specific licenses, complete lesson accessibility and the exact Stewardship
Ambassadors course intended remain unverified. No numerical success claims,
mandatory organization program, donor profiling, new dependency or external
vendor account is adopted by this evidence note.
