# D3 product challenge: no new exceptional continuity access

Research date: 2026-10-16 UTC. Research-only input to the Phase 28 grill; not a
formal specification, owner amendment, ticket plan, implementation or release
qualification. This pass edits only this evidence note. No application, GitHub,
provider, live-data or supporter action was taken.

The parent relayed the founder's selection of Q3 **A — No new continuity-access
right**, together with an emphasis on clean, useful data and simple, clear UX.
This pass reviewed the recorded Q3 and relevant existing owner clauses. It does
not infer an unspoken cleanup instruction from that quality goal.

## Verdict and exact candidate decision

**Accept with required amendments.** The narrow access choice is coherent and
simpler than adding an exceptional reader for a continuity need not yet
established. The amendments below preserve that choice; archive, deletion,
retention duration and return-access policy are not silently accepted.

> Departure, absence or inability to continue does not create a new right for
> staff, a replacement missionary, a coach or an administrator to read a
> missionary's still-unshared prospect identity/contact details or private
> preparation. Ordinary deliberate sharing remains the D2 action. Organization
> ownership, reassignment and a generic administrative role are not substitutes
> for current source authority.
>
> D3 does not revoke independently authorized access, hide existing shared CRM
> facts, erase accepted work, or stop its separately qualified continuity.
> Actual access loss fences the former reader; an absence or an unused account
> alone does not establish that access has been revoked. Real unfinished
> obligations retain source-owned accountability without revealing unshared
> details to an otherwise unauthorized successor.
>
> Clean, useful data and simple UX are required qualities. They do not select
> automatic archive, deletion, retention periods, permanent retention, departure
> publication or a new right to recover historical private preparation. Explain
> the current audience and the real consequence of each available action in
> plain language. A returning missionary receives exactly the access their
> current qualified source permissions and history rules allow; return alone
> neither restores ended grants nor establishes permanent exclusion.

This does not promise that information is inaccessible to every independently
authorized legal, security or records function. Those functions must establish
their actual existing authority; mentioning them does not create a backdoor or
an exception to D3. Nor does it settle the duration or conditions of their
retention. D1's restricted ordinary audience remains distinct from Phase 10's
protected-data classification.

## Necessity, strongest alternative and smallest viable approach

The actual problem is truthful restricted-start behavior across ordinary sharing
and changed personnel. The evidence does not establish that organizations need
to retrieve every tentative unshared prospect when a missionary cannot act.
Prospect entry alone creates neither an accepted duty nor permission to contact
the person. Building exceptional retrieval speculatively would introduce a new
access power, explanation, qualification and operational burden.

The strongest alternative remains Q3 B: tightly scoped authorized retrieval of
necessary contact details for a demonstrated continuity purpose. It could reduce
stranded information, but still needs a real purpose, qualified reader and
privacy explanation. Generic administrator browsing or automatic departure
sharing is a broader and weaker alternative. A should be reconsidered only with
evidence of a material unmet need and an explicit scoped amendment, not when an
implementation finds permission checks inconvenient.

**No-build is the preferred answer for the exceptional-access feature.** Do not
add that route, role, approval queue or browse screen. This does not mean no work
is needed to deliver D1/D2: their access boundaries, usable ordinary sharing,
source-owned obligations, recovery and all reached readers still require real
qualification. Voluntary sharing while the missionary can act is available;
making it a departure prerequisite would be a new compulsory workflow.

## Material concerns and exact proposed fixes

Likelihood is conditional on the stated shortcut, not an estimated incident
rate. Category numbers refer to the founder's 22-category review scheme. These
crosscutting findings feed the root review; this note does not independently
claim complete technical or all-category qualification.

### D3-PS01 — A quality goal could silently become data destruction

**Categories:** 1, 5, 10, 13–15, 20. **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:** Plausible
if “clean data” is translated into automatic archive or deletion on departure.

**What and why:** Access, active-list visibility, pursuit intent, deletion and
retention have different consequences. A departed missionary's preparation may
contain a legitimate retained record or link to independently owned work. A
generic cascade or timer can lose data without making its quality better.

**Evidence:** Q3 expressly excludes retention/deletion; D1-C13; P27-Q10/Q11;
the brief's explicit material-change rule in §13.

**Answer impact:** Accept A. Do not ratify a cleanup lifecycle as an amendment
that supposedly preserves it.

**Permanent fix:** Keep those meanings separate; any new cleanup command must
have its own selected outcome and source-qualified limits before activation.

**Exact clause:** “D3 adds no archive, deletion, retention-period or automatic
departure-disposition policy. Cleanliness is not permission to erase, reclassify
or conceal records; each later cleanup action must state its exact scope and
retained consequences under the owning source.”

### D3-PS02 — Accepted work could disappear with its former worker

**Categories:** 4, 10–14, 17–19, 21. **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:** Likely if
active work is filtered solely by active worker membership or cleanup cascades.

**What and why:** A promise to follow up, an actual Request, a task or unresolved
physical-gift handling is not merely optional prospect preparation. Hiding the
former person's records can orphan work; showing all their private content to a
replacement would violate A. A supposed empty queue is not proof of completion.

**Evidence:** P27-P05 including AC27-P05-04/05; ADR-0183 source-controlled
closure; P27 D25 explicitly protects unresolved handling through departure.

**Answer impact:** A applies to the proposed access right, not to independently
qualified accountability. Inability to complete work safely may be a visible
source exception; it is not permission to grant private access.

**Permanent fix:** Preserve real source work and its current qualified repair
path. Expose only owner-permitted facts to authorized actors; do not guess a
successor, reveal private details, create a universal orphan queue or mark Done.

**Exact clause:** “Departure and preparation cleanup do not complete, suppress
or delete independently accepted obligations. Keep their source-owned state and
qualified accountability, including an authorized needs-assignment exception
where required, without using that exception to reveal unshared preparation or
grant new access.”

### D3-PS03 — Absence could be confused with authoritative access loss

**Categories:** 2, 4, 5, 12, 13, 15. **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:** Plausible
if absence, inactivity or an unconfirmed personnel signal drives automation.

**What and why:** The Q3 scenario includes inability to continue, which is not
necessarily account termination. Automatically disabling or disposing of data
after silence invents a lifecycle and may prevent a valid user from working.
The opposite error lets an actually revoked user retain cached/server access
until a handoff has been arranged.

**Evidence:** Q3's scenario is broader than departure; D1-C13; P27-P05 requires
revocation first, independently of successor selection.

**Answer impact:** Preserve A and actual revocation requirements. Do not add
inactivity detection, automatic offboarding or a return approval ritual.

**Permanent fix:** Use authoritative current membership/access events for
fencing. Keep absence/inactivity as unproven operational context unless an
existing source rule specifically establishes a consequential event.

**Exact clause:** “Actual source-authorized access loss takes effect without
waiting for a successor. Absence, lack of login or missing voluntary updates
alone neither revokes access nor archives, deletes or transfers preparation.”

### D3-PS04 — Returning could either restore too much or strand valid history

**Categories:** 4, 7, 10, 12–15, 20–21. **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:**
Plausible if access follows stable author/login identity or a permanent departed
flag instead of current authority.

**What and why:** A returning missionary is not automatically entitled to every
historical record merely because they authored it. Conversely, D3 does not say
that their still-retained private preparation must be forever inaccessible
after a legitimate return. Either shortcut introduces a historical-access
policy that the founder has not selected.

**Evidence:** D1-C13's no restoration of ended access; P27 D26 current/historical
authority separation; Q3 excludes retention/other new access rights. Exact P12
return behavior was not newly qualified in this pass.

**Answer impact:** Do not choose automatic restoration, permanent exclusion or
mandatory fresh human approval under D3. Preserve already qualified rights.

**Permanent fix:** Resolve the exact current source/history rule. Returning
users must receive the truthful qualified result, with no stale grant revival;
if a needed product outcome is not governed, record a separate genuine choice.

**Exact clause:** “Return or account recovery alone neither restores ended
grants nor permanently excludes retained preparation. Access follows current
qualified source and historical-access rules. D3 adds no new return entitlement,
blanket historical denial or compulsory reapproval workflow.”

### D3-PS05 — Cleanliness administration could become a hidden reader

**Categories:** 5–7, 11, 15, 18. **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:** Plausible if
an administrator receives unshared names, counts, exports or raw diagnostics.

**What and why:** An orphan-record report, staff search suggestion, audit body
or support screen may expose the same details that A refuses to disclose.
Removing a normal profile link is insufficient when other surfaces reveal its
existence or content. A generic repair capability is not independent access.

**Evidence:** D1 lifecycle findings L2/L4/L7; ADR-0183 purpose-minimized list
facts and protected detail loader; P27-Q10 safe technical evidence.

**Answer impact:** A includes its reached derivatives. It does not require a
new “private orphan custody” dashboard or private-content monitoring feed.

**Permanent fix:** Reuse qualified source authorization on all reached views,
counts, audit/support queries and retained derivatives; keep technical evidence
minimized. Prove exact independently authorized functions rather than inventing
them as exceptions.

**Exact clause:** “A cleanup, reassignment, audit, support, export or recovery
surface cannot disclose unshared preparation merely because it is operational.
Each reached fact and derivative retains its qualified source audience;
ordinary technical evidence uses safe identifiers and reason codes.”

### D3-PS06 — Honest simple copy could become an absolute secrecy promise

**Categories:** 1, 5, 9, 15, 21. **Severity:** High for a false disclosure promise;
Medium for friction. **Likelihood:** Plausible if copy says 'only you ever' or
requires a long legal modal on every save.

**What and why:** A excludes a new continuity reader, not all conceivable
independently authorized organizational functions or retained records. Hiding
that distinction is misleading; explaining every internal permission mechanism
in the daily workflow would make a simple action unusable.

**Evidence:** Q3 A and its recommendation; P27-Q09's one ordinary Save and
explanation before material consequences; D1-C14 audience explanation.

**Answer impact:** Keep A and a plain explanation. Do not add a compulsory
checklist, routine approval or a new data-management control center.

**Permanent fix:** Explain ordinary audience at entry and actual material
consequences at the action. Put detailed owner-policy explanation in an
accessible secondary location; test whether users understand the short copy.

**Exact clause:** “State plainly that staff do not gain access to unshared
prospect details because the missionary leaves or cannot continue. Do not
promise absolute secrecy, automatic deletion or automatic recovery. Explain
each available action's real consequence without repetitive confirmations for
ordinary reversible work.”

### D3-PS07 — Stale-looking data could be mistaken for false or finished data

**Categories:** 1, 4, 5, 9–14, 18. **Severity:** High for invented business state;
Medium for clutter. **Likelihood:** Likely if elapsed time or missing notes is
used as a universal quality score or cleanup trigger.

**What and why:** An old contact can remain accurate; a relationship without
voluntary updates can remain active. A clean display can hide meaningful work
if it automatically treats age, silence or no gift as an abandoned prospect,
completed obligation, failed relationship or permission to contact again.

**Evidence:** Brief §§7–8; P27 D26 voluntary shared history; P27 honest-facts
paragraph; ADR-0183 requires an actual source work predicate.

**Answer impact:** The quality goal does not authorize compulsory recertification,
missing-log penalties, a productivity score or automated relationship disposal.

**Permanent fix:** Use source currentness/coverage and explicit legitimate
dispositions. Ask separately if the founder wants a user-facing cleanup outcome;
do not create tasks merely because a record has aged.

**Exact clause:** “Elapsed age, missing voluntary notes, lack of login or absent
gifts do not prove that contact details are false, a relationship has ended or
an obligation is complete. Show relevant source currentness honestly; do not
introduce compulsory logging, automatic disposal or missionary scoring as data
quality enforcement.”

### D3-PS08 — The smallest policy could grow into a second lifecycle platform

**Categories:** 2, 3, 8, 16–19, 22. **Severity:** Medium–High. **Likelihood:**
Plausible if every reserved question is implemented preemptively.

**What and why:** An archive service, offboarding workflow, new approval queue,
custodian role, private CRM, retention scheduler or generic restore engine is
not needed to omit the newly proposed access route. It creates additional
failure states and long-term administration without an established need.

**Evidence:** Q3 scope; ADR-0183 one source projection model; D1/E1 preserves
source architecture as a recommendation; roadmap future owners remain scoped.

**Answer impact:** Accept A with the no-build exceptional-access alternative.
Do not mistake safety obligations on reached paths for authority to build every
possible future lifecycle capability.

**Permanent fix:** Reuse the existing qualified source, permissions and shared
work boundaries. Qualify exact used paths; introduce additional lifecycle
behavior only for a selected outcome and demonstrated missing source seam.

**Exact clause:** “D3 adds no exceptional-reader role, approval queue, archive
service, private CRM or general offboarding/restore engine. Reuse existing
qualified owners and implement only the selected behavior and its reached
safeguards; later phases are dependencies only for their exact used seams.”

### D3-PS09 — Permanent retention could hide behind minimal implementation

**Categories:** 10, 14–17, 20. **Severity:** High for unlawful or unnecessary
retention. **Likelihood:** Plausible if 'no deletion decision' is treated as
'keep all content forever'.

**What and why:** Not choosing a new cleanup UX does not suspend already
applicable retention, minimization or legal disposition requirements. Keeping
every private body in audit logs/backups indefinitely creates exposure and cost;
deleting all evidence loses required lineage and effect identity.

**Evidence:** P27-Q10; D1 lifecycle L2/L7 and unresolved removal/retention;
D2-C19. This pass obtained no dataset census or approved duration.

**Answer impact:** Apply actual current source duties. Where exact retention is
unqualified, record that activation gap rather than fabricate a period or imply
unlimited custody.

**Permanent fix:** Qualify reached body/evidence/backup retention through their
existing owners, preserving only the required evidence for its accepted purpose
and period. A new product removal policy remains a separate decision.

**Exact clause:** “D3 neither extends retention nor authorizes unlimited raw
history. Existing qualified minimization, retention, correction and disposition
duties remain in force for bodies, derivatives and backups; missing exact policy
is an explicit qualification gap, not permission for indefinite storage.”

### D3-PS10 — Paper consistency could be mistaken for successful continuity

**Categories:** 6, 7, 12, 13, 16–21. **Severity:** High. **Likelihood:** Plausible
if prior synthetic probes are treated as proof of an untested application path.

**What and why:** A source may correctly deny an ordinary reader while a stale
cache, admin API, restored database or shared-work projection behaves differently.
No D3 runtime or missionary comprehension evidence was obtained in this pass.
Neither a universal deny nor an empty list proves usable restricted start.

**Evidence:** Session evidence limits; P27-Q09/Q11/Q12; founder brief §14.

**Answer impact:** Accept the policy with concrete activation proof, not a claim
that it is already implemented. No invented capacity target or incident rate.

**Permanent fix:** Test exact policy across source/service/readers and temporal
changes; prove accepted work survives and legitimate authorized work is usable.
Use representative journeys and measured workloads before setting budgets.

**Exact clause:** “Before activation, prove no new continuity reader, actual
revocation, independently authorized shared work, qualified return/history,
retained-state restoration and usable ordinary journeys through the actual
source boundaries. Documentation and prior disposable database mechanics are
not application qualification; performance budgets require measured workloads.”

## Necessary now, later decisions and proof

The necessary D3 amendments are boundary clarity, truthful UX, no covert
derivative disclosure, preservation of actual obligations, authoritative
revocation and qualified current/history checks. They do not select new
archive/delete/return rights. Existing owner-required retention and actual
source checks remain activation gates for the reached feature.

Proof should include at least these outcomes, without turning every scenario
into a new subsystem:

1. Staff and successor cannot read/count/discover still-unshared preparation
   merely because the missionary becomes unavailable or departs; independently
   authorized shared CRM records remain usable.
2. A real accepted obligation survives the former actor's access loss with the
   qualified accountability/exception path; no false Done, private payload leak,
   guessed recipient or new access grant.
3. Temporary absence causes no invented revocation, cleanup or obligation;
   authoritative revocation fences active sessions and reached derivatives.
4. A returning actor receives the actual current/history result, with neither
   stale permission revival nor a denial invented by D3. Restore/import respects
   later restrictions and accepted effect identities.
5. Stale-looking but legitimate preparation is not silently deleted, archived,
   completed or scored. Owner-required retention/disposition still executes.
6. Representative users correctly explain what staff can see, what Share does,
   and what their current available cleanup/recovery actions actually mean.

These are required proofs, not unresolved hazards to leave in “monitor.” After
qualification, any unauthorized continuity disclosure or disappeared accepted
obligation is an immediate regression to contain through the affected source
owner. This note does not prescribe a new monitoring product or numeric usage,
performance, retention or cleanup budget.

## One next genuine data-quality question, when the grill advances

Do not reask whether staff may access unshared records. The meaningful next
choice is what a missionary intentionally does with an unshared prospect they
no longer want to pursue. It must be framed narrowly enough to exclude shared
CRM facts and independently accepted work. The choice is not forced by D3.

**Situation:** Maya entered an unshared prospect, then decides not to pursue it.
The entry contains only her tentative preparation; it has no independent shared
contact correction or unfinished accepted obligation to dispose of. She wants
it out of her working list and wants to understand whether she can get it back.

**Single product choice:** should that action merely set the preparation aside
for possible reuse, or remove its eligible draft content under the owner's
retention/disposition rules?

- **Set aside:** preserves a source-qualified route to find/reuse the preparation
  while removing active-list clutter. This is a new deliberate lifecycle outcome
  if selected, not an automatic departure rule. It requires clear scope and
  retention; “recoverable” cannot mean “stored forever.”
- **Remove eligible preparation:** better serves minimization when the missionary
  means they no longer need the information, but the UI must honestly distinguish
  permitted content removal from retained evidence, backups and independent
  records. It needs the source owner's actual disposition and recovery limits.

**Recommendation for the next question:** prefer a deliberate **set-aside**
outcome for “not pursuing now,” because that statement does not establish that
contact details are wrong or should be destroyed. Offer deletion only under a
separately qualified removal policy. This is a proposed direction to present and
review, not a D3 amendment, a mandated second button, a new Archive entity, a
retention duration or a promise that every historical context can be restored.
If the founder instead specifically means “I no longer need these details,”
the minimization case for removing eligible preparation becomes stronger.

Correction of already-shared canonical contact facts remains another genuine
frontier item under brief §7. Do not combine that question, departure disposition,
retention duration, returning-user history and the above active-list choice into
one ratification. The next question should be asked only after the current D3
review and any material amendments are presented.
