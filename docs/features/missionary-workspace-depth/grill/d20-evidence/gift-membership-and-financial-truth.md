# D20 evidence — gift membership and financial truth

Research date: **2026-10-16 UTC**. Research only. The founder selected one current
special-gift follow-up covering compatible gifts received before the follow-up is
handled. The proposed clauses below make that grouping precise; they do not
select special-gift thresholds, historical-pattern detection, a new permission,
an accounting policy, or automatic communication. D19's ratified first-support
purpose remains separate. No app, schema, formal owner document, financial data,
or provider was changed or queried for this note. Proof items are required
qualification cases, **not executed tests**.

## Recommendation

Use one shared independent follow-up task with **durable per-gift membership and
coverage owned by the existing shared task owner**. P28 owns special-gift
qualification and source-qualified purpose admission; it does not maintain a
parallel task or completion ledger. P13's header identifies one business
contribution; its designation lines provide the exact relevant money evidence.
A line, posting, credit row,
provider delivery, criterion version, or task retry is not another gift.

The conceptual member identity is **Tenant + source contribution identity +
source-qualified follow-up Party + typed ministry subject + P28 follow-up
purpose**. Keep its source evidence and revisions separately. The initial P13
adapter resolves the source contribution to the exact contribution header;
another producer must explicitly qualify equivalent identity rather than using
an external event ID as a substitute. This is a logical identity contract, not
a proposed universal registry or final SQL schema.

Compatible members share all of:

- Exact Tenant and typed P9 Ministry Assignment or Ministry Project; either
  subject pins its Legal Entity. A public page, designation label, missionary
  login, Support Assignment, or current campaign hierarchy is not that subject.
- The same owner-qualified follow-up Party, with an eligible recipient/purpose
  route. This is not necessarily the contribution's frozen legal donor. The
  DAF sponsor, advisor, organization contact, household, and member are not
  interchangeable identities.
- The same accountable responsibility scope and compatible P28 special-gift
  follow-up purpose, independent-task completion authority, and currently
  permitted action/projection. Reassignment changes responsibility through its
  owner; it does not create a new gift or grant historical access.

The group is an ongoing **human work unit**, not the financial owner or a
cross-gift transaction. Its durable identity must survive changed member order,
withdrawal of its earliest member, and an empty current eligible set. Do not
derive group identity from `MIN(gift_date)`, `MIN(header_id)`, the first visible
row, a current total, or a browser selection. Once the work is handled, later
gifts do not append to that handled coverage. Their own eligible uncovered
membership follows the current task owner's next-work contract; this note does
not invent a time window or a successor engine.

Different currencies need not split one human follow-up. Show each authorized
gift's exact currency and relevant allocation/recognition meaning independently.
No mixed-currency scalar, converted eligibility threshold, blended hard/soft
credit total, or organization-wide header amount is needed for grouping. A
same-currency subtotal is optional presentation only and may exist solely when
its owner measure and nonduplicating membership are qualified; the minimal
profile needs no subtotal.

### One reviewed gift changes before Done

**Recommend a material-source-change conflict for the exact reviewed completion
command, followed by ordinary refresh and retry.** If Maya reviewed gifts A and
B, and A is refunded/reallocated or its recipient authority changes before
acceptance, the old command must not silently claim that both were handled or
silently substitute a different set. The source independently withdraws or
updates A's generated requirement; refresh explains the currently permitted
change and offers the remaining valid B. A fresh command can handle B. This is
not a permanent whole-group hold and cannot delay the financial correction.

An unrelated new gift C is different: C remains uncovered and actionable, while
the reviewed A/B command may succeed if all of its relevant evidence is still
current. Do not use a broad group revision that gratuitously invalidates every
review when an unrelated member arrives. Define the command's relevant member,
purpose, source, responsibility and permission fences exactly.

A mixed result such as “B handled; A no longer requires this follow-up” is also
technically possible, but requires an explicitly authorized mixed-outcome
command and truthful per-member receipt. It is **not** inferred from an old
“handle these reviewed gifts” action, and adds little to the minimal initial
flow. The recommendation is the simpler conflict/refresh contract. Existing
qualified interaction recording remains available for a call that actually
happened; a stale financial review cannot erase that history or force the user
to claim a financial fact.

## Proposed minimal owner extension

P28 needs a **qualified special-gift occurrence producer**, not another ledger.
P13 supplies contribution/header identity, current effective line evidence,
financial finality/correction and source origin. P14 supplies the exact permitted
recognition/recipient route where used. P16 supplies the authoritative commitment
comparison and its eligibility when that reason applies. P9 supplies stable
subject/responsibility identity; P12 supplies current authority. P28 owns the
bounded special-gift qualification and source-qualified gift-purpose admission.
The existing shared task owner owns operational group membership, immutable
reviewed-set disposition receipts and their per-member human coverage. Source
admission evidence and human disposition evidence remain distinct owner facts;
neither is a duplicate operational ledger in P28.

The producer must name the exact participating owner revisions, criterion
identity/version, contribution/line coverage, subject binding, recognition route,
prospective origin/activation evidence and current qualification result. A
declared source revision is insufficient unless it actually changes/fences every
material fact: P13 `effective_seq` alone does not prove that a P14 attribution or
P16 commitment/application has not changed. Require a coherent owner receipt or
current source command with the appropriate invalidation fences; the adapter
cannot simply join independently cached “latest” rows and declare them current.

Accept/update the source-qualified gift-purpose admission, its required local
evidence and durable task projection intent atomically in the source owner's
command. The shared task owner consumes that intent idempotently to maintain
operational membership. Its disposition command atomically records the exact
reviewed-set outcome, coverage and required local evidence; P28 does not write a
second completion record. Materialization may be asynchronous and recoverable
without losing source admission or pretending both owners committed together.
Source posting may precede qualification/materialization; recognition lag must
not roll back or block money. Until the affected source qualification is
coherent, show its permitted updating/unavailable state and do not fabricate an
eligible gift.
Reconcile committed source occurrences after lost/delayed events so a missed
notification does not become permanently missing work.

Exact special-gift criteria and permitted historical detection remain open.
Grouping does not authorize inferred exceptional generosity, a default amount,
automatic promotion of unmatched money, or use of a financial posting-risk
threshold. D20 consumes a qualified result; it cannot claim that producer is
already implemented. E7 gates only an affected unresolved association; unrelated
qualified direct scope continues. C-01 is not resolved by task grouping.

## Eight-dimension concerns and required clauses

### FG20-01 — Allocation and recognition fan-out multiply one gift

1. **Harm:** duplicate follow-up or one gift silently marked handled multiple times.
2. **Severity:** high relationship/data-integrity harm.
3. **Likelihood:** high if membership is keyed to lines, credits, events or criterion revisions.
4. **Cause:** confusing source evidence rows with the P13 business contribution.
5. **Evidence:** S02:784, 791–822; S04:325–345; S06:1058–1067.
6. **Implication:** one covered member per exact gift/Party/subject/purpose; evidence may be many rows.
7. **Clause:** “Multiple allocation lines, recognition paths, reason codes, source revisions or deliveries for one qualified contribution/Party/subject/purpose SHALL converge on the same membership. Source deduplication and canonical alias reconciliation SHALL precede admission; equal amounts, dates or names SHALL NOT deduplicate distinct contributions.”
8. **Proof:** one gift with two same-subject lines; one person direct plus household recognition; two distinct equal-value gifts; duplicate event with another request key; source header alias repair; one gift to two genuinely distinct subjects.

### FG20-02 — Legal donor is mistaken for the follow-up recipient

1. **Harm:** wrong-person work, exposure of donor facts, or prohibited member correspondence.
2. **Severity:** high; critical where protected identity/contact details are exposed.
3. **Likelihood:** high if a header donor or any positive credit becomes the recipient automatically.
4. **Cause:** collapsing payer, supporter recognition, purpose eligibility and contact authority.
5. **Evidence:** S04:325; S05:398–449; S07:1023, 1167–1177.
6. **Implication:** grouping consumes an exact source-qualified follow-up Party/purpose route.
7. **Clause:** “The source SHALL qualify the exact follow-up Party and allowed purpose independently of the legal-donor snapshot and recognized amount. Grouping SHALL NOT fan out to spouses, members, solicitors, organization contacts or all recognition rows, infer an undisclosed DAF advisor, or override church-member correspondence exclusion. Distinct authorized recipient purposes SHALL not be merged merely because they share a payer.”
8. **Proof:** two advisors behind one sponsor; anonymous advisor; organizational contact distinct from donor; household derivation; matched employee versus employer; via-church member who also has an unrelated direct gift.

### FG20-03 — Subject or responsibility grouping crosses an ownership boundary

1. **Harm:** one worker consumes another's work or sees another ministry/entity's gifts.
2. **Severity:** high, potentially critical for restricted ministry information.
3. **Likelihood:** medium/high if grouping uses page owner, current missionary ID, labels or shared assignee alone.
4. **Cause:** inferring compatibility from a convenient UI field instead of typed owners.
5. **Evidence:** S09; S01:169–181; S14:5–29.
6. **Implication:** exact typed subject and current responsibility/action compatibility are mandatory.
7. **Clause:** “Grouping SHALL require the same Tenant, exact typed ministry subject and its Legal Entity, qualified follow-up Party, compatible purpose/completion authority and accountable responsibility scope. Transfer, participant change, page change or identity merge SHALL reconcile through the owner without expanding access, creating new gift membership, or erasing historical attribution.”
8. **Proof:** same name/different subjects; same Party/different entities; two legitimate responsibility roles; worker replacement; merge collision; transfer while completion is pending; former worker loses access but work survives.

### FG20-04 — Group amounts become invented financial truth

1. **Harm:** overstated support, false generosity claims or cross-currency threshold results.
2. **Severity:** high.
3. **Likelihood:** high if header totals or recognition sums are convenient display inputs.
4. **Cause:** summing hard and soft credit, unrelated allocations, fee cover or mixed currencies.
5. **Evidence:** S02:784, 818, 822; S05:418–444; S06:1063–1069, 1127.
6. **Implication:** show source-qualified per-gift meaning; arithmetic is unnecessary for grouped work.
7. **Clause:** “Each displayed item SHALL preserve its source currency and exact permitted allocation/recognition vocabulary. Grouping SHALL neither expose unrelated header allocations nor sum legal and recognition values or currencies. Any permitted subtotal SHALL be separately source-qualified and shall not decide membership or fulfillment.”
8. **Proof:** split gift containing hidden allocation and fee cover; full-to-each recognition; USD and JPY in one human follow-up; two equal gifts; partial correction; no scalar group total accidentally emitted in notification/export/accessibility text.

### FG20-05 — Stale reviewed scope silently becomes partial or expanded completion

1. **Harm:** unreviewed gift is consumed, changed gift is falsely handled, or successful work is silently dropped.
2. **Severity:** high.
3. **Likelihood:** high under concurrent arrival/refund/reassignment without exact membership fences.
4. **Cause:** completing “all current gifts,” validating only the task row, or treating stale scope as best-effort success.
5. **Evidence:** S10:630–634; S11:111–113; S01:173–181.
6. **Implication:** complete the exact reviewed set atomically; material relevant changes conflict; unrelated additions remain residual.
7. **Clause:** “The completion command SHALL name the exact reviewed memberships and relevant qualification revisions, and re-prove current source, purpose, responsibility and permission under their invalidation fences. A material change to a reviewed item SHALL reject that human completion without partial coverage or fabricated outcome. Independent source correction may proceed. Refresh SHALL offer remaining valid work. An unrelated later member SHALL neither be consumed nor alone invalidate the reviewed command.”
8. **Proof:** A/B reviewed, C arrives; A reverses before completion; completion precedes reversal; amount or attribution changes; stale assignee; two tabs/different request keys; lost committed response; changed same-key payload. Verify both orders and the per-member receipt, not only task status.

### FG20-06 — Independent cached sources create a falsely current qualification

1. **Harm:** follow-up on reversed, misattributed or no-longer-above-commitment support.
2. **Severity:** high.
3. **Likelihood:** high because recognition is explicitly eventual and P16 owns a separate conserved journal.
4. **Cause:** using the roster, P13 cursor alone, raw provider events or independent latest-row reads as a coherent receipt.
5. **Evidence:** S02:792, 816–820; S04:345–375; S08:983–995, 1027–1031.
6. **Implication:** qualify the affected source adapter and current multi-owner evidence before admission or consequential acceptance.
7. **Clause:** “A special-gift result SHALL certify its exact source facts, currency/subject coverage, recognition and applicable commitment revisions, criterion version and invalidation fences. Behind, ambiguous or incoherent evidence SHALL not assert current eligibility. Financial posting SHALL remain independent of downstream recognition/task availability, with durable reconciliation of later-qualified occurrences.”
8. **Proof:** P13 correction commits while P14 consumer lags; attribution changes with unchanged P13 cursor; pledge reduction changes P16 result; out-of-order correction; lost task intent materialization; no source-qualified receipt; current recheck racing invalidation.

### FG20-07 — Source withdrawal destroys human history or repeatedly reopens coverage

1. **Harm:** erased real interaction, lost accepted undertaking or repeated thanks for the same gift.
2. **Severity:** high.
3. **Likelihood:** high if eligibility is treated as a recurring task state or terminal rows are deleted.
4. **Cause:** conflating current generated requirement, human deliverable and permanent gift-purpose coverage.
5. **Evidence:** S01:171–173; S10:622–628; S12; S02:789–817.
6. **Implication:** retain per-member source history separately from human handling; repair the same unconsumed purpose only through an explicit qualified owner command.
7. **Clause:** “Source correction SHALL update or withdraw only the affected generated requirement, retaining membership and outcome history. Separately accepted undertakings SHALL remain owned and actionable. A repaired qualifying result MAY restore the same still-unconsumed gift-purpose through an explicit current owner-qualified correction; replay, a new criterion version or eligibility toggling alone SHALL NOT reopen completed/Not needed coverage. A genuinely different new gift remains independently eligible; D19's lifetime-first consumption rule SHALL NOT be applied to all special gifts.”
8. **Proof:** A withdrawn while B remains; partial refund still qualifies; full refund then erroneous-refund repair; repeated qualify/withdraw/qualify; source correction after actual call/Done/Not needed; claimed/opened task versus deliberately accepted promise; later distinct live gift after earlier special gift was handled.

### FG20-08 — Historical repair or import becomes new acquisition work

1. **Harm:** flood of retrospective follow-ups, repeated old thanks, or lost legitimately delayed live work.
2. **Severity:** high operational/relationship harm.
3. **Likelihood:** high if arrival time or current eligibility substitutes for source origin.
4. **Cause:** losing original live/import provenance during reimport, correction or redesignation.
5. **Evidence:** S06:1128; S03:1093–1099; S01:171; S14:4859–4861.
6. **Implication:** preserve original occurrence and prospective activation evidence; do not choose a lookback here.
7. **Clause:** “Historical imports/backfills and historical reclassification SHALL not create catch-up special-gift work merely because they are observed or become classifiable today. A delayed genuinely live occurrence or correction repairing that same originally live unconsumed occurrence MAY qualify under its original source identity and approved activation contract. Source gift date, recorded time and delivery time SHALL remain distinct.”
8. **Proof:** old imported gift; repeated import; historical redesignation into another ministry; late live event; held live attribution repaired; gift-date correction crossing a year; no arbitrary age cutoff drops a valid live occurrence.

### FG20-09 — Grouping silently selects special-gift financial criteria

1. **Harm:** catch-up or advance payments are called exceptional; changed plans create fictional new gifts.
2. **Severity:** high correctness/relationship harm.
3. **Likelihood:** high if the consumer compares raw gift value with a nullable monthly commitment.
4. **Cause:** treating missing matches as no commitment, current excess as a new event, or posting-risk thresholds as stewardship policy.
5. **Evidence:** S08:966–1031, 1380–1393; S01:167; P13 posting-review predicate at 979–987.
6. **Implication:** exact criteria/historical detection remain unresolved; grouped presentation cannot fill the gap.
7. **Clause:** “D20 SHALL consume a qualified special/above-commitment occurrence, not derive it from visible amounts, missing data, reduced commitments, recognition relationships, unmatched money or posting-risk thresholds. P16's effective application and lifecycle facts SHALL retain their own meaning. Selecting grouping SHALL not ratify thresholds, history detection, noncash conversion or retroactive activation.”
8. **Proof:** reduced pledge yields applied-above-current-commitment without a new gift; catch-up across named periods; advance payment; unmatched gift; expectancy without money; pending ACH; historical credit backfill; threshold policy change alone.

### FG20-10 — A group count or receipt reveals a restricted member

1. **Harm:** hidden donor/gift existence, amount, relationship or ministry is disclosed through a secondary surface.
2. **Severity:** high, potentially critical for protected identities.
3. **Likelihood:** medium/high if the group caches full membership or reports skipped-hidden counts.
4. **Cause:** applying permission checks only to visible rows while totals, receipts or task titles use the full group.
5. **Evidence:** S06:1082–1088; S07:1167–1177; S11:113; S14:23–29.
6. **Implication:** the group is no new history audience; receipts are current-authority projections of retained evidence.
7. **Clause:** “Group titles, counts, lists, completion receipts, notifications, exports and history SHALL obey current existence/field/purpose authority. Revoked or hidden members SHALL not be exposed as a count, reason or skipped-item clue. When the exact reviewed command cannot remain authorized, return a permitted refresh outcome without fabricating success or revealing protected membership.”
8. **Proof:** restricted gift arrives; access revoked between review and Done; actor/tenant switch after response loss; past receipt under narrowed access; one hidden split allocation; transfer with no historical grant; notification and screen-reader count parity.

## Boundaries and verification status

No new accounting/history engine, money write, per-gift financial correction
authority, generic grouping preferences, shadow recognition store, or contact
right is needed. P13/P14/P16 owners need the bounded qualified occurrence and
invalidation seam; P28 needs source-qualified purpose admission and its durable
intent. The existing shared task owner needs exact operational gift membership,
immutable reviewed-set disposition/coverage receipts and the corresponding
conflict/correction contract. These are proposed owner amendments needed to
implement the selected behavior, not proof that such adapters already exist.

No thresholds, comparison history, permanent lookback, notification defaults,
noncash eligibility or automatic donor-facing message has been selected here.
The suggested conflict/refresh behavior is a minimal intent-preserving review
amendment. If a mixed per-member completion result is chosen instead, its exact
authorized scope and receipt must be explicit before implementation; the two
contracts must not coexist implicitly.

This note executes no financial, database, provider or protocol probes. Its proof
matrix is an implementation qualification obligation. A synthetic grouped-task
probe can demonstrate its assumed coverage protocol; it cannot certify the real
financial qualifier, special-gift criterion, temporal subject mapping, current
permission adapter or recognition lag handling.
