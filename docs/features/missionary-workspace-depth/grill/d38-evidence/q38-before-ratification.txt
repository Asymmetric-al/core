## The single choice

Maya receives a new referral. The organization already chooses the follow-up
period used when an eligible expired referral is deliberately continued.

**Should new referrals use that same organization-set period, or a separately
configured initial period?**

- **A — One organization-set period (recommended).** Authorized staff set one
  follow-up period for both new referrals and deliberately continued referrals.
  Maya sees the applicable end date without choosing a date herself.
- **B — Separate organization-set periods.** Authorized staff can set a different
  period for a new referral and for a deliberately continued referral. Maya still
  sees the applicable end date without choosing a date herself.

A keeps one understandable setting and avoids a distinction without a demonstrated
need. B is the strongest flexibility alternative: an organization may have a real
reason to allow different windows for first follow-up and a later return. No
research inspected here establishes that ministries generally need either rule.
The recommendation is a simplicity judgment, not a measured ministry norm.

Both choices preserve deliberate continuation, current permissions and existing
accepted end dates. Neither automatically renews a referral, authorizes contact,
changes the original introduction or extends data retention. The choice is the
scope of organization configuration, not a number of days or the new-referral
clock's start event.

## User journey and implementation consequences

Under A, staff maintain one **Referral follow-up period** with helper text saying
it applies to new and deliberately continued referrals. Under B, the settings
clearly distinguish **New referral period** and **Continued referral period**.
Those are illustrative labels, not an accepted schema or new policy. In both,
Maya sees the applicable end date in ordinary referral work and avoids a second
date-entry decision. No routine per-person/project exceptions are bundled into
this question.

The selected answer's review must qualify the initial source event, late-recorded
or imported introductions, original versus accepted dates, supported finite
bounds, trusted settings and current source disclosure. Merely saving an old
introduction now cannot silently be assumed to grant a fresh window. The exact
initial anchor is **not selected here**, and no ready source producer is claimed.
Continuation's ratified elapsed-day/acceptance semantics remain intact.

Any configuration adoption or change must preserve already accepted windows and
history, handle retained tenant settings deliberately, and apply the qualified
new policy prospectively. A missing setting is not zero, infinity or permission
to choose an invented fallback. Current consent/privacy/retention restrictions
remain independently governing. Defaulting, migration, concurrency, retries and
stale views are engineering obligations to reconcile after the scope choice,
not numerical questions for the founder to guess.
