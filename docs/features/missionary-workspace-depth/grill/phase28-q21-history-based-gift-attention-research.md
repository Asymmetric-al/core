## The single product choice

Grace has an established giving history for Maya's ministry, but no recorded
commitment. Her new gift is much larger than her earlier gifts. There is no other
qualified evidence that establishes it as extra support.

**Should that comparison with Grace's past giving itself create follow-up?**

- **A — Explicit qualifying facts only (recommended).** Keep permitted giving
  history available as context, but do not create automatic follow-up solely
  because a gift differs from earlier gifts. Required special-gift and
  above-commitment follow-up still uses its qualified source criteria; Maya can
  create ordinary follow-up herself.
- **B — Also flag unusual giving.** Allow a qualified comparison with the
  supporter's own giving history to create a clearly explained review task.
  Present it as a possible change worth reviewing, not confirmed extra support
  or a judgment about the donor's generosity.

This is an illustrative case, not evidence of an interviewed missionary workflow.
“No recorded commitment” must be established by the qualified owner, not inferred
from a missing row in Maya's visible list. A does not mean commitments-only,
disable already-required native gift tasks, or presume a staff-marked special
flag exists. B does not authorize donor ratings, a message, a financial mutation
or use of every accessible historical data source.

**Recommendation:** A keeps automatically generated work tied to explicit
qualification and avoids adding a noisy comparison product before its value is
established. B's strongest benefit is noticing meaningful changes for supporters
without commitments, including changes that a fixed amount threshold would miss.
Its cost is uncertainty, explanation and history-coverage requirements, not just
an additional comparison query. These are product tradeoffs; no measured false
positive rate, productivity benefit or donor preference has been established.
