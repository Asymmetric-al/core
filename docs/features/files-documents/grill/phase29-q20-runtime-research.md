# Phase 29 Q20 runtime/source research — deliberate recipient handoff

Research date: 2026-10-21, Asia/Bangkok. Q01–Q19 are ratified; Q20 has no selected answer. This note researches one proposed product fork. It does not implement or activate a notification, change canonical owners, or authorize any provider send.

## Recommendation and the genuinely open choice

The next useful decision is the default for the optional recipient-email choice in one deliberate **fresh access grant** review: **A, initially selected with opt-out**, or **B, initially unselected with explicit opt-in**. Both use the same integrated review and confirmation. The source occurrence and communication remain different authorities under either default.

My recommendation is **A**, a visible, initially selected **Email the recipient** choice when the exact contract and current source/communication gates permit it. This is product judgment about completing the usual staff handoff and reducing unintentionally omitted notification, not a conclusion forced by existing code or evidence of an optimal default. The final action must accurately disclose whether it grants access alone or also requests an email. Staff can deselect email; an unavailable or prohibited email must not silently become sent, move the access clock, or create a different access method.

The strongest alternative is **B**, the same visible choice initially unselected. Staff deliberately select email in the same review when useful. This reduces surprise external communication and suits already-coordinated handoffs; its tradeoff is unintentionally omitted notification if staff overlook the choice. It does not require a second post-grant step or inherently worse UI. Either option permits a grant-only outcome and keeps source success distinct from message success.

A later separate notification or governed message-repair action is only an alternative/recovery path where its own source contract permits it. Q20's default comparison does not itself introduce that capability or authorize repeated messages. It is never permission to copy a private bearer URL into an arbitrary channel.

Keep the question's cohort explicit: fresh unfinished admissions under Q13–Q15 and fresh completed-read admissions under Q08/Q16–Q19 are a coherent shared handoff decision if the founder is shown both. An initial collection request, fulfillment notice, reminder, automatic repeat grant, staff bell, digest, or notification of a revocation is not silently selected with it. The source-owned end, exact recipient and admitted actions are already selected for these fresh grants.

No earlier unresolved founder decision was identified that must block this fork. A potential no-Party protected-action conflict is already explicitly resolved in Q01 as a required compatible owner amendment. Its missing implementation remains an activation gate, not a reason to repeat the identity question. The exact source-to-message contract and capability mapping are necessary specification/qualification work; choosing the default does not assert those components exist today.

## Actual adjacent runtime and why it is insufficient

### Approval notifications: useful independence, unsuitable drop-in handoff

`packages/api/src/admin/contribution-operations/approval-notifications.ts:39–45` expressly gives approval in-app notifications an on-by-default posture while email needs tenant enablement and personal opt-in. This is a staff correction approval workflow, not the document-request recipient contract.

`approval-notifications.ts:279–315` upserts notification rows on `(tenant_id,dedupe_key)` with duplicate-ignore and returns newly inserted keys. `:532–589` rechecks pending status, inserts, records audit, and then sends only those newly inserted email rows. Replaying a recorded row stays quiet. It avoids automatically resending old entries after a provider key's retention window; it does not demonstrate durable recovery of a crash between insertion and actual email submission. That counterexample is a source-derived copying hazard, not a claim that a production document-request email was lost.

`packages/api/src/admin/contribution-operations/approval-notification-email.ts:14–20,169–187` describes synchronous best-effort delivery and skips absent settings. `:197–214` resolves recipient `profiles` inside a tenant; `:225–258` sends direct rendered content with a provider key derived from the notification key. Returned counts distinguish sent/failed/skipped locally but do not implement the P17 permanent prepared-message/submission-evidence state machine. Its `profiles.email` recipient lookup is not a native request-only principal/closed no-Party authority mapping.

The comments at `approval-notification-email.ts:150–153` cannot establish unlimited “cannot double-send” behavior. Actual call-site insertion filtering supplies a different bounded guard, and provider idempotency is not permanent business idempotency. No broad vulnerability assertion is needed to reject this helper as feature proof.

### Receipt delivery: useful reviewed choice, different recipient and purpose

`packages/api/src/admin/contribution-operations/receipt-delivery.ts:7–13` already distinguishes a saved correction from a staff delivery choice. `:16–32,46–62` defines email/PDF/defer and policy/capability defaults. `:100–160` computes current option availability based on donor email, email preference and actor capability, with fallback selection.

The reusable idea is one visible, server-validated choice in the source review, plus clear unavailability. The donor consent interpretation, PDF/defer fallbacks, tax receipt requirements and fallback selection order must not become Phase29 rules. An unavailable document-access email must not silently turn into a PDF attachment, fabricated donor, new recipient, copied secret link or a different grant.

### Current transport and consent helpers are lower-level seams

`packages/email/resend/send.ts:123–185` validates the caller's idempotency key and send options. `:229–262` builds a payload and returns `success: true` when `response.error` is absent, allowing an optional provider id in that result. `:265–309` funnels timeouts/errors through retry and `success: false` results. It does not itself persist the source occurrence, permanent communication identity, prepared artifact, pre-I/O fence or indeterminate evidence required by the adopted P17 contract. A wrapper result alone cannot prove “recipient notified,” delivery or human awareness.

`packages/api/src/email/consent.ts:110–137,147–169` loads donor flags and address-based suppression. `:31–35` and `:175–176` document failed lookup as fail-closed. Useful error semantics do not prove the proper request-only recipient consent/purpose classification. Do not infer the Phase29 message's legal classification or recipient policy from the helper's comments; the exact source/message owner must decide the adopted contract.

`packages/api/src/workflows/dispatch.ts:19–36,52–64` explicitly limits Inngest event-id dedupe to handoff and leaves product idempotency to the source. `packages/api/src/workflows/ledger.ts:117–175` upserts/reloads by tenant/key and throws if recovery lookup fails; it does not compare the complete immutable material intent. Reuse the existing durability boundaries where qualified, not the assumption that a new key or a queue result proves a new grant/message is legitimate.

A bounded source search over `packages/api/src`, `packages/email`, `packages/auth`, and SQL migrations for the documented plan compiler, `communication_intents`, occurrence-token and selector/verifier terms returned no matching implementation. This is limited evidence, not a census of every naming alternative or deployed database. It agrees with the owner documents' target/deferred state and does not establish native delivery readiness.

## Minimum permanent constraints for either option

1. **Separate authority, coherent deliberate outcome.** A source-authorized grant and any selected communication request are distinguishable facts. The source must durably preserve the reviewed notification choice/handoff meaning with its operation outcome; asynchronous preparation/provider delivery is not a condition that resets or fabricates grant issuance. A failed response or unknown outcome is reconciled rather than granting again. There is no second notification database or generic event engine.
2. **Do not use notification selection to change source policy.** A visible per-occurrence choice can narrow an adopted contract's allowed step; it cannot edit the tenant Delivery Plan, waive communication capability/consent/readiness, select a new address or recipient, alter scope, or extend an end. The exact grant-administration capability does not by itself prove authority to send every kind of message.
3. **Stable source effect, distinct communication occurrence.** Exact grant replay returns the old grant and old notification choice/outcome; it must not silently add an omitted email. A later explicit notification, if authorized by the selected product contract, is a new communication occurrence over the same still-eligible grant, not another grant. Changed recipient/contact/action terms are not an identical frozen message retry. Reissue of a credential is producer-owned and creates a new communication/prepared identity without resetting source access.
4. **Freeze and re-prove the proper things.** The message's reviewed source identity, safe facts and action reference stay pinned; current source/recipient/action/suppression/readiness fences still apply immediately before dispatch and protected use. A delayed worker does not send an expired or withdrawn “access available” invitation merely because the email was initially selected. Late accepted delivery evidence does not resurrect the access grant.
5. **No implication that delivery confers access.** Mailbox proof and exact current authorization remain Q01-owned. No automatic GET/HEAD redemption, generic email verification, hidden donor membership, raw reusable bearer-link copying, public URL, inline private attachment or provider-host fallback follows from this choice.
6. **Truthful recovery and adequate grant-only use.** Show confirmed grant state/end independently from email requested/queued/suppressed/failed/unknown/accepted state. Email skipped or declined should leave a useful source-authorized entry/help route where the source supports one, without promising already-available native UI. Source repair can recover proof/delivery without a new 30-day period. Do not block legitimate grant-only handling on a marketing integration, or claim every unavailable notification has a safe alternate channel.
7. **Keep scope small.** No reminders, expiry email, read receipt, notification center, attachment, freeform message composer, recipient picker, provider fallback, repeated-send policy, or automatic subscription is needed to decide this handoff. Notification actions are not reversible once provider acceptance may have occurred.
