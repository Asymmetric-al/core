# Phase 29 Q05 — earlier submitted files versus a concise submission history

Research inspected 20 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. Q01–Q04 and their reviewed amendments are ratified according to the parent. This prepares the next founder decision and presumes no Q05 answer. Scope is the ordinary native private-document request while the recipient currently has access. It does not decide how long that access lasts after closure or change specialized source histories.

Official-documentation research only. No product registration, authenticated comparator test, real submission, download, provider mutation, private-data inspection or code change occurred. Examples of participant needs below are hypotheses, not observed ministry demand.

## Recommendation

Recommend **current eligible files plus a concise recipient-safe receipt/correction history**, without ordinary self-service opening or downloading of superseded files. Show enough to understand what Asym received, which correction was received, and which files are now relevant. Preserve earlier evidence under its source's retention and staff-access rules. If someone needs an earlier file, use an existing source-authorized help/delivery route; do not create a new approval workflow merely for this decision.

For this question, earlier content means a prior occurrence that is no longer in the current source-authorized file set, such as the exact member corrected by a successor. An older supporting member that remains current is not historical merely because of its timestamp; it keeps its applicable Q02/Q04 actions. A multi-file item can have several current eligible members. Q05 must not revoke one of them by sorting for a single newest upload.

This is a bounded product judgment, not a conclusion that historical self-service is inherently unsafe or turns a task into a drive. The strongest alternative is a **task-specific history with qualified access to the recipient's earlier files**. That can help someone compare what they sent and understand a dispute or correction without staff intervention. It is compatible with the no-general-drive direction if current permission, exact versions, safety and source restrictions apply. It adds a real disclosure capability and a more complex distinction between current evidence and earlier, possibly wrong or sensitive, files.

The external evidence does not establish a universal default for superseded original attachments. Several prominent features called history or original download refer to prior applications, form-summary PDFs, administrative audit, or form-builder revisions rather than an applicant's earlier uploaded bytes. Do not use those labels as proof that Asym must expose all retained versions.

## Why the recommendation is A for this ordinary profile

The immediate participant questions are framed here as “Did the file arrive?”, “Did my correction arrive?”, and “Which files will staff consider?” Those are proposed core task questions, not measured frequency claims. Current content plus truthful correction history can answer them without making a retained upload self-service accessible merely because it is retained. B would grant task-specific historical access under current permission, not enduring or permanent access.

The most difficult case for B is not storage size: it is a file corrected because it contains the wrong person's information, an inappropriate attachment, hidden material or a disallowed format. Keeping it for source evidence does not settle whether it should remain available to every currently mapped request participant. Q01's recipient/actor binding and current source policy must govern even apparently “own” older material.

The most difficult case for A is an ordinary user trying to understand exactly what changed when filenames are the same. A log cannot show the missing page or the old contents. The source-approved help route must be real and understandable; “contact support” cannot conceal the absence of any permitted way to resolve a legitimate source dispute. If Conrad judges that routine content comparison is central to this ordinary request, B is a coherent choice. It would not require relaxing the no-general-drive boundary.

The recommendation should therefore be explained as a scope/friction/disclosure tradeoff, not “current-only is modern best practice” or “users never need old files.” There is no evidence here to support either blanket claim.

## Clear UX under either answer

1. **Keep the current item status outside the history control.** The current correction may be checking, unusable or awaiting review. It is not necessarily accepted, safe to preview or downloadable merely because it is current. Q03's predecessor fence and no-silent-revival rule remain.
2. **Use a concise history in the same item.** Show attributable source events, such as file received and correction received, with understandable dates/timezones and the relevant current/earlier relationship. Do not expose the staff audit log, every parser retry, internal note, private actor identity or the entire account history.
3. **Use accurate event language.** “Correction received” is a receipt fact; “Accepted” requires the source decision. “Earlier file” or “Corrected by a later submission” can communicate currentness without implying deletion. A retry of the same operation is not another correction event.
4. **Hide only secondary detail.** A compact Earlier submissions disclosure may reduce clutter, but an active error, missing required action or current status cannot live only inside it. [GOV.UK Details](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/details/) reserves disclosure for information only some people need and warns against hiding generally needed information. Applying that principle to history is product reasoning, not evidence that all histories must be collapsed.
5. **Choose semantics for the information.** File facts can use a definition/summary list. A chronological event sequence should use an appropriate list, not force each event into a table-like card. [GOV.UK Summary list](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/summary-list/) distinguishes key/value facts from simple lists and tabular data. Keep Core's shared components and tokens.
6. **Make repeated actions unambiguous if B is chosen.** Each View/Download identifies its exact earlier file and submission context, with a persistent Earlier submission label. Do not let a historical link silently resolve to current bytes. [WCAG 2.2 Link Purpose](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/link-purpose-in-context.html) supports meaningful names or programmatically related context.
7. **Do not announce an entire timeline on every update.** An actual important new status can be programmatically announced without taking focus; the static readable history remains available. [WCAG 2.2 Status Messages](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html) does not call for endless live narration. Actual keyboard, speech, screen-reader and mobile behavior remains untested.

No timeline component installation, graph viewer, diff editor, “restore” button, AI comparison or additional confirmation ceremony is implied. A short history can be rendered with existing shared primitives. Qualification must include similar filenames, multiple members, long/RTL text, correction races and current permissions.

## Authority and lifecycle points to review after selection

- **Metadata itself is disclosure.** A receipt log can expose a filename, count, actor, timestamp or the existence of an earlier confidential submission. It needs an explicit participant-safe projection and current rights; it is not automatically public because the byte action is absent.
- **Historical business truth and current delivery eligibility differ.** A prior successful receipt can remain true while its content is no longer permitted or retained. A later restriction must not rewrite receipt history or imply an old file never arrived; it can require suppressing particular details from this audience.
- **Readback does not undo correction.** B's old file view cannot make that file current, restart a review, move an acceptance or bypass Q03's fence. A separately authorized source correction remains a distinct act.
- **History cannot be discovered through a global uploader query.** Use exact request/item/member lineage and current actor/recipient scope. Reassignments, shared mailboxes, delegated action and reused bytes must not merge histories.
- **Multi-file requests need member-specific lineage.** Correcting one member preserves unrelated supporting documents. “Current” is not necessarily a single universal newest-file pointer.
- **If B is selected, every older representation needs present qualification.** Old clearance, a cached preview or a previously issued download URL does not permanently authorize retrieval. Preview and original delivery can have different current outcomes; Q04's browser/save truth and device-copy limit still apply.
- **No historical link is a lasting capability.** Existing stale pages/bookmarks must reauthorize. The product must not issue a permanent archive promise merely because a history entry is displayed today.
- **Retention and post-closure access remain separate.** A cannot delete earlier evidence just because the recipient cannot open it. B cannot retain or expose everything forever just because earlier content is useful. The source owns disposition, and the later continuity question decides participant access duration.

These are implications for the founder answer's adversarial review, not newly ratified rules. No numeric history length, pagination size, access window, retention period or incident threshold is invented here.

## Suggested next question

“After someone sends a correction, should the request show the current files plus a short record of what was received and corrected, or should they also be able to open and download their earlier submitted files?

I recommend current files plus the short history for the ordinary request. They can see that the correction arrived and which file is current without repeatedly exposing older files they may have sent by mistake. Earlier evidence stays under the source's rules. The stronger alternative gives more self-service access for comparing past submissions, but needs clear historical labels and separate current permission. Neither choice decides permanent access or creates a personal drive.”

Ask this before combining it with post-closure access duration. The question is what historical content is exposed while access presently exists; the next continuity decision can then determine when that access ends. If internal owner research establishes a stricter source requirement, preserve it rather than treating this generic profile decision as an override.
