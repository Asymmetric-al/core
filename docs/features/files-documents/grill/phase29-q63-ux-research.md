# Q63 — Ordinary records disposition: approved automation or fresh human release

2026-10-28. ## Frontier assessment and recommendation

The strongest next question is **whether a qualified ordinary records policy may authorize bounded automatic disposition, or whether every eligible ordinary disposition proposal also requires a fresh qualified records-operator release**. This is not the already-ratified Q62 question about applying a known policy during issuance. It determines what an authorized operator must do when that policy's actual disposition conditions are met.

The owner independently confirmed that no ordinary Q01–Q62 decision yet selects this automatic-versus-fresh-human behavior. Founder §14 already requires current policy, complete uses/copies, holds, destructive-admission fences and verified outcomes. Those safeguards are not choices to waive. Public Media is a concrete counterexample with its own already-selected manual rule: ADR0171 clause25 explicitly keeps retain-until-review/no automatic purge and one explicit D27 disposition authorization. Q63 cannot widen that source.

**Recommend A: permit the actual approved ordinary policy to authorize automatic bounded execution where it explicitly qualifies that route, with human handling for actual exceptions and source-required reviews.** A can carry a resolved policy through to timely disposition without requiring a routine fresh click on every eligible batch. It is not an automatic-safest claim, a blanket auto-purge setting or authorization for any current unqualified cohort.

**The strongest B is fresh records review of each finite proposal, with batch release allowed.** It gives a qualified person a final contextual opportunity to identify a wrong classification, missing hold or material fact. It is not a requirement to open every file, add an essay or conduct a second committee meeting. It must be executable within actual lawful timing; waiting for a person cannot silently extend a privacy ceiling indefinitely.

## Suggested single question and fair options

**When ordinary request records become eligible for disposition under an approved policy, should Asym be allowed to carry out that policy automatically after the current safety checks, or should a qualified records operator review and release each bounded proposal first?**

| Option                                                                  | Concrete behavior                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Strongest benefit                                                                                                                                                                                         | Cost and risk                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Allow approved automatic disposition (recommended)**              | For an ordinary source/cohort whose approved policy expressly authorizes automation, the source admits a finite exact operation after current policy, holds, references, copy coverage and writer checks. Operators see truthful outcomes and handle actual exceptions. Source-required human decisions remain human. | Routine eligible work does not wait for another click after the governing decision is already established. It can reduce avoidable retained-data backlog and leaves operators to resolve real exceptions. | A wrong policy or incomplete source facts can affect many records without a final human noticing. Requires actual qualification, bounded admission, attributable machine/policy provenance, effective stop/repair and all current guards. No measured Asym scale or savings are claimed. |
| **B — Require a fresh records review before each proposal is released** | A currently authorized records operator reviews the permitted evidence for a finite exact proposal and deliberately releases the selected eligible scope. One meaningful review may cover a bounded batch; no per-file click ritual is implied. Current guards are proved again before destructive admission.         | A final contextual check can catch a mistake or unrecorded material fact before irreversible loss. Provides clear personal accountability for the release decision.                                       | Creates continuing review work and potential delay. A click can become habitual and cannot establish complete custody/holds/reference proof. Requires actual operator coverage and timely escalation; it is not retain-until-someone-looks or hidden timeout approval.                   |

Both options require a real approved ordinary purpose policy. A source that requires manual review does not become automatic under A. A source/policy whose lawful timing cannot support B's human step cannot be presented as a qualified B cohort without a real timely authorized solution. No missing policy, guard failure or unknown outcome can be repaired by picking either option.

This is one interaction/authorization fork, not three tenant modes. Q63 does not automatically choose a retention period, grace interval, review cadence, email reminder, approval hierarchy, restore window, mandatory content inspection or a new governance application. Actual source-required controls still apply. Where an existing qualified source/operations surface supplies the necessary result, reuse it.

## Concrete staff, operator and participant journey

**Illustrative, not observed ministry practice:** Elena previously requested an equipment inventory from Nora. Much later, the actual approved source policy identifies some exact retained records as eligible for disposition. No number of months or years is assumed. Records operator Sam can see the permitted source/policy evidence and actual affected scope.

Under A, the eligible automatic cohort can be admitted for disposition without Sam approving this occurrence. The record distinguishes policy-authorized automated admission from a human review. If an actual hold, conflicting requirement or unknown reference is found, it does not proceed; a capable source/records owner receives the cause through the qualified existing route.

Under B, Sam reviews the finite proposal and deliberately releases the eligible selected scope. A new hold or source correction after review still stops destructive admission. Sam's click cannot authorize records outside the reviewed scope, overcome a missing adapter, or add newly eligible records in the background. If Sam cannot act, the actual responsible backup/escalation route must resolve the work within source constraints; no silent extension or automatic approval follows merely from inactivity.

Nora does not become a records operator, lose rights early because a row is in a review list, or receive a new mandatory email. Portal access, request completion, evidence withdrawal and actual retention remain separate. A later permitted resource/evidence view shows only truthful current availability/history; it does not claim all external copies were deleted. If physical disposal has completed, the UI does not offer a fictional Undo or suggest reupload automatically solves an incident.

## Seven founder checks

### What could go wrong with this answer?

A can execute an incorrect policy at scale; B can create a review backlog or habitual approvals that add no meaningful check. Both can mislead people by treating an eligible list or authorized action as physical completion. The same current source/custody proof, bounded scope, attributable provenance and effective repair are mandatory. These are plausible failure modes, not measured Asym incidents.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

Actual ordinary policy modes, legal periods, record volumes, operator capacity, completeness adapters, physical recovery and user-test results are not established. A is recommended only for an explicitly qualified automatic cohort. B is viable only with actual timely qualified human handling. Neither assumes staff remember undocumented obligations or can lawfully extend retention by waiting.

### How does this affect the whole product?

It settles one ordinary disposition authorization interaction while preserving other source owners, Q62 bindings/correction, complete Used in/copy coverage and actual physical execution. Public Media's human rule is unchanged. It does not create a global records engine, generic Trash, notification platform or implicit policy extension power.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

A removes a routine final click for already-preauthorized cases while keeping exceptions actionable. B gives a final contextual review of finite work with a real current operator. Ordinary staff and participants retain their existing access/help; neither is forced to become a records specialist.
