## One practical decision

**May authorized staff revise an outstanding ordinary item's material instructions
for the same document need, or should the issued instructions stay unchanged and
changed work use an independently qualified replacement or new request?**

| Option                                                                | Everyday experience                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Strong benefit                                                                                                                                                                           | Real cost or risk                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **A — Allow deliberate versioned instruction revision (recommended)** | Source-authorized staff publish new instructions for the same eligible outstanding, unaccepted item and continuing need. Earlier definitions and receipts retain their original basis; current work is reassessed through the actual source route. | Keeps one understandable item and its relevant evidence/history together when the original instruction was incomplete or mistaken. Can avoid an artificial retire-and-recreate sequence. | Expectations may change after the participant started or submitted. Requires clear version/effective meaning, current scope, honest review and no automatic rejection or forced reupload. It is a new source capability. |
| **B — Preserve issued instructions; handle changed work separately**  | Meaning-preserving explanations can use Q30. A genuine instruction change uses the actually qualified source correction/replacement path, potentially Q33 plus a Q36 item or a separately issued request where permitted.                          | Keeps the original requirement and its evidence easy to interpret; avoids introducing this new same-item revision power.                                                                 | Can split one continuing need across items/requests and require more coordination. Q33 and Q36 are separate actions, not a guaranteed atomic Replace operation; terminal fulfillment may intervene.                      |

Recommend A, narrowly. It should be possible to correct an incomplete instruction
without telling the participant that their earlier compliant work was wrong or
discarding its history. The recommendation is a product judgment, not measured
Asym preference, vendor consensus or a claim that every request should be editable.

Neither option authorizes changing the document need, intended recipient or
purpose; arbitrary requested-type/requiredness changes; editing prior evidence;
removing acceptance; replacing templates across requests; reopening a terminal
source; extending access; or adding a new notification purpose/default. Accepted
items and fulfilled/canceled requests stay outside this ordinary action.

Illustrative example, not an observed ministry workflow: Elena asks Nora for an
equipment inventory. Nora supplies it, and it has not been accepted. Elena then
discovers that the instructions omitted the serial numbers needed for that same
inventory. Under A, Elena can deliberately revise those instructions while the
source is eligible. Nora's original receipt stays linked to the instructions
under which it was submitted. The actual source review/correction path determines
what, if anything, must now be supplied. Under B, the issued inventory instruction
stays intact and Elena uses a qualified separate-work route. Neither option
automatically rejects Nora's file or retroactively says it was late or defective.

## Exact boundary: instruction revision is not a comment or new demand

A material instruction revision changes the source-authorized description of
what must be supplied for this same document need. It must be deliberately
published as a new applicable instruction revision. Preserve the old text/basis
and the exact association of every earlier receipt and review fact. Do not rewrite
stored history to make the participant appear to have received or satisfied new
instructions earlier.

A Q30 answer may explain unchanged instructions. Once the intended answer changes
what is required, conversational prose cannot quietly become the authoritative
amendment. Likewise, Q23 Changes requested is evidence-review feedback, not a
generic permission to rewrite the requirement; Q33 removal/correction is not
ordinary instruction authoring. This decision should not require staff to create
a fake comment or rejection to obtain an effect already named in another family.

The qualified source must decide whether the proposed change remains the same
document need. A staff label such as clarification cannot bypass that check. A
different document, purpose, recipient or specialist obligation needs its actual
source/new-item/new-request route. No automatic classifier or new general policy
designer is needed; the source owns a bounded command and its reviewable inputs.

Pure typography/nonmaterial editing is not a loophole for changing meaning. This
question does not need a second cosmetic-edit mode to be useful. At the same time,
it must not manufacture a new recipient action, mandatory acknowledgment or broad
proof challenge merely because formatting changed. The selected-answer review
must distinguish relevant effects using the actual source contracts.

## Prior evidence, current review and access

Earlier evidence remains received on its true instruction/revision basis. A
revision does not automatically reject it, declare it accepted under the new
revision, discard it or demand an identical reupload. The actual current source
reassessment/correction route must establish whether existing evidence can meet
the revised need and what additional participant or staff work is lawfully
necessary. Any new acceptance or permitted reuse must have its own exact basis
and attribution; keep participant assertions distinct from staff-supplied facts.

This is particularly important when instructions were wrong or incomplete through
staff error. The participant should see that the instructions changed, not an
unsupported claim that their earlier submission failed an instruction that did
not exist. Real earlier defects/lateness remain real; the revision should not
erase them either. Do not invent grace periods, reset requested-by targets or
backdate instruction applicability.

Material revision must use Q01's actual prospective successor/currentness rules,
with the relevant Q13/Q15/Q36 lineage and completed-reading consequences reconciled
where applicable. An old grant, token, proof or matching address does not silently
cover changed material instructions. Existing current proof may be reused only
where the owner qualifies it; a new challenge is not mandatory per edit. Preserve
the actual finite period/end/caps and current restrictions. Expired access,
wrong-recipient issues and missing authority retain their real source routes.

The same principle applies to ongoing work: preserve admitted receipts and lawful
unaffected upload/review/text activity; revalidate materially affected uncommitted
operations against the real revision rather than invalidating everything because
an aggregate counter changed. A known current instruction version and a useful
participant path must exist where participant action is needed. If the source
permits independently lawful staff evidence/review and no participant act is
required, do not force a new account/access period merely for that staff operation.
These are continuity constraints from actual owners, not automatic scope adoption
already granted by the existence of an Edit button.

## B's replacement route must be described honestly

B deliberately avoids this new revision capability. Unchanged-meaning explanation
continues through qualified Q30. For changed work, independently authorized Q33
No longer required and Q36 addition may be useful, or a separately reviewed new
ordinary request may be clearer. Those operations retain their own eligibility,
authority, historical meaning and consequences.

There is no ratified atomic Replace shortcut. If removing the old demand eliminates
the last blocker, actual Q22 fulfillment may occur before an attempted addition;
then Q36 must deny addition to the now-completed request. A new request may be
the qualified remaining route. Conversely, adding a new genuine requirement first
is a distinct source act with its own completion and participant consequences,
not a guaranteed harmless temporary duplication. Do not prescribe a universal
ordering recipe, hidden completion hold or a new compound transaction to make B
look simpler than it is.

Accepted items and terminal requests are outside A. Changing later needs cannot
unaccept evidence or reinterpret completed history. A new request addresses a
genuine separate need; it is not a remedy for unnecessary private data already
collected. Applicable source/history/privacy correction retains its existing
owner and actual powers.

## Simple experience, preserved progress and awareness

Use the existing ordinary item surface with a clear **Revise instructions** action
for source-authorized staff. Let them inspect the permitted existing instructions,
edit the proposed wording and deliberately publish the new version with the real
consequence. Do not require an essay, routine second approver, full form builder,
request-wide editor or mandatory read receipt. Only actually required source
facts belong in the flow.

On authorized return, the participant sees current instructions and a clear
**Instructions updated [date]** cue, plus enough permitted context to understand
what now needs attention. Earlier evidence stays visible according to its real
current rights/status, without a reset of all progress. Current instructions,
old receipt basis, source reassessment and any later Changes requested should be
distinguishable; a single overwritten text blob or a silent change to a progress
percentage does not communicate the same truth.

A person who is away from the product may not discover the change promptly.
This question selects no automatic instruction-change or added-item email,
reminder, channel, new inbox or read state. Existing outreach may be used only
where actually qualified; no daily-return, delivery or manual-response SLA is
assumed. Genuine subsequent events retain their original notice contracts.
The real awareness tradeoff remains visible rather than being hidden by a claim
that editing automatically informs someone.

Mobile, keyboard/AT, long translations, RTL/IME and slow-network use must preserve
reviewable text, effect and result. A stale view or lost response must not publish
the wrong revision, overwrite another staff member's change or require an
identical second revision. Forbidden context must clear on user/tenant switches;
no offline private transcript archive is selected.
