## Recommended complete product behavior

Keep an independently safe, published and currently authorized P28 resource available when its review target passes. Show an honest **Review due** status to permitted readers and in the existing curator-maintenance context. Keep the last actual review distinct from the next planning target, source publication, ordinary edits, rights/validity, withdrawal and retention. A missed target is not proof of inaccuracy; a future target is not proof of accuracy. Existing actual safety, rights and access restrictions always win.

Use one current advisory review target. An authorized deliberate **Change review date** can change that target and hence the current due projection, even after the previous target passed. Preserve the former target and change actor/time in protected history; do not fabricate a review or label the resource Fresh/Verified/Reviewed because the plan moved. Show **Last reviewed [actual date]** and **Review planned [current date]** where known and permitted. A concise save hint explains **Changing this date does not record a review.** A result can say **Review date changed; last review unchanged.**

I initially proposed retaining Review due after every missed target until review, with a separate new planned date. After comparing the practical costs with the parent and owner, I recommend the simpler current-target policy above. A sticky obligation would require two meanings, an unresolved review cycle and additional user explanation; Q55 A selected a planning signal, not a mandatory continuing obligation. The real counterargument remains: repeated postponement may make maintenance neglect less prominent. Preserve actual review/history and avoid a green reassurance badge; do not pretend this risk is eliminated or backed by observed usage statistics. Neither vendor documentation nor this review establishes an empirical optimum.

The ordinary next review target is explicitly optional in the proposed source amendment: a known **No review scheduled** state is distinct from unknown/unavailable data. A stronger existing source rule that requires a target remains binding and cannot be bypassed with Clear. The proposed ordinary clock is a civil date in a captured source IANA time zone. The full chosen day is available for review; due means trusted server time converted to that source zone has a local date strictly later than the target date. The reader's browser zone, travel and UTC-midnight conversion do not change that meaning.

**Record review** is a deliberate, attributed action on the exact current publication/content basis. It records the actual review and explicitly sets the next target in the same source action: today/future, a consciously retained valid future target, or known No review scheduled where permitted. A past target is completed by the review, not silently carried forward so the just-reviewed resource immediately looks overdue. The form explains that the prior target is being completed and allows the next date to be omitted. No automatic 30/90-day interval, repeated task or reminder program is inferred. Existing stronger source constraints remain.

Publication is not automatically this review action. A publisher may record an actual review together with publication only when that exact review intent is explicit and coherently admitted with the intended publication. Otherwise the new publication has its own truthful review state; a prior version's review is not presented as if it covered new content. No extra routine approver, minimum essay, forced preview or mechanical scroll/dwell test is needed to make the human act meaningful.

Mistakenly recording a review needs a permanent real correction path. A currently authorized source actor/operator can append an attributable correction that invalidates the erroneous review fact for current presentation while preserving the original who/when and correction basis. Recompute the last actual review honestly and consciously confirm the current target; do not rewind a newer independent review/plan, erase history, backdate a replacement event or fake a new review. This is a narrow source fact correction, not a general Undo/Restore platform.

The strongest rejected alternative remains B: stop affected library availability after the review cutoff until re-review. B can be the right continued-use condition for organizations that prefer withholding to overdue guidance, but it also removes useful references when maintenance is late and does not make remaining content true. The selected A does not weaken any independently mandatory validity cutoff. No second tenant mode or settings engine is required to preserve that distinction.

## Complete small reader and curator experience

Reader view: show the permitted resource's existing title/description/format/language and ordinary Open/Download actions. Add a concise textual review state and meaningful date context, without a warning modal or acknowledgment gate. Due guidance stays discoverable under A; do not hide it through a default “fresh only” filter and claim availability remains. A reader needs to understand that a review is planned or overdue, not be given the curator's private rationale, personnel history or raw audit record.

Curator view: use the existing resource management/detail context. Show exact current publication, actual review facts, current target and usable **Record review**, **Change review date**, source Edit/Publish/Withdraw and qualified ownership handoff actions. Access to a document or a staff role alone grants none of these powers. Keep ordinary curation free of another moderator or generic workflow stage. Filters such as Review due help find work, but empty, no matches, unavailable and permission-limited results remain different.

To record a review, the curator reviews the relevant current content/source context through their already-qualified routes, then deliberately records that act. The visible form identifies the publication being reviewed and the next target choice. One meaningful save can record the fact and next plan; no separate ritual checkbox, second confirmation, reason essay or fake content edit is required. Merely opening a preview never proves review. A recorded review expresses the authorized person's actual review of that source scope at that time, not legal validity, universal factual correctness or proof the reader understood it.

To change only the plan, **Change review date** opens the current known value or explicit No review scheduled choice. Unknown current data is not blank. The save makes the target change clear and leaves Last reviewed alone. Retain the old date and whether it had passed in lawful history. Planning can change the current due badge; no Fresh/Verified/Reviewed status is awarded. Past date entry can deliberately establish a currently due target where the source permits; the Record review next-target choice instead uses today/future/none so a completed prior target is not accidentally retained as the next obligation.

For an erroneous review record, use the actual qualified source correction action. The correction identifies the specific erroneous record and its basis, preserves original history, removes the false current-review claim and consciously confirms any current plan consequence. It does not restore every old value or erase a newer genuine review. A concise explanation may be necessary for an attributable correction, but this is not a blanket mandatory essay for ordinary review or rescheduling. If the ordinary curator cannot perform this correction, a real scoped source operator must be able to; Contact support alone is not completion.

For external resources, the current resource record/reference and any human review are bounded in time and scope. The curator may follow an already-qualified external destination; this feature does not fetch, ingest, archive, OCR or monitor arbitrary remote content. The library does not control the external site's later changes or recall content already downloaded. A recorded review must not imply that the mutable destination is an immutable reviewed document forever. Withdraw or correct misleading references through the existing source when required.

## Adversarial check

### What could go wrong with this answer?

Readers may overlook overdue review, and curators may repeatedly defer a target. More serious failures would show a fake actual review, revive prohibited content, leak protected review history or leave a false recorded review impossible to repair. Separate planning from actual facts, keep hard restrictions authoritative and qualify real correction and current-source recovery. A does not eliminate the maintenance risk.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

The ordinary cohort has a real P28 publication, meaningful source zone and capable current curator/operator. Actual usage, date choices, forgotten-review frequency and task benefit are not measured. Optional dates are an explicit proposed ordinary policy with stronger source rules preserved, not an inference from missing data. No daily dashboard attendance or automatic reminders fill the gaps.

### How does this affect the whole product?

It adds a small source-owned resource-maintenance behavior. It does not merge resource review with evidence acceptance, private request deadlines, content publication, custody safety, legal validity or retention. Existing resource/guide readers and qualified curators stay the audience; no new general library or notification purpose follows.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

Readers retain independently lawful guidance and honest review context. Curators can record a real review without altering unchanged bytes, or change only the plan without claiming review. Mistakes, absent dates, lost responses, departure and external references have actual distinct paths. No routine extra approval, essay, preview ceremony or recurring task engine is needed.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

Primary products demonstrate separate review states and availability, deliberate review actions and explicit timed unpublish alternatives. They disagree on visible staleness, AI, defaults and automatic clearing. This design adopts a specific source-grounded combination and rejects those conflicting imports; no universal best-practice or empirical-superiority claim is made.

### Does this fit Asym’s existing repo and product direction?

Yes as an explicit P28 source amendment using current P12/custody/native identity and shared Core UI contracts. Existing labels, generic task code and polished components do not already implement it. Current all-door authorization, temporal/source integration, actual correction and browser/user proof remain required before activation.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

Accept selected A with the exact simple current-target, separate actual review, explicit next-plan, truthful correction and current-source safeguards above. Do not retain the rejected sticky obligation as hidden enforcement. Keep B's availability tradeoff and repeated-deferral risk visible, without inventing a second product mode or claiming the current review is ratification.
