# Phase 29 Q19 — another deliberate completed-read period

Research date: **21 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok**. The parent reports Q18 A and its complete amendments ratified. **Q01–Q18 are the accepted baseline; no Q19 answer is selected.** Earlier working-copy headers that still await ratification are historical; this note uses the parent's current ratification report.

This is public-document comparative research and proposed founder-question framing, not the full post-answer adversarial review. Only this note was written. No canonical document, product, source permission, provider, account, dependency or UI was changed. No native grant, competitor account, browser/AT, representative-user or download test was performed.

## Recommended next single decision

**After a fresh completed-read period ordinarily expires, may authorized staff consider another separately approved period for that same completed request and intended recipient, or is the first fresh period the final one?**

Recommend **A — allow individually reviewed sequential fresh-read periods**, retaining the exact Q16 historical ceiling and current purpose/authority. Make its complete policy consequence explicit: **no new preset repeat-count or cumulative-time cap**, no overlapping periods for that same qualified lineage, and no automatic, prebooked or sliding renewal. B, **one fresh completed-read period only**, is a credible firmer finality rule.

This is the remaining useful product fork after the adopted source anchor and exact interval. It can resolve sequential eligibility, count and cumulative meaning coherently in one question. Do not add an arbitrary third option such as two repeats, a lifetime number of days, a cooldown or an escalating approval tier merely to make a longer menu.

The same finite thirty-day profile can be reused for each approved act without making the total history finite. **A can result in a long sequence of separate periods and staff decisions if the same real purpose remains eligible and no genuine overall cap ends it.** That is its real cost. It is not a promise of permanent, uninterrupted or on-demand recipient access, but it must not be marketed as mathematically bounded cumulative access.

## What is already settled

- Q08 is a deliberate source-owned read decision after eligible expiry, under the exact current issuing capability. Staff status or possession of a file is insufficient; issuer authority is distinct from body-reading and recipient proof.
- Q16 allows either actual fulfillment-created completed-view origin. The binding ceiling is that exact completed admission's permitted member/submitted-original-version/action/Q05-context scope, intersected with current eligibility and the newly reviewed subset. It is not all retained files, the wider unfinished scope or the union of later grants.
- A deliberately narrower prior fresh grant alone does not rewrite the historical completed ceiling as a permanent global limit. Conversely, genuine current or continuing restrictions remain binding. A later review can consider only what the unchanged ceiling and current source allow, not simply replay the broadest past grant.
- Q17/Q18 establish each qualified new source occurrence's fixed nominal end at **720 elapsed hours**, preserving the actual earlier-limited end. Never-entered access shares it. Visits, resends, proof refresh, repair, retries and late observations do not start another period.
- A matching semantic retry recovers its original source effect and end under current disclosure. A new period requires a new deliberate act; unknown prior commitment must be reconciled, not treated as a failed attempt that can be replaced.
- Genuine binding shortening, revocation, purpose/content-use limits, disposal and source dependencies cannot be bypassed by waiting until a nominal deadline also passes. Eligibility requires the relevant actual history and current authority, not just one old expired root.
- The source stays completed and read-only. No new fulfillment stage, intake, correction, later-file membership, superseded bytes, retention reset, arbitrary recipient change or resharing follows.
- Status/context-only roots and currently eligible partial scopes remain meaningful without manufacturing file access. Prior admission does not require a prior visit, preview or download.
- Q15's repeated **unfinished** access rule is not authority for repeated **completed-read** access. Q19 would choose this lane explicitly; matching thirty-day counts do not merge the policies.

These are accepted product contracts, not proof that the current native implementation is qualified. This question should inherit them, not introduce a second permission system.

The no-overlap condition proposed within A is local to this same qualified fresh-read lineage, not a global block on other recipients, sources or independently held rights. A fresh grant for a disjoint subset must not be an overlap loophole in that same lane. Current relevant history must also establish that the predecessor actually ended in an eligible way; an older ordinary expiry cannot bypass a later active, revoked, unknown or otherwise controlling result.

No mathematical aggregate end follows from thirty days per period. Conversely, **no preset policy quota is not a permission to ignore declared performance/rate budgets, genuine source limits or meaningful present purpose**. Those existing controls cannot be turned into an unannounced lifetime eligibility count either. If a cumulative policy is desired, it needs an explicit product choice, not a hidden implementation default.

## Recommendation and the strongest case for B

A fits the existing source-owned private-request direction: allow the source to consider a real later reading need using the same qualified context and currently permitted exact scope. It avoids requiring a new file destination or duplicating completed work merely because the recipient already used the first fresh period. This is a continuity/governance judgment, not evidence that ministries frequently need repeated copies or that a specific number of grants is optimal.

B is stronger if Conrad wants the first fresh admission to be expressly final even for an otherwise lawful later need, or if the owning source cannot make meaningful repeated decisions without turning them into routine approval. It should not be framed as “bad UX” or made toothless by promising a duplicate request. Its count rule is genuinely simpler in policy, while still needing correct concurrency, provenance and current disclosure so its finality cannot be bypassed.

A does not mathematically create permanent uninterrupted access, but many individually valid decisions can span a long history. A real current purpose is necessary; saying “the record still exists” or “the user might want it someday” is insufficient justification. The completed request is not reopened as unfinished to manufacture purpose. The retained originals, source audit and independently governed uses can persist without any active recipient permission.

## The no-new-grant path

Neither option requires staff to grant whenever someone asks. Before using the existing action, determine whether there is a new eligible recipient read need at all. A source-status question, independently permitted staff review, or repair of still-valid proof/entry/representation may be handled without a fresh period. No new feature, queue or access act is needed for that case.

A broken link, temporary preview failure, email delay or unknown issuance result does not automatically justify regranting. Recover the existing effect/current access where appropriate. If recipient permission actually ended and the need is to read protected material, independent staff access is not a basis to copy it into an email or external attachment. Help can explain the real result or route an authorized owner decision; it cannot promise a bypass or disclosure.

Thus B is the **no repeated-permission capability** option, while A can still choose no new grant in individual cases. Both retain real support and independent source work; neither creates a universal alternate delivery right.

## Lean practical experience

Keep **Grant read access** in the existing completed-request context. A small contextual note can explain that this is a **new up-to-thirty-day period** after the earlier one ended. It must not say “edit the old expiry” or “extend the old grant.” Reuse the meaningful review of intended recipient, current purpose, exact permitted scope and controlling limits. Do not add a repeat counter editor, mandatory reason essay, duration picker, second approval screen, full recipient account, new help queue or reminder campaign.

Preserve Q18's already-adopted actual-end display, main-content information, keyboard/focus/status, accessible proof, localized precision/timezone, mobile/reflow and low-bandwidth recovery. Repetition needs no new accessibility mechanism. A familiar action must still explain a different **new** effect when that distinction matters; an explicit new effect does not require an extra ceremonial confirmation.

## Proposed founder-facing framing

**After a fresh read-only period ends, should staff be allowed to approve another for the same completed request and recipient?**

- **A — Yes, with a new review each time (recommended).** Another period may be approved only for a real current need within the original completed scope and current authority. Each is up to thirty elapsed days from its own source issuance. No preset repeat-count or cumulative-time cap; no overlap or automatic renewal.
- **B — No, one fresh period only.** Once that period ends, this recipient lane receives no further fresh period. Existing lawful help and source work remain, without cloning or copying around the limit.

The prior periods, completed status, original-file scope and genuine restrictions remain unchanged under both. A preserves a controlled later path but may create a long history of individual decisions. B gives firm finality but may leave otherwise legitimate later reading needs without this path. No provider's configurable/recreated link establishes the answer for Asym.

## Evidence limits and handoff

The recommendation is a product judgment. No public source proves representative Asym repeat demand, a universal repeat default or that absence of a vendor's published limit means unlimited repetitions. Expired access, editable expiry, link reactivation, creation of a new link, delivery retry and a new immutable Core source effect remain distinct.

If the founder selects A or B, the later full adversarial review should qualify relevant history/ending-cause eligibility, concurrent new decisions, same-effect replay, source-purpose drift, protected scope/history, genuine restrictions and the actual recipient/staff/support journeys. That review must not invent a numerical quota, waiting period, escalation tier or notification policy under the guise of implementation.

No Q19 answer, new glossary term, canonical change, native implementation or launch readiness is selected or claimed here.
