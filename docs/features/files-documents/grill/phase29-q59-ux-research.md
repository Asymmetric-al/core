## 4. The smallest complete A journey

### Save and return

On an eligible resource card/detail/qualified return surface, use an accessible **Save for later** action. Saving should not require opening a preview, downloading bytes or acknowledging completion; actual permitted source metadata and personal-save authority must suffice where qualified. A clear saved state and **Remove from saved** action identify the consequence. A bookmark/star may supplement the text, not replace its accessible name or imply a rating.

Within Resources, **Saved** returns the reader's own chosen references in the current qualified organization/context. Normal search/navigation remains available. No separate app, curator console, new account, mandatory folder or custom label is required. The list's ordinary product privacy is personal: other readers, curators, coaches and staff managers do not gain the list through their role. Actual separately scoped security/privacy/records operations remain governed; do not promise that no authorized operator could ever process the underlying record.

The target is **a personal reference to the exact P28 source publication identity/lineage for later current-authorized return**, not an immutable-version snapshot or a saved file copy. A same-publication replacement may resolve through its already-qualified current head, with truthful current content/review information. A separate Q58 publication remains a different identity even if bytes/title match. Qualified successor guidance may help the reader navigate, but must not silently transfer the saved relationship, switch authored language or confer access.

The reader can remove a saved reference without withdrawing the publication, deleting its bytes, changing other readers' selections or sending a notification. A mistaken removal can be corrected by a fresh deliberate Save where the current publication/personal-save route qualifies; it does not require a Trash/Restore platform. Known saved state, no saved items, failed load and unknown operation result are distinct. A click is not proof of persistence, and an unknown response is not a reason to create duplicate references.

### Permission loss, withdrawal and privacy

Saving grants no access. Current source/field/action checks apply before saved titles, descriptions, thumbnails, counts, links, successor hints and bytes are returned. A formerly permitted resource must not remain readable through a cached saved card. Current unavailable or unknown content cannot be substituted with draft, archive, old bytes, another language, a matching filename or a separate publication.

The personal reference's own maintenance purpose needs explicit qualification. A reader must have a practical way to remove their own now-unavailable entry without being forced to regain content access or exposing the inaccessible title. Any minimal unavailable placeholder or personal count must be justified by current own-reference authority, not an assumed right to list hidden resources. The full selected-answer review should settle this lifecycle exactly; a hidden count or abandoned undeletable personal record is not a complete solution. There is no promise of perpetual personal retention, offline availability or an automatic access-request workflow.

No personal saved list becomes staff progress evidence, read history, training completion, urgency, assignment, report receipt or popularity analytics. Saving does not subscribe to updates or change existing independently qualified notifications. Q55 actual review/currentness and Q57 problem report/own-return purposes remain separate. P28 Getting started dismissal and Support D27-C guides are not automatically added to this corpus.

### Strong B as a finished experience

B still requires clear current Resources navigation, practical title/category/language search, honest authorized totals, source-qualified Open/Download, safe successor guidance and return to lawful list position. A copied resource URL must reauthorize. A browser bookmark is optional user behavior, not a substitute for fixing broken native search or a promise of private synchronization. B adds no Asym saved-list record or misleading disabled Save control. Neither answer may declare the existing reader outcome complete without actual source/UI/accessibility qualification.

## Adversarial check

### What could go wrong with this answer?

A personal saved list could expose a formerly allowed title, return a draft/old file after withdrawal, silently switch to a different edition or look like an offline copy. A Save click could claim persistence without a real supported reader path. It also could become staff surveillance of interests. Exact source identity, current projection, honest results and a personal maintenance purpose are necessary if A is selected.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

Readers may revisit a chosen subset of resources, and an in-product personal list may be worth its state/permission/support cost. We have no measured frequency, library size, device mix or preference. Existing search may already be sufficient. We do not assume browser bookmarks are private, cross-device or offline, or that existing saved-view settings implement personal resource references.

### How does this affect the whole product?

A adds one bounded P28 reader convenience alongside the accepted Q58 staff/missionary path, leaving P12 current permission, P29 custody, Q55 reviews and Q57 reports separate. B adds no saved-reference product. Neither selects a global favorites platform, new report audience, notification purpose, public sharing, LMS or retention exemption. Support D27-C remains its own source.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

A lets a reader deliberately build a short personal return list across qualified sessions/devices and remove choices later. Search remains available. B keeps the interface and state model lean, with good existing discovery and safe links; repeated discovery is its tradeoff. Both must handle unavailable guidance honestly and preserve complete accessible navigation.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

Current official knowledge/CRM products document intentional saved lists, often separately from Follow and Recent. Their unsupported-reader behavior, draft/archive/language fallbacks and broader permission models are cautionary examples. A is a reasonable bounded adaptation, not universal consensus or measured ministry evidence. Accessible state and failure behavior must be tested in Core.

### Does this fit Asym's existing repo and product direction?

It can fit as an explicit personal-reference extension to the real P28 publication owner. Q58's declared audiences and current reading remain authoritative, and saved query/guide-dismissal primitives are only possible implementation precedents, not transferred authority. No native Save capability, hosted source behavior, user/AT proof or latest runtime delta audit is claimed by this UX research.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

Recommend **A — Add personal Save for later**, bounded to current-authorized source references and simple personal removal, while preserving complete search/navigation and strong B. Do not add subscriptions, snapshot/offline copies, custom collections or a new settings platform. The founder still chooses; Q59 is unselected and its full selected-answer review has not occurred.
