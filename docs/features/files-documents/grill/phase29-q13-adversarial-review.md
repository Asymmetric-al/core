## Adversarial check

### What could go wrong with this answer?

A generic Renew button could erase expiry, bypass revoked authority or permit
any staff member to expose files. A stale upload could silently resume under
a new grant. A narrow grant could become a broad completed view. Separate
read and mutation scopes, preserve ended effects, require new source authority
and make every transition attributable and current.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

That the original admission and ordinary expiry are provable; the same recipient
and purpose still exist; the source remains unfinished; useful work remains;
qualified current permission and custody can be resolved; and later finite
policy can yield a useful journey. Missing proof is an activation/operation
block, not a reason to guess. No observed cohort establishes demand prevalence.

### How does this affect the whole product?

The native request owner must coordinate Auth/P12, source requirements,
custody/serving and durable communication owners. It must not create a fifth
principal class, file-manager permission engine or Workflow-owned copy of
authorization. A prospective completed-stage mapping is necessary; Q08's
further fresh-read eligibility is not expanded by implication.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

It can preserve the same task, received files and history, with one contextual
staff decision. The recipient sees only current permitted work and a truthful
next step. It must not require resubmitting everything, extra enrollment,
an essay or a confusing access-management screen. A denial or uncertain outcome
must not masquerade as lost files or rejected evidence.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

It follows recoverable, least-privilege task patterns. Reactivation itself is
not universal: Tresorit demonstrates the strongest simpler final-closure
alternative. External products' mutable dates, broad link permissions or
completed-to-active transitions conflict with Core and are not adopted.

### Does this fit Asym’s existing repo and product direction?

Yes as a new, bounded source-owned capability under the existing architectural
contracts. No as a patch that changes a generic task date, flips an active bit,
reuses a signed URL or treats current broad role helpers as full P12 evidence.
Actual implementation remains unqualified.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

Keep A, with required amendments. The important adjustment is from a vague
staff exception to a deliberate prospective source admission for exact work,
with old-effect separation and explicit completed-view scope. B remains simpler
but leaves the selected continuity need without an in-request response.

## Corrected decision to record

Allow a properly source-authorized staff actor to approve Fresh Unfinished
Request Access for the same intended recipient, request and continuing purpose
after proved ordinary unfinished-access expiry, while the source is genuinely
unfinished and otherwise eligible. This is a new finite prospective admission;
the original admission, end, adverse history, receipts and acceptance remain
unchanged. Do not require the expired recipient grant to authorize the staff's
new administrative decision, or mistake administrative authority for file-read
authority.

Revalidate the exact source issuance capability, P12 administrative scope/live
assignable ceiling, continuing dependency, recipient revision, original
requirement lineage and actual current restrictions at the source mutation
boundary. No generic staff/reviewer/support role, old sender status, service
credential or matching email suffices. A preservation hold grants nothing;
apply its actual scope. Genuine binding shortening or adverse endings are not
reclassified as ordinary expiry when their causes clear. Unknown proof remains
closed for affected actions without fabricated expiry or rejection.

Review permitted actions separately. Submission and Q03 correction apply only
to exact currently pending eligible requirements within the original admitted
purpose/requirement scope; they may create new immutable submissions for those
requirements. Current own-file readback may include independently permitted
accepted items. Accepted/frozen writes, historical bytes, new recipients,
new requirements and new purposes are not reopened. A narrower fresh grant is
not a global denial of rights independently granted elsewhere.

Declare the fresh admission's permitted completed-read scope in its reviewed
terms. Under this explicit prospective Q10 amendment, one actual eligible
normal source fulfillment while that fresh authority is valid can admit Q07's
read-only stage, resolving eligible current own-submissions within that scope
at fulfillment. The end is no later than 720 elapsed hours from the qualified
fulfillment occurrence, subject to genuine caps and current authority. Writes
stop. Neither expired grants nor write permission supplies additional reading.
The old/fresh unfinished-only end is not secretly an overall cap. Early
completion can move the final read end earlier. No receipt, generic task click,
later observation or backdated grant substitutes for fulfillment/eligibility.

Fresh authority must exist in the source causal order at fulfillment. A lawful
atomic fresh admission and fulfillment may qualify if the source proves this
ordering and eligibility; equal timestamps alone prove neither result. Actual
completion in a gap or before admission receives no retroactive access.
Recover a durably committed timely outcome from its original evidence under
current restrictions; observation time does not restart anything. Q08's
original completed-view eligibility is not silently broadened: further fresh
read access from this continuation lineage requires an explicit compatible
owner decision before activation of that further capability.

Keep one stable deliberate fresh issuance identity, bound material reviewed
input and immutable first-admission outputs. Coherently preserve source effect,
required audit and durable follow-up intent; retries recover the same outcome
and require current disclosure. Old unadmitted uploads/corrections/commands
bound to an expired admission cannot silently borrow the new grant. A new
currently authorized submission decision may reuse qualified retained custody
only where its source contract permits; it must not rewrite receipt time,
invent acceptance or require an unnecessary byte transfer.

Use the same source/request UI and one meaningful staff review/action. Show
committed, currently usable, proof-ready, delivered and entered facts separately.
Show actual ends only when resolved, approved timing terms/known caps otherwise.
Provide a truthful source-owned help route without leaking private existence
through an expired link. Preserve Base UI/base-maia, localized times, accessible
proof/focus/status and useful mobile/low-bandwidth recovery. No new account,
arbitrary recipient/date editor, routine second approval, mandatory essay,
automatic message, recurring reminder or general file archive follows.

Before activation, separately adopt complete fresh timing/activation/unused
bounds and repetition/overlap policy; no implicit repeat of Q12's sixty days,
unlimited repeats or one-use lifetime cap. Bind the exact native participant,
P12/source/Workflow contracts, qualify all private-serving and real-role data
paths, and prove race/replay/restore/UX behavior. Use existing shared systems.
Retained evidence, holds/disposal, independent uses and already-downloaded
copies keep their own contracts. Q13 is not a retention or copy-recall policy.

## Recipient, staff and recovery journeys

### 1. Return after expiry

The recipient reaches the existing request entry. Determine what current
authority permits revealing. Where authorized, explain access ended, distinguish
Requested by, and show the real help route. Otherwise use a neutral entry/help
response without filenames, counts, completion details or organization-sensitive
status. Do not create a new public renewal endpoint by implication.

### 2. Staff review and deliberate decision

The source-authorized issuer sees the exact intended recipient/revision, request
purpose, eligible requirement/action scope, proposed completed-read scope,
relevant previous outcomes and qualified timing terms/limits. They need the
administrative projection, not automatic permission to open originals. One
clear **Grant access to continue** action is sufficient under the actual source
policy. Material changes require refreshed review; mere clock advance under
unchanged reviewed terms does not create another ceremony.

### 3. Commit, response loss and handoff

Disable accidental duplicate UI submission for usability, while source semantic
identity provides correctness. Show progress and a persisted result, with a
bounded recovery route for unknown outcome. Closing the screen is not post-send
cancellation. Grant recorded, usable, recipient confirmed and message delivered
remain separate; no automatic email is selected. Existing P17/P6/source
communication procedures handle any later authorized message.

### 4. Recipient re-entry and continuing work

Apply Q01's current proof and exact source context, reusing compatible proof only
where qualified. No new broader account. Once access is usable and the end
resolved: **“You can continue this request. Access while it is unfinished ends
[date, time and zone].”** Otherwise show approved terms/known cutoff and the
actual unavailable or pending state. Keep safely disclosed received work and
eligible actions together; no forced download or general file browser.

### 5. Upload or correction interrupted by the old end

Preserve known custody/receipt truth and prevent the old command borrowing a new
grant. If bytes were durably admitted, show the authorized original result; if
not, explain a new submission is needed under current access. Qualified custody
reuse can avoid retransmission, but use the normal deliberate Submit/Send
corrected file action and revalidate bytes, target and authority. Do not add a
second confirmation modal or silently post the file when access is granted.

### 6. Mixed items and pending staff review

Accepted/frozen items display their actual status with only allowed read actions.
Pending items alone expose eligible writes. If everything was received and staff
review remains, say so and identify the next actor; do not mark the recipient
late because staff are reviewing. Source completion may proceed without fresh
recipient access. A new grant still needs a legitimate recipient purpose.

### 7. Completion, late observation and expiry

Eligible source fulfillment stops writes and resolves the declared read scope
and actual completed end. Explain the transition without claiming every change
extends time. If completion occurs before fresh admission or after fresh expiry,
do not fabricate read eligibility. Recover real earlier admitted effects rather
than requiring a second approval for a projection failure. Do not confuse an
expired view with rejected evidence or deleted files.

### 8. Support, adverse changes and safe correction

Support routes to the accountable source owner without gaining issuance rights.
Wrong recipient/scope issuance requires its real revocation/remediation path;
retain history and close current delivery as required, without claiming to
recall downloaded copies. A completed source, new purpose, identity change or
binding restriction needs its own legitimate owner process, not request cloning.
No automatic repeat or further fresh-read entitlement is inferred.

### Cross-journey usability and accessibility

Preserve shared Base UI/base-maia and semantic tokens across staff/donor/
missionary surfaces. Essential status, date/time/zone and next actor belong in
normal content, not hover-only tooltips or color. Use descriptive action names,
keyboard/focus/error recovery, programmatic status without repeated disruptive
announcements, usable narrow/zoomed layouts and touch targets. Test screen
readers and mobile proof/copy/paste, latency, disconnects and safe retry.

Respect international names and source-authorized contact revisions; do not
invent first/last-name requirements or ask for an address unrelated to the task.
Persist instants and display unambiguous localized dates/zones. No countdown-only
meaning or timezone-dependent authority. Short proof/upload timers need their
own accessibility assessment; a long business period does not qualify them.

WCAG 3.3.8 AA supports accessible proof; 4.1.3 AA governs qualifying status
messages; 3.3.7 A has a same-process scope and does not mandate cross-session
storage or eliminate different-method document uploads. 3.2.6 A concerns
consistent repeated help, not a new support entitlement. Actual browser/AT
and recipient/issuer comprehension must be tested; no pass is claimed here.

## Category-by-category adversarial review

### 1. Problem validity, necessity, and alternatives

**Material concern: yes. Severity: medium. Likelihood: medium.** A renewal
feature could serve an imagined demand or mask a bad original process. The
ratified model nevertheless leaves a real possible state: unfinished permitted
work with ended recipient access. B is simpler and protects finality; a new
duplicate request does not inherently improve authority and can split evidence.
Public products disagree; no measured Asym prevalence is available.
**Effect:** retain A as a product judgment, narrow it to genuine continuing
purpose. **Permanent fix/spec:** C01–C02/C24; qualify representative blocked
journeys and whether staff work alone suffices, not a fabricated adoption KPI.
Proof P01/P27/P32.

### 2. Brittleness

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: high.** Checking “expired”
plus task status breaks under delayed outcomes, revised recipient context,
changed policy, unknown history or an intake-only limit. Q10/Q11 and Workflow
explicitly separate these facts. A boolean or cached status cannot prove them.
**Effect:** narrow admission predicates and recovery classification.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C02–C05/C08/C13; resolve authoritative lineage and
current conditions, preserve unknown as unknown. Proof P02–P04/P16/P20.

### 3. Technical debt

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: medium.** Ad hoc Renew
routes or duplicated grant logic could diverge across staff, donor and missionary
surfaces. Current broad role helpers/pure projection are adjacent foundations,
not the intended full P12 contract. Copying them would entrench the gap.
**Effect:** changes implementation ownership, not A's capability.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C04/C11/C23–C24; one source command integrated with the
actual owner, shared state projections and explicit supersession mapping.
Proof P05/P21/P30.

### 4. Edge cases

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: medium.** Mixed accepted
and pending items, changed contact, late observation, previously uploaded
unadmitted bytes and completion during approval can yield wrong actions or
forced resubmission. Existing Q03 fences and Q10 causal rules forbid simple
request-wide reactivation. **Effect:** narrows scope and transition behavior.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C06–C08/C10/C14; the lifecycle matrix and per-action
current state are required. Proof P06–P09/P12/P17–P20.

### 5. Footguns

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: high.** A date editor,
“resend” action, repeated click or manual clone may quietly grant fresh rights;
grant authority can be mistaken for original-file read access. These are
plausible consequences of the bare answer, not observed production incidents.
**Effect:** changes the action and review contract.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C03–C04/C12–C14/C18/C24; one explicit source-bound grant
action, immutable original facts and current replay handling. Proof P05/P10–P14/P27.

### 6. Tenant safety

**Material concern: yes. Severity: critical. Likelihood: medium.** Reused IDs,
mailbox matching, shared request caches or jobs could mix recipient scope across
tenants. A user may legitimately act in several tenants; global identity is not
request authority. Platform boundaries and P12 require exact context.
**Effect:** mandatory qualification before any activation.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C04–C05/C15–C17; tenant-aware relationships, trusted
resolution, scoped lookup/cache/job keys and uniform protected denial.
Proof P05/P21–P24.

### 7. Database, RLS, and authorization safety

**Material concern: yes. Severity: critical. Likelihood: medium.** An allowed
row update could change recipient, tenant, deadline or read scope; service paths
may bypass browser policies. Single-column foreign keys can preserve existence
without tenant consistency. Current service wrappers and broad roles do not
prove the native capability. **Effect:** narrows the permitted mutation surface.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C04–C05/C11/C15–C16. Require trusted attribution,
non-null required identity/provenance, tenant-consistent keys/relationships,
finite time/state constraints, exact unique semantic effects, safe deletion and
no historical rewrite. Inspect effective USING/new-row checks and composed
policies, view security and function grants/search paths/owner privileges.
No money values are introduced; existing financial precision/owners stay
unchanged. Proof P10/P15/P21–P23/P30.

### 8. Overengineering

**Material concern: yes. Severity: medium. Likelihood: medium.** A capability
decision could grow into a permission editor, workflow engine, renewal queue,
custom access calendar or generalized document drive. No evidence requires
those additions. **Effect:** keep A but reduce its implementation scope.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C18/C21/C24; reuse existing source context and owner
infrastructure, one meaningful review, no arbitrary customization. Exact table
layout is left to design under invariants. Proof P27/P30/P32.

### 9. UX/UI and user friction

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: high.** “Approved” may be
displayed as usable access before activation/proof; expiry may look like rejected
or lost work; users may have to reupload accepted material. Entra's state
distinctions and WCAG help/proof guidance support clearer feedback, but do not
dictate Core's architecture. **Effect:** changes copy, state and journey rules.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C13–C14/C18–C19; eight journeys, meaningful next actor,
actual dates/zones, accessible existing UI and current-disclosure-safe help.
Proof P12/P25–P28/P32. No automatic reupload ban overrides genuine invalid
bytes or a newly required document from its proper source.

### 10. Source of truth, ownership, and domain invariants

**Material concern: yes. Severity: critical. Likelihood: medium.** A fresh grant
could become permission to change source completion or could widen its completed
view to old expired scopes. Q08 and Q10 have different admission predicates;
generic task status is mutable. **Effect:** expressly amends only the new
continuation-to-completed relationship and bounds reading independently.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C01/C03/C06–C10/C15; source-owned admission, requirement
and view lineage with no inference from write permission or old grants.
Proof P06–P09/P17–P20/P32.

### 11. Hidden coupling

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: medium.** Grant survival
may accidentally depend on sender employment, a Workflow pause, invitation
delivery, a task date or provider URL lifetime. P12 defines distinct grant-type
dependencies; Q11 separates original source admission and credentials.
**Effect:** narrows what may affect access.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C04/C09/C13/C17/C24; explicitly bind continuing
dependency and limit scope, never infer all grants die or all survive.
Proof P05/P13/P23/P30.

### 12. Failure modes

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: high.** Source commitment
followed by response/delivery/audit-projection failure can produce duplicate
grants, false failure or missing staff evidence. Workflow already separates
unknown outcomes and irreversible external effects. **Effect:** adds exact
partial-success and reconciliation contracts.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C11–C14/C20; coherent source/audit/intent commitment,
same-effect lookup and independently authorized delivery recovery; provider
calls outside locks. Proof P10–P16/P25/P29.

### 13. Lifecycle, temporal correctness, concurrency, and idempotency

**Material concern: yes. Severity: critical. Likelihood: high.** Two issuers,
completion, expiry, correction and revocation can each pass stale checks then
jointly violate the rule. Old commands may borrow fresh authority; retry can
restart an end. Date equality is not causal proof. PostgreSQL's normal snapshots
do not serialize the whole business predicate. **Effect:** requires a coherent
source order and exact effect binding.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C08–C14; source-required fences/current post-wait checks,
adopted repetition predicates, original outcome recovery and explicit temporal
proof. Proof P10–P20/P29–P30. No new clock/count is selected.

### 14. Data integrity risks

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: medium.** Cloning, upsert,
auto-rebinding or replay can create duplicate admissions, wrong receipt times,
broken correction links and completion membership drift. Retained bytes do not
establish a durable submission. **Effect:** adds historical integrity and
relationship requirements.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C03/C06–C08/C12/C14–C15; preserve immutable admitted facts,
scope exact successors and make illegal joins/states impossible at constraints
or the sole authoritative boundary. Proof P06–P12/P15/P21/P30.

### 15. Security and privacy risks

**Material concern: yes. Severity: critical. Likelihood: medium.** Stale URLs,
history counts, replay responses, previews, metadata or logs can expose sensitive
missionary/care/location information despite a closed page. A new grant might
re-enable old object handles. Q04 requires every private-serving path; provider
URL expiry may not terminate an in-flight transfer. **Effect:** preserves full
current-use protection and narrows recovery disclosure.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C02/C05/C07/C14/C17/C19–C20; protected neutral entry,
exact serving, minimized telemetry and no retention/copy-recall assumptions.
Proof P22–P26/P29–P30.

### 16. Scalability and performance risks

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: medium, increasing with
contention and tenant size.** Unbounded grant/history scans, global counters,
resident timers or a fresh provider lookup per file can make safe checks slow
or unreliable. P12 R6 already specifies at most one governance-row SELECT and
one ledger UPDATE for its governed-read scope, with its physical carve-outs.
**Effect:** imposes bounded design and measured activation budgets.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C11/C17/C21; indexed selective lookups, protected source
capacity, tenant fairness, pagination and existing qualified durable wakes.
Do not claim a universal latency target or apply R6 to the entire issuance
transaction. Proof P23/P29. No production load result is available.

### 17. Operational burden

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: medium.** Staff may need
developer date edits, repeatedly approve failed sends, or clone requests when
review exceeds time. Missing classification or owner handoff creates tribal

support powers. **Effect:** requires owned diagnosis/recovery and complete policy.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C02/C09/C12–C13/C18–C21/C24; source-visible actionable
state, semantic recovery and explicit owner runbooks. Proof P16/P25/P27/P29/P32.

### 18. Observability and auditability gaps

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: medium.** A generic “access

scope, ambiguous outcome or why a later submission was allowed. Conversely,

security, technical and recipient evidence.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C03/C12–C13/C20; immutable decision provenance, minimal
correlation, privileged diagnosis and Q05-safe history. Proof P10/P16/P24–P25/P29.

### 19. Dependency and integration risks

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: medium.** Provider retries,

lineage, current serving and typed unknown recovery. Proof P11–P14/P23/P29.

### 20. Migration, rollout, and upgrade risks

**Material concern: yes. Severity: critical. Likelihood: medium.** Legacy

**Permanent fix/spec:** C22–C23; compatibility matrix, dark effects, feature gate

### 21. Testability, traceability, and proof

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: high.** A polished screen,

**Effect:** requires explicit decisions and falsifiable qualification.
**Permanent fix/spec:** C09/C23; the 32 proof groups below link to exact clauses

### 22. Other development hazards

**Material concern: no additional independent concern found. Severity:
