## The genuine remaining choice

The next useful question is the **continuity model when an eligible issued ordinary Private Document Request needs a different intended person**. Correction/recovery is already required; do not ask whether staff may correct a recipient at all. Do not equate a different person with a source-proved correction of the same person's contact address or lost-inbox recovery.

**A — A guided, linked replacement request for a different intended person. Root recommendation.** In the existing request context, authorized staff prepare a new independent request with reviewed reuse of permitted current definitions, then complete the actual old-authority ending and new original issuance through their proper source commands. Preserve the old evidence, conversation, attribution and outcomes in the old source record. The new recipient gets their independently admitted request and proof path. This provides one consistent correction journey, including cases with prior activity, without manual rebuilding. It creates another request identity and requires a clear relationship/progress view and independently validated issuance.

**B — Keep the same request when it is provably unresponded; otherwise use guided replacement.** For a narrow source-qualified ordinary cohort, permit a deliberate versioned recipient successor on the same request, ending obsolete recipient authority and admitting the new recipient under an explicit new source basis. Preserve the identity and useful staff context when no participant response or evidence has accumulated. Once the request has an admitted response/evidence basis, use A's guided replacement. This saves an extra request record for early mistakes, but adds another eligibility branch, recipient lineage and clock/control reconciliation.

The strongest B is not arbitrary email editing, silent transfer of historical access or “add an alternate recipient.” “Unresponded” cannot mean merely zero visible/current uploads. A received contribution still counts after withdrawal or supersession; Q30 clarification can exist with no files. Any adopted B needs an authoritative whole-source eligibility predicate, rather than the filtered current screen. Source-owned staff evidence or acceptance also must not be rewritten or reassigned by this shortcut.

Illustration only: Jordan issued an equipment-inventory request to Nora, then learns that Samuel is the actual person who should respond. With A, Jordan uses a contextual replacement flow, reviews the reusable instructions and new recipient, and sees the old and new records' actual states. Nora's prior material or private questions are not copied into Samuel's access. With B, an independently proved unresponded request may keep its identity through a new recipient revision; otherwise the same guided replacement applies. A mere address typo for a source-proved same person is a separate qualified recovery case, not automatically this scenario.

## Independent recommendation and the real tradeoff

My initial independent preference was B **for a genuinely pristine, never-responded case**: it preserves the staff's existing context and avoids an unnecessary second request identity. Q01's versioned-successor discipline makes that a feasible alternative if its source and access contract are explicitly adopted. That preference does not extend to transferring a request with another person's received material or transcript.

Root's recommendation of A for the overall different-person journey is coherent with every reviewed owner and is a strong product judgment: one guided replacement path avoids branching on the meaning of pristine and keeps recipient-specific history/authority separated. Its real cost is another record and a complete linked-replacement experience, not retyping everything or abandoning correction. Neither choice is already compelled by Q01, and neither is demonstrated as universally better by a user study. Present B fairly rather than claiming in-place succession is intrinsically unsafe or forbidden.

For the question, use A as the overall recommendation while retaining the narrow B's convenience argument. Do not silently convert the scope into all contact edits, delegation, multiple recipients, representative authority or completed-request reopening.

## Concrete adoption boundaries for a complete answer

1. **Identity first.** Distinguish a genuinely different intended person from independently proved same-person contact correction, lost inbox, delegation and representation. Those are source/identity decisions; neither the staff label nor two email strings settle them. Do not make every same-person correction require a new request merely because A is selected for different people.
2. **Prepare is inert.** A contextual replacement draft may reuse only approved, currently compatible definition fields under actual origin-read and destination-authoring authority. Review free prose for old case/recipient facts; an allowlist alone cannot sanitize it. No invitation, old stop or new grant follows simply opening/preparing a draft.
3. **Actually stop old authority.** A known unsafe old recipient can be stopped through the actual source/P12/revocation/Q34 route without waiting for a new draft or recipient to be ready. Preserve old proof/receipt/audit facts. At the real replacement transition, show which old authority/request state ended and what remains historical. Do not imply that a reference link alone revokes access or that source retention grants old access.
4. **Actually issue the new request.** The new source identity, purpose/recipient, current definitions, Q24/Q25 readiness, finite access and original invitation/proof path must qualify. Existing Q13/Q15 fresh access is not this different recipient's original admission. No draft-only replacement or promise of a later grant is a completed correction journey.
5. **No invented atomicity/rollback.** A combined reviewed flow is possible only where all actual source authorities and durable transitions qualify. Do not assume a distributed Cancel+Issue transaction or roll back a known old stop because new issuance/mail fails. Preserve independent real states and support retry/continuation under original semantic identities.
6. **Protect old content.** Old evidence, accepted outcomes, questions/replies, source comments, actors, shares, message bodies and access proofs remain with their exact original uses. The link between requests is itself protected metadata. New participant rights cannot follow that link into old history. Any lawful evidence reuse needs a separate qualified Q28 or other actual source act.
7. **Keep clocks and communication honest.** New original issuance records its real occurrence and terms; do not backdate it from the old request or silently copy current/expired ends. Existing original/fresh/completed access, Q46 target applicability, Q20 fresh-access mail, Q35 cancellation choice and Q48 reminder occurrences stay distinct. No old sealed recipient is rerouted, no unknown old send is rekeyed and no plan/cap reset is disguised as correction.
8. **B's narrow shortcut must be proved.** If B is selected, define the exact source-wide unresponded cohort, including all durable contributions/history and clarification, not only current visible files. Preserve lineage and prior proof/access history while ending obsolete authority; admitting a new person remains an explicit source/P12/Q01 successor, not a live email-field edit. Unsupported cases use guided replacement.
9. **Do not broaden terminal/specialist rights.** Ordinary completed, frozen or adverse cases keep actual owner remediation, retention/privacy and access rules. Neither option selects generic reopen, deletion, representation, agreement/signature authority, alternate recipient or public sharing.
10. **One practical flow, real recovery.** Staff should see the actual old/new recipient, reviewed definitions, old-stop and new-draft/issued outcomes and a clear return path. A lost response reconciles the actual effect; partial progress is not mislabeled failure/success. No forced manual rebuild, unnecessary full account, mandatory essay, routine second approval, generic migration wizard or raw SQL repair is implied.
