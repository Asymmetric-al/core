## Recommended next decision

**Independent initial recommendation: resolve the finite end at the fresh source issuance itself, choosing the clock model before its numerical profile.** The strongest alternative is a prospectively finite latest-qualified-entry window followed by one finite working interval from the first qualified entry. Distinguish that model from a variant that clips the working interval to one hard outer unfinished cap. The parent has instead prepared complete profiles A30/B14/C7-then-30; the reconciliation and exact arithmetic are recorded below. That is a coherent alternative decision sequence, not a correctness conflict. In the earlier model analysis below, A means the issuance model and B the entry model; these conceptual labels do not name the parent's final three numerical options.

Anchor/activation shape has greater dependency value than choosing a count first. A has one source-established end; B needs a finite unused state, one entry transition, concurrent-entry/recovery identity and explicit pre-entry fulfillment semantics. Choosing a number without choosing those facts could silently authorize delayed activation or expire an admission before its promised working time. Q08 fresh-completed timing is a separate unresolved lane and does not resolve this unfinished capability.

A complete numerical profile could be a legitimate founder choice if fully specified, but it should not conceal this material branch among arbitrarily paired counts. Prefer the clock decision now and a complete concrete numerical profile next. This follows the useful original Q11/Q12 sequence without assuming that the original duration or even its clock choice must apply to fresh access. The original 1,440 hours is not a default here.

The recommendation for A is simplicity and explainability, not evidence of an empirically optimal recipient experience. It avoids a new business activation mutation on entry and gives staff one resolved source end. Its real downside is that delivery/proof delay, recipient absence and pending staff review consume the admitted interval. B can preserve working time for late entrants but adds states and potentially a later maximum unfinished lifecycle. ## Approval, source issuance and use are different facts

The anchor under A is the **new authoritative fresh source admission**, not original issuance I, the staff button timestamp, a preliminary favorable review, queue insertion, invitation preparation, provider acceptance, delivery/open/click, account login or first file GET. A committed fresh admission can be validly issued while recipient proof or delivery is incomplete. Its end does not imply the recipient can already use it, was notified or has entered. Conversely, missing source readiness or required proof cannot be fixed by calling an unqualified approval “active.” Display the actual source/admission/proof/delivery state, not one overloaded approved flag.

Under B, the first qualified entry is a source business transition with its own stable effect and exact bound grant/recipient context. Generic successful login, a bearer cookie, a bare verifier POST, a preview or analytics event is insufficient. ## Boundaries the eventual first-entry option must reconcile

**No GET-triggered business activation.** Protected landing visits, link scanning, page prefetch, HEAD, previews and file-body GET must not choose T or consume unused eligibility. A body read can occur only under already resolved current permission. P12's owned access metering/audit is distinct from changing the business grant clock. Deliberate, qualified producer entry belongs behind the existing doorway/current assurance; do not introduce another token system or count a scanner's POST as sufficient identity.

**One first entry despite concurrency and retry.** Two tabs/devices, duplicate POSTs and delayed replies must converge on the same T/U. Relevant current source/recipient state and time must be checked after waits; a request sent before L does not reserve activation if its qualified occurrence fails the boundary. A lost activation response recovers its original result rather than choosing another T. Failed lookup is unknown, not never entered. Once recorded, later visits, auth refreshes, replacement credentials or policy defaults cannot slide the end.

**Finite unused state, with its scope declared before admission.** B needs a finite L as well as a finite bounded working interval. At equality/after L, no new first-entry activation is admitted. If L is activation-only, a lawful U can be later; this is fulfillment of the originally declared conditional policy, not removal of an unconditional cap. Unknown or legacy limit semantics cannot be reinterpreted. Under A, the resolved unfinished end itself bounds unused access; a separate preapproval state does not become an unbounded permission.

**Pre-entry source fulfillment requires explicit owner semantics.** Q13 admits completed reading only when fresh authority actually exists and is valid in the source causal order at F, with historical eligibility. B must not assume an unused approval is such authority. Equally, it must not silently introduce an open-browser prerequisite for independently authorized source work. If B declares a real finite conditional pre-entry source admission until L, the owner must explicitly state whether it can support eligible fulfillment at F<L; that does not waive Q01 proof or grant recipient use without it. If its pre-entry object is only approval/preparation and no fresh authority exists, completion before actual admission cannot gain retroactive Q07 eligibility. Make this meaning clear in B before presenting it as fully coherent, rather than leaving a designer to infer it later. The previous original-clock owner reconciliation is a useful precedent, not automatic fresh-lane authorization.

**Completed means completed.** If normal fulfillment has already occurred, a later entry cannot start an unfinished interval. Recover any already-admitted completed result using its original F/end/current restrictions. A first-entry event cannot reopen the source or start Q07 at observer time. If F occurred in a gap without valid fresh authority, the new clock cannot backfill rights.

**Historical receipts keep their source occurrence.** Old accepted submissions and valid receipt facts remain original. Unadmitted old commands cannot borrow new activation. Where the source permits a new deliberate current submission referencing exact retained custody, its admission/receipt remains new and truthful; bytes existing beforehand is not earlier recipient authority. No unnecessary retransmission, forced preview or timer-start ceremony is implied.

## A's simplicity still needs qualification

A removes the business first-entry transition, not the need for exact source mutation/recovery. Its admitted issuance occurrence and resolved end must be durable, immutable and trustworthy after relevant waits. Preserve one deliberate effect across new HTTP keys, lost replies, restarted workers and changed defaults. Ordinary passage of time during preflight review is not different material intent, and newly calculated output timestamps must not become false replay conflicts.

Staff preapproval, source issuance, technical readiness, proof and delivery stay separate. A may start consuming time before the recipient can use the right; this must be disclosed and qualified against actual entry/help/reviewer journeys, not hidden by labeling an issuance error or a resend a new period. The later count decision must consider actual remaining opportunity under true caps. No source outage pause, automatic renewal or duplicate request is a remedy unless separately adopted by the proper owner.

Both models preserve authoritative instant/precision semantics and exclusive comparisons from Q11–Q13. A current wall-clock sample is not a physical commit timestamp; qualify occurrence/linearization, waits, durable commit, rollback and unknown outcome. Do not import short OTP/session TTLs, provider URL leases, work claims or storage retention as the business clock. No production clock, SQL or browser qualification was performed here.
