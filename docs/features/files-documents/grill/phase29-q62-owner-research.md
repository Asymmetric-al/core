## Recommendation and actual open space

Recommend **A — Apply the approved source-purpose policy automatically, with a clear summary and only any genuinely permitted alternative.** The alternative is **B — Require staff to explicitly confirm or select the compatible approved policy before issuing each new ordinary request.** Both consume the same actual source authority, preserve the same floors/ceilings/holds and require current qualification. This is a decision about how an already lawful policy binding enters the ordinary request journey, not whether source ownership or safe retention is optional.

The distinction is real but bounded. Founder §14 and the retained P29 contract already require owner-supplied versioned retention rules; Q02 already prohibits treating files as an ownerless drive or inferring retention from a folder. They do not select the ordinary request's default-versus-explicit application interaction. Q25 settles review-responsibility readiness, not records-policy selection. Q39 permits current valid instance defaults and restricts what reusable definitions may copy; it does not supply an ordinary retention catalog or authorize copying an old operative policy from a template.

If exactly one policy is lawful for the actual source context, B is an explicit confirmation of that policy, not a fictitious choice between legal outcomes. If the actual source has qualified multiple alternatives and authorized this actor to choose between them, both options may offer only that bounded choice. Neither implies that alternatives always exist, that every issuer is a records administrator, or that a person can pick a shorter or longer retention period merely because the UI presents a selector.

This choice advances R-POL-ordinary's application/default dimension. It **does not resolve the underlying schedule content**, identify a universal ordinary retention period, certify applicable law, or eliminate R-LEGACY. Those remain exact source-policy and qualification work. A polished summary cannot make an unapproved or ambiguous policy usable.

## Strongest practical alternatives

Illustrative example, not observed ministry research: Priya prepares an ordinary document request from an established source context. That source already has a current approved policy for this collection purpose. Under A she sees what governs the evidence and when the relevant clock begins, then issues normally; a separate eligible alternative is offered only if the source actually permits it. Under B she must explicitly apply/confirm the compatible policy before issuance, even when it is the only policy. Neither asks her to invent a legal period.

| **A — Apply the approved policy automatically (recommended)**        | Resolve the exact current source-purpose binding from trusted context; show a plain summary in ordinary preparation/review. | **B — Explicitly apply/confirm an approved policy for each request** | Keep preparation but require the issuer's deliberate policy application before new issuance. Offer only compatible choices; show one confirmation when only one is lawful.                                                                                                 | Makes exceptional or mixed operational contexts harder to overlook and provides a deliberate point to examine the governing purpose. Credible when staff have the actual contextual knowledge and authority. | Adds a step to every request and can become habitual confirmation or a false compliance attestation. It still cannot determine applicable rules from a click. A is the better ordinary default because the source already owns the purpose and should not outsource routine rule application to every request author. B's value is conscious context review, not more legal authority. A does not prohibit an actual source rule from demanding an explicit case determination; such a cohort follows its own qualified rule and cannot be auto-applied merely to preserve a frictionless screen.

The stronger alternative of **no records behavior until a later phase** is not acceptable: founder §14 already requires it. Nor is **free-form retain for N days** a valid option, because it invents a timer and owner discretion. A universal automatic default such as a legal example's seven years is equally invalid. A records center, custom rule language, mandatory second approver or per-file retention wizard is unnecessary for this fork.

## Twelve proposed question boundaries

These are research guardrails for a fair question, not an answer or a substitute for selected-answer review.

1. **Ordinary source cohort only.** Address new eligible ordinary requests and their collected-evidence retention binding. P18 official documents, P21 records, P22/P23 public assets, Support, resource publications and other specialist policies keep their owners. Q62 does not impose one global staff-facing policy interaction.
2. **Authority already required.** Both options require the actual approved applicable source policy. Retention content/approval remains source/records/privacy authority; issuance, policy administration and variant choice are different powers. Generic admin/issuer or a selector gives no new authority.
3. **No invented alternatives.** A summary may show a single applicable policy. Alternative selection exists only when the current source has qualified a compatible variant and allows the current actor to choose it. B with one lawful policy is deliberate confirmation, not evidence that staff may choose another.
4. **Distinct records and uses.** A request-level explanation is not one retention timer for every file, history, audit record, communication, staging object or copy. The binding may resolve separate admitted record obligations. Shared bytes, reused evidence and assemblies retain all independently authoritative use/hold requirements.
5. **Missing is not default.** Unknown, absent, stale, conflicting or unapproved policy does not become the longest policy, no retention, forever or caller choice. Preserve independently permitted preparation; block only the affected new consequential act and route to an actual qualified source/records maintainer. Resolving policy does not auto-issue or send.
6. **Actual boundary and original outcome.** Resolve and enforce the applicable binding at the source boundary, with current facts after waits. Recover a matching committed original operation before treating retry as a new request; disclosure remains current. A draft summary or prior click is not an enduring credential.
7. **Truthful version and later changes.** Preserve admitted policy/binding/trigger provenance. A later approved policy change uses the actual source's explicit successor/impact/correction path; neither silent rewriting of history nor permanent immunity from applicable later policy is implied. Exact operational semantics must be qualified before activation.
8. **Templates remain definitions.** Current context resolves the instance policy. A copied Q39 definition, folder, label, title, previous request or wrong-recipient replacement cannot smuggle an old policy or manufacture an extension. Any admitted template reference is subordinate to the actual current binding and source allowlist.
9. **Clocks remain distinct.** Requested by, original/fresh access ends, review due, technical rendition expiry, source retention and physical disposal are not aliases. Choice/application does not start a clock unless its actual typed trigger says so, and reading/download does not reset it.
10. **Holds and disposal remain separate.** A shorter alternative or end of a period never immediately deletes shared/held bytes. Current authority, required preservation, privacy ceilings, complete usage/copy coverage and verified disposition remain mandatory. The applicable owner resolves a genuine floor/ceiling conflict; a UI must not claim Deleted everywhere from metadata.
11. **Small useful UX and repair.** Show governing purpose, what is retained, meaningful start event and applicable limits in plain source-safe language, with detail progressively available. Do not promise a disposal date before its trigger/current holds are known. A wrong draft choice can be corrected before issuance; an already-admitted mistake needs an actual attributed source correction, not direct expiry editing. An issuer without repair power gets a capable route, not a powerless Help link.
12. **Stage and evidence.** Neither choice approves actual ordinary periods, jurisdiction/legal classification, new runtime policy engine, bulk migration, deletion, notifications, glossary term or formal publication. R-POL-ordinary and R-LEGACY remain exact open fields wherever not supplied. Current docs/code/UI are not native proof.

## Pre-question precision — lawful choice-required context and earlier preparation

The parent's two proposed refinements are compatible with the governing owners and clarify boundaries3/5/6/8 without selecting Q62. **A need not invent an automatic default in every context.** If the actual source has already approved all compatible alternatives, established their applicability and explicitly granted this actor the choice, but deliberately supplies no automatic default, A can present that required deliberate selection. This is a known lawful choice-required state. It is distinct from an unknown, incompatible, ambiguous or unapproved policy: presenting a dropdown does not repair those defects, and selecting the first/longest/most permissive policy is not a fallback.

**The policy summary at issuance does not authorize earlier data handling.** Q02 already requires lawful source-purpose admission before durable allocation and expensive processing. A saved draft, staged file, preparation derivative, retained request prose and eventual received evidence can have different actual purpose/copy obligations. Each earlier durable act must have its own qualified authority and applicable policy at that boundary; later issuance must not retroactively bless an unsupported upload or keep private preparation indefinitely. Independently lawful preparation can continue while the issuance-level binding is resolved. This preserves a useful small draft workflow without weakening source admission or creating a new policy engine.

These distinctions should appear in both options' common boundaries and the no-default/unknown/early-staging journeys. They add no staff override, records approval, legal period, automatic issuance, notification, post-issue policy edit or new glossary term.
