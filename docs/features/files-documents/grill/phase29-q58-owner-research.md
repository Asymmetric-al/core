## Recommendation and the genuine remaining decision

**Existing staff guidance is already owned.** The current, fully ratified Support D27-C contract supplies authored Internal Staff guides to current active-tenant Support readers, including consultation without a ticket. Q58 neither replaces that source nor claims that all staff guidance is ownerless. The remaining scope is P28 organization **document/resource publications** for a deliberately admitted broader staff-reader purpose, including eligible held documents or approved resource references. Support guides remain their own consultation-only authored-text source; no copying, migration, audience expansion or shared search is implied. The full governing D27-C clauses and companion detail were read after the first frontier pass; exact evidence is added below.

The strongest next question is the maintenance meaning of an organization resource that is useful to both staff and missionaries: **may one deliberately approved publication serve both independently qualified reader audiences, or must staff and missionary editions be separate publications?** This closes a real staff-reference outcome without asking whether the founder-required internal library should exist or choosing a physical catalog architecture.

Recommend **A — Allow a shared publication when the guidance is genuinely the same**, retaining separate publications when content, wording, language, rights, review timing or lifecycle needs differ. A avoids needless parallel maintenance for identical guidance while preserving deliberate audience approval. It does not make staff universal readers or curators. This recommendation is a product judgment, not measured Asym demand or demonstrated native capability.

**B — Keep audience-specific publications** is a serious alternative. It gives each audience an independently understood edition, replacement/withdrawal decision and review schedule. Both publications may reference the same exact lawfully reusable bytes; B does not require duplicate uploads or storage. Its cost is duplicated publication maintenance and the possibility that one edition becomes stale. A's cost is more consequential audience review and shared publication lifecycle: a replacement or publication withdrawal affects that publication for every remaining qualified audience. A is not a new engine for conditional prose, independently changing content per audience or custom access rules.

Illustrative example, not observed demand: an organization wants the same travel-safety guide available to its office staff and missionaries. Under A, a curator can deliberately publish the same exact guidance for both qualified audiences and maintain one shared publication. Under B, the curator maintains a staff edition and missionary edition, reusing the exact version where lawful. A staff-only incident-response procedure remains a separately restricted resource in either option; the example grants no staff-wide sensitive access.
