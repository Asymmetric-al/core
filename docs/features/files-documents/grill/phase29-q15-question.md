## Historical question and alternatives

**If a fresh thirty-day period expires normally and the request is still
genuinely unfinished, may authorized staff approve another fresh period?**

| Option                                                                  | Complete proposed policy                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Benefit and cost                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Allow further staff-approved periods after expiry (recommended)** | Each later eligible ordinary expiry can be considered for another deliberate source-approved admission using Q14's up-to-720-hour period. No overlapping unfinished admission in the same tenant/request/intended-recipient/original-admission lineage; no automatic renewal or pre-expiry extension. This standard lane has **no preset lifetime grant-count or cumulative-day ceiling**; genuine source-specific limits and current purpose/authority remain binding. | Preserves legitimate remaining work and its history without duplicate requests. Requires a real decision every time and can produce a long sequence of finite periods. Another approval or uninterrupted access is never promised.                         |
| **B — Permit only one fresh period per original request lineage**       | The first successfully admitted Q13 fresh period is the only one for that intended recipient/original request lineage. Its ordinary expiry ends further access through this unfinished lane, even if it was unused. Recovering that same effect is not another period.                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Stronger lifetime finality and fewer permission-changing decisions. Recipient continuation can end while legitimate work remains; existing staff work/help may continue, but cloning the same request or calling it a new purpose cannot bypass the limit. |

Both retain Q14's fixed source-issued end and unused-access behavior, Q13's
exact authority and scope, preserved history, genuine adverse restrictions
and separate eligible completion. Neither creates a duration menu, new role,
automatic email, archive policy or request-reopening capability.

## Recommendation and its honest limit

Recommend **A**. Q13 exists to preserve legitimate continuity within a real
Asym task. An arbitrary first-versus-second fresh grant distinction adds a
permanent stop without inspected evidence that it solves the underlying need.
Each new source decision can instead verify that the original purpose and
continued recipient access remain appropriate. This uses the already-selected
review/action and thirty-day rule rather than another settings system.

The tradeoff is real: **finite individual periods do not bound their total
number or aggregate lifetime**. A can support years of separate periods if
the source's original purpose genuinely remains current and each decision
qualifies. It creates no entitlement to uninterrupted access and does not
renew automatically. No new cooldown or total-day ceiling is proposed.
If guaranteed finality for this lane matters more
than further continuation, B is the honest choice.

No observed ministry demand or usability study establishes the right count.
There is also no inspected owner or comparator basis for inventing a middle
option of exactly two or three renewals. A recommendation is not a founder
answer, and a lack of a quota is not already settled by Q14.

## Practical illustration

This is a hypothetical decision test, not a claimed ministry workflow.

Maya received one staff-approved thirty-day period for an existing request.
That period ends while the source remains unfinished: perhaps a permitted
correction is still needed, or staff review remains and current recipient
readback still has an appropriate source purpose.

- Under **A**, the authorized staff actor reviews the actual current need and
  prior outcome. If eligible, **Grant access to continue** creates a new finite
  admission and shows its actual end. Old receipts and decisions stay intact.
- Under **B**, the same lane cannot issue a second fresh admission. Staff can
  continue independently authorized review, and existing help can explain
  actual remedies, but cannot promise another grant or manufacture a duplicate.
- Under **either**, completion, revocation, cancellation, binding shortening
  or ended purpose cannot be disguised as ordinary unfinished expiry. A failed
  sign-in, missing email or unknown outcome is not evidence that access expired.

The source may be unfinished while only staff review remains. Neither option
requires another upload to manufacture eligibility. Equally, an unchecked
“unfinished” flag is not enough to justify another period.

## Lean UX and authority consequences

Keep the existing request and staff action. Present the previous access outcome,
remaining original work/current permitted reading, intended recipient and
new fixed-period terms in context. After confirmed issuance, show the actual
new end and next actor. The recipient returns to authorized current files and
receipts without unnecessary reupload or another account.

No renewal dashboard, counter editor, mandatory essay, routine second approver,
scheduled future grant, reminder cadence or extra “activate” step is proposed.
If B wins, disclose its one-opportunity finality before the first fresh grant;
clicks and attempts proved not to have committed an admission do not consume
that opportunity. Failed delivery or a post-commit error does not erase an
admitted grant. Unknown commitment must be reconciled before deciding the
opportunity remains available.

An already-valid fresh admission cannot be replaced merely because proof or
delivery is temporarily unusable. Proposed nonoverlap applies to this exact
recipient/request/original lineage, not the tenant or all other participants.
It also disallows stacking multiple narrow fresh subsets within this lineage.
Current independent rights elsewhere are not removed.

Every newly reviewed scope stays within the original admitted purpose and
requirements, intersected with current eligibility. It is not a union of old
rights; nor does a prior deliberately narrow grant automatically become a new
permanent global ceiling. Actual binding restrictions remain effective.

For A, later consideration needs proof of the relevant preceding fresh period's
ordinary expiry and current complete lineage, while retaining original lawful
admission proof. The ancient original expiry alone cannot override a newer
valid, completed, unknown or adversely ended outcome. This explicitly extends
Q13-C02/P01's first-fresh eligibility wording and resolves C09/C12/P11's reserved
repeat/overlap policy if selected; it is not a discovery of already-granted power.
B resolves that reservation with one successful fresh admission instead.

Recover an existing deliberate source effect before considering a new one.
Two staff actors racing against the same prior state cannot issue overlapping
grants. A stale unadmitted intent must not wait through an intervening period
and silently become its successor: changed material lineage requires a new
current review. Use the existing source mutation/recovery boundary; do not
prescribe a new lock framework, counter service or queue to express this rule.

Source completion remains terminal for this unfinished lane. Eligible completion
under a valid fresh admission follows Q13/Q07 once; a completion in an access
gap does not retroactively qualify. A timely prior effect observed late recovers
its original outcome rather than permitting another unfinished grant. Q08's
separate completed-read eligibility/timing is not expanded by either option.

Comparable products do not present one universal repeat-count best practice.
The relevant proven patterns are explicit lifecycle actions, preserving prior
work and truthful consequences. None supplies evidence that unlimited repeated
Asym admissions, one lifetime opportunity, or a particular quota is optimal.
No external pattern overrides the source owner's permission or completion rules.
