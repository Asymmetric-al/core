The parent separately owns current repository/owner reconciliation. ## The most valuable next fork

Settle **whether the initial finite boundary governs only unfinished-request access or also caps completed reading**, before selecting its clock anchor, a number of days or more settings for Q08 fresh completed-read access.

Recommended founder question:

> When an ordinary request reaches normal completion while the recipient's original access is still valid, should completed reading follow its own Q07 window, or must it also stop at the original overall access deadline?

Recommend **A — A finite unfinished-stage boundary, followed by Q07 completed reading after eligible timely fulfillment, subject to genuine binding overall caps.**

This is a prospective ordinary-source policy: the initial boundary limits access while the request remains unfinished. If the source records eligible normal fulfillment while that authority is still valid, the selected Q07 completed-read policy takes over. A genuine overall permission end, purpose/security restriction or other binding cap still limits that reading. A finite unfinished-stage limit must never become an indefinite “until someone finishes” permission.

This is not an extension of expired access or a way to reinterpret an existing unconditional end. The policy's stage-specific meaning must be bound prospectively. A later name change, completion flag, policy relaxation or help request cannot turn an old overall restriction into a harmless unfinished-stage date.

B is the strongest alternative when one overall exposure boundary is more valuable than an ordinary completion-relative follow-up opportunity. It is legitimate; it is not inherently bad UX if the limit and consequences are clear. A is the recommendation because Q06/Q07 already select purposeful completed reading. That is product judgment, not measured evidence of ministry behavior or vendor consensus.

Do not add “until the person finishes” as a third option: without a finite unfinished boundary it conflicts with already-ratified finite authority. A sliding inactivity grant or automatic renewal is a different policy. A does not require either; it relies on the specific source fulfillment transition already needed for Q07.

No third option is needed to decide this scope. Clock anchor, numerical lifetime, source-supplied ends, modification powers and any active-expiry recovery can follow as distinct choices.

## Why this precedes fresh-access settings

Q09 makes the ordinary requested-by date a target. It therefore cannot supply the missing hard bound by accident. Q07 preserves earlier limits that actually bind; it does not independently choose whether this new ordinary source's unfinished-stage limit must also govern its completed phase.

Choosing an anchor or a number first can hide a more consequential assumption: does that date stop unfinished access, or every original read as well? The answer changes whether timely normal fulfillment can admit the intended completed view.

This question chooses the scope of the initial finite boundary only. It does not select an issuance/first-entry anchor, duration, mandatory due dates, configurable policy builder, active-request extension, reopening, reminder, unused-invitation interval, fresh-access timing/repetition, retention or new portal.

## The Q07 consequence must be explicit

Under ratified Q07, a genuine earlier binding overall access end continues to cap completed reading. Under prospective A, the selected unfinished-stage boundary is not such an overall cap merely because both are dates. Under B, it expressly is.

For explanation only, let U be a qualified unfinished-stage boundary and F normal fulfillment. Under A, fulfillment must qualify while original authority is still valid under U's source boundary rules. The completed end then follows Q07's already-ratified maximum and genuine overall caps; U does not become a cross-stage cap by accident. Under B, the original overall end E remains a cap on completed reading. These symbols explain the alternatives; they do not select storage architecture, a new timestamp function or a guaranteed interval.

- Under A, a timely qualified fulfillment can produce the Q07 completed view even if its end falls later than U. That possibility was prospectively authorized; it is not revival after U.
- Under B, source review close to E can leave a short completed view, and E must not be silently extended.
- Under either choice, if independently authorized source work finishes only after recipient authority already ended, the source may still fulfill but cannot revive recipient access through that act.
- Delayed handling must prove when fulfillment and recipient eligibility actually occurred. Processing time cannot substitute for F or reset its completed period.
- A genuine overall cap can shorten or prevent readback under either choice. Identify affected cohorts before claiming Q06/Q07 delivery; do not weaken the cap to make the claim true.

“Timely” here means eligible fulfillment before the applicable unfinished authority ends, not before Q09's requested-by target. A file can be received after that soft target and still proceed lawfully; current source eligibility remains required.

A's U is also not merely an upload cutoff: if the request remains unfinished when that access boundary arrives, original recipient access ends. A separate intake-only limit can stop writes sooner while independently valid reading continues. A request being submitted, all files being uploaded or a generic task becoming Done is not sufficient proof of normal source fulfillment.

Q08 covers deliberately authorized fresh **completed-read** access following its required prior admission. It does not supply active-request upload/correction reopening, manufacture a prior completed-view admission or automatically rescue someone whose original authority expired before fulfillment qualified. The help route may explain the permitted state and route to the source owner; it cannot promise an unselected extension.

## Calm, small user journey

### Staff preparing the ordinary request

Use the existing source context and intended recipient. Show the requested work and the proposed qualified timing in the same review; do not add a second approval, mandatory reason, new portal or a scheduling dashboard.

Under A, review the finite unfinished-stage terms and the existing Q07 transition, including any genuine overall cap. Do not show U as an unconditional final end for completed reading, or promise a completed period before eligibility is established. The sender should not need to manually build a grant policy; the ordinary source profile can supply the rule.

Under B, review the overall original ceiling and explain that it also caps the completed view. One boundary is simpler to communicate, but short available time near completion is a real cost, not a reason to conceal it.

For either choice, an unresolved future anchor must not yield a fabricated exact date. Once the chosen source act resolves a boundary, display the actual result. Ordinary time advancement alone should not cause an endless reconfirmation loop; material scope or authority changes remain guarded.

An end displayed to staff cannot be used as permission to expose private request metadata to someone whose role only allows limited administration. Existing scoped action and metadata authorization remain necessary.

### Recipient entry and return

- After qualified entry, keep **Requested by [target]** and the relevant access boundary understandable in main content. Under A, distinguish the limit while the request is unfinished from the possible completed-follow-up phase. Under B, state the overall ceiling. If a controlling earlier intake stop exists, show what action actually stops; do not make recipients calculate policy minima.
- If the entry code/link expires but source access remains valid, provide the existing qualified re-entry path. Do not say the whole request expired or require another submission.
- If original source access has actually ended, provide a safe ended-access state and the real permitted help route. Reauthentication is not the solution; no promised automatic extension.
- A recipient who received files/status before expiry must not be told their receipt was erased. Preserve trusted source facts even if the caller can no longer see the private record.
- For a person without current permission to learn that the request exists, use neutral entry/help behavior rather than exposing the deadline, filenames or recipient.
- At qualified timely fulfillment, show the real Q07 completed mode/end. Under A, the finite unfinished-stage boundary no longer governs that admitted phase; genuine overall caps still do. Under B, the original overall ceiling continues to limit it. Neither promises a full follow-up interval through another binding restriction.

Prefer a clear factual access limit over a ticking countdown or a frightening banner on every visit. Do not claim guaranteed availability until a date despite possible revocation. Under A, avoid presenting the unfinished limit as a recipient completion deadline: the person may have submitted everything while staff review remains. The precise copy should communicate current expectations and next action without asking people to learn grant mechanics.

Keyboard, speech, screen-reader, touch, reflow/zoom, long translated dates and timezone presentation need real qualification. “Accessible date label” is not proof of accessible task completion. A service can preserve already-admitted work across a session timeout while still enforcing source expiry; no private browser archive, automatic replay or upload-start grace follows.

## Stress tests the founder should understand

These are hypothetical product consequences, not observed ministry incidents:

1. Eligible normal fulfillment occurs shortly before the finite unfinished boundary. A can admit Q07 reading beyond that stage boundary, subject to actual overall caps; B stops original reading at its overall ceiling.
2. The recipient submits all material promptly but source review finishes after original authority has ended. Neither option invents timely fulfillment, revives access or labels the recipient overdue solely because staff review remained.
3. Fulfillment occurred while authority was valid but the event's consumer runs later. Admission must be proved from the actual historical source facts, then current authority checked; handling time cannot become a fresh anchor.
4. A genuine overall privacy/purpose limit is earlier than the prospective completed end. A and B both preserve it. A cannot relabel that cap as unfinished-only.
5. The recipient returns after actual expiry with a fresh sign-in code. Identity confirmation does not revive access or establish an absent completed-view admission.
6. The requested date passes but access and intake remain valid. Q09 continues to allow eligible work; no hidden date coupling.
7. The relevant access boundary arrives while submission or fulfillment outcome is uncertain. Existing source recovery preserves the actual committed fact; current disclosure permission still controls what can be shown. Mere upload completion cannot trigger Q07.
8. The source later requests more work after fulfillment. Its authority/date/eligibility must be explicit; it cannot silently reopen the completed phase, reset the original limit or become covered by Q08 read-only access.

## Evidence gaps and recommendation limit

No Asym recipient cohort, source adoption or actual active-grant runtime was qualified in this research. Before activation, the chosen source must bind the selected scope prospectively, choose its exact event/end and finite policy, preserve genuine overall caps and action restrictions, qualify fulfillment/recovery and prove real role/time/race behavior. A prospective stage-specific policy cannot rewrite the meaning of prior unconditional grants.

There is no measured basis here for picking a number of days, predicting staff-review or delayed-entry frequency, or asserting reduced support work. The evidence is sufficient to present the stage-boundary versus overall-ceiling choice honestly; further broad competitor research is unlikely to settle the founder's product preference.

Recommend A because it supports the already-selected completion-relative follow-up after timely qualified fulfillment while keeping unfinished access finite. Its cost is explicit stage semantics and a trustworthy transition; it does not solve completion after expiry. B remains the simpler overall-ceiling alternative, with the real cost that source review may consume the available completed-reading time. Keep the anchor, duration, source-supplied end rules, active-expiry recovery, extensions, reminders and Q08 fresh settings explicitly open. No Q10 answer has been selected.
