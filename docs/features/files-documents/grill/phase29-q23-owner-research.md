## Recommended next decision

Resolve the **ordinary item's negative review outcome** before designing reviewer assignment/pools. Q21 chose exact authorized staff acceptance; Q22 chose whole-request progression. The next missing part of the practical loop is what staff record when submitted evidence cannot yet be accepted and what that means for the recipient. Q03 already supplies eligible pending correction mechanics, so do not ask whether recipients may correct a submission again.

Suggested one question:

**Alongside Accept, should ordinary document review use a recoverable Request changes outcome, or should it also offer a separate terminal Not accepted outcome for an item?**

The single unresolved fork is **whether the ordinary native review surface adds a terminal item-level adverse decision**, not whether correction requests exist, which actor receives the review, whether to add a message, or whether to extend access.

**Recommend A for the ordinary native flow.** It makes the existing correction journey actionable without introducing a second adverse finality/exception system. This is a bounded product judgment, not an industry consensus or measured ministry preference. B is credible where a final item decision is meaningful and staff need direct explicit control; it is not rejected as inherently unsafe or unnecessary.

Do not frame A as “staff may only Accept or do nothing” or “contact the recipient elsewhere.” An actionable source-recorded Request changes outcome belongs in the same task context. Conversely, do not assume that selecting B also selects a universal Reopen, Waive, Cancel request, application Decline or staff override command.

- The founder brief asks for a responsible reviewer and visible missing work, but does not choose named person versus role/queue for this exact ordinary source.
- Workflow already requires currently eligible responsibility and distinguishes assignment from permission. Native routing still needs its own scoped decision and qualification; an arbitrary “all admins” pool is not available by default.

- Q22 makes this especially important: accepted evidence and qualified alternatives may permit automatic fulfillment, but an adverse outcome must never accidentally become a satisfied requirement or a canceled row omitted from an all-true calculation.

Reviewer responsibility, backup/queue/reassignment, bulk review and notification can follow as separate researched choices. Do not bundle them into Q23.

## Scope guards for either question option

1. **Item evidence, not the whole person's/application's decision.** Target the exact unaccepted ordinary item and source-relevant submitted evidence/instructions. A rejection or request for changes is not a legal authenticity judgment, application decline, public release decision or technical malware verdict.
2. **Meaningful actionable recipient direction, not an internal-note dump.** The source must provide safe approved wording and the real permitted next step. Do not adopt a general chat thread, mandatory essay/reason-code taxonomy, email default, attachment export or notification plan through this question. Whether a new public-to-recipient instruction is required and how it is edited belongs in the selected-answer review; private sensitive rationale is never exposed merely because staff reviewed it.
3. **No new access or clock.** Needs changes does not grant correction, reset a due target or access end, or promise that an expired recipient can act. Show the real source-help/fresh-access route only if eligible. Review can occur under independent staff authority after participant expiry without exposing results through expired access.
4. **Q03 exact correction remains settled.** A permitted correction is a new immutable successor, fences the predecessor at durable admission and needs new review. Request changes itself does not silently replace bytes, reopen accepted/frozen items, delete receipt/history or force reupload for a checker outage. Multi-file target scope stays exact.
5. **Current source decision and Q22 outcome remain distinct.** An unsatisfied required item does not fulfill a request. If the optional/adverse item is genuinely nonblocking, Q22's full source rule still controls completion; a negative label cannot hold everything open by itself. B's terminal item does not become a silent waiver or a canceled row dropped from completeness checks.
6. **No invented staff powers.** Exact source review authority and current basis/actor are required; role labels, assignment or file read permission are insufficient. Existing SoD and specialist protected outcomes remain. Choosing a new outcome requires its precise source capability mapping before activation, not another generic role/permission engine.
7. **No hidden recovery policy selection.** B must specify its finality and exact source-owned remedies before implementation; it cannot ship an unexplained dead end. Q23 does not itself authorize a generic reopen/waive/cancel route. A is not a guarantee of indefinite retries or a ban on existing qualified source final dispositions.
