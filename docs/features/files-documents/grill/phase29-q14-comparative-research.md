# Phase 29 Q14 — a complete finite timing profile for fresh unfinished access

Researched **21 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok**. The parent reports Q01–Q13 and their full amendments ratified. Q13 permits a deliberate new finite admission for eligible unfinished work, with a prospective completed-view consequence. **No Q14 profile is selected.** This note recommends and compares complete proposed timing profiles; it does not edit canonical documents, issue permission or qualify implementation.

Only public primary documentation and existing Q11–Q13 research were inspected. No private accounts, recipients, provider state, uploads, downloads, messages or live grants were tested. Only this research note was written.

## Recommended founder question

> What finite timing profile should apply when authorized staff grant fresh access to continue an eligible unfinished request: thirty days from fresh issuance, fourteen days from fresh issuance, or a seven-day entry window followed by thirty days from the first qualified entry?

Recommend **A — up to 30 elapsed days from the fresh authoritative source issuance**, preserving genuine earlier limits and current restrictions.

This is an explicit product judgment. It gives the continuing collection/correction-and-review task a concrete additional opportunity while choosing a shorter nominal period than the original sixty-day profile. It is not based on a measured ministry average, a source requirement, a claim that everyone has documents ready after asking for help, or the coincidental use of thirty days in Q07.

The next choice should be one standard fresh policy, not a menu offered to every sender. No option gives staff an arbitrary expiry picker or maximum within which they may invent a different duration.

The counts **7, 14 and 30 are proposed product values, not current vendor-derived optima**. An elapsed day means twenty-four hours, not a calendar day, a business day, local midnight, a month or a browser timezone calculation.

B is the strongest simpler, shorter-window alternative. Genuine independently binding overall or purpose limits can still constrain it.

There is a methodological tradeoff in this complete-profile framing. A model-first question would isolate the timing anchor more cleanly. Comparing complete policies reduces repeated small decisions, but C changes both the anchor and the maximum potential unfinished horizon. State that difference openly rather than attributing all of C's benefit to first-entry timing. This note supports the parent's complete-profile question with that caveat.

## Why A is the recommendation

Q13's fresh decision is already a consequential source act. A can resolve the same actual end for all authorized participants when that act occurs. The recipient's browser, mail transport, first visit and rendering success do not decide how long the source has authorized the unfinished stage.

This is especially understandable when prior files have already been received and staff review is the next action. The source can finish eligible work without requiring the recipient online or depending on an unused-entry event. C can also permit pre-visit completion, but its finite pre-entry boundary is a shorter deadline for that branch.

Thirty rather than fourteen gives more nominal opportunity to the selected collection/correction/review journey. That is a preference under uncertainty, not evidence of higher completion or lower support volume. A longer potential interval also leaves authority available for longer. Neither the privacy cost nor the benefit is quantified here.

Fresh access must not be described as a casual extension because the original request is old. Original and fresh admissions remain separate; prior history and adverse limits stay true. A grants only its own exact current scope. Q12's original sixty days does not restart.

The strongest objection is C's protection against late discovery: if a legitimate recipient sees the fresh grant late but enters before its seven-day boundary, a new working interval can remain useful. A spends time before that visit. The research contains no recipient-notification or help-request data proving that recipients are already available when staff grant fresh access, so do not dismiss that objection.

## Make C's unused opportunity and completion branch explicit

For explanation, let the fresh authoritative issuance occurrence be G, the nominal pre-entry end be L = G + 168 hours, first qualified entry be T, and the nominal working end be U = T + 720 hours. I remains reserved for the original issuance. Names here do not prescribe storage columns.

C is a prospectively authorized finite pre-entry/working policy. It is **not** an indefinitely dormant approval. Entry must qualify while the pre-entry opportunity is valid, including T < the actual applicable pre-entry end. Once that entry is durably established, later visits cannot reset it.

The seven-day boundary has a specific scope: it ends the unused opportunity. It does not also clip a timely activated working interval. Before entry, staff can know the enter-by date, the declared working rule and its bounded latest possible horizon; they must not invent an exact working end.

The strongest coherent C includes valid finite pre-entry fresh authority. Where the source already permits normal fulfillment without an online recipient, eligible fulfillment during that valid pre-entry authority can admit Q13's completed view. A later visit then opens the current completed view; it does not start an unfinished thirty-day working interval on a completed request.

This is different from an alternative that withholds all fresh authority until first entry. That alternative would block Q13 completed-view admission for source fulfillment before activation. It is not the C recommended for comparison here, and must not be introduced accidentally by a technical “inactive” flag.

Nothing in C bypasses Q01 proof for protected recipient use. A generic sign-in, old browser session, staff preview, old request visit or successful email callback is not necessarily the exact qualified entry to this fresh admission. No public metadata is exposed merely to start the clock.

## “First qualified entry” is not “first file opened”

Digify's viewing model provides a useful real comparison, but Asym must not copy its event without matching the task.

An unfinished request may have no uploaded file yet. A recipient may only need to read instructions or inspect a pending requirement. A file-preview failure must not make the permission clock unknowable, and selecting a download must not become mandatory activation. If C is selected, define one source-authorized recipient entry to the exact fresh request context, independent of a particular file format or a successful preview.

That event requires actual current Q01/source eligibility and durable source effect handling. It cannot be based on an email open, tracking click, prefetch, anonymous GET/HEAD, link scanner, staff visit or untrusted browser timestamp. A first-entry response lost after the source committed the event does not justify another interval.

Nor can the system infer that the person was ready, read the instructions or understood the consequence from the qualified event. The existing entry flow should explain the timing consequence plainly, without an extra timer-start ceremony. Defining a later “meaningful activity” heuristic would add a different policy and encourage ambiguity.

## Honest timing and state in the existing UI

### For A or B

Staff review the proposed source-relative rule and genuine known limits once. Before the authoritative fresh occurrence, a preview end is only an estimate. After issuance, show the actual resolved unfinished end. Material changes require source-safe review; ordinary time advancing under unchanged terms does not justify repeated confirmations.

After qualified recipient entry and current usability:

> Access while this request is unfinished ends [localized date, time and zone].

Do not say “you have thirty/fourteen days” after delayed arrival. Requested by stays separate. A source failure or proof timeout is not proof that the actual grant ended.

### For C before first qualified entry

The approved policy must communicate the finite enter-by boundary and working-period rule without falsely displaying a final working date. For example, where private disclosure is allowed:

> Enter this request before [actual pre-entry end]. Your first qualified entry starts the working period. We’ll then show the exact access end.

The product-facing version should use plain language appropriate to the actual Q01 entry flow; “qualified” here describes the contract, not required UI jargon. The thirty-day rule may be stated with the earlier-limit qualification, but it must not promise continuous unrestricted access or a full period after a true cap.

An unqualified expired link cannot display private timing merely to explain the rule. Show approved general terms through the safe doorway only where authorized, then actual request-specific state after qualification.

### For C after entry

Replace the pre-entry timing with the actual resolved working end and normal current actions. Do not continue showing the old seven-day boundary as though it closes validly activated work. Keep the source event authoritative; the browser's countdown is not permission.

If the source already completed while pre-entry authority was valid, show its current completed view/end instead. Do not make a returning person trigger a work period that no longer applies or force a download to acknowledge completion.

### For every option

Grant committed, currently usable, proof ready, notification attempted/delivered and recipient entry are separate facts. A failed invitation does not authorize re-anchoring. A valid end does not authorize every action through that date. Actual received work and the true next actor remain; timely submission awaiting staff review must not become recipient-overdue.

Use normal main-content timing, safe status changes and the existing help route. No extra timer-start button, routine second confirmation, mandatory explanation, account signup, reminder cadence or renewal entitlement is chosen.

## Preserve Q13's boundaries

- The clock belongs to the fresh prospective source admission. It does not edit or revive original I/U or use the original request's age as the new authority anchor.
- Original requirement lineage, exact intended recipient and current action/read scopes remain. New first submissions may be eligible; accepted/frozen writes, unrelated requirements and superseded content are not silently reopened.
- Completed reading remains within the reviewed fresh read scope and Q13's prospective mapping. Writing does not create a broader read right; expired scopes are not unioned.
- Eligible fulfillment uses its actual historical source occurrence and current authority. Completion in an unauthorized gap cannot be repaired by either anchor.
- Genuine adverse/purpose/overall restrictions retain their scopes and history. A pre-entry or unfinished-only deadline must not be relabeled as an overall completed cap; a real overall cap must not be demoted.
- Any old unadmitted upload or command needs a new deliberate authorized submission under the source contract. A new clock cannot silently rebind the old operation or rewrite receipt timing.
- Repetition/overlap remains separate. Another entry, fresh proof, reminder, returned help request or expired fresh interval does not authorize another grant.
- Q08's further fresh-read lineage and timing remain unexpanded and unselected.
- Source access, technical credentials, acceptance, custody, retention and device copies remain distinct.

## Why not a fourth option?

Scheduled activation could be a coherent separate product, and current Google PAM documentation demonstrates it for privileged resource grants. It would add another selected future time, no-show handling and a user/staff scheduling flow. There is no evidence that this unfinished request needs it, and Q13 did not grant a date editor or scheduling capability.

A free-form staff-selected end would similarly add administrative discretion and limits rather than merely answer the timing question. An indefinite “until they come back” clock violates the finite unused-opportunity requirement. A requested-by-plus-buffer rule would add target coupling and further numbers while Q09 keeps the target nonauthorizing.

The three proposed complete profiles cover a clear shorter choice, a simple recommended choice and a strong bounded first-entry alternative without inventing a general policy builder.

## Evidence to require after selection

Before describing the selected profile as usable, qualify its actual event and end under source concurrency, genuine caps, no-entry behavior, delayed delivery, lost responses, first entry from two devices, late proof, correction/acceptance races and completion before/after entry.

For C, specifically prove that only the exact current intended recipient's qualified entry activates one working interval; no scanner, generic login, staff view or old source visit can do so. Test the pre-entry completion branch and rejection of an activation after completion. For A/B, prove that transport and first visit cannot shift the original fresh end.

Test real comprehension of requested-by versus access end, the next actor when review remains, the difference between no entry and ended authority, and current permitted reading after completion. Keyboard, screen-reader, touch, reflow, low bandwidth and proof timers need actual qualification. No real testing is claimed here, and no unmeasured success or incident threshold is invented.

The next founder decision can choose one complete profile now. Its full adversarial review and ratification then make the exact selected count, anchor, pre-entry semantics where relevant and source boundaries reviewable. This research recommends A; it does not select Q14 on Conrad's behalf.
