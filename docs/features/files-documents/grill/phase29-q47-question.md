## Practical context

Illustration, not an observed ministry workflow: Elena submits the wrong document to an item Jordan requested. She notices the mistake while the evidence is still eligible for ordinary changes, but she does not yet have the right document ready.

Q03 already lets her send a correction when she has a replacement. Q28 lets authorized staff remove an eligible staff-added contribution from an item; it explicitly does not extend that removal power to participant-supplied evidence. Neither decision gives Elena an ordinary way to say, “Stop considering this particular submission,” without uploading something else.

With A, Elena deliberately withdraws the exact eligible contribution she submitted. It stops being current evidence for review. The receipt and required history remain, the request/item remains in force, and other supporting evidence is unaffected. She can supply appropriate evidence later only through the source's then-permitted path. Staff see the actual changed evidence basis before deciding; an already-open preview does not silently switch files.

With B, Elena uses Q03 when she has a correction. Without one, she contacts the actual source/help or privacy route to address the mistake. The system does not pretend that a message itself removes evidence, nor promise that Q28's staff-added removal works on her submission. B needs a genuinely capable owner response, not a generic button with no remedy.

## The question

**While an ordinary request remains eligible, should its currently authorized recipient be able to withdraw an exact current contribution they submitted from review consideration, without first uploading a replacement, provided it is not protected by an accepted or frozen source evidence basis?**

- **A — Allow recipients to withdraw their own eligible pending submission. Recommended.** Give recipients a deliberate in-context action to remove that exact contribution from current consideration. Preserve its receipt, history and required retention; leave other evidence and the requirement intact. This reduces dependence on staff when the replacement is not ready, but adds a real source command with current permission, exact-target checks, review-race protection and truthful recovery.
- **B — Keep correction plus source help.** Recipients can send a Q03 correction, but cannot independently withdraw an already-received contribution without replacement. When correction is insufficient, use the qualified source/help or privacy procedure. This keeps the recipient command surface smaller and source decisions centralized, at the cost of waiting for staff while the wrong contribution may remain current. No new staff withdrawal power is silently granted by choosing B.

**Recommendation: A, narrowly.** It completes a meaningful mistake-recovery journey without forcing a placeholder upload, a new request, cancellation or a staff-unlock ceremony. The action should be available under the actual current ordinary-source rules even if the evidence is still Checking or the request is waiting on staff; needing no preview is important. Accepted/frozen/closed material and privacy restriction/disposition remain their existing owners' work. This is a product judgment informed by the evidence below, not a claim that every comparable product implements this model or that Asym users have measured its benefit.

## Repository authority and current behavior

Q03 requires an immutable received correction, exact occurrence/use lineage, preservation of siblings and independent uses, admission-time fencing of the predecessor and no automatic revival if the successor fails. It explicitly grants no deletion. Q21 acceptance records an exact item/requirement/evidence basis; it is not necessarily an approval column per file. Q22 actual fulfillment, Q23 recoverable review, Q33 No longer required and Q34 cancellation remain distinct source decisions.

Q28's exact current unaccepted staff-added removal is a useful bounded-use precedent, but expressly cannot change participant-supplied evidence. Q47 A would therefore be an explicit new ordinary recipient mutation, not authority borrowed from Q03, Q28, read access, a folder or raw custody. Current P12/recipient context and actually qualified original/fresh unfinished action scope must authorize it. Completed access remains read-only; withdrawal cannot renew expired access.

Q02/Q04/Q05 own current-submission readback and participant-safe history. A withdrawn contribution is no longer current review evidence; a retained receipt/history row does not confer a new previous-file archive or continued original/preview access. If A is selected, its precise status/readback implications must be reconciled explicitly in the required amendment review rather than inventing a new historical-byte grant. Staff historical inspection remains separately source-authorized, and withdrawal is not a privacy erasure guarantee.

## Current primary research and fair counterevidence

[Dropbox's file-request guidance](https://help.dropbox.com/share/received-file-request), updated 9 December 2024 and checked now, tells someone who uploaded the wrong file to upload the correct one; both reach the requester. This is a strong precedent for Q03 and option B. It does not demonstrate self-service withdrawal, deletion or protected historical applicability.

[Submittable's single-piece withdrawal guidance](https://submittable.help/en/articles/3606786-how-can-i-withdraw-one-of-my-pieces-from-a-multi-piece-submission) tells applicants to contact the organization when one part of a multi-piece submission should leave consideration. The root's current page rendering identifies 16 September 2026; other relative-date renderings are not treated as different product semantics. This is direct counterevidence favoring B. Its [whole-submission withdrawal guide](https://submittable.help/en/articles/904887-how-can-i-withdraw-my-submission), dated 9 April 2026, is a different action; it must not be cited as proof of per-piece self-service withdrawal. Core does not import whole-application cancellation, mandatory reason text or a vendor account requirement.

[FileInvite's client experience guide](https://help.fileinvite.com/client-and-sender-experiences) describes uploaded-file deletion, while its [newer app-interface guidance](https://help.fileinvite.com/articles/user-interface-and-features) describes session-based download/delete restrictions. Its separate [staff deletion guide](https://help.fileinvite.com/delete-uploaded-files) calls deletion permanent. These document ways to remove mistaken uploads and materially different product choices, not proof that one FileInvite edition preserves Core's history, accepted evidence or current authority. Core should not copy permanent deletion or a browser-refresh/30-minute cutoff.

The useful modern principle is deliberate, understandable recovery with precise effect and current authority. There is no verified universal best-practice consensus requiring A. A is recommended because Core has already committed to immutable receipts, source-owned review and no unnecessary staff unlocking; a narrow withdrawal action fits those choices. A meaningful staff-assisted B is legitimate and simpler at the API boundary. Donor checkout and CMS asset deletion are weaker analogies than these document-collection and submission products.
