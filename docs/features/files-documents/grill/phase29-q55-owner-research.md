## Recommended next question

**When an internal resource reaches its stated review date, should it remain available with an honest review-due state, or automatically stop being available until a curator deliberately reviews it again?**

Recommend **A — Keep an independently safe and currently authorized publication available, clearly show that review is due, and make that maintenance visible to its authorized curator.** The date says when the organization intended to check the guidance again. Passing it does not itself establish that the content is wrong, dangerous or no longer authorized. The actual rights, safety, classification, audience, withdrawal and any independently binding validity limit continue to govern every release. A is not a permanent “safe until someone complains” guarantee or permission to conceal overdue review.

**B — Treat the review date as an additional publication end.** Once it is reached under the source's defined date semantics, ordinary library discovery/retrieval stops until an authorized curator deliberately re-reviews the exact material and qualifies publication again. This gives readers a firmer “only reviewed within its allowed period” library, useful for guidance that changes frequently or whose stale use is especially costly. It also creates real gaps when a curator is late even though the document remains correct. A scheduled stop is not deletion, recall or a cross-use access restriction; independent lawful uses and required retained history remain owned separately.

Illustrative situation, not observed Asym feedback: Maya is preparing a support conversation. Her organization published a practical guide, with Elena responsible for reviewing it by 1 November. On 2 November Elena has not completed that review. A lets Maya still open the currently lawful guide with an honest “Review due” signal. B makes the library entry unavailable until deliberate review. If a safety issue, expired license or actual source withdrawal independently blocks the guide, **both** choices stop the affected access immediately under that owner's rules. Merely moving a review date must not pretend a review occurred.

## Ten proposed question boundaries

1. **Use an actual existing owner.** First cohort is organization-curated P28 resource publications consumed by the missionary library and any already-qualified linked guide. No implied general staff catalog, public knowledge base, ordinary evidence or specialty Media extension.
2. **One date meaning.** Distinguish review due date, actual last review evidence, publication date/content version, hard source validity/rights expiry, access and byte retention. A review target cannot silently become a universal retention policy or assurance guarantee.
3. **Current source restrictions win under either answer.** A permits only independently safe, authorized, still-published content. Known withdrawal, revoked audience/rights, disallowed content or an actual hard limit still blocks affected delivery. B adds a review-based library-availability condition; it does not replace those gates.
4. **Actual review and publication are distinct acts/facts.** Reading, downloading, a cosmetic edit, moving the date or changing staff responsibility cannot fabricate Reviewed. Re-review can confirm unchanged exact content where actual source policy allows; it need not force a new file upload or byte version. Publishing revised material still follows C39's exact version and audience rules.
5. **No gratuitous cadence or second approval.** This fork chooses the effect of a source-owned date, not a compulsory interval, universal initial date, automatic notification, mandatory essay or extra moderator. The chosen answer's later review must resolve its ordinary current-state and repair semantics with minimal controls.
6. **Truthful reader/curator state.** A's due state is visible only under permitted metadata; B's unavailable result does not leak hidden resource names or withheld reasons. Curators have actual maintenance commands under current source capability, not a powerless Contact admin placeholder. A lost result cannot be reported as a failed review when it actually committed.
7. **No erasure or cross-use restriction.** Neither due status nor availability ending deletes bytes, alters independent use/hold/retention, retracts downloads, changes private shares or rewrites past receipt/review/publication evidence. Library-specific restriction and custody disposition remain separate.
8. **Keep guide/successor links honest.** C44 current link resolution applies. Safe independent guide advice may remain; unsafe dependent wording is withheld. No fabricated successor, automatic latest-byte substitution or whole-guide removal merely because a link changes. Existing invalid-reference curator maintenance remains owned; no new reader nag follows.
9. **Currentness, failure and races remain real.** Re-review, date edits, replacement, withdrawal, permission change and source-time boundary need source ordering, current expected basis and original-result recovery. Unknown review/publication/permission state cannot be inferred from empty data. Normal date interpretation is a source contract, not the browser's timezone or a copied Q46 request target rule.
10. **One coherent small resource experience.** Preserve existing text-first browsing, independent Open/Download, no forced preview, safe Back/Retry and usable phone/keyboard/AT behavior. No LMS, completion tracking, AI verification engine, new provider, generic review scheduler or new glossary term is selected here.
