# Phase 29 Q18 — a complete fixed fresh-read period

Research date: **21 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok**. The parent reports Q17 A and its full amendments ratified. **Q01–Q17 are the accepted baseline; no Q18 option is selected.** Q17 fixes the fresh completed-read end at qualified source issuance, including unused access. Q18 should now choose its finite numerical calculation profile, while leaving completed-read repetition/overlap/cumulative policy separate.

Only this note was written. Q17/Q08 boundaries and current primary product documentation were inspected. No canonical file, product, provider, permission, private account, UI or dependency was changed. Integer conversions were checked separately from source/runtime proof; no native grant, competitor account, browser/AT or intended-user test was performed.

## Recommended one-question framing

**What standard fixed period should apply to a new staff-approved completed-read grant: up to thirty elapsed days from its qualified source issuance, or fourteen elapsed days from that same event?**

Recommend **A — up to 30 elapsed days, exactly 720 hours from the new qualified source issuance**. Offer **B — up to 14 elapsed days, exactly 336 hours from that same event** as the strongest shorter alternative.

The recommendation deliberately gives more room for discovery, proof recovery and actual reading after Q17's clock has started. It does not assume immediate notification, recipient readiness, a simple one-file task or permission to renew later. Thirty is a product judgment in favor of useful opportunity; it is not measured Asym demand, a provider requirement or a universal modern-practice optimum.

Both options are complete ordinary profiles: an exact elapsed quantity, the already-ratified anchor, and genuine current scoped limits. Neither is an editable default, an administrator's selectable maximum, a new date picker or an instruction to copy a vendor's settings UI. One founder decision chooses the standard; source-specific higher restrictions retain their actual meaning.

## Why two choices are enough

Thirty and fourteen make the actual tradeoff clear: more opportunity after delay versus an earlier end to the authorized online reading opportunity. A third seven-day choice is possible as a product value, but the inspected evidence gives no reason to add it merely because a transfer provider uses seven days on some plans. Longer periods, a source-specific date editor and a duration menu likewise need a real product reason.

This should not reopen first-entry timing. Q17 already chose issuance. Nor should it ask separate questions for the unit, formula and unused behavior: the proposed complete profiles can make those meanings concrete in one decision. Q08's repeat/count/overlap/cumulative rules remain separately open; no duration answer supplies them.

## Exact unit meanings

The following conversions were checked as integer arithmetic, not by running the application:

| Proposed period | Hours |   Seconds |  Milliseconds |
| --------------- | ----: | --------: | ------------: |
| 30 elapsed days |   720 | 2,592,000 | 2,592,000,000 |
| 14 elapsed days |   336 | 1,209,600 | 1,209,600,000 |

An elapsed day here means twenty-four hours in the qualified source's supported instant semantics. It does not mean a calendar month, business day, end of local day, calendar-date subtraction, first visit or time actively spent reading. A calendar/DST change may alter the displayed local clock hour without altering the actual interval. Formatting must not round an exclusive source end into a different promise.

The proposal is an exact standard quantity under Q17's source event, not a maximum from which staff can pick any smaller amount. “Up to” preserves genuine scoped source limits and current restrictions. It does not authorize a date editor, sender discretion, hidden tier, arbitrary shortening or one minimum of every technical timestamp.

The full native profile still must validate supported precision/range, finite terms, exact source currentness and the meaningful exclusive boundary. Null/invalid/already-ended proposed terms cannot create live access. Q17's same-effect recovery preserves an expired historical result without recalculating it under a newer value. Existing source checks and serving fences enforce expiry; the numeric period does not justify a resident browser/process timer as authority.

## Hypothetical arithmetic that exposes the tradeoff

These examples assume all predicates hold, identical source issuance and no other restrictions. They are arithmetic illustrations, not claims about ministry behavior or successful recipient access.

| Scenario                                                                   | A: 30 elapsed days                                                                      | B: 14 elapsed days                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| First qualified visit after ten elapsed days                               | At most twenty elapsed days remain                                                      | At most four elapsed days remain                          |
| First visit at the fourteen-day boundary                                   | At most sixteen elapsed days remain                                                     | The exclusive nominal end has been reached                |
| Recipient never visits                                                     | Ends at the same source-issued thirty-day boundary                                      | Ends at the same source-issued fourteen-day boundary      |
| Genuine whole-grant limit ends seven days after issuance                   | The genuine limit can make the actual end seven days                                    | The same genuine limit can make the actual end seven days |
| One member has an earlier limit but authorized status/other members remain | Only that action/member is limited; the grant end does not promise it remains available | The same scoped behavior applies                          |

Earlier limits can erase the practical difference between the choices. That is a reason to qualify real source/cohort fit, not to clear restrictions, claim a guaranteed minimum or hide a later end. The old predecessor's ordinary expiry is historical, not itself a cap that makes the new Q08 grant impossible; genuine continuing overall/purpose/content-use limits remain binding.

Likewise, a failed proof step or renderer may reduce useful remaining time without being a source end. Do not calculate the end as the minimum of proof-token TTLs, session timeouts, provider URL expiry, worker deadlines, retention dates and all member dates. Each has its existing scope and owner.

## Keep the experience small

The staff actor uses the same **Grant read access** action with authorized scope and the selected standard period. There is no duration dropdown or calendar UI. Before the real source event, an end preview is explicitly an estimate; after a confirmed source result, show the actual end and current state. No extra confirmation is required just because time advanced under unchanged terms.

The recipient sees **Read-only access ends [actual date, time and zone]** in the existing completed context, with current allowed preview/download/status/history actions. A late visitor is not told they have a newly starting thirty or fourteen days. A legitimate status-only view uses the same rule without requiring a file to open or download.

Known source issuance is not notification, current proof, renderer readiness or successful human reading. Lost responses recover the same effect; failed delivery or proof does not reset time. A missing required end after reported commitment is an observation/contract failure, not first-entry activation pending. Current end, genuine file/action limits and historical results remain distinguishable.

After real expiry, use the existing safe source-owned help route without promising another period. The request stays complete/read-only; there is no new completion stage, upload/correction control, automatic download or clone. The unchanged historical completed ceiling and current purpose keep this a task-specific reading capability. Merely choosing thirty rather than fourteen neither creates nor prevents a generic drive; scope and authority enforce that boundary.

Preserve existing accessible proof/paste, keyboard/focus/status, touch/reflow and low-bandwidth recovery. Put essential timing in main content, not only a toast or countdown. A longer period is not a substitute for fixing a broken handoff, inaccessible proof or unavailable content. Neither option selects reminders, autoemail, a new help queue or a full recipient account.

## Boundaries the question must carry forward

1. **New read issuance is the anchor.** Use Q17's qualified fresh source event; not original issuance, old fulfillment, staff click, delivery, first visit or later observation.
2. **One end, including unused access.** No first-entry window, pause, activity reset, grace or sliding date follows. Retries and repairs preserve the original admitted result.
3. **Exact scope and real limits still win.** Keep Q16's actual completed member/original-version/action/context ceiling and current eligibility. Genuine later binding restrictions retain their scope; technical failure is not automatically permanent shortening.
4. **Read-only remains read-only.** No new file membership, writes, recipient change, business completion, retention/reset, resharing or off-platform copy entitlement is added.
5. **This is not a repeat policy.** Thirty/fourteen bounds one new admission, not total request history. Q15's no-preset-cap unfinished rule remains unrelated, and Q08 repeats/overlap/cumulative behavior still require an explicit choice before activation.
6. **This is not release proof.** Native source/roles/current-serving/races/restore and composed browser/AT/user qualification remain necessary. A correct conversion or vendor default does not qualify them.

## Suggested founder-facing question

**How long should each new staff-approved completed-read grant last from its source issuance, before any genuine earlier limits?**

- **A — Up to 30 elapsed days (recommended).** Exactly 720 hours from the new source issuance, with the actual end shown. More room for discovery and access recovery, at the cost of a longer authorized online window.
- **B — Up to 14 elapsed days.** Exactly 336 hours from the same source event. A tighter individual period, with less time remaining after delay.

Both use one fixed end even if never opened, preserve genuine source restrictions and stay within the existing completed-read scope. The choice sets one standard profile, not a staff settings menu. It does not decide whether another grant can later be issued.

## Evidence limits and next step

No source or study establishes an optimal Asym count. Vendor expiry defaults are feature/edition-specific; some numbers are commercial plan choices, configurable presets, maxima or retention periods. A's recommendation is a bounded product judgment favoring useful opportunity under Q17's fixed clock. B's shorter authorized window is a credible competing value.

Once the founder chooses, the adversarial review should qualify exact elapsed arithmetic, current scoped caps, late/no visit, failed proof/representation/handoff, historical-versus-current replay, status-only/narrow scope and the honest visible end. If actual applicable caps make the selected profile ineffective for a cohort, address that through the source owner rather than quietly extending it. Repeat/overlap/cumulative policy remains a separate unresolved activation gate.

No Q18 selection, canonical change, new UI, duration setting, provider operation or runtime implementation is made here.
