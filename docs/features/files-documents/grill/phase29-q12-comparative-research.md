# Phase 29 Q12 — the ordinary unfinished-access lifetime

Researched 21 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. The parent reports Q01–Q11 ratified, including the full Q11 amendments. **No Q12 option has been selected.** This note recommends a practical next founder decision; it does not alter canonical contracts, source policy, existing grants, product code or provider state.

Only current public primary documentation and the existing local Q11 research/question were inspected. No private accounts, product workflows, deliveries, recipient sessions or user studies were tested.

## Recommended single decision

> For a new ordinary private document request, should the initial unfinished-access period be 60, 30 or 90 elapsed days from original authoritative issuance?

Recommend **A — 60 elapsed days, meaning 1,440 hours**, with genuine earlier limits preserved.

This is a complete proposed ordinary policy, not a maximum within which staff choose any value, a sender-editable default, a new organization settings screen or a promise of continuous availability. The selected policy would resolve the original end once under Q11. Q10 continues to govern eligible transition into the separately bounded completed stage.

The recommendation is a product judgment: the fixed clock includes delivery delay, recipient preparation, submission, any permitted pending correction and source review. Sixty days gives those activities more nominal opportunity than thirty without choosing the longest of the three candidates. **There is no measured Asym evidence that sixty is sufficient, optimal or safer overall.** It is not recommended because it lies arithmetically between two other numbers, and no vendor establishes it as a general standard.

The strongest alternative is thirty days. It limits the period in which an unused or still-unfinished authorization can remain available. That is a real privacy/security benefit, even when every use still requires current proof and authority. A shorter period may also lead to more unfinished expiries; their frequency and operational cost are unmeasured. Longer lifetime and greater incident probability must not be treated as a quantified or linear relationship.

## Why choose a fixed issuance-relative profile now

Q11 selected when the source establishes the end, but did not yet determine the numeric policy or whether the end is calculated from a target date. Selecting one ordinary duration now is more useful than asking only whether the policy should be “source-defined,” since every valid option must already be owned and enforced by the source.

## Preserve the ratified boundaries

1. **Original issuance remains authoritative.** Draft creation, a send-button click, queue time, provider acceptance, delivery, email opens, scanner requests and first entry do not start or restart this period.
2. **The period applies to unfinished access.** It is not merely an upload deadline. Submission and staff review do not pause it. Existing item acceptance, correction, intake, purpose and safety restrictions still govern individual actions.
3. **Q09 targets stay separate.** Passing a requested-by date alone does not end otherwise-authorized intake. Moving that target, requesting a correction or adding later work does not reset or lengthen U.
4. **Q10 stays conditional.** Eligible timely normal fulfillment can admit the separately bounded Q07 completed view. Receipt, scanning and review activity are not substitutes for fulfillment. Source completion after actual authority ended does not revive recipient access.
5. **No full-period guarantee.** Genuine independent caps or restrictions can shorten actual opportunity. A cohort whose true restrictions leave inadequate or no usable access cannot be called successfully qualified merely because the nominal policy says sixty days.
6. **No new grant powers.** Selecting a duration does not authorize extension, resend-based reissuance, renewal, reopening, arbitrary recipient replacement, altered source identity or backdating. Q08 still needs an actual prior ordinary completed-view admission.
7. **Prospective adoption only.** Existing specialist, legacy or already-admitted ends keep their real meaning. A future approved policy revision is not permission to recalculate current grants. No immediate policy editor or administrative override is implied.
8. **Proof and source authority stay distinct.** A technical sign-in link can expire while source access remains valid. A proof outage is not proof of source expiry; protected use still fails closed until required current proof and authority exist.
9. **No retention change.** Ending recipient access does not imply deleting source evidence, originals, derivatives, receipts or audit history. Neither retained storage nor an already downloaded device copy creates continuing source permission.

For 60 days, eligible fulfillment near the unfinished end can lead to a later Q07 completed end, subject to its actual conditions and genuine overall limits. Do not present this as a guaranteed ninety-day grant or as a new overall ninety-day cap. It is a consequence of two differently scoped stages, not another product selection.

## Complete, calm UX under any of the three choices

The duration should appear as a source policy, not a new form the sender must fill out. Use the existing request context and shared Core components.

Before issuance, an authorized sender can understand the proposed resulting boundary and any genuine earlier restriction relevant to the task. A source-relative preview must not promise an immutable exact deadline before the authoritative issuance instant is established. After issuance, read back the actual stored end and source result. No new confirmation modal, scheduling capability or deadline editor is implied.

On qualified recipient entry, place the actual end in normal page content:

> Access while this request is unfinished ends [localized date, time and zone].

If a requested-by target exists, show **Requested by** separately. Use Q10's conditional completed-stage explanation:

> If the organization completes it before then, we’ll show any available read-only access and its end here.

Show the controlling end with sufficient precision and context; do not round it into a different permitted instant. “60 days” should not obscure that the person who first visits late has less time remaining, or that a true earlier limit applies. Essential information must not depend on hover, color, a ticking timer or a notification the person may never receive.

When the recipient has already submitted and the organization is reviewing, say **Received — awaiting review** or the true equivalent. Do not show recipient-overdue blame merely because source completion remains outstanding. Staff need the existing contextual evidence that review is pending and access has a real end; this is not authorization for a new queue, reminder cadence or service-level promise.

At actual expiry, distinguish it from expired sign-in proof or a temporary observer failure. Offer the already-governed, privacy-safe help route, without promising an extension or encouraging sensitive email attachments. Lost responses reconcile the same source result; retries and resends do not award another sixty/thirty/ninety days.

## Accessibility, clock precision and evidence limits

[WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/timing-adjustable.html) is Level A. Its longer-than-twenty-hours exception is not a recommendation to choose a particular number of days, a blanket security exception, or proof that the whole experience conforms. Short proof/session/upload timers still require their own analysis. Earlier source limits may also change the actual timing situation. This numeric choice does not add an extension control.

The W3C [Working with Time and Timezones](https://www.w3.org/TR/timezone/) document is a **26 July 2025 Group Draft Note**, not an endorsed Recommendation. Its distinction between instants, local dates and timezones supports careful calculation/display, not a specific Asym lifetime. Exact elapsed hours avoid confusing the policy with months or wall-clock calendar arithmetic; localization still needs testing.

No selected duration proves keyboard, screen-reader, touch, mobile, low-bandwidth, timezone or expiry-boundary usability. No actual browser, assistive-technology or native source policy test was performed for this note. No change to dependency choices or UI libraries is indicated.

## What later evidence should challenge

The policy should be qualified for actual ordinary source cohorts before anyone claims operational readiness. Useful questions are whether intended recipients can reach the correct request, whether timely submissions expire while source review is pending, whether late corrections are practically possible before the true end, and whether known early caps make the nominal period misleading.

These are evaluation questions, not a new telemetry program or invented numerical release thresholds. The existing authorized observability and support boundaries should determine collection, ownership and response. Do not infer that a person read the instructions from mail clicks, or record sensitive files and recipient details just to prove a duration choice.

A longer interval is not automatically more humane, and a shorter one is not automatically sufficient security. The chosen period only sets a finite opportunity under the already-required controls. Current authorization, minimum disclosure, proof quality, file safety, accurate status and no-revival enforcement remain necessary at every duration.

## Recommendation to the parent

Ask the complete numeric-profile question now rather than another abstract “who sets the policy” fork. Recommend **60 elapsed days**, present **30** as the strongest shorter-exposure alternative and **90** as the longer-opportunity alternative. Explain that this is an explicit preference for giving the fixed issuance-to-review lifecycle more room, not a verified ministry average or vendor mandate.

Keep the answer small for the founder: actual end fixed at original issuance, requested-by target separate, genuine earlier limits retained, no reset from visits/resends/corrections, eligible completed-stage policy unchanged. Defer newly requested recovery powers or policy customization; do not imply they are already available to compensate for whichever number is chosen.

The research supports making this tradeoff concrete. It does not select the answer on Conrad's behalf.
