## Recommendation: ask whether deliberately approved fresh readback is available

This is the strongest next unresolved branch. Q05 promised accountable help without promising release; Q06 preserved independent source work and barred implicit renewal; Q07 fixed the ordinary initial end but expressly left later fresh authority/extension unselected. The next decision should settle what useful assistance can mean when that **ordinary timebox** has genuinely ended.

Recommended framing:

> **After an ordinary completed request's access period expires, should an authorized staff member be able to approve fresh, time-limited read-only access for that same verified recipient when the source still permits the requested files and purpose?**

| Option                                                             | Concrete behavior                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Tradeoff                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **A — Allow source-approved fresh read-only access (recommended)** | Through the existing help/source work, a staff member with the exact current authority may deliberately approve another finite access admission to the same completed request's permitted current-submission view. The old grant and deadline remain ended; intake stays closed. | Helps legitimate delayed follow-up without asking for another upload or emailing a private attachment. Requires an explicitly adopted source command, qualified staff authority, current recipient/content/purpose checks, finite timing and audited recovery. Approval is not guaranteed. |
| **B — Keep this completed-request lane closed after expiry**       | The ordinary recipient view stays closed. Governed help can explain and route the need; any exceptional disclosure must use an already qualified independent source route.                                                                                                       | Simpler, more final timebox and less repeat exposure. A genuine need may require manual source work or have no permitted retrieval route. Do not promise an alternative delivery route when none is qualified.                                                                             |

Both are viable. A better serves a real return-later need without changing the original completion or silently extending the initial interval. B is defensible where the completed request purpose is strictly finished or the organization wants a final recipient timebox. We have not measured how often either case occurs or the resulting support load; A is product judgment, not an external best-practice mandate.

Two alternatives are enough. A third “automatically renew after mailbox verification” would conflate proof with source permission and risk making the fixed period an indefinitely repeatable archive. It could be a separate deliberately qualified product model, but it is not necessary to present as an equally strong default here. “Create/reopen a request to get the files” is not a clean alternative: it can fabricate collection work and alter settled Q03/Q06 semantics.

## Exact scope that keeps this one decision

The question selects **whether an explicit staff-authorized fresh readback path exists**, rather than deciding its numerical lifetime, an extension UI, or who belongs to a new role.

If A is selected, its reviewed meaning should be:

- The prior ordinary admission actually existed and reached its timebox. Do not infer this from a generic Expired badge or repair unproved historical admission by creating a purported renewal.
- The source still permits this exact recipient, purpose, current own-submission member set and individual representations. Qualified staff must have authority to grant this particular source access; reading the request, being assigned help, being an administrator or knowing the recipient is insufficient.
- The new source-authorized access has its own finite, explicit admission/end evidence and preserves the old grant, original completion, earlier ended deadline and history. This is a logical authority distinction, **not a mandated extra table/token taxonomy or second PDP**.
- No stale credential/session automatically becomes usable again. Any new issuance or continuation uses the existing qualified protected-action/native identity/P12 seams. Q01's exact mailbox/request/purpose/revision proof and explicitly qualified proof reuse still govern.
- Q06's completed read-only mode remains: current permitted status/history, Q02 preview and Q04 original actions as individually eligible. No intake, corrections, acceptance changes, source reopening, prior-version bytes, additional recipients or universal library access is implied.
- An approval cannot defeat a current source-purpose end, privacy/security floor, withdrawn/revoked authority, actual source-content expiry, disposal, hold-limited custody or other binding prohibition. Unknown or stronger source requirements fail the affected route safely.
- Earlier genuine source-authority shortening remains binding under Q07. This question does not create an override for it. It concerns the ordinary completed-view timebox, not reversing unrelated termination.
- A held or retained file is not necessarily available for ordinary participant serving. If content is unavailable or its permitted use ended, assistance routes the real problem; it does not copy the bytes into a new request to evade their source.
- Existing independently qualified official-document/staff/source routes retain their own rights and lifetimes under either answer.

The wording **“fresh read-only access”** is preferable to “reset the 30 days” or “restore the old link.” The latter two imply the very historical rewrite/revival Q07 ruled out.

This is a substantive prospective request-owner policy amendment if selected. Existing P18 fresh-grant behavior is an architectural precedent, not authority already inherited by an ordinary request.

## Why active-request duration need not be asked first

Active/open-request duration remains an actual activation dependency: the source must define finite, qualified current recipient eligibility and its exact earlier limits. Q07's at-fulfillment eligibility cannot be implemented by guessing this.

However, Q08's **product choice** does not depend on selecting that numeric active duration. It asks about an already admitted, normally completed request after the known ordinary completed-access timebox. Whether a deliberate new source read admission is allowed is meaningful under either an earlier fixed open-request policy or a qualified completion-transition policy.

Recommended sequence:

1. Decide whether this explicit fresh-readback path exists.
2. If yes, decide its bounded timing/anchor and repeated-use rules before activation; do not automatically copy Q07's 30 days.
3. Resolve the ordinary active-request access/due-date policy, or bind exact already qualified source-specific policies, before enabling the native request profile.

Do not present remaining timing as optional implementation discretion. Conversely, no need to block this founder choice behind an unrelated universal expiry for every source. The owner register explicitly requires exact source qualification and preserves narrower source clocks.

## Remaining decisions if A is chosen

These should be made explicit later, not silently borrowed from Q07 or P18:

- Fresh admission's finite duration, clock anchor, latest permissible end and behavior when applicable earlier limits shorten it.
- Whether and under what rule another fresh period may be approved again; limits and source review needed to avoid a de facto perpetual archive.
- Whether approval is available only after ordinary expiry or can be scheduled before it; this question is deliberately after expiry.
- Exact staff capability and delegation mapping, audit/approval semantics, current policy and recipient revision guards. The source may qualify a small-organization-compatible route without imposing arbitrary two-person approval.
- Whether the fresh authority covers the existing permitted completed view or a smaller explicitly selected scope. It must never silently expand to historical/staff/new recipient material.
- Any new protected-action communication or pending-help UX. Neither automatic email nor a new support queue is selected here; P17/P6 remain the existing owners.
- Exact active/open-request eligibility, due-date and finite access policy before initial-profile activation.

Those are dependent policy/qualification details, not reasons to obscure the initial founder question. A source can keep the fresh-readback capability inactive until its complete finite contract is decided and proved. Choosing B requires honest assistance behavior and cannot be used to remove previously qualified specialist rights.
