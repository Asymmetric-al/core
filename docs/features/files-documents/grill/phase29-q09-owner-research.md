## Recommendation: decide the ordinary due-date effect before fresh-access timing

The more foundational next choice is **what the displayed due date does to outstanding recipient intake**. It affects the normal request journey, how a recipient can finish it, and how the source distinguishes late intake from authorization expiry and completed follow-up.

The fresh-access clock is also a genuine unresolved choice, but concerns an exceptional later admission. Its duration/anchor/repetition contract remains an explicit activation gate under Q08; it is not lost or silently delegated by asking this ordinary-path question first.

Recommended question:

> **For an ordinary document request, if the recipient still has files to submit when the due date passes, should Asym still let them send otherwise-permitted late files, or stop new submissions until the source explicitly permits more time?**

State the common condition immediately: the recipient's separately governed access must still be valid; existing stricter source deadlines, item freezes and accepted-evidence rules always control. This is a default for the new ordinary request purpose, not an override of application, finance, care or another specialist owner.

| Option                                                          | Practical behavior                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Tradeoff                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Keep accepting permitted late submissions (recommended)** | Due date means the requested-by target. Outstanding recipient work can be overdue; otherwise-authorized initial submissions and pending-item corrections can still be durably received after it, visibly retaining their actual time. | Helps finish ordinary collection without a staff date-change step solely because a target was missed. Staff must distinguish lateness from evidence acceptance, and the separate finite source-access end still stops access.                             |
| **B — Stop new submissions at the due date**                    | Due date is the intake cutoff for its exact source scope. Later initial submissions and otherwise-permitted corrections are blocked unless a source-authorized change permits them.                                                   | Clearer cutoff and stronger operational finality. Late recipients depend on an explicit source action before sending, increasing interruption/help work. This does not automatically reject already received evidence or end separately authorized reads. |

Recommend A for ordinary administrative collection: receiving a late file can be useful without pretending it arrived on time or that it satisfies the source requirement. B is appropriate where the owner needs an enforceable intake cutoff. No empirical Asym late-return, staff-workload or deadline-success data was obtained; this is product judgment.

A third option allowing staff to configure target-versus-cutoff on every request would add a policy-setting decision rather than sharpen this default. Do not offer it merely to avoid choosing. Exact specialist owners may already have stricter rules without making the ordinary request a general policy builder.

## The central UX constraint: recipient work versus staff review

**An unfinished request is not necessarily overdue recipient work.**

- A file durably received by its applicable due instant is still timely while hygiene, preview preparation or staff review is pending. Do not mark the recipient late simply because the source has not accepted evidence or completed the request.
- Receipt does not imply that a requirement is satisfied. Keep “received / being checked / under review / needs another submission” distinct, using source meaning.
- Outstanding recipient intake can be overdue under A. A timely admitted submission can remain under review without an overdue-upload badge under either option.
- A later correction is a distinct admission with its own trusted receipt. Preserve the original timely receipt and exact Q03 lineage; do not rewrite it as late because the successor arrived later.
- If review later requests another item/correction, the source must supply the applicable action and due-date meaning. This question does not invent an automatic new correction interval or declare every later correction overdue.
- Date edits/retries must not rewrite accepted receipt times or historical source decisions. Later detailed lateness/current-deadline projection must reconcile source revisions explicitly; no automatic backdating or retroactive authority is implied.

Use “due date,” “submission received,” “under review” and “access ends” for their actual distinct meanings. The founder is selecting late-intake behavior, not a single universal Expired/Overdue state.

## Common guardrails for either answer

1. **Source specificity.** Existing hard owner intake deadlines, accepted/frozen items, current source denial and source-role restrictions remain controlling. This question applies to the new ordinary native request profile where that choice is genuinely open.
2. **Finite authority still required.** A due-date target does not become “upload forever.” Current recipient/source access remains independently bounded. Its actual active duration/anchor/earlier-cap policy must still be decided and qualified before activation.
3. **No blanket recipient lockout.** B closes the specified intake actions, not automatically every permitted status/history/preview/original read. Both options preserve current separately qualified read authority and source work.
4. **No source decision from time.** Passing the date does not satisfy, reject, waive, cancel or complete the request. No reminders or escalation messages are activated by this question.
5. **Exact scope.** Apply the source date to its declared request/item/recipient action scope. Do not silently make one member's date close unrelated supporting work or invent one-file-per-requirement semantics.
6. **Authoritative admission.** Browser selection or transfer start is not durable receipt. Final byte/source admission and current scope govern; replay recovers the same receipt. Deadline-race details must be adversarially reviewed after selection, preserving Q03.
7. **No historical rewrite.** Timely received evidence stays timely evidence even if reviewed later. Later corrections/date changes retain their own facts and lineage.
8. **No automatic time extension.** Opening, sign-in, resending, upload retry, review delay and a changed display timezone cannot renew finite source authority. Any permitted source deadline amendment remains explicit and revision-aware.

## Remaining decisions and sequence

Q09 chooses only the ordinary due date's effect on **outstanding recipient intake**.

It does not choose:

- How many days the original active access lasts, its anchor, its hard outer bound, or whether a source uses another explicitly qualified existing timing policy.
- Default due-date suggestion, whether date-only or time-specific entry is offered, timezone/cutoff precision or item-specific date UI.
- Who may amend a deadline or admit a late exception under B, and how a material date change is reviewed.
- A new correction due period after review, notices, reminders, escalation frequency, cancellation or rejection.
- Fresh Q08 period, activation anchor, repeat count or overlap behavior.

Those remaining policies must close before the affected profile activates; they are not engineering defaults to guess.

Recommended order from here: settle due-date late-intake behavior; resolve the ordinary active source-access policy and its relation to intake/review; then settle the fresh-readback timing/anchor and repeat policy. The parent can adjust sequencing based on the founder's answer, but should not bypass original finite eligibility while polishing the exceptional renewal path.

## Why not choose the fresh-readback anchor first?

Issuance-relative versus bounded-first-qualified-use is a real choice. Issuance-relative is simpler and spends time while delivery/help is delayed; first-use-relative can improve recipient utility but requires a finite latest activation/outer end and careful proof/delivery/replay semantics. Neither is selected here, and Q08's staff review explicitly accommodates reviewing a future timing rule rather than pretending an unknown exact end already exists.

That fork is less foundational than whether the ordinary due date lets a recipient submit at all. Settling the ordinary rule first also helps reveal whether repeated fresh-readback requests are compensating for a poorly defined initial source-access policy. Do not use this prioritization to infer a recommendation for an unasked fresh-clock question.
