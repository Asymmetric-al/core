## Adversarial check

### What could go wrong with this answer?

“Sixty days” may become two months, sixty calendar days across DST, a configurable
maximum, a refreshable timer or a token lifetime. Taking the earliest of every
date could close valid reading at an upload cutoff. An old expiry job could
erase valid completed access. A longer number may hide unusable delivery or
review practices. Exact arithmetic, scoped limits and truthful journeys are
necessary amendments.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

We have not measured typical collection or staff-review duration. A sixty-day
nominal interval may be heavily clipped by genuine restrictions or consumed
before first entry. It is not guaranteed opportunity or proof of accessibility.
The native source, continuing permission dependencies and provider paths still
require qualification. No extension is assumed.

### How does this affect the whole product?

One ordinary source policy must resolve consistently across staff, donor,
missionary and request-only surfaces. It changes no specialized document,
payment, care, CMS, credential or retention owner. Workflow's fourteen-day
wording must be mapped explicitly at authorized integration; one number cannot
silently overwrite different lifetimes.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

The sender need not choose a date. Recipients see the actual end and next actor,
not “sixty days remaining” after a late visit. Early completion can move the
read-only end earlier than the old unfinished end; that conditional rule must
be understandable. Received work stays received, and staff review is not
recipient lateness.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

It uses precise finite authority, current checks, durable original facts and
separate delivery/proof/retention states. No standard says every ordinary
request should last sixty days. Modern practice supports making and testing
the policy deliberately, not treating a matching vendor number as validation.

### Does this fit Asym’s existing repo and product direction?

Yes, if the selected count stays inside Q11's source command and Q10's scoped
lifecycle. Preserve the sole PDP, shared custody and Base UI/base-maia, without
fake membership, a new policy builder, a general drive or duplicated app clocks.
The already-ratified native participant successor still needs owner integration.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

Retain sixty days as the selected ordinary profile, with exact elapsed
semantics and qualification gates. Thirty remains the strongest tighter
alternative; it limits unfinished opportunity sooner but leaves less room for
the same fixed collection/review journey. Ninety provides more time at a longer
possible exposure. None is empirically optimal here. No-build alternatives
mean reusing qualified source infrastructure, not leaving ordinary access
unbounded or claiming an unrelated existing timer already implements it.

## Corrected decision to record

For newly admitted ordinary Private Document Requests using this qualified
profile, the source SHALL calculate the nominal unfinished-access end once as
the original qualified source access-issuance occurrence plus **1,440 elapsed
hours**: 60 times 24 hours, or **5,184,000 seconds** in the qualified source
instant model. It SHALL preserve the admitted original result and calculation
provenance under Q11. This is not two calendar months, sixty local/business
days, a local end-of-day rule, a requested-by buffer or time from first entry.

The original whole-unfinished-stage end SHALL be no later than that nominal
end and genuinely applicable earlier limits on that stage. “Up to” does not
grant staff or tenants arbitrary duration choices. Every limit SHALL retain its
actual action, representation, item, stage, purpose and overall scope. An
upload cutoff, per-file restriction, session/proof expiry or requested target
SHALL NOT be promoted to a whole-request end merely because it is earlier.
Actual current permission and genuine shortening remain enforceable; clearing
a true shortening cause does not restore a later end.

The selected policy SHALL apply prospectively only to the exact ordinary
source cohort. It SHALL NOT rewrite existing admissions, specialized owners,
credential lifetimes or retention. If Workflow's suggested fourteen-day expiry
maps to an entry credential, preserve and qualify that shorter mechanism and
safe re-entry. If an exact binding instead treats it as this ordinary source's
expiry, explicitly reconcile/amend that owner clause during authorized
publication. No silent relabeling, old-grant extension or imported renewal.

Current source state/time SHALL govern each operation at the exclusive boundary.
No activity, workflow pause, upload start, correction, delivery/verification
outage, resend, retry, reauth or source review pauses or replenishes the period.
Resolve existing semantic issuance before any new calculation. Preserve Q11's
qualified occurrence versus physical-commit distinction, relevant after-wait
checks, durable same-effect recovery and fail-closed uncertainty.

Normal eligible fulfillment SHALL immediately use Q10/Q07's separate read-only
policy, from the original qualified fulfillment, with at most 720 elapsed hours
and genuine applicable caps. The completed end can be earlier or later than
the old unfinished end. Do not retain writes, use the later of the two ends,
or impose the old unfinished-only deadline on valid completed access. First
receipt is not fulfillment; actual completion after unfinished authority ended
does not revive recipient rights.

The interface SHALL show the actual applicable date/time/timezone and current
allowed actions, with Requested by separately labeled and an understandable
conditional completed-stage explanation. No full-sixty-day promise, hidden
date rounding, forced download, extra confirmation or countdown-only status.
Late delivery and staff review must not become recipient blame. A later
requested target is a timing mismatch to surface, not a universal target-only
rejection or implicit extension.

The implementation SHALL preserve exact source-time precision and bounded
arithmetic, reject malformed/nonfinite/overflowed values and incompatible
authority, and never enforce expiry through a single long process/browser timer.
Stored source ends and current authorization remain authoritative; durable
wakes/cleanup are recoverable projections. Use existing source, worker and
permission infrastructure rather than adding a timing platform.

Before activation, qualify the actual remaining opportunity for intended
recipient/reviewer/help journeys, exact role/RLS/service/storage enforcement,
concurrency, representation delivery, DST/precision, mixed versions, restore
and accessibility. The count is a founder policy choice, not proof of fit.
New editing/extension/recovery powers, reminders, Q08 fresh timing and disposal
rules remain separate decisions.

For explanation only, let `I` be original qualified issuance, `N = I + 1,440h`,
and `U0` the original resolved whole-unfinished-stage end. These names do not
prescribe columns. A qualified live admission has finite valid values and
`I < U0 <= N` at its authoritative decision point. Genuine current restrictions
can produce an earlier applicable `U` or deny particular actions independently.

Do not compute `U` as the minimum of every date on a record. A hard upload end
`C` may deny new intake at `C` while otherwise-authorized reading and source
review continue before `U`. Per-file safety may deny one representation without
ending the request. True overall limits apply to both stages only where that
is their actual meaning. A requested target contributes no permission by itself.

An eligible normal fulfillment occurrence `F` must satisfy Q10's actual
historical predicates and `F < U`, in the qualified source order. Its completed
end `E` satisfies `E <= F + 720h`, subject to genuine applicable caps.
For the original ordinary lifecycle, this implies:

`E <= F + 720h < I + 2,160h` — strictly before 90 elapsed days from issuance.

This is an explanatory consequence, not a new stored cross-stage cap, guaranteed
access, storage lifetime or limit on separately authorized Q08 fresh access.
For example, eligible completion at day 10 can end completed reading by day 40,
so the earlier displayed unfinished day-60 date is not a minimum entitlement.
Never “fix” that intentional stage rule with `max(U, F + 720h)`.

One original admission has one original policy result even across new transport
keys and multiple permitted credentials. Immutable means preserved while
retained under owned records policy; it does not require keeping personal data
forever. Unknown history cannot be replaced with current defaults. Trusted
source authority, subject, tenant and attribution are derived/validated server
side, not accepted as caller-controlled assertions.

### Arithmetic and enforcement constraints

PostgreSQL's calendar-day/month components can differ from elapsed-hour
arithmetic across DST; timestamp values and JavaScript representations can also
have different precision. These are concrete implementation hazards, not a
reason to introduce a new calendar system.
[PostgreSQL time arithmetic](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/functions-datetime.html),
[date/time types](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/datatype-datetime.html).

Use the admitted source instant and selected elapsed quantity. Do not replace
the calculation with local `setDate`, `addMonths`, a midnight conversion,
date-only parsing or rounded display text. Qualify all conversions and stored/
compared precision; reject invalid/nonfinite/overflowed states instead of
guessing a timestamp. No custom leap-second/astronomical clock is selected:
the source clock/instant semantics must be consistent and qualified.

Sixty days is **5,184,000,000 milliseconds**, larger than Node's documented
maximum single timer delay of **2,147,483,647 milliseconds**. Node documents
that excessive delays are reduced to 1 millisecond. A long `setTimeout` can
therefore expire immediately rather than wait sixty days. Keep durable source
deadlines authoritative; a timer or scheduler can only wake bounded,
idempotent work that rechecks current stage/authority.
[Node.js 24.21.0 timers](https://nodejs.org/download/release/v24.21.0/docs/api/timers.html).

Source expiry still holds if a wake is late, a process restarts, a tab sleeps
or every cleanup worker stops. Conversely, an early/stale wake cannot revoke
valid access, undo a completed transition or delete evidence. Authoritative
current-time checks after waits and Q11's occurrence/commit/recovery contract
remain; clock or precision arithmetic alone does not prove eligibility.

## Recipient, staff and recovery journeys

### 1. Prepare the ordinary request

Authorized staff use the existing source workflow. Show the intended recipient,
purpose and timing rule; do not add a duration dropdown, arbitrary date editor
or consent-like timer checkbox. The source verifies exact issuing capability
and current constraints rather than treating a broad staff role as enough.

If a known restriction clips the interval, show the applicable consequence to
the appropriately authorized staff. A requested target beyond the end needs
clear treatment before the request is represented as workable; its mere order
does not create a new universal rejection rule. Do not manufacture a due date
for undated work. Specialized source terms are not overwritten by this profile.

### 2. Issue and understand the result

Q11's original source effect determines `I` and the admitted end. If preflight
uses an estimate, identify it as such and read back the actual result after
issuance. Normal time advancing under unchanged approved terms does not demand
another confirmation. Material source/recipient/authority conflicts follow the
owning command's safe conflict process.

Distinguish **Request issued** from invitation pending/failed/unknown. After an
uncertain response, reconcile the same effect; do not tell staff to create a
duplicate request. Repairing delivery cannot restart time. A known unsupported
entry route remains an activation failure, even if sixty days seems generous.

### 3. Enter after immediate or delayed discovery

Use the existing Q01/ADR-0025 confirmed-recipient doorway and qualified proof.
An unqualified link reveals no private request title, recipient, date or
existence. No new account enrollment or timer-start ceremony is introduced.

A late entrant sees the actual remaining opportunity through the stored date,
not “you have sixty days.” Expired proof can be replaced only through its
qualified route within still-valid source authority. Verification outage or
unknown source state blocks affected actions without a false claim of source
expiry. A source that has already validly completed opens the appropriate
current read-only view.

### 4. Submit and make an eligible correction

Keep Requested by separate from the actual access end. The source still
enforces exact pending-item correction fences, hard intake limits, accepted/
frozen work and representation safety. A future overall date does not promise
that every upload/correction action remains permitted.

Transfer progress is not receipt; unknown finalization must be reconciled.
Preserve a confirmed receipt and the user's safe context across re-entry or
network interruption. Do not promise that a local file selection survives
reload, or claim an unsent file was saved. A start before expiry is not a
reservation. Corrections do not award another sixty days.

### 5. Wait for checks and source review

Show **Received — awaiting review** or the actual checking/next-actor state.
Receipt is not business acceptance and does not start the completed interval.
Staff review consumes the same finite unfinished opportunity.

Do not label a recipient overdue solely because staff review remains, require
reupload to keep access alive, or pressure staff to certify deficient evidence
before a timer ends. The authorized reviewer sees the real access consequence.
Actual source cohorts must demonstrate workable recipient and reviewer journeys;
the number alone is not that proof. This adds no automatic reminder queue.

### 6. Complete early or near the unfinished end

Eligible normal fulfillment changes both mode and applicable end immediately.
Ordinary writes stop; current permitted preview/original/history remain under
the completed rules. The completed date can be sooner than the former
unfinished date, or later when fulfillment occurs near its end.

The prospective explanation must prevent a promise of “at least sixty days”:

> Access while this request is unfinished ends [date, time and zone].
>
> If completed while your access is valid, this request may remain available
> to read for up to 30 days from completion. We’ll show the exact date.

This is conditional, not guaranteed admission. At completion, replace the
obsolete unfinished message with **Request complete** and the actual current
read end. Do not call the change an “extension” when it shortens the potential
remaining opportunity. No forced preview/download or extra consent step.

### 7. Reach expiry, interruption or an adverse restriction

At actual expiry, remove only actions the source no longer permits; retain
business facts and independent source work. Show the safe ended-access state
only when current authority establishes it. Where the old unfinished date has
passed but a timely completed transition may exist, resolve that state rather
than treating the old timer as the final answer.

Current proof/observer uncertainty is not permission to serve, nor proof that
files were deleted, the request rejected or original issuance failed. An
isolated preview/history failure should not hide other independently authorized
actions. Provide the actual owned help route without promising extension,
fresh completed access or an attachment-by-email workaround.

### 8. Support, changed circumstances and legitimate new work

Support sees only evidence within its authority: original issue, admitted
terms, current restrictions, delivery/proof status and relevant source outcomes.
It cannot repair expiry by editing SQL, resetting a token, changing a due date,
cloning a request or impersonating the recipient.

Recipient/contact/host changes, issuer dependencies, new work or reopening use
their exact domain procedure. A current role/name/email match is insufficient.
If the real task needs a different profile or active-expiry recovery, that is
an explicit source/product decision. Repeated workarounds are evidence that
the ordinary cohort is not qualified, not a normal operating model.

Retained evidence, official documents and shared media uses remain owned;
sixty days introduces neither personal-drive entitlement nor automatic deletion.
Already downloaded copies cannot be recalled. Q08 remains a separately
qualified fresh read-only capability with its own unresolved timing.

### Accessibility and temporal presentation

Preserve shared Base UI/base-maia, semantic Zinc tokens and the existing surface
boundaries. Essential dates and status belong in main content, not a hover,
color, toast or countdown. Meaningful asynchronous changes need suitable
announcement without focus theft or per-second screen-reader interruptions.

Display one truthful instant with explicit timezone and supported precision.
Relative labels may supplement, not replace it. Avoid ambiguous numeric dates,
fake end-of-day promises, silent rounding later and reliance on the device
clock for authority. Local wall-clock hour may differ across a DST change while
the elapsed duration remains exact.

Qualify keyboard, speech, screen readers, paste/autofill, touch, zoom/reflow,
RTL, long translations, mobile file providers and low-bandwidth interruption.
Names/addresses must not become new mandatory Western-style identity forms.
WCAG 2.2.1 is Level A; its longer-than-20-hours exception does not prove short
proof/session/upload timers conform. Current restrictions can shorten the
actual opportunity. Accessible authentication and status messages have their
own requirements; no static review proves browser/AT success.
[Timing](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/timing-adjustable.html),
[authentication](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/accessible-authentication-minimum.html),
[status messages](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html).

## Category-by-category adversarial review

### 1. Problem validity, necessity, and alternatives

**Material concern: yes. Severity: medium. Likelihood: plausible.**
The selected number can be mistaken for proof that ordinary work fits. No
measured Asym cohort establishes that; thirty is a credible shorter-exposure
alternative and ninety a longer opportunity. The real problem is a complete
finite initial policy, not a need for configurable clocks. **Effect:** retain
A as explicit product judgment, narrow claims of sufficiency. **Permanent
fix:** bind ordinary scope and require actual recipient/reviewer qualification.
C01–C03, C22; P01, P29–P30.

### 2. Brittleness

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: likely if implicit.**
Calendar helpers, local midnight, month arithmetic or a sixty-day process timer
can work in a simple example and fail at DST, restart or delay overflow.
Official PostgreSQL and Node semantics make the risk concrete.
**Effect:** refine the unit/enforcement contract, not the selected count.
**Permanent fix:** exact elapsed arithmetic from the original instant, preserved
precision and durable current-use enforcement. C01, C04–C05, C14; P02–P05,
P08, P24–P25.

### 3. Technical debt

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: plausible.**
Scattering `60` through task UI, SQL, tokens and workers creates competing
meaning and risky upgrades. Current task/date helpers have different purposes.
**Effect:** constrain implementation to source-owned policy/result.
**Permanent fix:** one qualified ordinary profile and stored admission
provenance using existing owner interfaces; generated views remain projections.
C04, C10–C11, C19, C21; P06, P22, P28–P30.

### 4. Edge cases

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: plausible.**
Month ends, leap years, DST, offset changes, precision loss, early completion,
late corrections and a target after access end can yield inconsistent outcomes.
Q09–Q11 distinguish these states, while current date helpers do not implement
their permission semantics. **Effect:** narrows shorthand “sixty days.”
**Permanent fix:** explicit unit, exclusive boundary, scoped limits and the
lifecycle/journey cases. C01–C02, C05, C07–C09, C15; P02–P05,
P10–P16, P26–P27.

### 5. Footguns

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: likely without guardrails.**
“Up to” can become a sender-editable maximum; refreshing credentials or editing
a requested date can become a renewal button. Taking every earlier date as a
cap can also incorrectly remove valid access. **Effect:** limits the meaning
of A and rejects those incidental powers. **Permanent fix:** a fixed ordinary
profile, explicit scoped restrictions and immutable outputs.
C02–C04, C08–C11, C18; P06–P07, P10, P17, P22–P23.

### 6. Tenant safety

**Material concern: yes. Severity: critical. Likelihood: plausible.**
A globally mutable profile/cache key or mismatched source cap could apply
another tenant's context or leak a private deadline. A future timestamp does
not validate recipient or tenant. **Effect:** A requires structural exact
scope, without a new permission model. **Permanent fix:** same-scope
relationships, trusted contextual admission, current replay/disclosure and
privacy-safe entry. C04, C06, C12–C13, C20; P07, P18–P21, P30.

### 7. Database, RLS, and authorization safety

**Material concern: yes. Severity: critical. Likelihood: plausible.**
An allowed update could swap recipient, tenant, anchor, profile or end; nullable
checks could admit missing times; service/RPC paths could evade intended guards.
Q11's original immutability and P12's actual capability rules must be proved,
not assumed from RLS presence. **Effect:** adds exact release proof.
**Permanent fix:** C05–C06, C12 and P18–P20 require same-tenant foreign
relationships, state-dependent non-null/finite rules, semantic uniqueness,
trusted attribution, protected immutable fields, indexes and deliberate delete
behavior, plus the source mutation boundary.

Inspect actual grants/columns, both `USING` and effective `WITH CHECK`, composed
policies, views, RPC execution/owners/search paths, service and storage paths.
Omitted `WITH CHECK` may inherit `USING`; do not report absence alone as a
bypass. Test allowed-old/forbidden-new rows and trigger-transformed results.
Time-varying CHECKs do not continuously enforce expiry. RLS remains P12's
coarse tenant defense, not duplicated capability logic. No money schema or
precision changes are introduced.
[PostgreSQL policies](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/sql-createpolicy.html),
[constraints](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/ddl-constraints.html).

### 8. Overengineering

**Material concern: yes. Severity: medium. Likelihood: plausible.**
A constant can prompt a policy builder, business calendar, settings screen or
new scheduler. None is required by the chosen ordinary profile. **Effect:**
retain A while narrowing architecture and UI. **Permanent fix:** existing
source/worker/PDP boundaries, one elapsed rule and actual-date display; no
generic duration customization or new vendor. C03, C14–C15, C19; P22,
P24, P28, P30.

### 9. UX/UI and user friction

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: likely without explicit states.**
“Sixty days” can falsely promise sixty remaining days, confuse due/expiry or
hide that early completion produces an earlier read-only end. Staff review
can become unfair recipient-overdue status. Comparator and accessibility
guidance support distinguishing actual work states. **Effect:** requires
conditional copy and verified journeys. **Permanent fix:** C07, C09,
C15–C16, C22 and P11–P16, P26–P27, P29–P30; no extra timer ceremony.

### 10. Source of truth, ownership, and domain invariants

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: likely if limits are flattened.**
`min(all dates)` promotes an intake or proof limit into whole-source authority;
`max(old end, completed end)` defeats Q07. Current task targets, timestamps and
source facts have different owners. **Effect:** exact action/stage applicability
changes the bare shorthand. **Permanent fix:** one original profile/result,
scoped current restrictions and Q10 transition. C02, C04, C07, C10–C13,
C20; P06, P10–P15, P20, P22.

### 11. Hidden coupling

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: plausible.**
Credential TTLs, template dates, display timezone or Workflow's suggested
fourteen-day setting could unexpectedly drive the sixty-day source right.
**Effect:** requires explicit owner mapping; not every apparent fourteen/sixty
conflict means one must be overwritten. **Permanent fix:** distinguish scopes
and amend only the exact conflicting ordinary-source binding during publication.
C04, C09–C11, C21; P06, P16–P17, P22, P30.

### 12. Failure modes

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: likely over time.**
Lost replies, delayed wakes, offline clients or proof outages can cause new
deadline calculation, false expiry or duplicate uploads. Q11 already requires
same-effect recovery; sixty days does not remove transport ambiguity.
**Effect:** preserve A with exact fault handling. **Permanent fix:** immutable
source outcome/current disclosure, independent statuses, durable reconciliation
and no timeout-as-proof. C04, C06, C08, C14, C16, C20; P07–P09,
P16, P24–P25, P27.

### 13. Lifecycle, temporal correctness, concurrency, and idempotency

**Material concern: yes. Severity: critical. Likelihood: plausible.**
At the boundary, issuance, correction, restriction and fulfillment may race.
Old transaction time or an old expiry job can create a locally valid but globally
wrong result. **Effect:** A inherits and concretizes Q10/Q11 source ordering.
**Permanent fix:** precise exclusive comparisons, relevant current checks after
waits, preserved original semantic result and stage-aware recovery. C04–C08,
C13–C14, C21; P04–P09, P13–P15, P24–P25, P28.

### 14. Data integrity risks

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: plausible.**
Recomputing end from current defaults, truncating precision or restoring old
profile versions rewrites historical access. A conversion from a date-only task
value can introduce a different day. **Effect:** requires immutable admitted
result and explicit precision/provenance. **Permanent fix:** C04–C06,
C12, C20–C21; P02–P08, P19–P20, P28.
No new history system is necessary; the exact source fact must remain recoverable.

### 15. Security and privacy risks

**Material concern: yes. Severity: critical. Likelihood: plausible.**
A longer allowed source interval could be misused as a long bearer token,
cache permission or storage retention rule, increasing exposure or leaking
sensitive ministry data. No measured incident-rate increase is claimed.
**Effect:** retain the count only under current-use and minimization controls.
**Permanent fix:** shorter independently qualified technical capabilities,
exact every-path authorization, separate custody and safe audit/support.
C11–C13, C17–C20; P17–P23, P26.

### 16. Scalability and performance risks

**Material concern: yes. Severity: medium to high. Likelihood: plausible at load.**
More concurrently unfinished requests can increase stored metadata, retries and
review inventory, but sixty versus thirty does not prove doubled production
cost. Long-lived resident timers per request, authorization dependent on sweeps,
or full-history reads can fail at tenant skew. **Effect:** requires measured
bounds, not speculative infrastructure.
**Permanent fix:** existing durable source/worker seams, indexed bounded reads
and actual cohort load budgets. C14, C19, C22; P24–P25, P28–P29.
Preserve P12 R6's at-most-one governance-row SELECT plus one ledger UPDATE
logical governed-read budget and its documented audit/physical carve-outs;
that is not a total business-query or issuance-write limit.

### 17. Operational burden

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: plausible.**
If earlier caps or slow review make the actual interval unusable, staff may
clone requests or edit dates repeatedly. A nominal sixty-day label can hide the
problem. **Effect:** restrict activation to usable cohorts; it does not authorize
extensions. **Permanent fix:** real end/readiness visibility, owned help and
cohort qualification; recurring bypasses trigger an explicit source fix.
C09, C15–C16, C18, C22; P16, P23, P26–P30.

### 18. Observability and auditability gaps

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: likely if only a duration is stored.**
Without original profile/anchor/result and scope evidence, staff cannot explain
why sixty nominal days became less or why completed access changed the end.
Provider logs cannot reconstruct that source history. **Effect:** add minimal
durable explainability. **Permanent fix:** original terms and genuine
restrictions/source outcomes in authorized history, minimized diagnostics.
C04, C10, C17, C20; P06–P07, P20, P22–P23, P26.

### 19. Dependency and integration risks

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: plausible.**
Runtime timer limits, database/calendar semantics, proof lifetime, provider
URL limits and finite dedupe windows can disagree with the source interval.
No vendor guarantees the whole native journey. **Effect:** qualify interfaces
without changing providers or adopting their defaults. **Permanent fix:**
source-owned time/result, current serving, distinct capabilities and durable
same-effect recovery. C01, C04, C10–C14, C19–C20; P02–P08,
P17, P22, P24–P25.

### 20. Migration, rollout, and upgrade risks

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: plausible.**
Old workers may apply fourteen/thirty/calendar-month expiry to new records;
new code may recalculate older valid ends as sixty days. Restore may erase a
later restriction. **Effect:** limits adoption prospectively with exact
compatibility. **Permanent fix:** explicit policy binding, preserve old valid
terms, no guessed backfill, compatible workers and restore/rollback gates.
C04, C10, C21–C22; P06, P22, P28–P30.

### 21. Testability, traceability, and proof

**Material concern: yes. Severity: high. Likelihood: likely without exact evidence.**
A passing formatter or arithmetic check could be misreported as safe expiry.
“Up to sixty days” remains ambiguous without units, scopes and boundary cases.
**Effect:** exact falsifiable clauses/proofs, not implementation claims.
**Permanent fix:** C01–C22 mapped to P01–P30 and source-to-release trace;
the sixteen existing glossary terms remain sufficient. C22 and P30 require
explicit authorization/publication/qualification status.

### 22. Other development hazards

**Material concern: no additional independent concern found.**
Checked new money effects, legal retention prescriptions, general-drive growth,
new dependency needs, extra surfaces, notification consent and bespoke
calendar/crypto infrastructure. Q12 requires none. Their relevant risks are
already captured by C03, C10–C11, C17–C22 and governing owners. This scoped
negative result is not a full-repository security approval or claim that all
future implementation issues are known.
