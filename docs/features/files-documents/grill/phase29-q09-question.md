# Phase 29 Q09 — what an ordinary submission due date means

**Status:** Historical researched question prepared 2026-10-20. Conrad
selected and ratified A with the complete required amendment package on
2026-10-20: allow otherwise-authorized late submissions and pending corrections.
Q01–Q09 are ratified grooming intent. No active-access duration, new date-edit
capability, grace, notification or fresh-access timing/repetition rule is selected.

## Historical question and alternatives

For an ordinary private document request with outstanding recipient submission
work or an otherwise-eligible pending correction, **when its requested due date
passes while intake is otherwise open and recipient authority remains valid,
should late submission and otherwise-permitted pending corrections remain
available, or should that date stop those actions?**

Recommend **A — Treat the ordinary due date as a target; allow otherwise
authorized late submissions and pending corrections, with truthful late status.**

This concerns the ordinary native document-request profile. A stricter
source-owned intake deadline, security restriction, finite access end or
completed/closed request still controls. It does not change a competitive
application, statutory, financial, program or other specialized owner's
deadline by importing an ordinary-file default.

## Why this is the next question

Q07 settled the ordinary completed-read maximum and Q08 permits a deliberately
authorized fresh read admission. Both depend on a coherent original request.
Before choosing more fresh-access timing settings, settle what the earlier
recipient due date actually does.

The existing decisions separate due dates, source access, completion, proof/
session and custody. They do **not** choose whether the ordinary source allows
late intake. Workflow's runtime contract explicitly leaves late acceptance to
the source policy. The generic request workflow remains unqualified, so a
suggested reminder period is not a native intake or access default.

This question chooses the effect of the due date. It does not choose a number
of days, create an expiry policy or decide whether every request must have a
due date.

## Two practical options

| Option                                                                        | What happens after the date                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Benefit and cost                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Due date is a target; eligible late intake remains open (recommended)** | Outstanding submission work can show as overdue. If source intake and recipient authority remain valid, the recipient can submit or make Q03-permitted pending corrections. Record the actual admission and applicable late status.                                                 | Avoids an extra staff extension merely to receive otherwise-permitted material. Gives staff clear timing evidence. Requires explicit closure/access controls and truthful language so the target is not mistaken for a hard cutoff.      |
| **B — Due date is also the intake cutoff**                                    | Stop new ordinary submission/correction admission at the source-defined cutoff. Existing independently authorized status/history/file reads and staff review continue under their own rules. Further intake needs an explicit source-authorized change where that owner permits it. | Provides a firm collection boundary and predictable review set. A legitimate late or corrective submission may require staff intervention or may be unavailable; starting an upload before the cutoff does not itself establish receipt. |

Both options preserve the actual receipt, source decision and access history.
Neither automatically fulfills, rejects, cancels or deletes a request. Neither
extends permission, sends reminders or grants another access period.

**Receiving a late file is not approving its evidence.** Safety checks, review
and the requesting source's business acceptance remain separate.

## Recommendation and strongest alternative

Choose A for the ordinary request. A requested-by target should help people
coordinate the work without automatically blocking a useful, otherwise
authorized submission. The source can still enforce any real hard intake
boundary; finite recipient authority and completion remain effective stops.
This fits purposeful document collection without making Asym a general drive.

That is product judgment, not a measured claim that ministries routinely submit
late or that leniency improves outcomes. We have not measured deadline
comprehension, submission patterns, staff intervention or review costs.

B is the strongest alternative when the ordinary collection process needs the
requested date to freeze incoming work. Its cost is deliberate: people with
still-valid access may read their record but cannot submit another file.
Do not call that source expiry or imply their previous submission disappeared.

Do not create a third ordinary choice that uses the due date to end every
read permission. Q01–Q08 already require distinct current read authority.
Likewise, a hidden late-submission grace period, a special late-approval queue
or a new scheduling engine is unnecessary to decide this one behavior.

## Current official evidence

- [Dropbox file-request recipient guidance](https://help.dropbox.com/share/received-file-request),
  updated 9 December 2024, documents that requesters can allow late uploads,
  which are received and marked late. The deadline is expressed in the
  requester's timezone. This demonstrates that a due date and an intake
  cutoff can differ; its uploader identity and correction/version behavior
  are not Asym's policies.
- [Dropbox file-request guidance](https://help.dropbox.com/share/create-file-request)
  describes deadline options and explicit request closure. This supports
  separately understandable collection states, not an unlimited Core request
  or a chosen grace-period length.
- [Submittable Next's soft/hard date guide](https://next.support.submittable.com/hc/en-us/articles/41923721981719-Set-a-Public-and-Private-Deadline-for-a-Cycle-Soft-and-Hard-Close-Dates)
  distinguishes a public soft date from a private hard date. It also permits
  in-progress drafts to submit after the hard date with Late/Approval Needed
  treatment. These details show why labels alone do not establish admission
  rules. **Do not import hidden grace, an approval queue or its application
  policy into the ordinary Asym document request.**
- [Foundant's follow-up logic](https://support.foundant.com/hc/en-us/articles/4411213334935-Follow-Up-Submission-Logic)
  separates submission, administrator completion and access to subsequent
  work. Due dates can order that work; this is not evidence that every overdue
  form is accepted or that reviewing a received form makes its recipient late.

Current products differ. Their documentation supports explicit, truthful
meaning for each date rather than one universal deadline policy.
The [comparative research](phase29-q09-comparative-research.md) records
additional distinctions and source limitations.

## A clear recipient and staff journey

Under the researched A option, use language such as **Requested by [date/time/timezone]**.
When outstanding submission work is late but still permitted, make the next
action clear: **The requested date has passed. You can still submit while this
request is open and your access remains valid.** Keep the actual applicable
access/cutoff state visible where relevant; a target does not promise that
permission will last until it or forever afterward.

Avoid shaming, an urgent countdown, a compulsory late explanation or an extra
confirmation simply for being late. Record the actual received act and its
timing; do not label it accepted or approved. Staff can distinguish overdue
outstanding work from received material awaiting review.

Under the researched B option, use **Submit by [date/time/timezone]** and state that intake
closes then. Afterward show the source-permitted **Submissions closed** state
and real help route, preserving independently allowed status/history/read
actions. Do not use a generic Expired screen for still-valid read access.
Source-authorized reopening/extension is not automatically promised.

Use the same shared Base UI/base-maia request controls and existing accessible
date/status patterns. The important date and current next action belong in
main content, not only a tooltip, toast, color or collapsed history. Preserve
keyboard/speech/AT/touch, localization/RTL and small-screen clarity.
Source time governs; the browser's clock is presentation, not authority.

## Do not confuse recipient timeliness with staff completion

These cases are hypothetical boundary examples, not claims about a particular
ministry workflow:

| Situation                                                                                          | Meaning that must be preserved                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| All required recipient material was durably received on time; staff review continues past the date | Show the appropriate submitted/awaiting-review state. Do not label the recipient overdue solely because the source has not fulfilled the request.                     |
| Some required recipient work is still outstanding after the target                                 | A may allow otherwise-authorized late receipt; B stops ordinary intake. Existing received acts remain true.                                                           |
| A pending correction is offered after the target                                                   | A permits it only if Q03 and all current source rules still allow correction. Its own timing does not rewrite the original on-time receipt or acceptance history.     |
| The source later assigns a new clarification, correction or additional item                        | Use the source-declared applicable due terms for that work. Do not retroactively mark it late against an unrelated original date or invent a new correction interval. |
| Source already fulfilled the request                                                               | Q06's completed business fence stops ordinary submissions/corrections under either option. The date cannot reopen it.                                                 |
| Recipient authority expired or a stricter source cutoff applies                                    | Both deny the affected action. A is no extension, grace period or new grant.                                                                                          |
| Staff grants Q08 fresh completed-read access                                                       | It remains read-only. It does not reopen intake or authorize a late correction.                                                                                       |
| Upload started before the relevant cutoff but has not reached durable source admission             | Transfer progress is not a receipt. Source admission and the eventual chosen boundary rules govern; no undocumented upload-start grace is assumed.                    |

The source owns what recipient work remains outstanding and which item/member
an applicable date governs. A count of files, a generic Task Done flag or the
absence of business acceptance cannot safely substitute. Required work,
submission receipt, safety readiness, correction and fulfillment remain
distinct under Q01–Q08.

## Scope and sequencing

Q09 chooses **target versus intake cutoff for the ordinary requested due date**.
It does not select mandatory dates, active-request access duration, date-change
authority, a grace interval, reminder cadence, first-submission acceptance,
fresh-read duration/anchor, repeat allowance or a late-review queue.

After an answer, the adversarial review must reconcile exact admission-time
semantics, cutoff precision/timezone, current outstanding-work projection,
date changes, correction/completion races and preserved receipt history.
Those checks must not invent more founder choices through implementation
defaults. The source's complete finite active-access policy remains required
before activation under either answer.

At question preparation, no ADR-0009 or glossary term had been created for
the unanswered question. No tests, product/schema/provider/permission/dependency
changes or formal publication occurred for that preparation. `/to-prd` and `/to-issues` remain the explicit
publication boundaries. Fresh-access timing/repetition remains open after
Q08; this foundational question does not settle it indirectly.
