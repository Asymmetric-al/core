## Recommendation and the genuine choice

Ask **whether ordinary staff view authors may directly make their own eligible saved view available to an explicitly permitted staff audience, or whether only designated view managers may publish shared views**. Recommend the ordinary-author route, within actual source/P12 publish authority and current definition-disclosure rules.

Shared saved views are already part of the founder's outcome. Q42 explicitly retained that outcome while adopting private actor-owned views and qualified source/system views for its first increment. The unresolved choice is publication responsibility and its routine gate—not whether shared views exist, whether records may be disclosed, or whether a database visibility flag is available.

| Option                                                                                 | Concrete behavior                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Benefit and cost                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **A — Ordinary staff view authors may publish their own eligible views (recommended)** | The ordinary source's view-authoring/publish scope permits staff to deliberately make an owned eligible view available to a permitted staff audience without a separate designated-manager publication gate. The source still validates the exact definition and audience. | Useful working views can be shared where the work happens. More people can add shared definitions, so clear ownership, deliberate saving, understandable audience and maintenance are needed. This does not authorize arbitrary staff to edit everyone else's views.                                   |
| **B — Designated view managers publish shared views**                                  | Staff retain personal views. Only staff separately designated with the source's shared-view management scope can make a definition available to the staff audience. A manager prepares or publishes the appropriate shared view through that actual qualified route.       | A smaller publisher group can keep common views consistent and the shared list deliberately curated. Routine team-specific views depend on that group's availability and may involve extra coordination. No new approval-request queue or ability to disclose someone's private definition is assumed. |

Both options need current source-qualified definition and audience authority, current result authorization, maintenance/recovery and deliberate publication. Neither is permission inferred from a staff role string, record Read, review assignment or personal view ownership. A must make a meaningful difference from B: its normal ordinary-author scope includes publishing an owned eligible view within the permitted audience; it must not hide the same designated-manager gate behind the word authorized. B is not merely a temporary technical bridge or an inherently inferior choice.

Illustrative situation, not observed ministry practice: Elena saves a **2026 Visit — Equipment** view of ordinary request evidence using permitted folder/label/filter fields. Nora and other eligible colleagues want to reuse that definition. Under A, Elena can deliberately make it available to the permitted staff audience as part of ordinary work. Under B, a designated view manager provides that shared definition. When Nora opens it, she sees only her currently permitted matching entries, which may differ from Elena's. A shared name/filter definition is not a shared snapshot, complete review queue, source assignment or grant to all files Elena can see.

## Necessary question boundaries

1. **An actual ordinary source, not a universal shared-view service.** The first cohort is Q42's ordinary request evidence discovery. Source-approved fields, namespace and metadata classification continue; equal bytes, a visible file or a private query grant no publication authority.
2. **A meaningful publisher choice.** A enables ordinary source-qualified authors to publish their own eligible definitions directly. B reserves that act to designated managers. Neither is all staff by role string; both require exact current source/P12 powers. Do not hide B's gate inside A or imply that view authors can administer everyone else's work.
3. **Definition disclosure is distinct from results.** The name, filters, predicates, folder/label values, descriptions and attribution need approved disclosure to the actual audience. Each viewer's resulting entries/actions are current personal source permissions; results need not be identical and are not a fixed or complete work roster.
4. **No guessed audience.** Use only the exact source-qualified staff audience; do not assume everyone in the tenant, a Support team, a CRM relationship or an external file recipient qualifies. Team/everyone remains reserved until the actual narrowed owner path is qualified. A source capability name or new dropdown is insufficient.
5. **Publishing is deliberate; browsing is not editing.** Personal filter exploration must not silently modify a published definition or another person's view. The selected-answer review must close exact owner/maintenance/update/withdrawal and departed-author recovery without creating a collaborative editor or a routine approval platform.
6. **No policy bypass or unnecessary second approver.** Existing field/purpose/audience policy changes retain ADR0211 and P12. An ordinary write within an already-qualified source definition/audience policy is not automatically a new projection widening. The actual operation determines the rule.
7. **Preserve existing metadata semantics.** Q38 excludes general body matching; Q42 labels are nonauthorizing, one home remains one home, and missing/denied predicates cannot be discarded. A shared filter is not an acceptance status, reviewer assignment, source queue, bulk selection or entitlement.
8. **Clutter and continuity are real tradeoffs.** A needs understandable authorship and deliberate maintenance; B has coordination and manager-availability cost. Do not invent measured usage, automatic curation/expiry, a new approval queue, notification default, personal pin-order overwrite or all-team default merely to solve them.
9. **Native qualification remains.** Current source, capability, query, definition/audience and UI paths must be proved together. New registry code is prepared only; current gift-history defaults are adjacent evidence, not the source contract. No source data, provider or runtime changes were made during research.
