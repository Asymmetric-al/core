# Phase 29 Q09 — what an ordinary request's due date does

Researched 20 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. The parent reports Q01–Q08 and all amendments are ratified. The next question is not selected by this note. This is public primary-documentation research and product reasoning only: no private applicant/client account, product setting, grant, upload, message or runtime test was exercised.

## Recommended next question

Resolve the **meaning of the ordinary active request's due date** before choosing the fresh-access clock's detailed start event.

Suggested single decision:

> When an ordinary private-document request passes its requested due date and the recipient still has requested work to send, should a currently authorized recipient still be able to send eligible files and permitted corrections, with their actual late receipt recorded—or should that date automatically stop new submissions and corrections?

Recommend **A: the ordinary due date is a requested-by date, not an automatic intake or access cutoff**. Late receipt remains possible only while the source's finite active authority and ordinary intake/correction eligibility still permit it. An explicit source closure, successful fulfillment, binding intake end or security/privacy restriction still takes effect. Existing specialized hard-deadline contracts are unchanged.

This is a judgment for the first generic ordinary profile, not evidence that all modern nonprofit systems prefer soft deadlines. **B: stop ordinary intake/corrections at the declared deadline** is the strongest alternative and is coherent when that cutoff is essential to the request's purpose. Both options must keep reading, review, source completion, session expiry and retention distinct.

Do not add an artificial third option consisting of a configurable policy builder. If existing source contracts already require two modes, honor that authority; otherwise “let every sender choose” postpones rather than resolves the ordinary default and adds controls.

## Why this is higher leverage than fresh-access activation timing

The original request's due date may be visible to every recipient, influence corrections and staff review, and accidentally become the initial authority cutoff. Q07 and Q08 are downstream: they require trustworthy original admission, fulfillment and expiry. A correct fresh-read grant cannot repair a request whose ordinary due date silently closed the wrong action, invented completion, or never had a qualified finite access contract.

This question also prevents an ever-deeper sequence of exceptional post-expiry controls before the normal request is understandable. It is a narrowly scoped product decision, not an instruction to redesign the full workflow.

The competing next question remains legitimate: for Q08 fresh access, should its finite period run from issuance or from a qualified first use bounded by a separate finite unused-admission limit? Issuance gives a simple immediately known fixed end but delivery delay consumes the period. First-use activation could preserve useful reading time but requires a separate outer limit, a precisely defined non-scanner/non-prefetch activation event and more states. **No interval, anchor, activation rule, repeat count or notification is selected here.** Those are still Q08 activation dependencies.

Choosing due-date meaning first does not remove the need to settle initial active-access lifetime or fresh timing. Neither feature may activate with missing authority contracts.

## Two practical ordinary-profile choices

Illustrative case: a source asks Alex for documents by Friday. Friday passes while one required item is missing. The request has not been completed, cancelled or otherwise closed, and Alex's finite source authority has not ended.

| Choice                                                                 | What happens after the requested date                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Benefit                                                                                                                                                                                             | Cost / strongest objection                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Due means requested by; late intake can continue (recommended)** | Show the missed requested date and the truthful current state. Eligible new files and Q03-permitted corrections can still be admitted while the source's independent active authority/intake rules remain open. Record their actual receipt timing. | A person can finish still-needed work without contacting staff solely to bypass a planning date. Keeps the simple ordinary collection task usable while preserving source review and late evidence. | The word due must not falsely imply a hard cutoff. Staff cannot treat the requested date alone as a stable final evidence set. An independent finite active-purpose/intake boundary remains necessary; this is not “open forever until someone remembers.” |
| **B — Due also closes ordinary submissions/corrections**               | At the declared cutoff, deny new intake and correction admission. Preserve independently authorized reading/history and staff review; the source may still act on previously admitted evidence.                                                     | A clear fixed intake boundary, useful when review or selection requires a defined set received by a time.                                                                                           | A legitimate late or corrected file needs its separately governed source path. Slow transfers and exact boundary conditions become more consequential. The cutoff does not itself mean source success, rejection, deletion or ended read access.           |

The recommendation for A is limited to the first generic ordinary request profile. It does not weaken a source's already-binding deadline, legal/program restriction, security decision or earlier permission end. B is appropriate where a hard cutoff is part of the actual purpose; that case is not merely bad UX.

No measured evidence establishes how often Asym recipients submit late, how much staff effort A saves, or whether mobile/field connectivity makes one choice superior. Those are plausible hypotheses to validate, not fabricated ministry workflows.

## Keep the lifecycle facts separate

| Fact                              | What it means                                                                                                        | What it must not stand in for                                                                 |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Requested due date                | When the source asks the recipient to supply work; Q09 would choose whether the ordinary default also closes intake. | Recipient identity, access expiry, source completion or approval of files.                    |
| Intake/correction eligibility     | Whether a particular new submission/correction may currently be admitted.                                            | Existing-file read permission, proof freshness or retention.                                  |
| Source access end                 | When a particular recipient/action/purpose authority stops.                                                          | Merely being overdue, deleting records, or ending staff's independent authority.              |
| Receipt/admission fact            | The source durably admitted exact immutable submitted bytes under current authority.                                 | Starting upload, local progress, scan completion, preview generation or business approval.    |
| Hygiene/preview state             | Whether the submitted original/representation can be used or shown under its own rules.                              | A new receipt time, timeliness decision, fulfillment or renewed authority.                    |
| Review/acceptance                 | The source's explicit decision about the actual evidence and request requirements.                                   | Seeing a file, opening a reviewer screen, a technical job completion or passing the due date. |
| Normal fulfillment                | The source's qualified successful outcome under Q06/Q07.                                                             | Intake closure, a generic task flag, failure to receive everything or a timer.                |
| Cancellation/withdrawal/rejection | Distinct source-owned outcomes, with their own allowed effects.                                                      | Ordinary success or automatic late-state behavior.                                            |
| Session/entry expiry              | Need for qualified proof/re-entry while any underlying authority may remain.                                         | A missed due date or ended source permission.                                                 |
| Retention/holds                   | What the source must keep or dispose of.                                                                             | Permission to upload, read, reopen or grant access.                                           |

The new ordinary question should not revise already-governed specialist states or introduce a universal lifecycle engine. The point is to prevent one generic status called **Expired** from accidentally deciding all these facts.

## Interaction and copy implications

For A, make the continuing action explicit when it is actually allowed: **“The requested date has passed. You can still send the requested files.”** Keep the actual requested date visible. A new accepted receipt after that date should be distinguishable from source approval; wording such as **Received after the requested date** is clearer than an unexplained **Accepted late** badge.

**Do not mark the recipient overdue merely because the source has not fulfilled the request.** If all then-required files were durably admitted on time and only scanning, preview work or staff review remains, show the actual processing/review state. Staff delay is not recipient lateness. Determine whether there is currently outstanding recipient work from the source's exact requirements and admitted evidence, rather than a generic incomplete flag.

| Example                                                                  | Truthful recipient state                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Required files admitted before due; staff review still pending           | Received / awaiting review, with the real receipt facts. No recipient-overdue label merely because normal fulfillment is later.                                                                                                        |
| A required file is genuinely still missing after its applicable due date | The source may describe that outstanding item as overdue and show whether A or B currently permits sending it.                                                                                                                         |
| An on-time original is later corrected                                   | Keep the original receipt/time intact. Describe the correction's own admission time and current review state; do not rewrite the original as late or assume the correction has an independent due date that the source never assigned. |
| A technical check finishes after due on bytes admitted before due        | Show that check's actual state, without substituting check completion for source receipt or marking the admitted file late.                                                                                                            |
| A new source clarification or additional item is requested later         | Show the newly assigned recipient work and any source-declared due terms. Do not retroactively treat the person as late under an unrelated earlier request date.                                                                       |

For B, say **“Submissions closed [date/time/timezone]”** when that is the actual source state, with the real source help path. Do not say **Request complete** or **Access expired** merely because intake closed. If reading remains independently permitted, keep the current own-file/readback actions available.

Before either boundary, the recipient should understand whether the date is a requested-by date or a submission cutoff. Use readable localized date/time/zone; do not copy a provider's end-of-day convention, private hidden grace date or unsupported promise of more time. Display any independent earlier access end accurately where needed, without requiring the recipient to calculate the controlling minimum.

Source changes to due dates need actual authoritative state/history and truthful current display. This research does not select an automatic email, reminder cadence, manual-extension UI or per-request exception builder. A public date should not knowingly misstate the operative rule merely to motivate recipients.

Current outcome and next action belong in ordinary main content, not only a color, tooltip, historical event or countdown. An old tab cannot keep an expired intake permission alive. A page's permitted current metadata must be refreshed or invalidated according to the existing authority rules, without exposing details on a neutral ended-link screen.

## Edges that the answer's adversarial review must resolve

1. **What happens to a correction near the due date?** A timely original and a later correction have separate receipt facts. Do not rewrite the original timestamp or silently report the whole request as timely merely because an older predecessor existed. Q03's successor/fence semantics remain binding.
2. **Which event counts for a hard cutoff or lateness?** Upload selection/start, storage transfer, durable source admission and asynchronous checks are different. Use the ratified admission model; do not charge the recipient for a later scanner/preview job or infer a grandfathered right from upload start. Any special in-flight policy needs explicit source treatment.
3. **What if the source has a stricter current bound?** A soft ordinary due date does not override a real earlier intake/access end. If that end already stops all late intake, say so rather than claiming A gives a grace period.
4. **What if bytes were admitted before the cutoff but the response arrives later?** Preserve the admitted fact and reconcile lost outcomes; a retry does not create a second receipt or move the first one past the boundary.
5. **Can staff review continue after intake/access closes?** Only under staff's independent current source authority. No recipient write or read grant follows from review activity.
6. **Does closure mean completion?** No. An incomplete request can stop accepting work without becoming normally fulfilled; Q07's completion clock must not start from that closure.
7. **Does Q08 reopen writing?** No. Fresh read access after ordinary completed-view expiry remains read-only and cannot be used as the late-submission/correction mechanism.
8. **Can unavailable or invalid policy be treated as open?** No. Unknown current action authority remains unavailable; “soft due date” is not a fail-open instruction.
9. **Does A supply an unlimited active lifetime?** No. The source profile still needs its finite authority/intake rules and any closure behavior qualified before activation. No active lifetime is invented here.
10. **Does B select an extension process?** No. A real owned help route can explain the state, but a new writable period, individual extension, automatic reopening or exception approval is a separate source policy.
11. **Who actually owes the next action?** A source still awaiting review does not imply the recipient owes more files. Late/overdue projections must follow the actual outstanding recipient requirement and relevant admission facts, with Q03 correction history kept distinct.

## Why not choose the fresh-access clock now?

Both issuance-relative and bounded first-use timing remain viable Q08 policies. The first has fewer hidden states and lets staff know the fixed result immediately; the second could avoid consuming the usable period during a delay but creates unused-admission expiry and activation semantics. Either must preserve current authority and earlier limits, avoid scanner/prefetch activation, and keep repeated issuance explicit.

Those differences are real, but choosing them now risks optimizing an exception before the original request's routine due/intake/access relationship is settled. Q09's proposed due-date question helps define what ordinary expiry and ordinary closure mean in the first place. This recommendation is about interview order, not permission to ship fresh access with an unresolved clock.

If the parent source review finds that the relevant ordinary source already defines due-date effects conclusively, do not re-open that settled contract. In that case the fresh-access activation-clock question becomes the better next unresolved choice.

This external comparison does not claim that the repository's source-owner contracts are silent. The parent's owner reconciliation must confirm that the proposed ordinary default is still an open founder decision; comparator behavior cannot supersede an already-binding Core contract.

## Suggested final question framing

> For the new ordinary private-document request, when the requested due date passes, should we **keep accepting eligible late files and permitted corrections while the source still allows intake**, or **stop new submissions and corrections at that date**? In both cases, current read permission, review, successful completion and record retention remain separate.

Recommend A as the small, useful generic collection default, with B fairly explained as a genuine hard intake cutoff. Scope to a currently authorized incomplete request and preserve binding specialist/earlier limits. Do not imply a number of extra days, a hidden grace period, a notification, unlimited active access, a late-submission appeal workflow or a fresh-access policy.
