## What the question must preserve

1. **Prove the original and relevant predecessor.** For the first fresh admission, prove original lawful admission and eligible original ordinary expiry. For a later one under proposed A, retain that original lineage and prove the relevant preceding fresh admission's eligible ordinary expiry plus the current lineage state. An old original expiry is not enough to ignore a newer live, unknown, revoked, cancelled, shortened or completed outcome. This is an explicit prospective specialization of Q13's original-expiry wording, not proof that repeats were already authorized.
2. **Scope nonoverlap correctly.** The proposed rule concerns the same tenant/request/intended-recipient/original-admission lineage; it is not a tenant-wide, whole-workflow or all-recipient lock. A current but temporarily unusable admission does not count as expired. A second concurrent staff intent cannot create parallel grants or reserve a future grant; the existing source/P12 boundary resolves current history coherently. Do not prescribe a new table, lock family, renewal queue or timer system.
3. **Keep original scope as the ceiling.** Each new reviewed requirement/action scope is bounded by the original admitted purpose/requirements and current eligibility. Avoid an ambiguous “same scope” that means all previous grants are unioned, or that one deliberately narrow prior grant permanently denies all other original eligible work. Q13 already permits eligible first submissions and exact pending corrections, distinct from independently allowed current own-file reading. Accepted/frozen writes, new purposes/requirements/recipients and historical bytes do not reappear. Any proposed wider restoration still must fit the original ceiling and a genuinely current deliberate review, not automatic carry-forward.
4. **Normal expiry is not cleared adverse history.** Revocation, real binding shortening, cancellation, purpose expiry or independent prohibitions cannot become ordinary merely because the clock later passes or their cause is removed. Temporary representation/proof outages remain separate; they do not create either an entitlement to a new grant or a permanent revocation by themselves. Apply restrictions at their actual scope.
5. **Completion is terminal for this unfinished lane.** Source fulfillment can proceed independently during a gap, but Q13 forbids retroactive recipient eligibility. A completed source cannot be made unfinished by another grant, retry or request clone. A genuine timely prior fulfillment observed late recovers its original outcome; it must not be mistaken for still-unfinished eligibility. For a valid new admission, Q13's narrow completed-view transition and Q07 fulfillment-anchored bound apply once, unchanged. Q08 eligibility/timing is not expanded.
6. **Review-only work can still be genuine.** The source may be unfinished while only staff review remains. Do not require a new recipient upload, forced correction or fabricated new requirement merely to make A eligible. Conversely, leaving a source marked unfinished is not independently sufficient: current original purpose and source predicates still have to be true. Expiry never means evidence rejection, deletion or an obligation to keep the case alive.
7. **Retry is not repeat.** Lost responses, failed proof, resend, new transport keys, same-intent recovery and delayed callbacks cannot buy another period. Each intentional admission needs its own source-owned deliberate identity and current review; repeated attempts at that same admission retain the original result. Old unadmitted submissions cannot silently switch to the new grant. Under B, count successful durable source admissions, not button presses or failed attempts; unknown commitment requires reconciliation rather than a guess that the one opportunity remains unused.
8. **Reuse the established UX.** Keep the same context, one meaningful staff review/action and real source-owned help. Show the current source state, previous access outcome and actual result without another duration editor, policy builder, mandatory essay, universal second approver or new portal. No automatic messages, reminders or scheduled future approval follows. Explain B's finality before the first fresh decision if B is selected; explain A's non-guaranteed further consideration without promising staff availability or approval.

These are question scope guards and inherited-owner consequences. They are not a new full adversarial package or a substitute for reviewing Conrad's answer.

## Strongest challenges to the recommendation

**Can A keep a request alive for years?** Numerically, yes: without an additional source cap, repeated deliberate valid approvals can yield a long sequence. Do not conceal that consequence or claim finite individual grants prove a bounded total lifetime. The defense is the continuing source-owned original purpose, current evidence/eligibility and real per-admission decision, not an invented automatic “progress” score. If Conrad prefers guaranteed lane finality, B is the honest alternative. Do not introduce a new inactivity detector, AI purpose judgment, automatic archive, arbitrary count or recurring governance ceremony to make A sound safer than it is.

**Does B force duplicate requests?** It must not authorize them. B intentionally gives up further same-lineage access after the first fresh grant. Existing independently legitimate source routes and qualified help may exist, but changing IDs, copying requirements or inventing a new purpose to obtain another clock is not a solution. This limitation belongs in the choice, not a later operational surprise.

**Does “never overlap” quietly add a broad global restriction?** It would if left undefined. State it for the same recipient/request/original-admission lineage. Other participants and independently admitted source purposes keep their owners. The proposed standard intentionally disallows stacking even narrow fresh subsets in this same lineage while an unfinished fresh admission remains valid; that should be explicit as part of the complete simple repeat policy, not hidden in a database uniqueness constraint.

**Does the latest narrower grant become the permanent ceiling?** Not automatically. Use original lineage plus live eligibility and the newly reviewed subset; do not union historical rights. Actual binding narrower purpose/action restrictions and adverse decisions remain effective. A transient previous choice of a narrower subset alone does not invent a permanent overall restriction. If the source's narrowing was explicitly binding, preserve that distinction.
