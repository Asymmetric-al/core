## The practical choice

We now have a concrete reference: staff collects and reviews a partner's requested agreement document for an onboarding handoff. The remaining purpose question is whether that first profile should **also intentionally support staff consulting the document during later partner operations**.

Both options preserve the required evidence of what was requested, received, reviewed and handed off. Both preserve independently lawful existing uses, current permitted historical inspection, holds, correction and actual records obligations. Those settled protections are not being re-voted. The choice is whether to add a continuing operational purpose to this first profile.

| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Keep the first profile to collection/review/handoff evidence (recommended)** | Staff can return to the source's evidence/history where its current approved purpose and permissions allow. This first profile does not itself establish a standing partner-reference use for later operations. Independently lawful uses remain valid, and later genuinely needed reuse follows its actual qualified source route. | Keeps the first policy anchored to the business need documented in Core and avoids creating an unspecified continuing-use obligation. | If ongoing partner reference is an intended routine need, it must be explicitly admitted through the responsible source; collection history cannot silently stand in for that purpose. |
| **B — Also support ongoing partner reference**                                     | In addition to the collection record, the responsible partner source admits a qualified use so eligible staff can consult the exact document during subsequent partner work.                                                                                                                                                        | Makes that continuing business use explicit and supports a clear return path rather than stretching the collection purpose.           | Requires its own source ownership, current readers/actions, version meaning, policy, ending/correction and recovery. The relevant native binding must qualify before reliance.         |

**Recommendation: A for this first profile.** Core documents agreement collection, review and handoff; it does not establish continuing operational reliance on the collected agreement. A is therefore the smallest evidenced scope. **B is the better choice if ongoing staff reference is an intended product outcome.** B can reuse the exact qualified bytes and existing source-context UI; it does not require a second upload, duplicate storage, extra confirmation page or contract-management platform.

Illustrative distinction, not a claim about a particular ministry: checking what was received and reviewed during onboarding is historical/source inspection; using the document as a reference for later partner work may be a separately admitted business use. The actual source purpose determines the distinction. The mere passage of time does not turn one into the other.

**One question:** Should this first profile **A — stay focused on collection/review/handoff evidence**, or **B — also include a separately qualified ongoing partner-reference purpose**?

## Adversarial check

### What could go wrong with this answer?

A could be mistaken for delete-at-handoff or removal of lawful history; B could be mistaken for perpetual access or a legally authoritative agreement register. The question fixes the historical floor and makes only the additional business purpose optional. Neither interpretation is permitted.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

We do not know that intended tenants routinely rely on these documents during ongoing partner operations. That is why B is a real owner choice rather than an automatic inference. Current docs establish collection/review/handoff, not that extra purpose or its policy.

### How does this affect the whole product?

It identifies the first profile's intended use without changing shared custody, existing historical duties, independent lawful uses, the source Files entry point or other consumers. B adds a purpose to qualify, not a second agreement/CRM owner.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

Staff retains current permitted evidence/history under both options. B can make later partner work clearer when it is needed, through the existing source context and exact version. Neither requires reupload, extra governance forms or new participant work.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

It combines contextual document access with explicit purpose and source rights. Current CRM examples make the interaction familiar while showing why a record relationship or attachment list cannot be treated as universal authority. No vendor behavior supplies Core policy.

### Does this fit Asym’s existing repo and product direction?

Yes if framed exactly as the additional purpose. Q02 already permits multiple independently admitted uses, Q28 is narrower ordinary evidence reuse, and Q64 did not select an ongoing partner-reference use. Asking again about history, tabs or general safe reuse would be redundant and is deliberately excluded.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

Recommend A for this first profile under the currently evidenced need. Choose B if continuing partner reference is deliberately desired; it is practical with qualified source authority and does not require duplicated bytes. No selection, policy period, new ADR or glossary term is inferred from this preparation.
