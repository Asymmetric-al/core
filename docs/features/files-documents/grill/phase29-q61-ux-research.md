## 4. The two critical journeys are fixed founder outcomes

The founder explicitly requires one complete private request-to-review-to-authorized-share journey and one complete public asset-to-placement-to-withdrawal journey. These are not new use cases selected by this note. The named-person situations below are hypothetical task material, not observed ministry practice.

### Private request journey

Elena requests an equipment inventory and packing list from Nora. Nora confirms the intended participant context, submits permissible documents on a phone and returns after a weak-network interruption. Elena inspects the received versions, requests a genuine change where needed, reviews or corrects an accidental acceptance, and sees the actual request outcome. An authorized colleague receives only the exact independently admitted private share.

**What a lightweight prototype can investigate:** whether Nora recognizes receipt versus technical checking versus staff review; whether a safe unknown-response state avoids duplicate work; whether Elena understands item acceptance versus whole-request fulfillment; whether Correct acceptance is distinguished from Request changes and reopening; whether participants understand what they must do next and which access has ended; whether the share's recipient/version/action/end is intelligible.

**What it cannot establish:** actual intended-recipient/native identity assurance; source/P12/RLS/Storage authority; immutable bytes and exact rendition coverage; transaction/finalization/F races; permanent original-intent recovery; provider dispatch and every-call suppression; current range/conditional/private read behavior; retention/disposition or backup integrity. Those need actual source contracts and native/provider qualification. A drawn denial state proves only that it was drawn.

### Public asset journey

Mara prepares an image for a ministry page. The image's relevant source rights and safety evidence are established, a separately qualified output is placed with actual contextual accessibility/credit metadata, and the owning page/release process activates that exact version. Later the rights owner withdraws it. Mara must distinguish private original, approved output, draft placement, active released generation and the limits of external cache/copy control.

**What a lightweight prototype can investigate:** whether the asset and placement are distinguishable; whether the user knows that uploading is not publishing; whether current rights and withdrawal impact are understood; whether an older release/current successor is clear; whether blocked or partially failed withdrawal has an honest next action; whether keyboard/mobile/AT operation is comprehensible.

**What it cannot establish:** sanitization/discarded provenance, real source rights and output qualification, immutable release-manifest binding, current origin/cache restrictions, external forgetting, complete Used in/hold coverage or verified deletion of physical copies. A mock success cannot stand in for those facts. P22/P23 ownership remains separate from ordinary evidence.

## Adversarial check

### What could go wrong with this answer?

A could become an endless rewrite that delays useful feedback. B could produce a polished but false permissions/recovery story or be mistaken for native qualification. Required output, exact source trace, explicit simulation limits and unchanged proof obligations prevent those shortcuts. Any discovered missing consequence remains visible.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

We assume the selected sixty decisions describe the intended scope well enough to consolidate or test without another optional feature vote. The bounded census found no stronger gap but cannot prove none will emerge. Participant availability, task prevalence, device mix, operating costs and native support are not established by this note.

### How does this affect the whole product?

Both paths preserve common custody with distinct ordinary, official, financial, public, Support and resource owners. They retain exact future consumer and repair obligations. The sequence cannot authorize a second library, general drive, permission engine or an unsafe omission of difficult specialist work.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

A makes the later UI's promises more trustworthy; B can expose confusing status/action language and accessibility barriers earlier. Both must preserve complete correction, expired-access, partial-failure and operator journeys. Neither should make users repeat valid documents or understand internal architecture to accomplish their task.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

Primary GDS guidance supports whole-user-problem scope, small risk-led prototypes and actual task evaluation. W3C supports early human involvement together with conformance assessment. These support both alternatives and do not prove one is universally better. No current Asym user test or measured outcome is claimed.

### Does this fit Asym's existing repo and product direction?

Yes. Founder §§1/2/16–19 require complete source-grounded outcomes, research before formal stages, coherent private/public journeys and evidence beyond screenshots. Q02 rejects a general drive; Q60 closes the identified acceptance-remedy gap. Remaining native/source integration and custody evidence stay mandatory rather than becoming optional new questions.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

Recommend A now, with B as a strong fair sequencing alternative and targeted experiments available when needed. Do not treat A as no user research or B as no source reconciliation. Q61 remains unselected; no formal `/to-prd`, implementation, outreach, native qualification or readiness certificate follows from this recommendation.
