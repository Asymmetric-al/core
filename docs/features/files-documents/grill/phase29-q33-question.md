## Historical question and alternatives

**Should authorized staff be able to mark an outstanding ordinary requested
item “No longer required” when it was requested by mistake or is no longer
applicable, while the request is still unfinished?**

Q21 requires real staff acceptance for ordinary file evidence. Q22 completes
the request when its complete qualified source requirements are satisfied.
Q23 keeps ordinary review recoverable, but expressly does not create a new
waiver/cancellation power. Those decisions preserve existing authorized source
outcomes; they do not yet provide this ordinary-item correction action.

| Option                                                                                 | Everyday experience                                                                                                                                                                                | Benefit                                                                                                                                                          | Cost                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Allow authorized staff to mark an item No longer required (recommended)**        | Staff who may change this source requirement can end the demand for that exact outstanding item. Its history and any received evidence remain under their existing protection and retention rules. | The participant can stop working on an unnecessary item and continue the rest, without a dummy upload or false acceptance. Staff resolve the mistake in context. | Requires a clear distinction between changing what is required and accepting what was supplied, plus current source authority and honest completion consequences.                            |
| **B — Use existing source correction routes without adding this ordinary-item action** | Staff resolve the mistaken requirement through an already-qualified, capable correction/resolution route owned by the requesting source. No new ordinary No longer required capability is added.   | Avoids another action where the source already provides an effective way to make the correction.                                                                 | Can send staff out of the request and leave the participant confused. It is viable only if the source actually supplies a usable resolution; an imagined cancel/reissue route is not enough. |

**Recommend A.** Correcting an unnecessary demand is different from obtaining
better evidence. Keeping that correction in the item context is the clearest
path for both people. Preserve actual source permissions and specialist
requirements; do not give every reviewer a universal waiver button.

These are two product alternatives, not two tenant modes to implement. Tenant
flexibility comes from actual source-authorized staff capabilities and the
particular request's needs. No new settings platform, multi-stage exception
queue or mandatory second reviewer is proposed.

## Concrete example and user journeys

An ordinary request contains three supporting items. Staff establish that the
third was included by mistake and is unnecessary for this request's legitimate
purpose. They should not ask the participant for a blank file, pretend an absent
document was accepted, or repeatedly request changes. This is an illustrative
test scenario, not an observed ministry workflow or a claim about its frequency.

| Journey                                                                              | Proposed experience and boundary to verify if A is selected                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Participant has not started the item                                                 | Show No longer required in the same request with no upload demand for that item. Keep the remaining eligible items usable. Do not silently make the item disappear.                                                                                                                                     |
| Participant already supplied evidence                                                | Preserve the original receipt and history. No longer required does not relabel evidence Accepted, remove a quarantine, delete bytes, or waive independently required safety/review work.                                                                                                                |
| Participant is uploading during the change                                           | Final source admission decides the ordered outcome. Preserve a receipt already committed; if the change wins first, explain that the item is no longer requested. Transfer progress alone is not receipt. Resolve an uncertain result without a duplicate submission or deletion of shared files.       |
| Staff can review but cannot change requirements                                      | Inspection/acceptance or Q24 team responsibility alone does not enable this action. Explain the missing capability and expose the actual authorized source/help route without displaying restricted information.                                                                                        |
| The last outstanding item becomes unnecessary                                        | Explain the possible completion consequence before the meaningful action. Q22 still evaluates the complete current source rule; it does not count only visible file rows. Show the actual durable result.                                                                                               |
| Every ordinary item is no longer needed                                              | An eligible, explicitly permitted complete empty/non-file source predicate may fulfill. Zero visible/outstanding items alone cannot. If the entire request purpose ended, this is not a way to manufacture successful fulfillment; whole-request cancellation is a separate decision.                   |
| Another staff member accepts or edits the item, or relevant clarification arrives    | Recheck the exact current decision basis. An older affected decision conflicts rather than overwriting newer facts. No new Read/Reply/Resolve gate or undo of committed history follows.                                                                                                                |
| Staff click twice, lose connectivity, or switch tenants                              | Recover the original committed action and current permitted view; do not repeat the business effect. Discard stale responses after tenant/user/request changes. A failed refresh cannot erase confirmed success.                                                                                        |
| Someone made the No longer required decision by mistake                              | Preserve the historical decision. Use only a source-authorized correction that rechecks current requirements and lifecycle; do not promise universal Undo, silent reopening, fresh access or a new obligation from replay. The selected-answer review must make the supported correction path concrete. |
| Participant uses a phone, keyboard, screen reader, another locale or slow connection | Use a short text status, clear action/next step, preserved focus and truthful persistent results. Avoid color-only meaning, hidden disappearing rows and success communicated only through a toast. Show dates in the appropriate locale without changing actual occurrence time.                       |

Staff should see the exact item and the meaningful consequence in the existing
request context. A concise explanation may help the participant; internal notes
and source history keep their own disclosure rules. Do not freeze a mandatory
essay, extra confirmation or pixel layout before evidence shows it is needed.
No longer required should be visually distinct from Accepted and Changes requested,
with attention directed toward the work that remains.

## Current primary research and strongest alternative

The [comparative research](phase29-q33-comparative-research.md) retains primary
links, differences and applicability limits. The evidence supports the need to
correct requests after sending them; no examined product establishes Core's
exact state, authority or history contract.

- [TaxDome's document-checklist management](https://help.taxdome.com/article/document-checklist-management)
  documents editing before and after sending, including removal of items that
  are no longer relevant. The guide is explicitly for US firms and contains
  separate Beta behavior. This supports the practical correction use case,
  not adoption of its checklist deletion, completion or client-access rules.
- [Content Snare's permissions](https://contentsnare.com/help/knowledge-base/team-permissions/)
  distinguish reviewing answers from editing/removing fields. This is strong
  evidence against equating reviewer assignment with requirement-change
  authority. Its post-send edit/deletion guidance, detailed in the comparative
  note, also warns about effects on client answers; Core must preserve its
  own receipt/history and retention boundaries.
- [Submittable's Additional Forms](https://submittable.help/en/articles/2670501-how-can-i-track-additional-forms)
  provide an organization-side Rescinded state distinct from Completed and
  prohibit rescinding already completed forms. This is a whole-form analogy,
  not evidence of item-level waiver or a reason to copy its access or email
  consequences into Core.
- [GOV.UK task-list guidance](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/task-list/)
  favors clear task/status text and drawing attention to remaining work. It
  also says to simplify a service before adding a task list. Use those UX
  principles with Core's components; its particular completion model is not
  Core's source-owned fulfillment rule.

The strongest argument for B is real: a requesting source may already have a
good correction capability, and a second mutation path would duplicate it.
A should expose the source's one authoritative command in the ordinary request
experience, not create a competing File Manager override. Where existing source
commands can safely supply it, reuse them. Where they cannot, this decision
would authorize a bounded ordinary-source capability with qualification.

The strongest risk of A is staff using it to clear difficult requirements.
That narrows the recommendation to actual no-longer-applicable/mistaken ordinary
items, an eligible source and authorized staff. A missing, inconvenient or
poor-quality required document remains required until its owner lawfully
changes that requirement. No policy exception is inferred from the label.

No Asym staff/participant interviews, correction-rate measurements or measured
time savings support this recommendation yet. It is a product judgment grounded
in the known request/review model and current comparable capabilities. Later
representative-user proof must test understanding of No longer required versus
Accepted and what, if anything, remains to do.

## Repository reconciliation and scope boundaries

The [owner research](phase29-q33-owner-research.md) reads the founder brief,
Phase 29 owner constraints, the Q01–Q32 frontier and relevant full source
contracts. The [runtime research](phase29-q33-runtime-research.md) separates
current implementation from prospective native qualification.

1. **Source ownership:** Q02 and Phase 29 assign private files/requests to their
   real purpose owner; custody, shared tasks and workflow coordination cannot
   create their own waiver truth. Actual source/P12 requirement-change authority
   is distinct from read, acceptance, assignment and recipient-access powers.
2. **A genuinely open ordinary capability:** Q21–Q23 preserve existing source
   not-required outcomes but explicitly add no new waiver. Q28 Remove from this
   item withdraws an exact staff evidence association; it does not withdraw the
   requirement. Q33 therefore needs a new product choice rather than a silent
   reinterpretation of those decisions.
3. **Narrow lifecycle:** outstanding unaccepted ordinary item, unfinished
   eligible request. Earlier acceptance, fulfilled requests, specialist/consent/
   finance/application requirements and whole-request cancellation retain their
   own commands. No hidden unaccept, backdating, predecessor revival or reopening.
4. **Complete fulfillment:** preserve Q22's explicit full predicate, genuine
   non-file/safety obligations and actual durable fulfillment occurrence. A
   lawful last-item change may complete; missing/filtered facts cannot. No
   dummy evidence, false successful request or new routine final approval.
5. **One current decision boundary:** order requirement changes with evidence
   admission/review, relevant clarification and closure. Preserve trusted
   tenant/source/actor attribution, revision and durable history. Repeat calls
   recover the same effect; current disclosure still applies. UI state and
   generic task status are not authoritative.
6. **Access and notices remain separate:** Q06–Q19 clocks and restrictions
   continue. No new access period, participant credential, automatic email/bell
   or reminder is selected. Q26 Request changes, Q31 staff-reply notices and
   Q32 participant-message notices are different occurrences. Existing queued
   effects keep their actual current source-purpose rules; do not repurpose them.
7. **Recovery without overbuilding:** use existing source work/history/help,
   protect independent file uses/retention and qualify safe current correction.
   Do not add a waiver engine, workflow builder, duplicate inbox or a dependency
   on the whole future Studio. Explanation, error prevention and mistaken-action
   recovery must be proportionate and concrete in the selected-answer review.
