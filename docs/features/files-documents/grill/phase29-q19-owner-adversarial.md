## Corrected meaning

Allow the exact currently authorized source actor to consider a new Q08 read-only admission after the relevant preceding period's **eligible ordinary expiry**, for the same intended recipient and lawful Q16 completed-admission context. The context and historical completed ceiling remain fixed across either Q16 origin. Every new approval is meaningful and current; its scope is that historical ceiling intersected with current eligibility and the reviewed subset. Each new effect uses Q17/Q18, with no overlapping fresh-read admissions within the context, no preset native count/cumulative-day ceiling, no automatic or advance renewal and no mandatory waiting gap. Repeated finite periods can cover a long aggregate time; this is the selected policy consequence, not a guarantee of another approval or retained content. Original facts and adverse history stay intact. Existing source/P12 effects, review, recovery and serving mechanisms suffice.

The strongest alternative remains one successfully admitted fresh period per context, including unused periods. A avoids arbitrary finality where a legitimate current purpose remains, but costs recurring decisions and a potentially long access opportunity. Neither public analogues nor repository evidence prove the optimal repeat count. Do not turn that uncertainty into an invented quota, second approver or annual review framework.

### Important boundary examples

- Root ceiling contains submitted originals X and Y. A prior fresh grant covered only X. Once that period ends normally, a newly reviewed Y grant can be considered if currently eligible; X-only was not a global denial. While X's fresh period is live, a separate Y grant cannot evade Q19's context-wide nonoverlap.
- Source revoked the relevant prior grant before its nominal end. Waiting until the old nominal date does not convert that adverse ending into ordinary expiry. Any independently permitted owner remediation remains separate; Q19 does not authorize it or turn every unrelated old restriction into a universal ban.
- Two approvals review the same expired predecessor. One commits; the other cannot silently wait through that new thirty-day period and then act. If the second operation was actually the already-committed semantic intent, it recovers that original result instead of failing a “latest predecessor” test.
- A recipient never used the original completed view, or its deliberate scope was status-only. Those histories can remain eligible, but repeats neither require telemetry proof of use nor add missing file rights. Qualified new renditions of the same authorized Submitted Original remain possible; no substituted original is admitted.
- No overlap does not require a mandatory gap. It also does not promise gap-free service, add a future reservation, stop independently authorized staff work or automatically revoke unrelated rights.
