### A — Further individually approved periods after eligible ordinary expiry (recommended product judgment)

Each subsequent period requires a new deliberate current source approval, eligible ordinary expiry of the relevant preceding period, and all inherited Q08/Q16 requirements. Each uses Q17/Q18's fixed new-source-issuance end: up to 720 elapsed hours, with genuine scoped earlier limits. No overlapping fresh completed-read admissions within that fixed completed context, including different narrow subsets. Other participants and independent source rights remain independently governed.

The complete proposed repeat policy has **no preset native lifetime grant-count or cumulative-day ceiling**. This does not promise another approval, uninterrupted access or perpetual content availability. There is no automatic renewal, pre-expiry extension, reservation of a future grant, mandatory waiting gap or new date/quota editor. At an exclusive old end, a new eligible independently authorized source act need not wait an invented cooldown.

Advantage: legitimate contextual readback can receive assistance without pretending the original grant was extended, reopening intake or moving files into a new request. Cost: repeated discretionary approvals can provide access over a long aggregate span; finite individual periods do not bound lifetime access. That consequence must be explicit in the question. Current purpose, true binding limits, original scope and retention/disposal still constrain every new admission.

### B — One successfully source-issued fresh period per fixed completed context

After that one fresh period ends, this lane cannot issue another. Independent governed assistance remains available where its own owner permits it, without request cloning, invented source purposes, raw-attachment fallback or fabricated intake to evade the limit.

The one allowance is consumed by an actually committed fresh source admission, even if unused or delivery subsequently fails. Failed or rolled-back attempts do not consume it; unknown commitment must be reconciled. Same-intent recovery is the original effect, not another allowance. A changed subset, new operation identifier or a replacement transport credential cannot reset the lifetime limit.

Advantage: a simple lifetime boundary for this lane and less repeated authorization exposure. Cost: a legitimate later readback need cannot receive this convenient fresh-read route even if the source still permits the content and purpose. No automatic special exception should silently turn B into A.

Recommendation is A because it preserves narrow, deliberate help under the already-selected source controls. This is a product judgment, not an empirical security/usability optimum or a claim that owners already adopted it. B remains a viable clear policy. A fixed numeric quota of two/three periods is an additional unsupported product quantity; it need not become a third option merely for symmetry.

## Required scope guards for either answer

1. **Original completed ceiling remains fixed.** Each reviewed scope is that actual completed admission's members/submitted-original versions/actions/Q05 context intersected with current eligibility and the newly reviewed subset. Neither the wider unfinished requirements nor accumulated fresh grants can enlarge it. A narrow earlier Q08 subset alone does not globally narrow the historical ceiling, but genuine binding restrictions remain effective.
2. **Relevant history, not any old expired row.** For A, preserve the original lawful completed-admission proof and add the relevant preceding fresh period/current-lineage evidence. The old root's expiry cannot ignore a live, unknown, revoked or adversely ended later period. Ordinary expiry and genuine purpose expiry/shortening/revocation remain distinct, including coincident causes. Missing evidence is not ordinary expiry or an unused allowance.
3. **Approval is separate from use.** Current exact source issuance capability, P12 administrative scope/live assignable ceiling and the actual continuing grantor/delegation dependency govern issuance. Neither generic staff status nor file-reading rights suffice. Recipient proof/current every-door authority still govern use; the expired recipient grant need not authorize staff consideration.
4. **Safe recovery precedes new issuance.** Recover an already committed matching deliberate intent on its original terms under current disclosure, even if a later period exists. Do not turn a lost response into a new period or return the latest grant as the earlier result. For an unadmitted intent, revalidate the reviewed predecessor/material state and current eligibility. A stale intent cannot silently survive an intervening period and become valid when that later period expires. This reuses source/P12 fences and semantic identity; it does not require a renewal engine or full-history scan.
5. **Read-only completed scope stays read-only.** No intake, correction, acceptance changes, new completion stage, old submitted-version archive, other-recipient scope or file resharing follows. Source completion and receipts remain unchanged. This question does not reopen Q13/Q15 unfinished access.
6. **No retention promise or purpose laundering.** Repeated access does not reset custody retention, holds, disposal, original received facts or independent source rights. Asym remains contextual file use/production, not a personal archive. No arbitrary new request/recipient/subset can create a new lifetime allowance or evade a genuine source end.
7. **Usability and repair are distinct.** A still-valid grant with expired sign-in proof, temporary representation failure or delivery failure calls for the existing qualified repair, not another period. Prior non-use is not absence of historical admission. Deliberately admitted status-only scope remains status-only; partial proof cannot fabricate missing file rights.

For A, explicit sequential/nonoverlap plus absence of a preset count/cumulative cap are the complete policy consequences of allowing further periods, not hidden implementation defaults. They require founder selection and subsequent adversarial ratification. No automatic notification, issuer-role invention, reminder, new retention rule or operational quota is bundled into the question.
