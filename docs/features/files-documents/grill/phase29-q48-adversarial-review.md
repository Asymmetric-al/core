## Research and strongest alternative

The strongest alternative is B: a complete native deliberate staff reminder, not a workaround telling staff to email someone elsewhere. B simplifies autonomous state and lets a person consider context, but requires staff to notice each follow-up. A preserves that path and adds optional automation. This is the better product fit as a judgment because the founder already requires practical reminders; no quantified time-saving or completion benefit is claimed.

Current primary comparisons show variation, not consensus. Control causes, source work and delivery evidence are separate facts, not one universal enum. Multiple pauses/stops may coexist. The implementation must make their valid combinations and the one admitted occurrence/lifetime budget true at the authoritative boundary.

## Adversarial check

### What could go wrong with this answer?

Automation can nag a recipient who already responded, expose private context, oversend after an outage or lose irreversible possible-call truth. The exact source predicate, minimal material, current call fences, lane/count semantics and prospective controls address those failures. A checkbox without these invariants is not the selected feature.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

The ordinary source must expose trustworthy current work/recipient/action facts and record real work-ending transitions. The existing communication and execution owners must be qualified for this new purpose. Neither native proof nor Asym user preferences are established by research. Numerical profile choices are explicit product judgments with bounded tenant control; native and intended-user proof remains a release requirement.

### How does this affect the whole product?

Q48 introduces a narrowly owned reminder plan and actual source operations; it does not make task dates, calendar jobs or provider events authoritative. Q01–Q47, other notice purposes, financial/specialist truth and public/checkout surfaces retain their owners. The new recipient Stop is not a general preferences center or an access grant.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

Tenants can choose automation, staff can intervene without rewriting schedules, and recipients can stop automatic follow-up without losing the request. Received work is not chased while staff reviews it. Exact next time, count, control cause and delivery uncertainty are visible and recoverable on accessible mobile and desktop surfaces.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

Primary products support automatic and manual controls and also show harmful stop/snapshot assumptions. The recommendation takes clear bounded controls, true current eligibility and durable outcomes, rather than claiming consensus or copying a vendor's identity/default/cadence. PostgreSQL and provider semantics are checked against primary documentation; no exactly-once or free-operation guarantee is made.

Yes, with the explicit new source/P12/P3/recipient-control and P17 material/binding amendments. Native requests use P9/P6/P17 without the whole Workflow Studio, while applicable guarded activation remains. The current tenant guard is useful but narrow. Contribution-approval reminders and task schema are adjacent code, not this purpose's proof.
