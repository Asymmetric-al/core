## Source handling is the audience anchor

**Q30's clarification-handling route, not Q24's document-review roster, anchors this notice.** The two may coincide where the exact source proves it, but cannot be treated as synonyms. Four concrete cases establish the distinction:

1. A participant can ask before the first upload. There need not yet be Q27 actionable evidence for a reviewer.
2. An accepted item can remain discussable within an unfinished request. Asking a question does not unaccept the item or manufacture new evidence-review work.
3. An admitted message can require owned follow-through after final human review while Q22 fulfillment is deferred; it does not create another final approval.
4. Participant expiry or closure after admission does not delete the message or the source's staff-handling obligation. Staff access and safe follow-through can survive under their independent authority.

The correct practical recipient phrase is “staff authorized to handle this clarification through its source-owned route.” It is not automatically “the reviewer,” “the person who last replied,” “request creator,” “all admins,” “every team member,” “task assignee” or “the participant's contact.” Being accountable, currently employed, in a named team, assigned a task or able to send mail does not itself grant access to this sensitive source/use. Do not present a fictional single mailbox, team cohort, queue or cardinality as already established.

Q30 already requires a durable source-work route with an authorized person/team or a qualified protected recovery path before acknowledging receipt. Email supplements that route; it neither creates nor repairs it by broadcasting. Missing optional staff-contact/egress proof can block mail without losing lawful native receipt/handling. A genuinely absent/unknown required native handling route still follows Q30's exact admission and capable-recovery contract; Q32 cannot weaken that by promising to “email someone.”
