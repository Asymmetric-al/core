# Q62 — Applying the approved retention policy to an ordinary request

Research date: 2026-10-28. Independent UX/comparative research for question preparation. Q61 is ratified; Q62 is unselected. This note proposes an interaction choice, not retention periods, a legal opinion, a new glossary term, an ADR, implementation, or native qualification. Only this work note is authored by this reviewer.

## Finding and proposed question

**Recommend A: show and apply the source-resolved approved retention policy during ordinary request issuance, with a plain summary and only genuinely permitted approved alternatives.** This avoids asking staff to repeatedly make a policy decision that the responsible source has already made. It is a contextual product judgment, not a documented universal standard or an observed Asym user preference.

The genuine choice is how the approved binding becomes part of a new eligible ordinary request. Source authority itself is not optional. A request issuer cannot create a retention period, override an obligatory purpose, or choose a different legal meaning merely because a dropdown exists. B is a real alternative: require the issuer to deliberately apply the compatible approved binding on every request, selecting among alternatives only when several are actually approved for that source and context. B can help when relevant classification context genuinely comes from the issuer; it also adds a repeated decision even when there is only one lawful result.

**Suggested one question:** When an eligible ordinary request has a current approved retention policy for its purpose, should Asym apply it automatically and show a clear summary, or require staff to explicitly apply the approved policy on every request?

| Option                                                                 | Concrete experience                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Strongest benefit                                                                                                                                                                               | Real cost or risk                                                                                                                                                               |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Apply the approved policy with a visible summary (recommended)** | The source resolves its current applicable approved binding. Staff see what evidence it covers and its actual trigger in the normal request review. An authorized Change action appears only where another approved compatible choice really exists. Ordinary Issue remains the deliberate final action. | Routine requests do not turn into records-management quizzes. The governing policy remains visible and can be checked without another mandatory confirmation step.                              | An unnoticed incorrect purpose or stale default could propagate a wrong binding. A plain summary cannot replace the source's actual applicability proof or policy-owner review. |
| **B — Explicitly apply the approved policy for every request**         | Staff explicitly apply the compatible approved binding before issue. With one valid policy, this is application/confirmation, not a fabricated choice. With several permitted alternatives, staff select one using the context the source requires.                                                      | Makes the decision salient and accommodates a real distinction the source cannot infer. Particularly defensible for heterogeneous request purposes with genuinely different approved treatment. | Repetitive application may become habitual clicking; it cannot by itself establish legal correctness. More form work remains even for an unambiguous purpose.                   |

Both retain the same actual approved source policy, current eligibility, protected history, holds, other-use dependencies, disposal authority and evidence gates. Neither chooses a universal schedule for all request data. Neither makes an unknown policy optional. This is not an automatic-versus-manual deletion question, nor a choice between a library-wide rule engine and a records dashboard.

## Why this remains open after Q61

The completed Q61 source register explicitly carries **R-POL-ordinary**: actual ordinary-purpose classes, typed triggers, floors, ceilings, permitted choices, hold authority and disposition still require the responsible source/records/privacy owner. The current program `decisions-and-gates.md` retains the P29/30 policy and legacy-profile decision boundary. A retention binding is already required; the ordinary issuance interaction is not selected.

The owner review independently confirms that Q02 rejects a governance form for every upload and Q39 preserves valid current defaults. These favor the smaller A interaction, but neither grants a universal default nor selects the Q62 command. This note relies on that explicit owner reconciliation rather than claiming to have reread every line of all 61 decisions in this turn. The prior full founder and source-register review remains relevant; the R-POL-ordinary row was freshly checked here.

Scope is the **ordinary request-evidence policy-binding journey**. Request metadata/history, participant clarifications, staff comments, notification material, previews, generated assemblies, exports, reused original bytes and backups have their own actual source/copy purposes. A concise request summary may describe an approved policy comprising several classes, but cannot erase those distinctions or imply one deletion date covers everything. P18/P21/P23/D32 patterns are useful owner constraints; their actual periods and special fallback rules are not transplanted into ordinary requests.

No policy is manufactured by a template, folder, staff display name, file extension, uploader role, file digest or browser state. Q38's approved metadata discovery is not a body-classification service. No AI/OCR classifier or new retention platform is proposed.

## Practical example and smallest complete experience

**Illustrative only:** Elena prepares an ordinary request for Nora's equipment inventory. This is not a recorded ministry workflow or evidence of request volume. Suppose the responsible source has approved a policy called “Ordinary equipment-request evidence.” The name is an example; no actual period or trigger is assumed here.

Under A, Elena sees the actual approved policy summary with the request's recipient, items and access details. She can inspect details or choose a genuinely allowed alternative without losing her draft. Under B, she deliberately applies the displayed approved policy before issuing. If the source needs a real purpose distinction, the form asks for that distinction; neither option makes her invent a retention duration. The request can be issued only after the source can establish the actual applicable binding.

The summary should answer the questions the issuer can act on: **which approved policy; which evidence/purpose; which source-defined event starts the relevant period; whether an authorized alternative exists.** It must distinguish an event that has not happened from an unknown event or policy. A fully determined effective date may be displayed when current facts support it; an event-relative rule should remain event-relative rather than fabricate a date.

Example copy, subject to actual source facts:

| Situation                                      | Recommended concise copy or action                                                                 | Qualification                                                                                                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Applicable approved policy is known            | **Retention policy: [approved name]**; **Covers: [safe scope]**; **Starts: [actual source event]** | This is a resolved governing fact, not evidence the issuer personally authored or approved the policy.                                         |
| More detail is useful                          | **View retention details**                                                                         | Show current authorized scope/trigger and relevant constraints. Do not reveal confidential hold or legal-case facts to an unauthorized issuer. |
| A compatible alternative is actually available | **Change retention policy**                                                                        | Limit choices to those presently approved and permitted for this request; preserve other draft fields. No free-text period entry.              |
| Purpose context is missing                     | **Choose the request purpose so Asym can apply the approved retention policy.**                    | Only if that is an actual source-required input and the actor can provide it. Do not add an invented purpose questionnaire to every request.   |
| No applicable approved policy exists           | **An approved retention policy is needed before this request can be issued.**                      | Preserve the draft and provide the actual qualified policy-owner repair route. A dead Contact admin link is not a complete supported flow.     |
| Applicability could not be checked             | **We could not check the retention policy. Your draft is still available.**                        | Keep unknown distinct from no policy and from a successful binding; retry the original preparation safely.                                     |
| Current policy changed before issue            | **The retention policy has changed. Review the updated summary before issuing.**                   | Appropriate only for a meaningful change to the reviewed basis; a harmless identical refresh should not create a needless conflict ceremony.   |
| Access ends independently                      | **Access ends: [actual qualified value]**                                                          | Separate from retention. Never imply participant access lasts as long as retained records.                                                     |
| Issue response is lost                         | **We could not confirm whether the request was issued. Check status.**                             | Recover the original operation; do not issue another request or bind a replacement policy merely to clear the screen.                          |

Typing, selecting a proposed alternative or opening details does not issue the request or delete records. One deliberate normal Issue action can include the valid binding; A does not require a second policy-specific modal, extra approver, mandatory reason essay or “I read this” checkbox. B may require explicit application in the existing review form without imposing an additional screen for its own sake. A select control must not conceal unresolved policy authority as an empty value.

The recipient is not made a records-policy author. Any participant-facing privacy/retention explanation follows its own approved source disclosure and legal requirements. Q62 does not invent consent, a new email, a new policy-change notice or permission to reveal confidential holds. A participant can lose portal access while records remain lawfully retained; retained records do not restore the portal or an expired proof.

## Seven founder checks

### What could go wrong with this answer?

A could silently make a bad default look authoritative; B could create a mandatory clicking ritual without improving the actual decision. Both can mislead people into thinking access expiry, request completion or a displayed date proves physical deletion. Visible scope/trigger, current source proof, truthful lifecycle language and a capable policy-owner repair path address the specific failure modes. No quantified error reduction is claimed.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

We do not yet have actual approved ordinary-purpose schedules, the distribution of policy alternatives, staff decision competence, production request volume or user-test results. A is recommended for the already-resolved common case, not because all requests share one schedule. Where the source needs real classification input, that fact must be supplied rather than invented by a default.

### How does this affect the whole product?

The decision changes the ordinary issuance interaction. It leaves official finance, tax receipts, Support, public Media/Page/Article assets, resources, exports, previews, messages and legacy import classes with their actual owners. It preserves source references, shared-byte custody, holds, business history and physical disposition gates. It does not make a request policy a universal file policy or create a new administrative engine.
