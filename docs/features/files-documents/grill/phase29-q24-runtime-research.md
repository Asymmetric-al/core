# Phase 29 Q24 runtime research — accountable review responsibility

2026-10-21, Asia/Bangkok. Q01–Q23 and their complete amendments are ratified. Q24 is unselected. This is bounded next-question research, not a full selected-answer adversarial review, implementation plan or proof that ordinary native review is deployed.

## Recommended next dependency and genuine fork

Reviewer responsibility is the next useful choice. Q21 requires deliberate attributable staff acceptance, Q23 supplies recoverable review, and Q22 derives fulfillment from the full actual source predicate. Those decisions need a capable, accountable route to the next staff actor; they intentionally did not choose a universal named reviewer or shared review pool.

Recommend **allowing the requesting source to bind review responsibility to an eligible individual or a qualified team route, without universally requiring an additional named individual assignment**. Preserve stricter specialist sources and their current capabilities. This is flexibility within an explicit source-owned responsibility, not permission for any tenant staff member to view/review any request. A team label or an empty queue is not a successful resolution.

The strongest alternative is **require a named eligible accountable individual for the ordinary request's review responsibility**, with an actual qualified reassignment/backup route if they become unavailable. That can make personal accountability and continuity easier to understand, at the cost of an extra assignment dependency and handoffs where a team already owns the work.

The distinction must be a genuine **responsibility requirement**, not the name of the person who ultimately makes a decision. Every actual Q21/Q23 review decision already records its authorized human actor. If B merely says “a reviewer must be identified when they click Accept,” it reasks an existing invariant and is not a meaningful alternative. Conversely, a named accountable assignee does not automatically mean exclusive source authority, a mandatory Claim button, a permanent edit lock or a second reviewer. Those consequences require their own source support and must not be bundled implicitly.

Suggested lean question shape: **Can ordinary document review be owned by a qualified source-bound person or team, or must every request's review responsibility resolve to one named eligible person?** The final question should say which review work waits if B's named responsibility is unresolved. Do not silently introduce a new invitation/intake stop, deadline extension or staff exclusivity rule merely to sharpen the comparison.

## Actual current implementation and limits

### Assignment helper and shared task source

`packages/api/src/admin/mission-control-tasks/assignment-policy.ts:6–30` returns input IDs for three modes:

- `actor_only`: actor profile, no queue.
- `queue_only`: no assignee, supplied queue.
- `actor_and_queue` (helper default): actor and supplied queue.

The helper does not resolve source eligibility, team membership, current review capability, absence, ambiguity or a fallback. Its `actorProfileId` can be null. With `actor_only` and a null actor it produces both IDs null. This is a source observation, not a demonstrated exploitable path: callers own its preconditions, and native review has not been implemented/qualified here. It shows why returning an assignment-shaped object cannot prove successful review responsibility.

`service.ts:11–44,48–84` accepts source/issue/actor/queue, computes assignment, inserts a task, then links and an event through injected dependencies. The same actor input is used for assignee and creator in this task helper. It does not independently distinguish the person who requested the document, the staff member creating a task and the staff actor eligible to review it. A native source must derive each role intentionally; copying “assign to actor” could otherwise route the work to the wrong person. Sequential inserts are also not proof of atomic source/task recovery.

The types remain contribution-oriented: `types.ts:10–37` enumerates financial/supporting operational issues and linked records such as contribution, staged gift, donor and audit event. No generic ordinary document review binding is established by this type inventory. Q24 does not authorize expanding that union or inventing a new task table now.

## Keep these distinctions explicit in the question

1. **Responsibility, actual actor and authorization differ.** A team may own work, but a human makes and is attributed for the exact decision. A named responsible person does not acquire source access merely by assignment, and other authorized actors do not automatically lose authority merely because a name is present.
2. **An available route is not a queue record.** A permitted team route needs a currently eligible way to act and accountable recovery when none is eligible. Do not silently pick the first matching staff row, broadcast sensitive metadata to all admins or treat `needs_assignment` as success.
3. **Source responsibility is not per-request manual configuration by default.** Existing owner-defined responsibility can resolve automatically through a supported binding; Q24 does not require the founder or recipient to configure every item, choose a reviewer, create Teams or operate Workflow Studio.
4. **Named reviewer does not imply exclusive locks.** B can be meaningful as mandatory personal accountability without silently adding a global claim engine or exclusive capability. If the desired B really requires formal reassignment before another qualified person acts, that stronger consequence must be explicit rather than hidden in “named.”
5. **Choose the level of responsibility without freezing schema.** Use the exact source review work for the request, preserving its genuinely distinct item-level responsibilities where applicable. Do not force one new task per file, a person for every file version or request-wide assignment as a universal evidence boundary.
6. **Reassignment is not re-review or reauthorization.** Preserve exact received evidence, current concerns, past actors, satisfaction and source clocks; current candidate/permission checks still apply at the next decision. Existing source-required re-review or separation of duties is respected, not generalized.
7. **No automatic message or timing follows.** Q20 remains fresh-access notice only; Q23 did not choose review email. Assignment does not select reminder deadlines, response promises, access extensions, escalation or automatic acceptance.
8. **Capability/help must work before activation under either choice.** A can fail through diffusion of responsibility; B can fail through an absent named owner. Both need a truthful explicit unresolved state and an actually capable owned resolution route. A label alone does not satisfy Q23.
