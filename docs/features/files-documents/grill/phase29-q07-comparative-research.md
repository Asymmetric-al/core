# Phase 29 Q07 — duration and anchor for ordinary completed-request access

Researched 20 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. The parent reports Q01–Q06 and their amendments are ratified. Q06 retains a bounded read-only completed view after normal source-confirmed fulfillment; it does not yet choose its duration, start event or renewal policy. **No Q07 answer is selected by this note.**

This is public primary-documentation research and product reasoning. No authenticated comparator account, private request, provider setting, real download, participant journey or accessibility test was exercised. Only this research note was written.

## Recommendation for the next single decision

Propose **a maximum of 30 elapsed days from the one authoritative normal-fulfillment instant for this ordinary completed request**, always capped by any earlier applicable source-authority end and subject to current restrictions. It is a fixed completion-based cap, not 30 days from the recipient's next visit, successful sign-in, notice delivery, preview generation or last download.

This is a reasoned initial product policy, **not a demonstrated industry standard, a legal retention period or a measured optimum**. It gives the selected completed view a useful but finite follow-up purpose. A shorter 14-day cap is a credible privacy-first alternative. Keeping only an already-fixed source end is a credible source-calendar alternative, especially where a real program/event boundary governs access.

An adopted completion cap must be part of the source-authorized policy. It cannot manufacture a grant from a null policy, add 30 days after an earlier permission end, override revocation or silently revive a closed grant. Q06's rule that completion does not incidentally renew authority remains intact.

**Why not make 90 days the recommended default?** Ninety elapsed days from the same event is a coherent finite option and would accommodate a longer delayed return. It also keeps the protected purpose available longer without current evidence that ordinary recipients need that interval. It is not an archive by definition, but the longer ordinary period needs a stronger product-purpose justification than copying a familiar quarter. If Conrad prioritizes a longer period, 90 days can replace B in the choice set; it should not be silently included in A or presented as independently researched demand.

Avoid an option list that offers only arbitrary numbers with no anchor. Thirty days from request creation, upload, fulfillment, first view and last view are different products. In particular, a last-view rule can keep an old grant alive indefinitely through repeated use; a first-view rule can leave an unopened request pending without a finite outer boundary. Neither is selected by Q06 and neither is recommended for this ordinary profile.

## Meaning of the proposed clock

This is a conceptual decision explanation, not an implementation specification:

- **Anchor:** the source's one durable normal-fulfillment outcome for the relevant request/evidence scope. Not a generic mutable task-complete timestamp, client clock, scan, delayed webhook-processing time, projected banner or staff email.
- **Elapsed duration:** a day means 24 elapsed hours for A/B. Thirty days means 720 hours and fourteen means 336 hours; neither means a calendar month, business days or “through the end of that date.” The human page shows the resulting actual cutoff.
- **Effective bound:** a completion cap limits already-authorized readback; it never grants permission by arithmetic. Any earlier applicable source end still limits it, and current privacy/safety/recipient decisions can deny it earlier. Missing permission/policy is not repaired by adding a duration.
- **No sliding:** later viewing, renewed browser proof, link resend, download retry, worker regeneration, staff help or replay of the same fulfillment does not advance the fixed end.
- **No revival:** an already-expired or revoked source grant stays denied. A new source-authorized remediation/grant is a separate explicit act, not a side effect of completion or visiting help.
- **No retention consequence:** the completed view's end does not erase the organization's evidence, discharge a hold, change an independent use or recall copies already saved.

Illustrative arithmetic only: normal fulfillment at **20 October 2026, 15:00 UTC+07:00** gives a 30-day cap of **19 November 2026, 15:00 UTC+07:00**. If applicable source access already ends on 24 October, show that earlier end. If source authority had already ended before completion, the 30-day calculation does not authorize a completed view. The displayed result, not a promise of “30 days remaining,” must reflect those cases.

Do not silently choose what happens when an administrator later changes a policy for already-issued grants, when a source corrects a genuinely erroneous fulfillment fact, or when an accommodation needs fresh authority. Preserve the ratified immutable history/current-source distinction and require explicit source policy for those actions. This question does not authorize a configurable exception builder or a new renewal workflow.

## Recipient and staff experience

At completion, keep the already-selected request page and show a plain, durable line such as **Access ends 19 November 2026 at 3:00pm (UTC+07:00)** when that is the actual current end. Place it with the current outcome and remaining actions. Do not show a countdown as the only explanation or require opening Submission history to find the date.

Before completion, if the proposed policy has not yet produced a fulfillment date, describe the rule rather than inventing a calendar end: for example, **After this request is complete, access lasts up to 30 days. Any earlier access end still applies.** Exact copy remains subject to the source's real limits and ordinary recipient comprehension testing. Where a fixed earlier end is already known, state it directly rather than burying it in a general rule.

An “Access ends” date is the latest currently permitted bound, not a guarantee through earlier restrictions. Avoid “Your files will always be available until…” and avoid implying that every current representation is healthy merely because the end is in the future. The Q06 independent unavailable-file and temporary-outage states still apply.

On return, distinguish the actual cause:

| Current state                                                  | Clear meaning                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Entry link/proof expired but source readback remains permitted | Use Q01's safe re-entry path to the same request; do not tell the person their completed request expired.                            |
| Browser session expired but source access remains              | Reconfirm as required, without enrolling again or restarting the source clock.                                                       |
| Completed-view access actually ended                           | Show only the permitted neutral ended/help state; do not imply their submission failed, was rejected or was deleted.                 |
| One file is currently unavailable or restricted                | Explain only the permitted file state; do not falsely end every independent permitted view or automatically substitute another file. |
| Current policy cannot be resolved                              | A truthful temporary access-verification problem, not a fabricated deadline or unlimited access.                                     |

Staff should be able to explain the relevant source outcome and actual authorized cutoff through their existing permitted tools. “Contact us” is not a promise of extension, an automatic permission-change request or consent to email private attachments. Do not select reminders, automatic expiry emails, extra confirmation screens, a renewal button, first-view activation, offline caching or a fresh account in this duration decision.

## Practical tradeoffs that are hypotheses, not findings

Mobile/field recipients may return later because of intermittent service, shared-device constraints, travel, caregiving or waiting for a suitable device. A longer period could help; it could also delay a necessary follow-up or leave more time in which a compromised account can access permitted material. We have no measured Asym distribution of return times, connectivity gaps, staff response time or support requests to quantify either effect.

Short expiry can shift work to staff if people legitimately need another copy. That does not prove a longer period reduces total support, and it does not justify a staff bypass. Conversely, longer expiry increases the period of continuing authorization without proportionally measuring actual attack probability; a 30-day policy is not “twice as risky” as 14 days.

The simple design should work on a small screen and slow connection without loading all current files to explain expiry. A recipient can read the date before starting a download. The date should remain readable with zoom, keyboard and assistive technology. No selected elapsed-day interval establishes WCAG compliance by itself; Q06's separate assessment of session/content-set timing and source-authorized accommodation still applies.

Before production qualification, validate whether representative ordinary recipients understand the actual end, the difference between sign-in expiry and source access end, and the owned help route. Collect only approved minimal evidence about meaningful completed-view use and ended-access needs; this research does not authorize tracking private filenames, identities or file-content history in general analytics. Revisit an adopted duration only through an explicit product/source decision, not automatic renewal based on activity.

## Suggested wording for the one founder choice

> For an ordinary request that the source has genuinely completed, should the existing permitted read-only view end **up to 30 elapsed days after that completion**, **up to 14 elapsed days after completion**, or **at the finite source end already assigned to the request with no additional completion cap**? In every case, an earlier source restriction wins, returning never restarts the clock, and an expired/revoked grant is not revived.

Recommend A as a clear initial ordinary follow-up policy. Treat B as the strongest shorter-exposure alternative and C as the strongest fixed-purpose-calendar alternative. Explain that no source proves one number correct for every ministry or document type. This is one choice of duration **and its anchor**, while reminders, renewal, specialist exceptions and retention remain separate.
