## Main conclusion

The smallest complete result is a contextual **Use for partner reference** action on exact currently qualified source evidence, followed by current-purpose reading, deliberate replacement, **End reference**, and fresh qualified re-admission when an End was mistaken. The ordinary partner-document-reference source owns those acts and their history. Existing CRM/partner identity and relationships, original request/receipt/acceptance, custody, permissions and coordination remain with their existing owners. A reference is neither another file upload nor a new authoritative agreement register.

Admission must preserve the actual Q21 evidence basis, including multiple supporting files and Q56 assemblies. A source-qualified subset can be used when independently sufficient; it must not be given a fabricated per-file acceptance or expose the entire original request/history. Suppress only equivalent active uses under the actual target/purpose/qualified need/exact adopted basis. Do not impose one current agreement or one reference per partner or originating item. Distinct legitimate versions, periods and needs may coexist under actual source rules.

The most consequential edge is later adverse evidence. A Q60 acceptance correction or source restriction must prevent inappropriate current reliance without rewriting the original acceptance or automatically deleting the admitted use. A genuinely new acceptance is a new adoption basis even for equal bytes. Conversely, correcting a mistaken blocker may restore current eligibility of the same still-admitted basis without reopening an explicitly ended/replaced reference. Reads and reliance must not be collapsed into a single historical accepted flag.

The strongest simpler alternative was A alone: complete collection/history and later independently qualified uses only. It reduces source admission/lifecycle work but does not fulfill the now-selected optional continuing-purpose outcome. The combined answer is coherent with the amendments below; its cost is one narrow owned source lifecycle and qualification, not a new contract-management platform or generic metadata owner.
