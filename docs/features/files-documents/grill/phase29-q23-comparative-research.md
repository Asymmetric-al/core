## The useful remaining decision

**Should ordinary negative review use a recoverable Request changes outcome, or should this native review surface also have a distinct terminal Not accepted outcome?**

Recommend **A — Request changes as the ordinary negative path**, with exact source-owned scope and existing Q03 correction/access rules. Staff can make clear that the submitted item is not yet acceptable and what needs attention in the same request. This provides a usable next step without giving an ordinary file-review control the meaning of application denial, waiver, cancellation or permanent rejection of bytes across other uses.

This does **not** re-ask whether staff may give useful feedback or whether recipients can correct pending submissions. The founder brief already requires a real owner review/negative-result journey, and Q03 permits recipient-initiated correction while the exact unaccepted item remains eligible. Q21/Q22 explicitly left actionable negative-review outcome scope unresolved. Q23's fork is whether to add generic terminal negative finality to the ordinary review surface, not whether to leave recipients guessing.

## Why A is the better ordinary recommendation

The usual correction problem can be handled in one context: staff identify the exact item/member needing work, the participant sees what needs attention and uses the existing qualified correction flow, and staff review the new exact evidence. The source records a real negative review result and next work, so it is not silent pending status. A retains Q03's independent self-correction; users do not have to wait for a staff Request changes action to fix an otherwise-eligible pending mistake.

This is a product rationale, not a frequency claim about ministries. Request changes is useful when there is a legitimate possible correction. It must not promise acceptance after any replacement, imply new access or tell a person to reupload every supporting file. A rendering outage, pending safety result or missing staff authority is not necessarily a defect in the recipient's evidence and must not be labeled a business rejection.

Useful guidance belongs next to the exact item and relevant file/member, in the current authorized request view. It may be concise and may use existing qualified instructions. This question should **not** silently select a mandatory free-text essay, reason-code taxonomy, minimum character count, template library, per-file checkbox sequence or second approver. The later selected-answer review can ensure guidance is actionable without bundling those implementation/product choices into the scope fork.

### B's strongest case, without invented evidence

Sometimes a source may have a legitimate final determination that this item will not be accepted for its current purpose and that further routine corrections should not be invited. If that is an ordinary native requirement, a terminal outcome can be clearer and kinder than an endless “needs changes” state implying another upload will resolve it. That is an **illustrative need**, not an observed Asym workload or competitor file-item claim.

B would need exact terminality: **what stops, for which current item/evidence/purpose, and what legitimate next step remains?** A terminal result for an item must not silently become a permanent global rejection of the same bytes, deny the enclosing application or remove the item's requiredness. A required item left terminal-negative still blocks successful request fulfillment unless a separately authorized source action genuinely changes/resolves the requirement. It cannot be dropped from the predicate to make Q22 finish.

If B only means rejecting one candidate while the same ordinary correction path continues without a distinct finality rule, it is not the proposed terminal alternative; it may be another presentation of A. Conversely, if B ends that ordinary path, its reconsideration/replacement/repair and communication must be source-qualified before activation. Do not promise a generic Undo, automatic replacement request, new recipient, waiver, fresh grant or duplicate-request workaround.

A also needs honest treatment of non-remediable cases: use the actual existing owner disposition/help where qualified, rather than always presenting a misleading Request changes button. Preserving specialist terminal outcomes does not add them to the generic ordinary surface. If no valid next path exists for a required ordinary case, that is an explicit owner/product gap for later qualification, not a reason to manufacture completion.

## Practical same-context example

**Illustrative only:** one requested item has two supporting files; staff find that one is incomplete. A source-owned Request changes result can identify the relevant member and describe the missing content. The recipient sees the item needs attention and, while authorized, sends a new immutable correction for that target through Q03. The other supporting file stays intact. The received correction has its own technical checks and review; it is not automatically accepted. The request cannot fulfill while its real required condition is unsatisfied.

Under B, staff additionally have a distinct final-negative decision when that is the real source intent, rather than using Request changes. The recipient must be able to distinguish “there is an available correction path” from “this item will not be accepted under this decision; follow this qualified next step.” That clarity cannot be supplied by two similar red labels with unclear authority and consequences.

No exact staff-reason requirement, notification default, reviewer routing, bulk operation or mandatory whole-request lock is selected by this example. No instruction is sent to a real recipient. The actual meaning of whether/how an earlier change request is resolved by a corrected submission, source reassessment or another legitimate outcome must be bound by the owner during the selected-answer review; a stale note cannot automatically reject unseen successor bytes or silently remain an unrelated permanent block.

## Important user-flow constraints to carry into either selected-answer review

1. **Exact source scope:** the command targets the current still-unaccepted item and relevant inspected evidence/purpose, not a filename/latest pointer or all items in the request. Multi-file items need specific useful guidance without resetting unaffected evidence.
2. **Meaning, not a comment side effect:** ordinary comments, file checks and message sends do not independently create negative business decisions. The source records the selected outcome and its attributable basis. Conversely, a meaningful recorded outcome must be visible through the correct permitted task/request projection rather than only a transient toast.
3. **Q03 remains independent:** a recipient may still correct eligible pending mistakes without waiting for staff; Request changes does not grant mutation authority, reopen accepted/completed items, admit a replacement or reset an access clock.
4. **A real next step:** show what needs attention and the current permitted action. If access has ended or the issue cannot be fixed through this ordinary path, do not promise Upload now or an automatic regrant. Use the existing source-qualified help/disposition path without exposing protected status through an expired link.
5. **Receipt is still true:** an item needing changes was received. Do not erase its receipt, call it never uploaded or rewrite original timeliness. Later work uses its own applicable source target under Q09; source/staff delay is not retroactive recipient lateness.
6. **Change and acceptance races:** source ordering governs Request changes versus acceptance, recipient correction and fulfillment. No message composed against an old version may retarget a successor silently. A lost response is reconciled as the same operation; no duplicate negative effect or inferred new email.
7. **Private actionable guidance:** feedback must obey current disclosure, including sensitive record names and reasons. Preserve untrusted-content handling and authorized text semantics. An internal note is not automatically safe participant copy, and a document's own “approved/rejected” wording is not app authority.
8. **Accessible calm interaction:** one meaningful action in the existing context, clear persistent text/next step, keyboard/AT/mobile/reflow, safe retained input where authorized and no color-only/error-only semantics. Distinguish technical validation from review judgment. Actual composed content/AT/user proof remains required; source manuals are not that proof.

These are inherited or necessary review topics, not the full22-category package. Exact business effects, requiredness, capability, stale-outcome resolution and admission/closure contracts must be qualified under the native source/P12/P9/custody/P6/P17 owners. A flexible interface cannot substitute for those meanings.

## Proposed one-question framing

**For ordinary item reviews that cannot be accepted, should staff use Request changes, or should they also be able to make a terminal Not accepted decision?**

- **A — Use recoverable Request changes (recommended).** Keep a clear same-request correction path for eligible unaccepted work. Terminal application/source decisions remain with their specific owners; do not mislead people into endless reupload when another disposition is needed.
- **B — Also add terminal Not accepted.** Support an explicit final negative item decision as well as Request changes, with distinct authority, effect on ordinary correction and an honest qualified next step. It cannot waive the item, fulfill the request or deny the larger application by implication.

The plain-language consequence of B must be included in the founder question; the word terminal alone is insufficient. Neither option selects a whole rejection taxonomy or reason/notification/routing policy. No third manual-follow-up-only strawman, universal staff-role mapping or configurable workflow is necessary.

## Recommendation and limits

Recommend **A for the ordinary native surface**, while preserving genuine existing specialist final decisions and explicit help/disposition for problems that cannot be corrected here. Direct file-request evidence supports recoverable revision, and the larger-scope decline examples show why negative finality must remain purpose-bound. B is not inherently poor UX; it is a consequential new ordinary capability whose exact demand and repair semantics are unmeasured.
