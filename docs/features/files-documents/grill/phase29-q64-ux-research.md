## Suggested question and strongest alternatives

**Which real ordinary document-request workflow should anchor the first retention-policy profile?**

| Route                                                        | Answer needed now                                                                                                                                          | Benefit                                                                                                                                     | Cost and limit                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **A — One representative workflow (recommended when known)** | Its ordinary name and what staff need its documents to do or decide. An existing approved policy reference is useful if known, but not required to answer. | Gives the next investigation a concrete actor, outcome and lifecycle without a large questionnaire.                                         | One case can miss other purposes. It is a reference case, not a universal policy or an exclusion of other intended launch workflows. |
| **B — A short inventory of intended purposes**               | A brief list of real intended ordinary requests and why each is needed, when choosing one would be a guess.                                                | Exposes material differences before selecting a representative case. It is the stronger route when scope is genuinely diverse or uncertain. | More initial context; keep it bounded. No complete ministry taxonomy, records audit submission or new catalog product is required.   |

The response can be **“A — [workflow], so staff can [outcome]”** or **“B — [brief purpose list]”**. An answer of “A” alone does not establish the missing workflow. “No approved schedule yet” and “I do not know whether one exists” are distinct, valid states; neither means retain forever or delete after an invented interval. A policy title or reference is a lead to reconcile, not proof of current authority.

**Illustration only:** Elena might request an equipment inventory so staff can plan a particular activity. That would identify a possible purpose to investigate; it would not show that any ministry uses that workflow, that the records are legally ordinary, that the same policy covers every inventory, or that they should be retained for a particular duration. The actual answer must come from real intended work. Avoid offering this example as a preselected factual answer.

The candidate is appropriately small. The founder should not have to name a database table, authenticate a provider, supply private people/documents, choose a legal period, or describe every exception. Existing discoverable evidence remains the agent's responsibility. If the named use is an expense, official document, care record, application or another specialist workflow, route it to its actual owner rather than making it ordinary because it involves a file.

## Seven founder checks

### What could go wrong with this answer?

A convenient illustration could become a false universal policy. Preserve real purpose, actual owner and explicit applicability; keep other launch uses in scope for reconciliation.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

The first actual ordinary use and its representativeness are not known. An approved schedule may be absent. A is conditional; B permits the uncertainty to be resolved without pretending a sample exists.

### How does this affect the whole product?

It supplies missing policy context while preserving specialist ownership, public Media rules, copy/reuse distinctions and the required legacy/native work. It does not create a new policy engine.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

The answer is a short description of real work. Later adopted request/review/records language can be checked against that use. Participants do not gain a records-management form and staff do not gain another routine approval step.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

The cited primary guidance supports explicit needs, purpose and policy evidence before configuration. The conditional recommendation is our judgment; no universal nonprofit consensus or actual Asym user validation is claimed.

### Does this fit Asym’s existing repo and product direction?

Yes as a context question addressing R-POL. It retains Q62/Q63, owner-specific clocks/powers and shared custody boundaries, rather than turning a missing schedule into a feature default.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

Recommend A only when a real representative workflow is available; otherwise B. After the actual answer, surface any genuinely unresolved source-policy decision with its evidence. Do not fabricate a policy or architectural decision merely to keep question numbering moving.
