## One decision

**When an issued, unfinished ordinary Document Request needs to go to a different person, should staff use a guided linked replacement request, or change the recipient within the same eligible request?**

| Option                                                         | Practical experience                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Benefits                                                                                                                                                                                               | Costs and boundaries                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Guided linked replacement request (recommended)**        | From the original request, staff prepare a new request for the new person, reusing selected, reviewed, safe item definitions and instructions. The original and replacement remain linked for authorized staff. A qualified handoff ends the original outstanding request/recipient authority and issues the new request; preparing a draft alone does not do either. | One consistent correction path; clear attribution and access boundaries; earlier submissions, decisions and conversation remain with their original context. Staff do not rebuild everything manually. | Two request records must be presented coherently. Safe definition reuse, current readiness and a recoverable handoff are real requirements; a linked draft is not proof that the old request stopped or the new one was sent.                                                |
| **B — Change recipient on the same narrowly eligible request** | Staff use a deliberate versioned recipient successor where the source can prove the request has no ever-admitted contribution (participant-origin or staff-supplied), adopted evidence or participant clarification to transfer. Other cases use the guided replacement path.                                                                                         | Preserves the same request reference for a genuinely untouched mistaken-recipient case and can reduce a visible extra record.                                                                          | Adds an eligibility branch, a new authority revision and explicit access-clock/control semantics. No currently visible files is not proof that the request was never responded to. It must not transfer history, reset Q48 protections or silently mutate a recipient field. |

**Recommendation: A.** Preserve continuity through the requesting record and a clear authorized relationship between the requests, while keeping each person's evidence and access attributable. The guided reuse step captures most of B's convenience without maintaining two different correction mechanisms. This is a product judgment grounded in Core's boundaries, not a claim that all comparable systems choose replacement or that the workflow has been tested with Asym staff.

Illustration only: Jordan sent an ordinary request to Elena, then learns that Marcus is the person who should provide the material. Under A, Jordan chooses the still-appropriate item definitions for Marcus, reviews their instructions and recipient details, and completes the qualified handoff. Elena's prior submissions and discussion do not become Marcus's history. If Elena has already sent useful material, it remains correctly attributed and available only through independently authorized source operations. This is a scenario to test, not an asserted ministry practice.

## Why this question is next

The founder's original brief explicitly requires recipient-change recovery in section 10. Q01 already establishes source ownership, versioned correction, obsolete-proof/access fences and preserved attribution. It does not settle whether an ordinary change to a different person continues the same issued request or creates a linked replacement. Q13/Q15 continuation is for the same intended recipient; Q36 additions are for the same purpose and recipient. Those actions cannot be reused as a recipient-retarget shortcut.

Q48 has just resolved reminder initiation and bounded controls. Recipient correction now needs a clear relationship to those controls and to outstanding work. Other candidates were checked: Q29 already decides paired version reading; Q45 decides exact-version staff comments and locations; the founder already requires identity-preserving rename. Those settled capabilities should not be asked again. Staff comment attention and requested-date change awareness remain separate potential decisions, not additions to Q49.

This question is explicitly about a **different intended person**. A typo or changed mailbox for the same actual recipient, lost-inbox recovery, authorized representation, adding a collaborator and moving application ownership are different operations. Q01's existing source-owned same-person recovery remains required; mailbox/name similarity or a staff checkbox cannot prove person identity. This question neither requires creating a new request for every address correction nor grants an unqualified address-edit shortcut.

## Initial adversarial check

### What could go wrong with this answer?

A can become a disguised clone-and-send that copies private instructions, loses receipt history or leaves both recipients active. B can treat current emptiness as never-responded and transfer identity/history. Both require explicit source operations and coherent results; the question does not declare either implemented.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

That a different intended person is legitimately eligible for the requesting source and that some definitions can be safely reused. These must be checked, not inferred from a staff-entered address. Actual Asym frequency and usability benefit remain unmeasured. Same-person recovery, representation and ownership transfer remain distinct.

### How does this affect the whole product?

The source retains meaning, evidence and fulfillment. P12/P3 retain permission/contact boundaries; P17/P6 retain communication. Q39 reusable definitions, Q34 cancellation and Q48 reminders are dependencies with specific semantics, not a generic cloning/transfer authority. Public pages, donors' financial identity, signatures and specialist records are outside the choice.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

A should present one guided correction from the source request and coherent linked staff history. It avoids mandatory retyping and hides no active-access side effects. The new person sees only their own authorized request. Old and new states, original versus replacement work, and source success versus delivery must be easy to distinguish.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

There is no universal pattern: primary documentation supports both bounded recipient replacement and cancel/new-request flows, while broader ownership transfer has materially different disclosure. A is recommended for Core's ordinary evidence boundary; B remains a credible convenience alternative for an explicitly proved narrow cohort. No vendor role, authentication, deletion or notification rule is imported unchanged.

### Does this fit Asym's existing repo and product direction?

Yes as a proposed source-owned recovery shape with explicit adoption and proof. Q01 already requires correction; Q13/Q15/Q36 preserve same-recipient continuation; Q21/Q22/Q34 preserve evidence and terminal truth. Current nearby Auth/settings helpers do not implement or authorize this ordinary operation. Whole Workflow Studio and a new permission system are unnecessary.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

Recommend **A — Guided linked replacement request** for a different intended person. Retain reviewed definition reuse and an efficient handoff so this is a complete staff recovery flow. Do not add the pristine same-request branch unless the founder values that continuity enough to accept its extra eligibility and lifecycle model. The answer remains the founder's decision.
