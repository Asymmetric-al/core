## Historical question and alternatives

**After a fresh completed-read period ordinarily expires, may authorized staff
approve another period for the same recipient and completed-request context,
or should the first fresh period be the final one?**

Q08 already permits the first qualified fresh admission. Q16 establishes both
eligible completed origins and their exact historical ceiling; Q17/Q18 fix
each new source-issued period at up to 720 elapsed hours, with earlier limits
preserved. Q15's unfinished-access repetition rule does not decide this lane.

The count, cumulative meaning and nonoverlap above are explicit proposed Q19
terms, not existing defaults inferred from Q15. No arbitrary third option such
as two renewals, a lifetime-day budget or escalating approval tiers is supported
by the evidence gathered. A is a policy choice to allow repeated consideration;
B is a policy choice that the first fresh opportunity is final.

For B, an attempt confirmed to have failed or rolled back before source
admission does not consume the opportunity. Failure after admission does not
restore it; unknown commitment requires reconciliation. Recovering the same
already-admitted effect does not count as another issuance.

## Concrete example

Illustrative scenario, not a claimed observed ministry workflow: Maya is the
intended recipient of a completed request. Its ordinary completed-view period
ended; staff later approved a fresh read-only period, which has now also ended
normally. Maya later needs to view an eligible submitted original again.

Under A, a currently authorized staff actor can review whether that same
original purpose and exact scope still justify access. If admitted, the source
issues another finite period and preserves both old periods unchanged. Under B,
this completed context has used its one fresh opportunity, so it cannot issue
another. Staff may help only through independently permitted work; they cannot
clone the request or email an ungoverned attachment to evade the rule.

If Maya's current grant is still valid but her sign-in proof or preview is
broken, neither option calls for a new grant: repair the existing authorized
path. If the purpose has ended, the material was disposed of or a genuine
restriction prevents access, repetition is not a workaround.

## Research and recommendation

**Recommend A.** Let the source consider a real later need within the original
completed context, under today's authority, rather than deny consideration
solely because one fresh period was previously issued. This is a continuity
and governance judgment, not a measured ministry demand or an industry-wide
best-practice repeat count. A's potentially long cumulative access is an
acknowledged cost; thirty days per grant is not a total lifetime bound.

The strongest case for B is genuine finality: if this later opportunity should
be the last one, say so and enforce it. Its cost must also be honest: no hidden
clone, attachment fallback or assurance that staff can disclose the file elsewhere.

## Keep the journey simple

Use the existing completed request and **Grant read access** action. Show
authorized staff the relevant prior outcome, current eligible purpose/scope
and the standard timing terms; show the actual admitted end after success.
Previously narrow grants do not automatically become permanent scope ceilings,
and previously broad grants do not establish current permission.

For the recipient, preserve the completed read-only context and truthful
access state. Where current disclosure permits, explain that read-only access
has ended and provide the existing help route. A technical failure, an expired
proof, an unknown issuance result and expired business access need distinct
recovery. Do not tell someone to request another period when an existing one
is usable or its outcome still needs reconciliation.

An approval is not proof that a message was delivered, that current recipient
proof is satisfied or that every file is usable. Keep those states separate.
Preserve localized actual dates/zones, accessible status/focus, keyboard/touch
and low-bandwidth recovery. No new account, quota editor, duration picker,
mandatory essay, routine second approval, renewal dashboard or automatic
message/reminder is required. This question does not add a recipient request
queue or promise renewal.

## Boundaries both options preserve

- Each issuance requires exact current source/P12 administrative scope and
  the live assignable ceiling. Each use requires current recipient authority,
  proof and purpose, including the applicable continuing grantor/delegation
  dependency. Do not infer automatic revocation on every issuer role change.
  An old expired row is insufficient when newer or adverse history controls
  the same lineage.
- Q16's exact original completed member/submitted-original-version/action/
  permitted-context ceiling remains authoritative for both origins. Do not
  union past grants, admit later files or silently ratchet to the most recent
  narrow subset. Real binding restrictions remain.
- Another period requires a new deliberate source effect. Recover a matching
  committed intent on its original terms, even if newer periods exist. An
  uncommitted stale approval cannot silently move to a later predecessor;
  unknown commitment requires reconciliation. Concurrent approvals must obey
  whichever count/nonoverlap policy is selected at the source mutation boundary.
- Every admitted period retains Q18's exact interval, actual earlier-limited
  end, unused behavior, source precision and current metadata/byte-serving
  checks. No reset of the ended grant or first-visit start.
- Ordinary expiry does not erase revocation, shortening, purpose/content-use
  limits, disposal or other continuing prohibitions. Preserve their actual
  scope and source-owned remediation; waiting is not a bypass.
- Completion, receipts, accepted/frozen evidence, correction lineage,
  immutable originals, retention/holds/disposal and independent rights do not
  reset. No upload/correction reopening, broader participant scope or personal
  drive is created. A genuine independent source purpose uses its own contract.
