# Phase 29 Q07 — honest and usable completed-view expiry

Researched 20 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok. The parent reports Conrad selected **A: up to 30 elapsed days from authoritative normal fulfillment, with all earlier binding source limits preserved**. Q01–Q06 and their amendments remain ratified. This note proposes adversarial refinements for Q07; it does not itself ratify them or establish working software.

This is public primary-documentation research and product reasoning. No private account, real applicant/request, live permission, file download, browser/assistive-technology journey or participant study was exercised. One synthetic time calculation was checked using the local platform's timezone data; that is arithmetic evidence, not a Core test. Only this research note was written.

## Recommendation

Keep the selected cap. Present the **actual current access end**, not an advertising promise of a new month. Use the same completed request, with current permitted outcome, eligible own-file actions, concise Q05 history and one real help route. A return visit, sign-in, delayed notice or technical retry never restarts the source clock.

The most consequential UX amendment is that a cohort can honor the cap and still offer little or no post-completion access because an earlier limit wins. That must be exposed during source-cohort qualification and in the actual recipient state. It is not a reason to weaken the earlier limit. Nor should the product claim that the selected 30-day policy gave that person 30 days.

Keep reassurance factual and brief. Access ending does not mean the recipient's submission failed, but it also does not authorize disclosure of protected completion details after their access ends. Do not add repeated warning dialogs, forced downloads, a new portal, reminder campaigns, a renewal button, an exception-policy builder or a “download everything” step.

## The cap can be non-binding — make that visible

Treat the 30-day rule as a maximum within existing source authority:

| Source situation                                                   | Actual recipient consequence                                                      | Required product response                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Earlier binding end is more than 30 elapsed days after fulfillment | The completion cap can determine the end.                                         | Show the cap's resolved date/time, subject to current restrictions.                                                                                                                                                         |
| Earlier end is one day after fulfillment                           | Only that one day remains; A and B would behave the same for this request.        | Show the one-day actual cutoff, not “30 days remaining” or a promise to restore the rest.                                                                                                                                   |
| Source authority ends at or before fulfillment                     | No completed-view access opens from the cap.                                      | Preserve source history but expose only the currently permitted return/help projection. Do not issue a new grant by arithmetic.                                                                                             |
| A source always ends access at completion                          | Its existing policy prevents the selected completed-view benefit for that cohort. | Report the conflict to its source/product owner before claiming the cohort delivers Q06/Q07. A separate explicit source-policy decision is needed if that purpose should support readback; do not silently relax the limit. |
| Existing end or policy meaning is unknown                          | No safe end can be promised and no permission follows from the number 30.         | Keep the affected protected operation unavailable until its owner resolves the authority. Unknown is not infinity and not evidence of expiry.                                                                               |

No minimum number of guaranteed post-completion days is invented. A legitimate earlier restriction is not a product bug merely because it shortens access. The failure is hiding the effect, claiming an unavailable benefit or introducing a silent extension to make the screen look consistent.

## Time and delayed completion

The actual source fulfillment and the time a person learns about it can differ:

- If a source outcome was durably committed on 20 October but the completed projection arrives on 25 October, the original cap has already been running. Show the actual remaining access; do not shift the anchor to projection, email or first visit.
- If the source has not actually completed its work until 25 October, do not invent a 20 October fulfillment from an upload date or generic task status. The source's qualified outcome controls.
- If a replay, cache repair or retry delivers the same outcome again, the current completion label and access end must not jump forward.
- If access has already ended when a delayed message is opened, do not promise a usable completed view or imply the recipient failed to act. Show the allowed ended/help state. Q07 does not authorize a replacement message, automatic notice or a renewed grant.
- A source correction to an erroneous outcome needs its existing explicit owner procedure. Do not silently rewrite the historical anchor to repair an inconvenient deadline.

Thirty elapsed days means 720 hours. The display may cross a DST transition and show a different local hour. A synthetic calculation checked locally gives:

| Same event represented for the recipient | Instant                             |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Fulfillment in New York                  | 20 October 2026, 10:00am, UTC−04:00 |
| Fulfillment in UTC                       | 20 October 2026, 14:00 UTC          |
| Plus 720 elapsed hours                   | 19 November 2026, 14:00 UTC         |
| That end displayed in New York           | 19 November 2026, 9:00am, UTC−05:00 |

This is an illustrative arithmetic check, not a tested production clock or a reason to explain DST internals to every recipient. The page should show the correct resulting local date/time and identifiable zone/offset. Use the relevant instant's timezone rules, not today's offset pasted onto a future date. Travel or changing display timezone may change the human rendering but cannot move the stored permission end.

Avoid a bare ambiguous numeric date, a zone abbreviation such as CST without enough context, or “midnight” with no clear date boundary. Locale controls readable formatting; it does not choose authority. A raw IANA identifier need not be the only human label. At exact cutoffs, use sufficient visible precision not to imply a later end; do not round the cutoff forward. Seconds can be exposed where necessary without creating a constantly ticking display.

Use factual access-end language rather than putting “may be withdrawn at any time” legalistic warnings beside every file. A visible date is a current upper bound; the current state and independent file qualification remain authoritative. If policy changes or a safety restriction occurs, update the actual permitted state and avoid stale guaranteed-availability copy.

Retention is ordinarily not the main task on this page. Do not announce that the organization deleted the file merely because request access ended, or that it will keep a record forever. Explain retention only through accurate source-approved contextual information or the owned help route when needed. Q07 is not a records-retention decision.

## Accessibility, mobile, shared devices and poor connectivity

Use Core's existing Base UI/base-maia structure and controls. Put the outcome, actual end and next action in ordinary page content. Do not hide them in a hover tooltip, collapsed historical event, icon color, transient toast or narrow desktop-only table. Reflow long translated dates, timezone labels and filenames without obscuring the end or the help action.

Keyboard, touch, speech and screen-reader users must be able to find View, Download submitted file, sign-out and the real help action using meaningful names. Repeated file actions need the same safe contextual disambiguation already selected in Q04. No new tiny clock-icon interaction is needed to reveal the essential date.

When a meaningful current state changes, make its status available to assistive technology. Do not interrupt reading with a new announcement every second, every poll or every re-render. If a restriction removes the focused action or private view, move focus only as needed to a meaningful safe status/recovery target; do not leave focus on a removed node or repeatedly drag it to the top.

The source cap does not settle timing accessibility. Apply the actual WCAG 2.2.1 mechanism/exception to each content-set limit. The long cap may have an applicable exception, but a shorter binding source end, session timeout, entry expiry or timed interaction needs its own assessment. Do not claim every such timer qualifies because the profile is called “30 days.” Reauthentication alone is not compliance proof, and an accommodation cannot silently revive denied source authority. AAA reauthentication/data-loss guidance should not be presented as a mandatory AA renewal feature.

Keep Q01 entry usable with supported paste/autofill and the source-required assurance. Do not increase repeated manual code entry on each file merely because the request is complete. A lost network response should produce bounded truthful retry/recovery, not a demand to create another account.

For intermittent connections, load the essential authorized outcome/end before optional heavy previews, avoid all-file prefetch and keep retries explicit and scoped. Offline state is not evidence of permission loss; conversely an old cached page is not permission to keep protected actions active. Do not solve connectivity by adding an unselected private offline archive or by moving the cap to the next successful visit.

On a shared device, provide the existing explicit sign-out path and actual server invalidation. Browser close is not a trustworthy substitute. Where useful in the established download context, a brief note can explain that downloaded copies may remain on that device after sign-out; it should not become a repeated permission dialog or claim every click saved a file. Clearing app state cannot guarantee erasure of screenshots, browser-managed downloads or previously seen information.

Benefits for delayed field/mobile returns remain hypotheses. No recipient interview, network study or support-volume evidence was gathered. Do not describe a particular ministry's travel, staffing, device access or connectivity pattern as established fact.

## Future acceptance outcomes

These are proposed verification obligations, **not tests passed** in this research:

1. A completion-cap case and a materially earlier-source-end case display their actual different cutoff; neither says a fresh full period is guaranteed.
2. A source cohort that always ends at/before completion is identified as offering no completed readback under those limits. No code or copy silently extends it.
3. Late projection, notification, replay and first return preserve the original end. The page handles an already-ended window without false failure, blame or automatic renewal.
4. The selected elapsed-hour arithmetic matches exact before/at/after enforcement across DST, non-hour offsets and display-zone changes. Human labels agree with the same instant.
5. Active-session, expired-session, expired-entry-token, ended-source, restricted-file and temporary-authority-failure cases produce distinct truthful recovery where disclosure permits.
6. Bookmarked return uses current source authority. Before proof or after denial, private metadata cannot be inferred from a descriptive error or retained content.
7. Actual keyboard, speech, screen-reader, touch, small-screen, zoom/reflow, long-name and translated-date journeys expose the main date and controls without hover or color dependence.
8. Meaningful expiry/restriction changes announce an appropriate status; repeated fetches do not chatter and removed controls do not strand focus.
9. Each content-set timer has its applicable timing/accessibility assessment. Q01 supported paste/autofill and safe re-entry work without accidentally renewing purpose access.
10. Poor network and interrupted downloads retain honest outcomes and bounded current-authority recovery. Partial/local copies are not falsely reported saved or erased.
11. Sign-out/context switch/back navigation/late responses do not restore private completed content under the wrong principal. Browser close is not the only invalidation mechanism.
12. The help route has a responsible existing source owner and gives a truthful permitted outcome without implicit renewal, old-file release, extra membership or an unselected email.
13. Representative recipients can explain the actual cutoff, the difference between needing to sign in again and ended access, and the next help action. Report sample and untested populations honestly.

## Remaining decisions and evidence

This review does not select renewal/extension, reminders, notification lead times, a new policy builder, active-request lifetime, specialist override rules, retention, or a minimum guaranteed period. Q01–Q06 current authority and exact-version/history boundaries remain unchanged.

No additional arbitrary numeric threshold is needed to state the UX contract. The selected 30 × 24-hour cap is explicit; actual response-time budgets, timing mechanisms and source-cohort policy exceptions belong in their existing authorized contracts. A confirmed false deadline, permission revival, private end-state leak or blocking supported accessibility failure is a defect to repair, not a normal rate to tolerate until an invented percentage is reached.

The practical unknown is whether ordinary users need more or less time than the selected cap and how often earlier source limits make it non-binding. That requires real, minimal, authorized evidence after the design is qualified. Public documentation cannot resolve it, and lack of evidence does not authorize a sliding or indefinite fallback.
