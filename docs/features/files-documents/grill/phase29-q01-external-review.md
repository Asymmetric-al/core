# Phase 29 Q01: external review of confirmed-recipient private requests

Inspected 2026-10-20, Asia/Bangkok. Research and proposed clarification of Q01A only; not a new founder decision, implementation, or specification. The parent reports the founder selected confirmation of the intended recipient for every private request. No real invitations, codes, emails, signups, uploads, permission changes, or purchases were performed. Sources are current official documentation and publicly readable pages; none constitutes a logged-in product test.

## Assessment

Keep the founder's universal rule that a private request is for a confirmed recipient, with a narrow request experience rather than mandatory full-platform onboarding. Correct the meaning of confirmation: proving access to the invited email address is evidence of mailbox control, not verified civil identity, sole mailbox ownership, source-domain authority, approval of a submission, or multifactor/phishing-resistant authentication.

The invitation address must be bound to the source-authorized recipient and request. A signed-in unrelated user is not sufficient. A source requiring stronger or fresher authentication keeps that requirement. “Private” describes disclosure, not a single assurance level for passports, expense evidence, routine resources, and signatures alike. Whether email control alone is adequate for each family is a material product/security determination, not a hidden implementation default.

## Standards and assurance interpretation

[NIST SP800-63B-4](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/63/b/4/final), final2025, [§3.1.3](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/#out-of-band-devices), prohibits email as out-of-band authentication. It explicitly distinguishes address-confirmation/recovery codes from authentication. That exception does not make a code used to authorize private-document access a conforming authenticator just by calling it confirmation. Do not claim NIST AAL, MFA, or identity proofing for the proposed email-only route. This standard is assurance guidance in its stated context, not a universal legal ban on commercial email login. Different assurance needs require deliberate policy.

[OWASP MFA guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Multifactor_Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html), living source: email confirmation is easy and requires no extra device, but relies on mailbox security and remains phishable. Receiving an invitation and then a code in the same mailbox does not supply two independent factors.

[OWASP Authentication](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html), living source: generic authentication responses must include consistent HTTP/error/timing behavior; throttling, safe session handling, reauthentication, and controlled email changes matter. Identity proofing and authenticating a digital identity differ. IP/device characteristics are risk signals rather than independent authority.

[OWASP Recovery](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Forgot_Password_Cheat_Sheet.html), living source: cryptographically random, securely stored, expiring single-use tokens; submission throttles to prevent inbox flooding; uniform responses against enumeration. Recovery after compromise must not rely solely on newly changed recovery data. Applying these principles to request access is an engineering inference, not a claim that password-reset behavior should become the document workflow.

[W3C WCAG2.2 SC3.3.8 AA](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/accessible-authentication-minimum.html), living Understanding document: do not force manual transcription; support whole-code paste and user-agent autofill. Multi-box OTP controls that paste only one digit fail the intended accessible path unless another suitable method exists. The journey also needs named fields, visible focus, recoverable errors, and testing with assistive technology. No runtime accessibility test was performed.

## Corrected decision wording for parent review

Suggested wording preserves Q01A while removing overclaims:

Every private document request requires access bound to the source-authorized recipient before private request details or permitted document actions are exposed. The recipient gets a limited request experience and is not forced through full-platform signup merely to respond. An already appropriate session may satisfy confirmation. An emailed challenge can establish control of the invited address where the source permits that assurance; it does not prove the human's identity or representative authority, and it cannot weaken a stronger source requirement. Sharing or forwarding a link alone never authorizes a different recipient. Address corrections, helpers, recovery, and restricted cases follow their accountable source/identity rules with explicit attribution.

If the founder understood “confirmed recipient” to mean verified natural-person identity in all cases, email-only confirmation is materially weaker and needs correction before ratification. Likewise, automatic access for shared-inbox members, blanket exclusion of helpers, or adding mandatory government-ID checks would be material changes. They must not be smuggled in as minor safeguards.

## Coverage limits

No inspected competitor proves the whole proposed Asym contract. Sharing, uploading, signing, donor self-service, and authentication are different product purposes. No authentication integration, recovery process, threat simulation, mail delivery, customer account, or assurance certification was tested. The proposed controls still need reconciliation with Core's Phase3/4/6/17 identity/communication owners and each consuming source. Provider choice, final numeric limits, exact restricted categories, and representative eligibility are not resolved by this external research.
