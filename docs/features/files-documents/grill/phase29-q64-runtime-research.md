## Recommendation and strongest alternative

**Recommend A: name one real, representative ordinary document-request workflow intended for launch, and explain what staff need the records for.** Start with the actual business work, not a retention number. A concise description of who asks whom for what, why the material is needed, what staff do when collection finishes and any already-known policy owner is enough to begin. The user need not supply API symbols, a database design, legal conclusions, sensitive example files or a complete retention schedule.

**B: give a short inventory of intended ordinary purposes first when no single representative workflow is established.** Compare those actual purposes and select a justified reference case before drafting a policy profile. This is preferable to treating a convenient invented example as representative. It does not require a new catalog product, exhaustive organization audit or approval to continue already-authorized research.

This is a request for missing business context. It is **not** another vote between consolidation and prototyping, automatic/default assignment and repeated confirmation, automatic and manual disposal, safe and unsafe retention, or migration and deletion. Q61, Q62 and Q63 already settle those relevant interaction choices. Neither A nor B approves a schedule, legal period, trigger, hold power or supported launch cohort. Both preserve all-source coverage and the actual R-POL/R-LEGACY qualification work.

The practical advantage of A is an end-to-end concrete reference: separate actual source decisions, records, clocks, custody copies and repair needs against one understandable workflow. The risk is overgeneralizing from it; the profile must state its applicability and exclusions. B costs an additional short clarification but avoids that error when the use mix is genuinely unknown. A known representative example need not mean all organizations, jurisdictions or document types share one policy.
