## The practical question

A staff member prepares a new ordinary document request. Its source purpose already has an approved applicable records policy: the rules governing what evidence is retained, the event that starts each relevant period, and the applicable limits. Should Asym apply the approved policy and explain it in the normal review, or require staff to explicitly confirm or select it for every request?

The actual policy remains the responsible source's decision under either option. Staff are not being asked to invent a period or provide a legal attestation. The question concerns a request with a qualified policy; it does not assume that the missing ordinary policies in R-POL-ordinary have already been approved.

| Option                                                                              | What staff experience                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Strongest benefit                                                                                                                            | Tradeoff                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Apply the approved policy automatically, with a clear summary (recommended)** | The current source resolves its approved default. Staff see the policy, safe scope and actual start meaning beside the ordinary request review and use the normal Issue action. Only genuinely approved, compatible, currently authorized alternatives are offered. A source-required individual choice is still explicit. | Consistent routine application without repeatedly asking staff to decide a settled records rule. The policy remains visible and inspectable. | A wrong purpose or default could be overlooked. Actual source applicability, a meaningful summary, current validation and capable repair remain essential; the visible name alone proves nothing. |
| **B — Require explicit confirmation or selection for each request**                 | Staff deliberately apply the compatible approved policy before each new request is issued. With one lawful policy, this is confirmation; with several authorized choices, it is selection. The step can live within the existing review form.                                                                              | Makes the policy decision more prominent on every request, which can help when staff must distinguish materially different contexts.         | Adds repeated interaction even when there is only one valid result. Habitual confirmation is possible, and a click cannot establish legal or source correctness.                                  |

**Recommendation: A.** Let the responsible source determine the routine rule and show its consequence clearly. Ask staff for a real missing fact or authorized choice when one is needed. Do not disguise unresolved classification as a preselected answer, and do not require an extra policy checkbox merely to repeat a settled fact. B remains defensible if deliberate policy application on every request is the desired operational experience; it grants no additional authority.

**One decision:** For eligible new ordinary requests, should Asym use **A, a visible approved default with genuine exceptions**, or **B, explicit policy application on every request**?

## Smallest complete user journey

1. Staff enter the existing source context and request details. If a real source-required purpose fact is missing and the actor can provide it, ask for that fact; do not add a speculative classification questionnaire.
2. Show a concise **Retention policy** summary in the normal review: the actual approved name, safe covered purpose/evidence and source-defined start meaning. Keep **Access ends** separate. A policy containing several record classes must not display a false single deletion date.
3. Offer **View retention details** when useful. Offer **Change retention policy** only for actual compatible alternatives the actor may choose. If the source explicitly requires an authorized individual choice, prompt for that choice under A as well. Otherwise no extra confirmation is needed under A.
4. A meaningful change to the reviewed purpose or policy is explained before the final action, retaining the draft. An identical refresh does not create a needless reconfirmation. Opening details, editing, blur, IME composition and Cancel do not issue, send or delete anything.
5. The normal deliberate Issue action uses current authoritative facts. If its response is lost, recover the original operation and result; do not issue again to clear an uncertain screen.
6. If there is no qualified applicable policy, or it cannot currently be checked, preserve independently lawful preparation and provide the actual repair/retry path. Do not pretend that a generic Contact admin link is sufficient when that person lacks policy authority. Repair never auto-issues the request.

The summary is a view of authoritative source policy, not a new write authority. Participant explanations remain independently authorized and comprehensible; internal hold/case facts are not exposed by the staff summary. No extra recipient consent checkbox, policy notice, automatic email or retention-management task is selected by Q62.

## Thirteen boundaries shared by both options

1. **Ordinary scope and actual authority.** Apply this question to eligible new ordinary request-evidence bindings. Specialist finance, public assets, Support, resource publications, imports and other source contracts retain their owners. Policy approval/administration, request issuance, variant selection and viewing details are separate powers.
2. **Known default, real choice and unresolved policy are different.** A uses an actual approved default. Where the fully qualified source instead defines a selection-required case with compatible approved alternatives and current actor choice authority, ask deliberately; never pick the first, longest or shortest option arbitrarily. Missing, incompatible, ambiguous or unapproved applicability cannot be cured by a dropdown or confirmation. Mandatory source case determinations/approvals remain under either option.
3. **No invented retention meaning.** Neither option creates a policy catalog, numeric period, jurisdictional rule, blanket forever/default policy, new extension power or universal disposal trigger. Remaining actual source-policy fields stay explicit rather than being relabeled engineering defaults.
4. **Admission precedes consequences.** The actual qualified policy must govern each authoritative consequential act, with current revalidation after waits. Earlier durable preparation, allocation or expensive processing needs its own admitted Q02 purpose and policy; a later issuance summary cannot retroactively authorize collected bytes. Preserve only independently lawful drafts and custody when a new act is blocked.
5. **Original result and current authority.** A matching already-committed operation is recovered before treating its retry as a new request; current disclosure rules still apply. No duplicate effect, new retention clock, replayed stale grant or retroactive false draft. Relevant races use actual source ordering, not a frozen browser confirmation.
6. **Several obligations can sit behind one summary.** Evidence originals, versions, assemblies and selected inputs, previews, history, audit, messages, exports, staging and backups retain their actual record/copy purposes. Independent source uses of shared bytes remain independent. A request label cannot collapse them into one period or disposition claim.
7. **History and later correction are both real.** Preserve admitted policy/binding/trigger attribution and historical evidence. Actual approved successors and mistake corrections use the source's authorized impact/correction path; neither silent rewriting nor permanent immunity from a later applicable rule is implied. Correcting an inert draft does not grant post-admission override power.
8. **Templates do not carry live authority.** Resolve actual current source bindings for reused definitions and new requests. Template text, folder, label, filename, digest, old request and caller-supplied policy ID cannot manufacture retention authority, reset earlier clocks or transfer another source's permissions.
9. **Clocks and outcomes stay distinct.** Requested by, participant access ends, review due, receipt, technical checking, acceptance, fulfillment, cancellation, withdrawal, technical-copy expiry, source retention and physical disposal retain their ratified meanings. A valid event not yet reached is different from an unknown trigger. No status is a universal retention start/end; no false deletion date is shown.
10. **Holds and complete custody remain mandatory.** Choosing a policy neither grants read/use nor defeats holds, current dependencies, privacy limits, complete source/copy coverage or current disposition authority. A genuine conflict goes to its capable owner. Metadata change is not proof of deletion everywhere; restored data does not revive old access or policy eligibility.
11. **Existing authorization architecture.** Use source/P12 current policy and the prescribed coarse Tenant RLS boundary rather than inventing a second capability engine in per-row policies. Trusted actor, Tenant, environment and source context govern the act; caller references are validated selectors, not authority. Effective grants, old/new-row scope, raw API/RPC and privileged/import/worker paths require native proof. Neither view permission nor a dropdown grants mutation authority. No schema or function name is frozen here.
12. **Useful, accessible and bounded interaction.** Safe scope/start explanation, a capable repair route, preserved draft/focus, truthful status and minimal steps must work on keyboard, assistive technology, touch, mobile/zoom, RTL, translated labels, actual date/zone semantics and weak networks. Keep source lookups and histories finite and measurable using the inherited budget process; no N+1 per file, whole-history scan or unbounded option catalog. No unsupported latency target is invented.
13. **Honest stage.** This question selects no answer, new glossary term, ADR, implementation, policy substance, migration, physical disposal, provider action or formal publication. Actual ordinary policy, legacy profiles, native authorization/custody/recovery and intended-user qualification remain required. No product pattern or existing field substitutes for those proofs.

## Seven founder checks on the recommendation

### What could go wrong with this answer?

A can make a wrong default look settled; B can normalize unthinking confirmation. Both can falsely conflate access expiry and disposal. Current applicability, visible safe scope/start, deliberate real choices, capable correction and preserved outcome history address those failure modes. No quantified probability or error reduction is claimed.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

Actual ordinary policy content, prevalence of alternatives, staff classification knowledge, production volume and user-test results are not supplied. A applies to a resolved source fact; it does not assume every request has the same policy or permit a guessed default. A source-defined choice remains a choice.

### How does this affect the whole product?

It changes the ordinary new-request interaction while keeping every specialist source's authority and Q01–Q61 scope. P29 custody, source retention, P12 policy, shared bytes, independent copies and history remain separate. No new global policy engine, permission layer or portal duty is introduced.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

Staff review a meaningful summary and finish the normal request flow. Real alternatives and repairs stay discoverable; repetitive policy confirmation is avoided under A. Recipients retain their already-ratified upload, clarification, correction and access journeys, with only authorized explanation. Draft and unknown-outcome recovery are essential, not optional polish.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

Current primary products support both defaults and manual application. GOV.UK supplies useful counterevidence about preselected answers and guidance for review/correction. The synthesis is transparent application of a known rule, explicit selection for a real unresolved authorized choice and no fake default. This is a defensible product judgment, not universal consensus or observed user validation.

### Does this fit Asym's existing repo and product direction?

Yes, within source-owned policy, Q02 admission, Q25 recovery, Q39 definitions and existing P12/Tenant boundaries. Current remote evidence does not establish the ordinary native path; the actual policy and release gates remain open. External financial schedules, folder semantics and provider defaults do not supersede Core authority.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

Recommend A with the thirteen boundaries above. Its exception is a real source-required determination/choice, not a hidden escape hatch or new authority. B is the fair alternative if Conrad wants explicit policy application on every request. Either answer receives the full selected-answer adversarial review before ratification; neither is inferred from prior choices.
