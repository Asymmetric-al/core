## Recommended question and answer

**When staff deliberately publish an eligible ordinary requested-item addition
or material instruction revision, should an optional email to the recipient
start On or Off?**

Recommend **A — Start the eligible optional notice On, with a clear staff choice
and bounded tenant controls.** The purpose is awareness of an actual published
update to this request. It is not a generic notification for any field edit,
an automatic demand for another upload, or proof that the participant has read
the update. Native on-return visibility remains required under Q36/Q37 even
when the notice is Off, unavailable, blocked, delayed or unsuccessful.

**B — Offer the same eligible notice, initially Off**, is a strong alternative.
It requires staff to choose email for the particular publication and better
supports frequent successive changes, sensitive communications and established
direct coordination. Its cost is a separate remembered choice when the recipient
would otherwise have no reason to return. We have no Asym evidence about edit
frequency, monitoring habits, missed updates, email sensitivity or completion
improvement that establishes the magnitude of either tradeoff.

A's recommendation is a product judgment: a deliberate material publication is
a useful moment to make recipient awareness visible in the ordinary staff flow.
It depends on source-qualified purpose, audience and content plus a genuinely
visible optional action. It is not a universal modern best practice or an
automatic consequence of Q31/Q35's earlier choices. The research supports both
integrated notification and deliberate manual communication patterns.

The two alternatives concern the initial product default for the same bounded
capability. They are not two tenant modes or a new global notification engine.
For either, propose initial tenant availability through the existing qualified
plan surface, tenant authority to disable this purpose or change its initial
per-publication choice, and staff ability to decline it for an otherwise lawful
publication. These controls need their own explicit Q40 adoption; nothing here
changes another notice's default, audience or stop rule.

## Practical A/B tradeoff

| Choice                                         | Good everyday fit                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Real cost                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A: Optional notice starts On — recommended** | Staff deliberately publish materially different participant-facing requirements, and a factual, eligible cue helps a recipient who is away from the portal learn that the request changed. Staff can turn it Off before publishing. | Preselection can cause unwanted mail; repeated publications can create noise. Sensitive contexts require reliable suppression and careful content, not trust in a default. |
| **B: Same notice starts Off**                  | Staff already coordinate directly, make several successive adjustments, or work in contexts where avoiding an unintended communication outweighs the chance of a missed cue. Choosing email remains available in the same flow.     | Staff must remember to opt in; a recipient may work from remembered instructions or not return. On-return display is not an attention guarantee.                           |

The stronger no-build alternative is native visibility plus an actually qualified
manual source route. Content Snare/FileInvite show it is plausible. It avoids
qualifying this new email purpose but leaves staff to perform and remember a
separate action. It is not presented as a third configuration mode, nor does
calling something manual exempt it from actual recipient/disclosure authority.
The coordinated question compares A/B because both can preserve this manual
route while choosing the integrated cue's initial behavior.

## Concrete journey and minimal wording

Illustrative only: Elena has asked Nora for an equipment inventory. Nora has
already supplied a file. Elena publishes corrected instructions for the same
inventory to clarify that serial numbers belong in it. The existing file remains
received under its earlier basis. The current next step may be Elena reviewing
whether that file already includes the numbers. An email saying Nora must upload
again would be false. A separately qualified factual update cue can still make
Nora aware of the changed instructions and lead to the current authorized view.

For Q36, Elena might instead deliberately add one eligible ordinary item to the
same continuing request. The portal shows the newly published work and unchanged
existing progress. The notice may identify that a request update exists, but it
does not automatically reset the target or access window, open a completed
request, reject earlier evidence or grant rights to a newly introduced item.

Retain **Add item** or **Update instructions** as the authoritative source
action. Adjacent to its consequence summary, propose the clearly labeled choice
**Email the recipient about this update**, with the safe actual destination
visible to staff who may see it. The effect text should distinguish publishing
the update from submitting its notice for delivery. Off leaves the lawful update
intact. Do not hide the choice in a collapsed settings section, infer On from a
missing input, or make toggling the control send anything.

An initial default is a starting selection for a genuinely new review; changing
that default later must not silently overwrite already initialized/reviewed
staff intent. Availability, destination and relevant authority still require
current checks. A known material change before publication needs a useful inline
explanation and deliberate lawful publication-only continuation where permitted,
not silent downgrade of a reviewed combined intent or a mandatory modal ritual.

The email must be minimally useful, not a useless generic broadcast. It needs
safe, unambiguous context sufficient to distinguish the actual request from a
parallel or replacement request. It should not copy full instructions, before/
after bodies, filenames, private reasons or internal history merely because
staff can see them. Even a request title may be sensitive; qualification must
cover the actual chosen identifier and destination. A safe protected-entry
carrier, source contact or envelope can remain when independently qualified
under its own contract. No new body-bearing telemetry or public fallback follows.

Email eligibility is its own exact current disclosure/contact-purpose decision.
It is not proved by an old invite address, general Party access or previous mail.
Portal expiry alone does not universally decide whether truthful informational
mail is useful; it never renews web access. A call to act/view requires the actual
current qualified route and must not imply inaccessible instructions can be read.
Any informational/help variant must be declared and qualified, not improvised
after the original action-bearing notice becomes false. Closure, supersession,
new acceptance or staff handling is tested against the actual notice purpose;
do not copy another purpose's blanket stop predicates or keep a false action
claim simply because the publication once occurred.

## Accessibility and ordinary usability basis

[W3C On Input, SC 3.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/on-input.html)
supports predictable controls: changing the email choice should not itself
publish, send, redirect or open another context. This does not prescribe a
particular control library or justify an extra confirmation step.

[W3C Status Messages, SC 4.1.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html)
supports programmatically available status without unnecessary interruption.
Publication success, mail submission and unknown delivery need separate truthful
meaning; an aggressively repeated announcement or success toast alone is not a
durable recovery path.

[W3C Reflow, SC 1.4.10](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html)
supports preserving content and actions at narrow equivalent viewports. The
recipient/effect/choice must remain discoverable together on mobile and zoom.
These are informative explanations of accessibility criteria, not proof of
native conformance. No browser, screen-reader, keyboard, localization or email
client tests ran for this research.
