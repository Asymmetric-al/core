# Phase 29 Q20 — a visible email choice with truthful separate outcomes

Review date: **21 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok**. The parent reports Conrad selected **Q20 A: default-on optional recipient email in the existing fresh-grant review**, requesting excellent flexible UX and no overengineering. Q01–Q19 and their complete amendments are ratified. **The Q20 full review and amendments remain pending ratification.** This note neither ratifies that package nor selects another feature.

Only this research note was written. The current Q20 question/comparative note and relevant owner/runtime research boundaries were inspected. Public primary product and accessibility guidance was refreshed. No real message, private account, provider, permission, product, UI, dependency or canonical document was changed. No native dispatch, browser, assistive-technology, delivery or representative-user test was performed. Adjacent code and owner contracts are evidence of boundaries, not proof that the new native notification lane works.

## Disposition

**Keep A with focused safeguards, while acknowledging real counterevidence for preselection.** The selected behavior is one visible optional email choice in a deliberate staff review, not automatic mail on a state change. On each genuinely new eligible fresh-grant review, it starts selected; the staff actor can turn it off. Their actual choice must survive validation, back navigation and safe recovery of that same operation. A retry is not a new opportunity to apply the default.

The same interaction applies to first and later fresh unfinished grants and fresh completed-read grants. Their source meaning and permitted actions remain distinct. No original-request invitation, identity-confirmation email, reminder, bulk send, other channel, staff alert, tenant preference editor or specialist required notice is silently included. Optional email does not become an eligibility prerequisite for every lawful grant-only case.

The simplest useful experience is the current source review plus one clearly labeled checkbox, authorized destination/context and a final action that accurately says whether email is included. Source success and the access notice's state then appear in that same context. A new composer, account, dashboard, request queue, routine extra confirmation or manual-toggle ritual is unnecessary. Clear outcome language is necessary because provider delivery can fail or remain unknown after a valid grant.

## Strongest integrated default-off alternative

B offers the **same integrated review**, initially unchecked. Staff can select email and authorize it with the grant in one action. It is not a separate workflow and not inherently worse UI. Its strongest value is avoiding an unintended outward message when staff fail to notice the preselection; the current GOV.UK guidance gives this concern explicit design support. B's cost is an omitted selection when a notice was wanted, with the source access clock already running. Neither cost is measured for Asym.

A is therefore a bounded product judgment favoring a normally included handoff, not a consensus best practice. Preserve Conrad's selection, make the checked consequence and destination unmistakable, and qualify intended-user comprehension. Do not claim that a clearly labeled button proves people noticed the choice. If real supported-user testing exposes material unnoticed sends, return that finding for explicit product review rather than silently switching the default or adding a compulsory extra confirmation.

No comparison justifies hidden emails, tracking pixels/read receipts, automatic resends or a global preference switch. A checkbox already checked need not be toggled off and on to count as a meaningful selected outcome: the deliberate final review/action authorizes its visible choice when the exact source/communication requirements are met.

## Eight practical journeys

These are proposed behavior and copy intents, not tested UI screens. Protected recipient information and source details appear only to actors who may currently inspect them. A single useful status block is preferable to a wall of warnings or a notification dashboard.

### 1. Eligible checked review, for either fresh-grant type and later repeats

Keep the source's existing action/context. Show **“Notify recipient by email”** and the authorized recipient/destination reference. The option is visibly and programmatically checked when eligibility is established. The final action makes the selected consequence clear, such as **“Grant read access and email recipient”** or the corresponding unfinished-access action. Use a concise approved notice; no custom subject/body-writing task is required.

The grant review still covers the actual source scope, purpose and timing. A repeated completed-read period does not create an upload task; a fresh unfinished period does not imply all requirements are editable. Source admission has its own current checks and immutable effect. A selected email records a separate qualified communication intent for that result. If it is accepted for asynchronous owner processing, show the actual queued/requested state rather than “Recipient notified.”

### 2. Staff turns email off, then encounters validation or returns

The actor unchecks the option because a notice is not wanted. The action now clearly grants only. Fixing another field, resolving a source validation error, navigating back within the same review, or recovering the same draft must not turn email back on. A missing checkbox value in a request must not be interpreted as consent to the default. If the complete reviewed intent cannot be safely recovered, do not silently submit inferred choices.

After confirmed grant-only admission, show the source result and **“Access notification: Not requested.”** This refers only to this optional notice; it does not promise that independent mandatory/security messages were suppressed. An already-committed grant-only intent cannot become grant-plus-email merely because an old form now displays a check. A later message requires its own qualified meaning where actually available. A genuinely new future grant review can apply A's initial default; no global remembered preference is silently added by this decision.

### 3. Email path is unavailable or its eligibility is unknown

Do not present a checked, disabled option that visually promises email while hiding why it cannot happen. Give an authorized, concise explanation such as **“Email notification is not available”** or **“We cannot confirm email availability.”** Keep the reason in ordinary accessible content, not only a hover tooltip. Do not expose private suppression, recipient or configuration details to an actor who may not see them.

If a lawful grant-only path and its qualified handoff exist, staff can deliberately choose it and proceed; do not require a marketing integration or unrelated message permission just to grant. If they confirmed grant-plus-email and eligibility materially changes before admission, explain the changed result/review rather than silently degrading to grant-only. Once source admission is known to have committed, report that fact separately and recover the actual notification state; do not pretend nothing happened. An operational provider outage with valid durable queuing is not necessarily missing send authority—show the owner-supported state accurately, without inventing guaranteed delivery.

### 4. Recipient or destination changes during review or preparation

The destination belongs to the exact intended recipient and owner-qualified address revision, not an arbitrary To field. If a material destination change invalidates the reviewed message, show the permitted new facts and obtain the required current review through the existing source path. Do not silently send to a replacement address, treat shared mailbox text as identity, or offer CC/BCC to solve the problem.

Source grant identity and message routing are separate: a contact-data change does not authorize retargeting the grant. A previously sealed message cannot mutate recipient/content under its old immutable identity. Producer-owned recovery may prepare a new qualified occurrence/credential where allowed; that is not an automatic new grant or an unselected general resend feature. Display only the identity/destination detail needed for authorized meaningful review, without imposing unnecessary personal-name/address conventions.

### 5. Grant committed, but preparation/send failed or outcome is unknown

Keep a clear source result, for example **“Read access granted. Ends [actual date, time and zone].”** Add the actual state of this access notice. When evidence proves no submission occurred and the notice failed, **“Access email was not sent”** can be truthful. If submission may have occurred, use **“Email status is not confirmed”**; do not tell staff the recipient definitely received nothing.

The recovery path belongs to source/P17/P6 according to the failure. It may reconcile, resume authorized preparation or require a governed later action. Do not expose a generic Send again button that blindly resubmits after an unknown outcome or provider deduplication window. Do not revoke/regrant, extend the source end or clone the request to make email look successful. Once provider acceptance may have occurred, there is no guaranteed Undo email or recall. Source notification intent, provider submission and human awareness must not share one undifferentiated success badge.

### 6. Double-click, lost grant reply or repeated screen recovery

Repeated submission of the same deliberate intent converges on its exact grant and chosen-notice outcome. Disable duplicate UI activation where appropriate, but do not treat that as the backend guarantee. If staff navigate away after dispatch, the committed operation is not canceled. Recover actual state on return.

A selected notice that was already durably authorized can recover through its existing identity; the UI must not imply that recovery creates a second notice. A grant-only choice stays silent. Conflicting checked/unchecked or changed material payload under the same committed operation is not a valid same-effect retry. If an old result returns after later grants or changed source state, show its historical meaning only where authorized and the actual current result separately; do not restart the latest deadline or rebroadcast an old access invitation. Two staff still obey the inherited fresh-grant nonoverlap/authority rules.

### 7. Source state changes before dispatch, or the message arrives late

Current owner checks must prevent stale protected-action claims. If the grant is revoked/expired, the purpose ends or dispatch becomes disallowed, a queued old “access available” message cannot be sent simply because the checkbox was once selected. If an unfinished request is fulfilled before dispatch, do not send instructions to upload against the old stage. Exact source/P17 recovery determines whether the old notice is canceled, blocked or a differently authorized notice exists; Q20 does not select a completion notice or authorize rewriting the old message into one.

A provider may already have accepted a notice before a later restriction, and delivery can arrive afterward. Then the existing qualified doorway must truthfully enforce current proof/source authority; the email cannot grant access itself. Approved content must not promise a full new thirty days from receipt or perpetual availability. State the actual admitted end where appropriate under the approved contract; in-product current terms and restrictions remain authoritative. Delays, retries, opening and safe credential repair do not reset Q14/Q18 clocks. No automatic resend, alternate channel or new grant is selected as compensation.

### 8. Mobile, assistive technology and privacy at the recipient doorway

Staff can find and operate the checkbox by keyboard and touch, hear its actual state and recognize the final action's visible words in its accessible name. A label click can provide a usable target without a tiny isolated hit area. Changing the option updates the consequence predictably without moving focus, navigating away, dispatching email or flooding announcements. Meaningful errors/results remain in the page and are programmatically available; success is not only a disappearing toast or a color.

Supported narrow/zoomed and localized layouts keep recipient reference, selected consequence and actual end understandable without clipping. Use approved plain-language content for the correct grant type and the source-qualified locale; do not add a per-grant language/date editor. The email subject, lock-screen preview and body disclose only approved minimal context, with no raw file attachment or provider URL fallback. Scanners and accidental GET/HEAD opens stay inert. The recipient follows the existing qualified proof path with supported paste/autofill and no new account solely for this notice. Wrong/unqualified callers do not learn private request existence, content or history from an expiry/help response.

## Existing qualification versus adopted intent

The owner's source separation—native admission, P17 approved presentation/protected action and P6 dispatch/history—already governs. Q01's closed document-request recipient/no-Party amendment remains accepted intent awaiting its exact canonical/native qualification; this review does not reopen identity choices or borrow a newsletter exception. Current source and recipient gates are substantive even when the visible checkbox is simple.

The runtime research identifies adjacent choice, email and idempotency helpers with narrower purposes. Reusing their UI pattern may be sensible, but donor-receipt fallbacks, generic profile-email resolution, best-effort send booleans or short-lived provider deduplication do not prove the new source-to-notice contract. This note did not independently execute those helpers or their tests.

The chosen default must be added through the exact optional message contract and source effect, preserving current authority, immutable reviewed terms, coherent required intent/recovery and separate delivery outcomes. That is required source/communication integration—not a new notification engine, email composer, scheduling system or reason to build an entire future Workflow Studio before its qualified native consumer.

Use existing source, P17/P6, security, product/accessibility and support owners for material defects. No new SLA, resend cadence, quantitative comprehension target, analytics program or alert dashboard is selected here. A confirmed unauthorized notice, choice reversal, duplicate/rekeyed effect, stale protected action or privacy disclosure requires containment and exact regression proof; future measurements cannot excuse an unqualified authority path.

The reviewed disposition remains **A with required safeguards**, not a claim that default-on is empirically best or that the feature is ready. Strong B and the explicit preselection caution remain in the record. The eight journeys and the risk/proof groups above are future acceptance obligations, not tests reported as passed. The full amendment package still requires ratification, and actual source/dispatch/browser/AT/intended-user qualification remains separate.
