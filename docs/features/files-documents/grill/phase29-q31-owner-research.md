## Recommended next question and scope

**When staff shares a clarification reply on an ordinary document request, how should its participant learn that the reply is waiting?**

- **A — Offer an email notice, initially on when eligible (recommended).** Keep the reply in its authorized item context. In the same deliberate staff Send interaction, show an optional “Email the request recipient about this reply” choice, initially selected when the exact source, tenant plan, recipient and qualified communication path permit it. Staff may turn it off. A minimal approved notice directs the person back to the protected context; it does not carry the clarification text or files.
- **B — Offer the same email notice, initially off.** The same source reply and same qualified optional notice exist, but staff deliberately selects email in that interaction. This reduces accidental outward notices and suits teams already speaking with participants, while making omission easier. It does not mean the tenant plan is Off: staff cannot override a tenant-disabled step.
- **C — Keep replies in the request only.** Add no automatic clarification-email capability. Participants see replies when returning while authorized, and staff use an existing independently qualified help/contact route when needed. This reduces delivery work but requires an explicit, credible operational way to avoid participants waiting without knowing a reply arrived. It must not remove Q30's native clarification or create a mandatory separate send wizard.

Example: Nora asks which dates her document must cover and leaves the page. Elena replies the next morning. The native answer is preserved either way; this decision is whether and how Elena offers Nora an extra cue to return. A makes that cue part of the existing act without requiring Elena to remember a second workflow. Its cost is overlooked preselection or unnecessary mail; B is a real alternative, and C is a defensible simpler native product if the organization accepts its awareness burden. The recommendation is a product judgment, not measured Asym preference or evidence that mail is read.

The question must explicitly admit the new notice capability in A/B. Asking only which default applies would wrongly treat clarification email as already selected. Conversely, it need not ask Conrad to choose event IDs, queue design, retention numbers or provider mechanics.
