## One decision

**When a published resource passes its review date without the intended review, should it remain available with a clear Review due status, or stop being available through the library until it is deliberately reviewed again?**

This concerns the existing **P28-owned resource publication**: currently qualified missionary readers, authorized staff curators and its optional getting-started guide links. It does not make all stored files into resources or decide a new general staff/public catalog. A broader staff-reference audience remains a coverage item to qualify under a real source owner, not a secretly completed capability or a reason to invent a second library.

| Option                                                              | Practical experience                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Main benefit                                                                                                                                                                | Real tradeoff                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Keep available with a clear review-due status (recommended)** | The otherwise published, safe and currently authorized resource remains available. Readers see truthful review-due context; curators can find due resources and perform an actual review, correct/replace or withdraw.                    | A missed maintenance target does not unexpectedly remove useful guidance. Review freshness remains distinct from an actual access or validity deadline.                     | People may continue using guidance that has not been rechecked on schedule. A badge alone does not guarantee understanding or curatorial follow-through.                                              |
| **B — Stop library availability until deliberate re-review**        | After the source-defined review cutoff, affected library discovery and new retrieval stop. The curator retains independently authorized maintenance access and must actually review/requalify the publication before it is offered again. | Makes periodic review a condition of continued use through that library. Credible when the organization prefers no guidance over guidance whose scheduled check has lapsed. | Missed maintenance can interrupt useful reading and guide links even if nothing became incorrect. Every delivery path needs dependable cutoff and recovery; a delayed job cannot extend availability. |

**Recommendation: A for the general review target.** The date tells staff when guidance should be checked; its passage alone does not establish that the content became incorrect. Keep actual rights, safety, withdrawal, access and hard source-validity restrictions independently enforceable under both choices. Known unsafe or prohibited material never stays available merely because A was chosen.

B is a serious alternative for intentionally time-bounded assurance, not an unnecessary approval process. It deliberately accepts the operational cost of temporarily losing references. Neither choice proves factual accuracy. This is one choice about review-date meaning, not a proposal for two tenant modes, a recurring reminder program, a universal review interval or a new policy builder. Source-specific mandatory validity rules remain mandatory under either answer.

## Concrete reader and curator journey

Illustrative scenario, not an observed ministry workflow: an organization publishes an equipment-inventory guide for its permitted readers. Its actual review occurred on 1 September; its next review target is 1 December. The target passes without review, while publication, safety, rights and reader access remain independently valid.

Under **A**, a reader still finds and opens it. The ordinary resource information says **Review due** and presents the meaningful date context. **Last reviewed 1 September** appears only if a real source review supports that fact and it is appropriate to disclose. There is no blocking acknowledgment, warning modal or compulsory preview. The curator can filter existing resource management to find due work, inspect the exact publication and deliberately record an actual review, publish a corrected successor or withdraw it. Reviewing unchanged guidance must not require re-uploading identical bytes or a fake content edit.

Under **B**, the next protected library access after the qualified cutoff receives a safe unavailable state, with a real permitted successor or a return to Resources where possible. It must not look like a network failure or claim the file was deleted. The curator can still reach the separately authorized maintenance context and complete a real re-review plus current publication qualification. Extending a date alone must not masquerade as completed review or covertly lift a review-based block.

Under both, moving the next target, changing an owner, reading, downloading, scanning or saving an unrelated edit does not become an actual review. A new draft is not the published successor. A review/date change cannot revive an independently withdrawn publication. A truthful source-approved successor may be offered; a hidden successor's title, identity or existence is not exposed. Earlier delivery/history keeps its exact version. Already downloaded copies and external sites cannot be recalled by changing this field.

P28 requires actual review truth but does **not** establish a named native Record review/Mark reviewed command or completed schema. The proposed review/date action and its complete recovery are an explicit narrow source amendment to qualify after selection, not an assertion that existing software already provides it.

## Twelve proposed boundaries for the choice

1. **Existing owner and scope.** Apply the selected meaning to the qualified P28 resource publication. Retain actual reader/curator audience and P29 custody. No generic file expiry, second catalog, new all-staff curator permission or donor/public access follows.
2. **Distinct facts.** Review target, actual review, published exact version, last content change, current access, rights/safety validity and retention are separate. Known unsafe/withdrawn/prohibited content stops affected access under A and B. Neither option guarantees accuracy.
3. **Honest A.** Due status is visible in the ordinary permitted reader context and discoverable in existing curator maintenance. Do not conceal all due items through a default filter and call that continued availability. Missing/unknown review facts are not silently Fresh, Never expires or Not reviewed.
4. **Honest B.** The cutoff applies to affected new library discovery/retrieval, including metadata, preview/original/download, range/conditional/cache and indirect guide paths where applicable. It cannot depend on eventual cron/UI cleanup. Independent curator access remains possible; no deleted/recall claim or hidden reason/successor leak.
5. **Actual review and useful correction.** A deliberate attributed review binds the intended exact current publication/content basis. Unchanged content may be reviewed without new bytes. Date correction/rescheduling, title/owner changes, reads, scans and restores are not actual review. Under B, date extension alone cannot counterfeit the required review or covertly lift a review-based block. Separately, where the source can establish that the recorded date was factually wrong, an authorized correction preserves the original evidence and follows the proved corrected basis; it is not ordinary postponement, a generic override or a fabricated new review. Any resulting availability still requires all current publication/access conditions. Any review/date command is an explicit source amendment, not an assumed existing API.
6. **Current authority and durable history.** Trusted context supplies tenant/organization/actor; current source/P12 capability and relevant version guard every mutation and response. Native old/new-row, grants, RLS/RPC/service, projection/cache and Storage paths must preserve the same boundary. Retain actual prior facts; do not backdate review, change its author or rewrite earlier recipients' exact version. A later withdrawal/restriction defeats stale retry or restore.
7. **Declared temporal meaning.** Use the qualified source's calendar/instant precision and zone, not each viewer's clock or arbitrary UTC-midnight parsing. Specify equality, due-day boundary, DST/zone changes, missing/unknown values and correction in the selected review. Do not invent a universal cadence, grace period, date cap, optional date policy or retroactive deadline for ambiguous legacy data.
8. **Complete recovery.** Curators can actually review, correct/replace or withdraw through current permitted actions. Conflicting edits preserve safe input; lost responses recover the original durable operation under current disclosure before retry. An unavailable curator has a qualified maintenance/handoff route, not universal staff access, impersonation or a powerless Contact support placeholder.
9. **Current/successor and independent uses.** Only the actual source-published version is current; a new draft/upload is not automatically adopted. Offer successors only when real and permitted. Preserve prior history, independent source uses, retention and copies already delivered. External-site freshness/availability is not controlled by this field. Guide dependencies follow C44; no blanket guide shutdown or new reader obligation.
10. **No implied communication or platform.** Neither option selects auto-email, review reminders, tasks per date, recurring chores, AI verification, reader tracking, acknowledgment, subscriptions, another moderator or a policy builder. Independently qualified effects retain their contracts. A can derive due status on authorized reads without scheduled writes; B can enforce a time predicate without a new scheduler. Neither is promised free.
11. **Accessible and efficient UI.** Use shared Base UI/base-maia/packages/ui patterns. Show text/programmatic status, understandable date context, usable keyboard/AT/mobile actions and accessible underlying content. Preserve safe filters/scroll/input across recovery; distinguish No results from load failure. Qualify 320 CSS-pixel reflow, 200% text, 400% zoom, Core's 44px targets, long international names, RTL/IME and low-bandwidth behavior; do not confuse Core's target with WCAG's separate minimum.
12. **Proportionate proof and stage.** Use bounded permission-aware maintenance queries and current derived-state/cache behavior, without scanning every held file or generating reminder storms. Qualify exact roles, clocks, races, original recovery, restore/mixed versions and intended-user comprehension before activation. No schema/provider/new term or numeric capacity promise is fixed by this question. Formal source/OpenSpec adoption remains /to-prd and tickets /to-issues.

## Adversarial check

### What could go wrong with this answer?

A permits continued use after a missed check; readers may overlook the status and curators may accumulate due work. B can remove useful material and break references because maintenance is late. More serious shared failures include fake reviews from date edits, expired rights hidden by a future target, unauthorized status/successor disclosure and stale retry revival. These concerns narrow both options with real review, current source authority and complete recovery; they do not establish that either is universally correct.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

The illustration assumes a real source publication, meaningful date and capable curator. We have no observed ministry review frequency, missed-review harm, staff SLA, task-time gain or daily dashboard-use evidence. Date precision/zone and legacy truth still require qualification. No routine reminder, universal interval or automatic re-verification is inferred to fill those unknowns.

### How does this affect the whole product?

It resolves freshness behavior for an already-required P28 resource purpose while P29 keeps custody. It does not turn ordinary request deadlines, official receipts, public Media, expense evidence, Support attachments or access/retention expiry into the same concept. Shared bytes do not create shared review authority. General staff-reference coverage remains visible but unselected.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

A preserves useful reading with honest uncertainty; B prioritizes withholding after a missed check and must provide clear unavailable/successor/return behavior. Curators need a short real maintenance path, including unchanged-content review, correction and handoff. Readers need no extra acknowledgment or task. Neither a generic badge nor a powerless error can replace usable mobile, AT and low-bandwidth recovery.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

Current primary products document both expiring trust/review indicators and explicit scheduled unpublishing. Their differences argue against a universal rule. A is a proportionate judgment for ordinary review targets; B suits an intentional continued-use condition. Vendor automation, identity, intervals and fees are not imported. Direct nonprofit evidence for this exact choice was not found, so no invented ministry consensus supports the recommendation.

### Does this fit Asym’s existing repo and product direction?

Yes if this is a narrow P28 review/date amendment with P12 and P29 boundaries, not another CMS, all-file expiry, task engine or implicit public access. The formal source already requires publication/withdrawal and real review truth, while current inspected consumers do not prove the proposed command. Later source/OpenSpec adoption must explicitly reconcile the new semantics; documentation is not native proof.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

Recommend **A — Keep available with a clear review-due status**, with all independent validity restrictions, actual review evidence and usable curator maintenance preserved. Present B fairly. Select only the date's effect here; after Conrad's answer, perform the complete category-by-category adversarial review and settle the exact selected normal lifecycle, temporal semantics, safeguards and proof without silently introducing another product mode.
