## One practical question

**When an otherwise permitted resource reaches its review date, should it stay available with a clear “Review due” status, or become unavailable until a curator reviews it again?**

This is the effect of the resource's stated **review target**, not whether resources need an owner, current permission, publication or hard safety/rights limits. Both options preserve existing actual expiry, withdrawal, confidentiality, rights and source-validity restrictions. Neither lets a stale review badge override a known unsafe or prohibited publication.

| Option                                                                    | Reader and curator experience                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Benefit                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Real cost                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Keep available with honest review-due status (recommended)**        | An independently safe/currently authorized publication remains available. Readers see that review is due and any actual last-review information they may receive. Curators can find due resources in existing maintenance and deliberately record a real review, publish a correction/successor or withdraw.                            | A missed maintenance date does not unexpectedly remove useful guidance. Review status remains distinct from a true validity deadline; readers and curators can see the uncertainty.                           | Readers may still use guidance that has not been rechecked on schedule. A status label is not a guarantee they notice it or interpret it correctly, and curatorial follow-through still matters.                                                                                                                  |
| **B — Stop availability at the review date pending deliberate re-review** | The resource stops being offered through the affected library when its qualified review cutoff is reached. Safe old-link handling explains unavailability where permitted. A curator must deliberately re-review and satisfy current publication conditions before it is available again; changing a date alone is not proof of review. | Stronger protection against continued reliance on guidance whose scheduled freshness check has lapsed. This is credible when the organization treats that review checkpoint as a necessary condition for use. | A missed review can remove otherwise useful material, including when the content has not changed. Readers may lose an expected reference; curators need dependable coverage, precise cutoff semantics and an actual recovery path. Withholding does not establish that remaining resources are factually correct. |

**Recommend A for the general resource review target.** A review date says when someone should check guidance; it does not itself establish that the guidance becomes wrong at midnight. Preserve the real power to withdraw unsafe or obsolete material immediately and every independently binding validity deadline. Treating those separate meanings distinctly is clearer than making every maintenance target an accidental access expiry.

B is not an unreasonable or merely bureaucratic alternative. If an organization would rather show no resource than show one whose periodic check is overdue, B enforces that product expectation even when staff are away. The tradeoff is interruption and operational pressure, not a proof that B is inherently safer in every real task. These are mutually exclusive product alternatives for the review-date meaning, not two new tenant modes or a promise to add a general policy builder.

This recommendation is a product judgment informed by actual source boundaries and documented external patterns. No ministry interviews, usage counts, staff SLA, error reduction or measured task improvement were obtained. We do not assume curators check a dashboard daily or that readers will always see a badge.

## Concrete illustration and complete ordinary flow

Illustration, not observed ministry behavior: Elena curates an organization resource, “Preparing an equipment inventory,” which Nora can lawfully read. It has an actual recorded review on 1 September and a review target of 1 December. The resource remains the source's published version; no separate rights/safety expiry is reached.

Under A, once the source-declared review boundary is reached, Nora can still open the permitted resource with **Review due** and the actual review date context. Elena sees it in her permitted due-review maintenance view. Elena may check that the guidance still applies and record that deliberate review, prepare/publish a corrected version, or withdraw it. Merely extending the next target, renaming the resource, opening its preview or changing its owner must not be represented as a completed review.

Under B, Nora's next attempted library access after the qualified cutoff finds a safe unavailable state instead of the document, with a permitted replacement or return to Resources where possible. Elena can still reach the curator's independently authorized maintenance route. A legitimate re-review and current source/publication admission can make guidance available again. A cron job running late, an old cached card, a restored database or a forgotten link must not quietly continue the ended reader availability.

If the inventory guidance instead becomes known unsafe, loses rights, is withdrawn or has an actual hard validity end, both options stop affected delivery through the existing owner. A “Review due” label is not a safety override. If a new approved successor exists, both use the source's actual current/successor relationship and independently permitted disclosure; neither opens an arbitrary newer draft or reveals a hidden replacement's title.

Previously downloaded copies remain outside any promise of recall. Changing the review target cannot rewrite which exact version Nora previously received. A resource reading event is not proof of understanding, course credit, acknowledgment, agreement, or confirmation that every statement is true.

## Practical UI meaning without a new platform

For a reader under A, a concise **Review due** label and a relevant date belong with the resource's ordinary information, not in a blocking warning modal. Use **Last reviewed [date]** only when an actual admitted review supports it. **Review date not recorded** or an equally truthful neutral state must not become “Current,” “Never expires” or “Not reviewed” by inference. The resource's actual source current version is a separate fact from review freshness. Avoid generic **Verified**, **Safe**, **Approved until** or **Guaranteed current** badges.

For a curator, the existing resource-maintenance context can expose review target, actual review history and due filtering beside the already-owned Edit/Publish/Withdraw actions. A deliberate **Record review**-type act, if adopted in the selected review, must refer to the exact current publication/version and actual actor. A date-only reschedule cannot impersonate that act. No minimum essay, new approver, recurring task engine, grading score or compulsory preview/dwell count follows. An unavailable responsible curator needs a real scoped operator/curator handoff, not a permanent stuck state or universal staff power.

For B, reader unavailability must not present a network error or imply deletion. An authorized exact source status can explain that review is pending; otherwise show the existing neutral unavailable state. A protected successor is not exposed as a link/title to someone who cannot receive it. Curator access for the re-review is independent of the reader cutoff. Neither branch adds a right to browse old versions or a new completed-document archive.

The date's meaning must be understandable and deterministic. A civil review date needs a source-declared time zone and explicit due boundary; a qualified instant needs its existing precision. Do not silently use each reader's browser zone, mutate the date on travel, convert a calendar day to UTC midnight, or invent a universal review cadence. This question does not choose new numeric intervals, reminders or repeated-review defaults; those are not smuggled into either option. Exact clock semantics will be required in the selected adversarial review, and B additionally requires real cutoff enforcement rather than a late cleanup job.

## Adversarial check

### What could go wrong with this answer?

A can leave overdue guidance in use if readers overlook its status or curators do not act. B can unexpectedly remove useful material at a maintenance target. Both fail if the product conflates a review date with actual safety/rights validity, fabricates review from a date edit, or leaks a protected successor. Honest status, exact source qualification and a real curator path address those risks; neither policy proves factual accuracy.

### What hidden assumptions are we making?

The illustration assumes a qualified resource publication, a meaningful review target and an available curator. It does not establish daily dashboard use, a review SLA, the frequency of time-sensitive guidance or a universal interval. Actual date meaning, current source rights and capable owner recovery need qualification. Unknown historical facts remain unknown.

### How does this affect the whole product?

It completes a required freshness behavior in the existing resource library, beyond the heavily developed ordinary-request path. It does not change public Media, official receipts, expense evidence, Support attachments, document request dates/reminders, staff upload or general search. Shared custody does not make their review semantics interchangeable.

### How does this affect the end-user experience?

A favors continued useful access with a visible limitation; B favors withholding after a missed check. Curators can see what needs attention without a new platform. Readers get truthful current-version/successor/unavailable behavior, not a misleading generic failure or a demand to acknowledge guidance. Mobile and AT users need the same clear meaning and recovery.

### Does this follow modern best practices?

Both patterns have current primary precedents. Guru distinguishes unverified content from deleted content, Notion records expiring verification, and Contentful offers deliberate timed unpublish. Their differences make a universal verdict inappropriate. A is the recommended product judgment for a general review target while preserving all actual hard cutoffs; B remains stronger where the organization intentionally treats review as a condition of availability.

### Does this fit Asym’s existing repo and product direction?

Yes when the amendment belongs to the already-owned P28 resource publication and its current audience, with P29 custody and P12 enforcement. It meets founder §12 without inventing a separate library, LMS, automatic verification or new notification program. Current repository/UI implementations are not asserted by these documents.

### Should we adjust the recommendation?

Recommend A with the stated honest status, real maintenance and strict independent validity boundaries. Present B fairly and ask only the review-date availability question. Do not preselect a cadence, notification default, tenant mode, broad staff resource audience or automatic approval mechanism.
