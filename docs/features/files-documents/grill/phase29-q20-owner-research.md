# Phase29 Q20 — recipient handoff when staff grants fresh access

Research only, 2026-10-21 (Asia/Bangkok). Q01–Q19 are ratified grooming intent; Q20 is unselected. No new ADR, product policy, source contract or implementation is adopted by this note.

## Recommended next question

**When staff grants fresh access to a private document request, should “Notify the recipient by email” start selected in that review, or start unselected and require staff to opt in?**

Apply this one choice to the ordinary native **fresh unfinished** and **fresh completed-read** paths, including their ratified repeated periods. Keep the underlying purposes, grants, exact actions, completed/unfinished states and message-source identities distinct. This is one recipient-handoff policy, not a merged grant or permission type.

### A — Notify as part of granting access, with a clear grant-only choice (recommended)

The existing grant review visibly offers notification and normally selects it **only when the actor, source, intended recipient, Live message contract and effective Delivery Plan permit it**. Staff can deliberately grant without an email when appropriate, such as while already assisting the person. The successful source action records the chosen authorized message intent through the existing P17/P6 path; dispatch occurs separately. Show the actual grant outcome and notification outcome independently.

Benefit: fewer forgotten handoffs and less time lost while an unused fixed access period runs. The person receives a governed way back to the existing request without staff copying a secret link into another system. Cost: another email may be unnecessary when staff and recipient are already in contact; a careless default could produce unexpected mail or disclose context. The visible choice, fixed intended recipient, narrow message purpose and existing dispatch gates constrain that risk.

This proposes a default for this exact source action, **not** an organization-wide Delivery Plan edit, automatic recurring email, recipient opt-in override or permission inferred from the ability to grant access. Ineligible or uncertain messaging must be shown truthfully; it cannot silently claim notification or switch to a different sender/recipient/channel. Grant-only remains subject to its existing source authority and qualified handoff requirements.

### B — The same review, with notification initially off

The same grant review offers the same visible notification choice, initially unselected. Staff explicitly selects it when the handoff should include email. If selected and authorized, the same source operation records the independently governed message intent. If not selected, the action grants access only. The same exact recipient, message/source/Delivery Plan, proof, suppression and currentness rules apply.

Benefit: email always follows an explicit opt-in in the current review, reducing unintended mail when staff is already helping the person. It needs no separate post-grant screen or command. Cost: staff can overlook the choice, so the fixed period may run without the recipient learning it is available. “Grant succeeded” must not be presented as “recipient notified.” No promise of an alternative lawful channel or manual attachment fallback is implied.

Both choices require a real qualified notification route if that route is offered; neither permits arbitrary email or a copied bearer URL. Neither guarantees successful delivery or exactly-once physical mail. A separate later send is a possible independently governed recovery only where an owner already permits or later explicitly qualifies it; it is not a required third option, a new guaranteed action or a selected resend policy.

**Recommendation: A**, as a product judgment about a complete, low-friction handoff, not an empirical claim about ministry demand. The default is visible and source-gated; the meaningful staff choice stays in the existing review rather than adding a second routine confirmation or a new notification center.

## Inherited constraints, not new choices

- **Existing identity amendment:** Q01's exact tenant/environment/source/request/revision, intended recipient/address revision and purpose remain. No fabricated Party, broader membership, new recipient address picker or Phase28 newsletter-authority reuse. Reconfirm only as Q01/P4 requires; a notification is not proof of identity or document acceptance.
- **Separate authority:** issuance permission is not notification permission. The source must have a qualified message meaning, exact intended recipient and action; P17 prepares approved presentation; P6 owns dispatch/history. P17's code-owned requiredness and tenant/recipient choices can only be narrowed where the contract permits. This question cannot override a specialist mandatory notice or suppression/provider rule.
- **Existing clocks stay fixed:** notification selection, preparation, delivery, retry, opening or recipient entry does not start, extend or renew access. Source G remains the clock anchor. A late message has only the real remaining usable opportunity. No resend repairs an expired grant; no new grant repairs a mere transport failure.
- **One coherent effect, separate outcomes:** for A, the selected authorized secondary intent is durably linked to the exact successful grant under the existing source/P6 transaction or equivalent fail-closed recovery boundary. Providers remain outside locks. Replaying the grant recovers its original effect/message choice; it cannot send again under a new random operation identity. No message should describe a grant that never committed. Provider acceptance, delivery, proof, access and reading remain distinct.
- **Currentness and material change:** before external dispatch, re-prove source applicability, exact recipient/contact revision, current permission and action validity, consent/suppression, sender/reply/connection and immutable presentation. A stale or ended permission cannot be advertised through an old ready message. An in-flight provider submission cannot be recalled by pretending it never happened. Source-approved recovery does not edit sealed bytes or substitute the latest grant.
- **Private presentation:** use only a source-approved minimal fact wall and protected request doorway. Do not attach originals, expose filenames/sensitive reasons in subjects, include a direct provider-storage URL, add tracking to protected secrets or claim a neutral link itself authorizes access. Exact permissible facts are purpose-owned and must be qualified; this note does not invent a universal message body.
- **Failed or unavailable mail:** preserve the actual grant and actual notification state, surface the permitted next actor/recovery and use existing owned help. A transport error must not roll back known granted access, restart its fixed end, change recipient, fabricate a send, or force an ungoverned fallback. Missing required intent durability is a source consistency problem, distinct from a later email failure.
- **Simple UX:** keep the existing grant action, source context and Base UI/base-maia controls. One visible notification choice under A is not a second confirmation ceremony. Do not force file-body reading, new enrollment, reupload or copying secret links. Protected email and the accessible handoff remain independently qualified; a default cannot stand in for those checks.
