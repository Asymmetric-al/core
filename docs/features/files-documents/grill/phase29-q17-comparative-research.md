# Phase 29 Q17 — when a fresh completed-read period starts

Research date: **21 October 2026, Asia/Bangkok**. The parent reports Q16 and its full amendment package explicitly ratified. **Q01–Q16 are the accepted baseline; no Q17 option or duration is selected.** This note addresses the clock model for Q08 fresh completed-read access, before the remaining period values and count/overlap choices.

Only this note was written. Q08's ratified decision, the Q16 successor context and current official public guidance were inspected. No canonical file, product, permission, private account, provider, dependency or UI was changed. No live competitor, native source, browser/AT or intended-user test was performed.

## Recommended single product decision

**When staff grant fresh read access to a completed request, should the reading period start at the qualified source issuance, or on the recipient's first qualified entry within a separate finite entry window?**

Recommend **A — fix the end from fresh source issuance**. It gives staff and the recipient one known end and avoids an additional entry transition. The recommendation is an explicit simplicity/product judgment, not an industry rule or an inference that the recipient is immediately ready to read.

Offer **B — finite entry window, then a full finite reading period from first qualified entry** as the strongest real alternative. It protects the nominal reading opportunity from delay before discovery, provided entry occurs in time and current authority still qualifies. It needs two timing terms and a trustworthy first-entry event. Its entry deadline is not automatically an overall cap on the subsequently admitted reading interval.

Both models are practical. A third choice based on email opens, delivery receipts, every later visit or per-file timers would introduce different dependencies without a demonstrated need and is not required to make this a fair question.

This is one choice of timing model. It deliberately does **not** choose a number of days/hours, a staff-editable date, a repetition/count/overlap rule, notification channel, reminder cadence or general clock service. The selected model must receive finite quantified terms and qualification before actual issuance is enabled.

## Why Q08 still needs this choice

Q08 ratifies a deliberate new finite read-only permission after eligible ordinary completed-view expiry. Q16 extends consideration to either lawful completion-created origin, bounded by that actual completed admission's scope and current eligibility. Neither establishes the new read period's anchor, duration, unused-admission policy or repeat behavior.

The earlier source fulfillment is the origin of the historical read ceiling and old ordinary view. It is not automatically the starting instant for the new grant. Q14's source-issued thirty days govern fresh **unfinished** access; Q15's sequential no-preset-cap policy governs that same unfinished lane. Neither can fill Q08's open decisions.

Both Q17 alternatives must retain the same completed/read-only state, exact historical ceiling, current purpose/recipient/action restrictions, old grant history and Q08's semantic effect/recovery boundaries. This question does not create a completed view where one never existed, change its scope, or reopen source work.

| **B — Enter within a finite window, then start the reading period** | Qualified issuance admits a finite pre-entry opportunity. No entry means the opportunity expires. Once entry is established, subsequent use never restarts it. | Timely delayed discovery does not by itself consume the nominal reading interval. The strongest B does **not** shorten the reading interval simply because entry occurs near the end of the allowed entry window. A pre-entry deadline controls whether entry may activate that interval; it is not also its overall end unless a genuine independently binding source limit says so. Making it both would weaken B's stated delayed-discovery advantage and must not be hidden in a minimum-of-all-dates calculation.

There is an honest horizon tradeoff. If A and B later use the same nominal reading duration, B can extend the maximum time from issuance by its separate allowed waiting interval. A source's real overall purpose/content-use limit can shorten either model. No quantity is selected here, and this is not evidence that a longer horizon is better.

### Simple notation for evaluating the model, not a proposed schema

Let G be this new read grant's qualified issuance occurrence, D its finite reading duration, and H the finite entry allowance if B is chosen. These are conceptual symbols, not reused original-request timestamps, column names or a new clock service.

- Under A, the nominal reading end is G + D; genuine controlling limits preserve their actual scope.
- Under B, the nominal entry end is L = G + H. The first qualifying entry T must occur while the pre-entry authority and current predicates remain valid, including T before the actual entry end. It establishes a nominal reading end T + D. L does not itself clip the new reading interval; genuine overall limits still may.
- No qualifying entry before the B entry end means no later start under that admission. A does not have a separate entry end.
- Neither formula defines a pause, sliding renewal, later grant, mandatory gap or count limit. Units, positive finite values, supported instant/precision semantics and full adopted profile still require explicit completion before activation.

These models are future-authorized fresh access. Neither revives the old completed admission, restarts its fulfillment clock, changes historical scope or erases a gap between old and new rights.

## What “first qualified entry” would need to mean

If B is selected, use entry to the **exact newly granted completed-request context by its intended recipient under current Q01/source authority**, rather than the ambiguous phrase “first file opened.” A legitimate Q16 status/context-only view may contain no file. A file may not have a supported preview, while another permitted action or the context itself remains available. Downloading an original must not become mandatory activation.

The ordinary deliberate entry into that request should be sufficient. Do not create an additional Start timer dialog, timing consent checkbox or second staff approval solely to implement B. A source-qualified entry event can be part of the existing entry flow, but its precise durable effect and currentness proof would need actual design/qualification if B wins.

Email open tracking, a mail provider's delivered event, anonymous GET/HEAD, link scanners, prefetch, a staff preview, a generic account login and proof completed for another request are not that event. A recipient may already have reusable compatible proof; another verification ceremony is not required just to start a business clock. Conversely, successful verification alone is not proof they entered this scoped request.

Do not anchor to an untrusted browser timestamp, a renderer callback or proof that a human read or understood bytes. If the source durably admits qualified entry but the response is lost, reconciliation returns the same T/end; the next attempt cannot start a second full interval. If no qualifying event occurred, an incidental failure must not fabricate one. A slow or failed particular rendition after a valid context entry does not silently pause/reset the selected model.

B's pre-entry state is itself finite and source-governed. It is not an indefinitely dormant approval or a staff permission promise awaiting an arbitrary future visit. Current prohibitions, the exact historical ceiling and ongoing recipient/action checks still govern entry and all later protected use.

## Why A is the lean recommendation

A fits the existing consequential source action: staff deliberately grant access, the source records a finite end, and the same request can display that end. It removes the need to explain two clocks or diagnose whether an entry occurred. It also avoids making the source lifetime depend on a particular file format or initial renderer success.

This is especially valuable for a small, governed reading capability with exact scope. That rationale is not a claim that a recipient will read immediately, that every later request is urgent, that files are always ready, or that support can always regrant after a delay. The eventual duration and supported cohort need to provide useful real opportunity without assuming unselected repeat behavior.

B is stronger when protecting the recipient's full reading opportunity after delayed discovery matters enough to justify finite pre-entry state and its additional explanation. Email delay, intermittent connectivity, device access or a slow sign-in could create that need, but these are plausible scenarios rather than observed ministry prevalence. The finite entry deadline itself can still be missed; B cannot promise an unlimited wait or overcome a genuine earlier restriction.

No new setting menu is needed under either choice. The founder chooses the standard model; source-specific higher restrictions retain their actual scope. Staff continue to use one meaningful Grant read access action rather than selecting clock modes for each recipient.

### Minimal copy direction

Under A, once the source result/current disclosure is established, use **Read-only access ends [actual date, time and zone]**. On a delayed return, do not say the person has a full newly starting duration.

Under B, the existing authorized context can explain **Enter by [actual entry deadline]. Your reading period starts when you first enter this request.** Include the eventual adopted duration terms and genuine known caps. After qualified entry resolves the reading end, show the actual end in the same familiar place. The way pre-entry terms are communicated must fit the actual Q01 entry flow without leaking metadata or adding an unnecessary approval screen.

These are model illustrations, not shipping copy with missing durations. Neither model selects automatic email or reminders. After either true end, use the existing safe help route without promising another approval. For technical uncertainty, say what cannot be confirmed rather than calling the request rejected, files deleted or the business permission expired.

## Accessibility and security boundaries

The selected anchor is a business-permission rule, not the authentication/session timeout. Q01 proof, source authority, representation availability, provider URL expiry and device-copy possession are separate. No fixed or first-entry model means “stay signed in for the whole period.”

Use current terms in main content, with precise localized date/time/zone when resolved, recognizable actions and appropriate focus/status handling. Avoid countdown-only authority, per-second screen-reader chatter, hover-only essential terms or artificial urgency. Existing paste/autofill, narrow-layout, touch and low-bandwidth requirements remain. A status-only scope and an unsupported preview must have understandable outcomes.

Actual WCAG timer analysis remains necessary for the eventual durations and short proof/session/UI timers. The twenty-hour exception is not a reason to select a period or claim conformance. Neither A nor B implies automatic grant extension as an accessibility mechanism; supported accessible recovery must respect actual source authority and the applicable criterion assessment.

## Suggested founder-facing question

**When staff grant fresh read access to a completed request, when should its reading period begin?**

- **A — When the source issues the grant (recommended).** One fixed end is established immediately. Never-entered access ends then too; late discovery leaves less reading time. This is the simplest existing-action experience.
- **B — On first qualified entry, with a finite entry deadline.** The recipient must enter before that deadline, then receives the full defined reading interval subject to genuine earlier limits. Never entering still expires the opportunity. This protects delayed discovery but adds two timing terms and an entry transition.

The choice is only the clock model. We would then settle its finite duration value or values, followed by the remaining completed-read repetition/overlap rules. No number, quota, notification or repeated-grant policy is included in this answer.

## Limits and next evidence

No Asym data establishes an optimal anchor, discovery delay distribution, support availability or duration. Both external patterns are real, but the exact proposed B entry-window model and A source boundary remain Core product/design choices. Do not present format-specific file sharing or enterprise role provisioning as proof that Core's native completed-read implementation works.

After a model is selected, its full review should test the actual source occurrence/entry semantics, unused expiry, identity and scanner separation, status-only views, exact historical scope, earlier limits, lost/reordered responses, current proof/representation failures and clear accessible terms.
