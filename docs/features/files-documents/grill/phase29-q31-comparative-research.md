## Recommended single decision

**When staff deliberately share an eligible item-clarification reply, should the
optional email telling the request recipient about it start On or Off, or should
this phase keep awareness inside the request?**

Recommend **A — Start the optional participant email On, with staff control**.
Nora may ask which certificate is needed and then leave the request. When Elena
answers, a minimal safe email can give Nora a reason to return to the permitted
exchange. Staff can turn that email off for a reply already covered in their
existing conversation, a low-value acknowledgment, or another appropriate
source-specific situation. Email does not carry the sensitive answer itself.

This is a product judgment about discoverability, not a measured Asym benefit.
There is no evidence here that every recipient reads mail, that mail causes
faster completion, or that default On is universally preferable.

| **A — Optional email starts On, with staff control (recommended)**      | Elena posts an outward reply with a visible, eligible email choice selected. | **C — Keep reply awareness native for now**                             | Nora sees replies when she returns under current authority. Staff use the existing approved contact/help route when needed.           | Avoids this additional outbound purpose and its delivery/recovery burden; credible when that contact route already works well.                                                                    | Requires returning to the request or deliberate follow-through elsewhere. It must not become an unowned or invisible answer. Q30's native conversation and accountable work still apply.                                                     |

For B, distinguish a per-reply choice initially Off from an administrator having
disabled the entire eligible email step. A staff member cannot override Tenant
Off by checking a reply box. A and B should compare the same supported capability
and authorities; their principal difference is the initial per-reply choice.
The exact qualified default plan must be explicit rather than implied.

The staff-facing question—whether a new participant clarification should create
a staff email notice, and its default—is a separate event/audience decision.
Reserve it. Q27's Off default for actionable document evidence does not decide it.

## Practical UX and privacy requirements for the question

1. **One ordinary outward reply.** Use the existing source composer and shared
   Base UI/base-maia controls. A possible label is “Email the request recipient
   about this reply,” to be checked with actual users. Show the authorized
   destination safely. Do not add a second message, arbitrary To/CC field,
   confirmation dialog, standalone notification composer or required admin step.
2. **Posting and email are separate outcomes.** A reply can be posted while
   email is Off, suppressed, unavailable, queued, failed or uncertain. Show the
   truth without making staff post the same answer again or suggesting that a
   recipient read it. Preserve choice and permitted draft/focus during recovery.
3. **A cue rather than a copy.** Use only approved minimal subject/body and a
   qualified protected action. Exclude reply excerpts, filenames, file contents,
   internal notes, sensitive missionary names/locations/care circumstances and
   attachments. Even generic subject/sender text can reveal a relationship, so
   source restrictions and actual recipient preferences still apply. Do not
   claim that minimal content eliminates all exposure.
4. **Current action at dispatch and use.** A valid reply posted earlier is not
   proof that the participant may still read it. Expiry, closure, revocation,
   recipient change and restriction require current handling. Never promise
   “read your reply” when the permitted exchange is no longer available. Do not
   add access time, reopen a request or expose completed conversation history.
   A help-only variant is usable only if independently source-qualified; it is
   not an automatic substitute for every suppressed reply notice.
5. **No email dependency.** Participants can see currently authorized shared
   replies on returning through the existing qualified doorway. Staff retain
   accountable source work and the capable existing help route. A blocked email
   must not block a lawful native reply, evidence review or Q22 fulfillment.
6. **Proportionate noise control.** Staff control and event-specific tenant
   configuration provide the useful initial flexibility. No automatic reminder,
   digest, escalation, bell, global inbox, read receipt, typing indicator or
   inference that the participant is currently online is needed for this choice.
   A source reply is not an upload, Request changes, acceptance or completion.

## What must be tested later, rather than claimed from this research

Use representative participants who check the request infrequently as well as
people who already coordinate with staff. Test whether they understand that an
email is a cue and find the permitted answer; test staff recognition of the
selected/Off choice and the difference between posted and emailed. Include a
low-value acknowledgment, sensitive context, missing/suppressed contact, tenant
Off, duplicate/lost-response recovery, access ending before dispatch, access
ending before click, and a returned participant with email Off.

Check keyboard, screen reader, mobile/reflow, international text and slow-network
journeys in the composed source UI. Measure task success, avoidable follow-up,
choice comprehension and unwanted notice reports without logging private bodies.
Do not treat opens, delivery receipts, dwell time or screenshots as evidence that
someone understood the answer. These are future qualification requirements, not
tests performed by this research pass.
