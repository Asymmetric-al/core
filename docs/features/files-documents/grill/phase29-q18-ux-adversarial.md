## Disposition

**Keep A with focused numerical and presentation safeguards.** It specializes Q17's existing source-issued fixed end rather than creating another lifecycle. One standard interval is **720 elapsed hours = 2,592,000 seconds = 2,592,000,000 milliseconds**. A day means twenty-four elapsed hours. It is not a calendar month, thirty civil dates, business days, time since delivery, time since the recipient arrives or active reading time.

Keep the existing **Grant read access** action, current completed context, exact authorized scope and actual end. Do not add a date picker, duration selector, activation step, routine second confirmation, recipient account, countdown ceremony, reminder schedule or exception dashboard. Ordinary time passing under unchanged reviewed terms does not require another confirmation. Material recipient, scope, purpose or binding-limit changes still require the existing correct review.

The useful opportunity can be shorter than thirty days because arrival, proof and technical recovery take time, or because a genuine applicable limit ends it earlier. Thirty does not repair a nonfunctional entry path or make unavailable files accessible. Choosing it is a product preference for more opportunity under an already-running clock, not a promise of successful reading or an empirically optimal ministry period.

## Strongest fourteen-day alternative

B is the same source-issued model with **336 elapsed hours = 1,209,600 seconds**. It is a credible preference for ending the authorized online opportunity sooner after work is already complete. It does not reduce the impact of a disclosure that already happened or revoke a device copy. There is no evidence here quantifying breach likelihood or comparing actual support burden.

A offers sixteen more elapsed days than B where the source occurrence and applicable limits are identical and neither is clipped. That gives more potential opportunity after discovery/proof/technical delays; it does not demonstrate that recipients will use or need the additional time. Neither profile may be defended by assuming staff will issue another grant: completed-read repetition is still unselected. A genuine whole-grant end at day seven would make both actual windows the same. If source/cohort qualification finds that earlier caps or handoff failures routinely erase useful access, the owner must address that fact explicitly; the UI cannot silently bypass limits or promise a minimum.

Matching other thirty-day counts is at most a possible comprehension benefit. Q07's fulfillment-created completed view, Q14's fresh unfinished period and this new Q08/Q17 read issuance retain different anchors, scopes and lifecycle identities. A specialized source's fourteen-day guest rule likewise does not establish B for this ordinary profile.

## Eight supported journeys and exact copy intent

The examples below are proposed copy only. Protected recipient, filename, scope and timing details appear only when their current disclosure is authorized. They are not universal unauthenticated error pages. Use ordinary language such as “access” in the product, rather than exposing source-event identifiers or internal grant terminology.

### 1. Authorized staff reviews a current read need

In the existing completed request, show the intended recipient, meaningful permitted read scope and any controlling limits the actor is authorized to see. Keep **Grant read access** as the one deliberate action. A compact statement can say **“Up to 30 days from when access is granted.”** If the UI previews a date before the real source occurrence, label it **“Estimated end”** and explain any relevant shorter limit. There is no staff-editable period.

The current eligible root does not mean a grant is necessary. If the need is staff-only review or repair of existing usable access, follow that existing path without granting new recipient permission. Do not require the issuer to have file-body reading rights merely to act under its qualified issuing capability, and do not reveal bodies as part of scope review.

### 2. One issuance, known result or lost response

When the qualified result is known, show **“Read access granted. Ends [actual date, time and zone].”** This reports the source act, not delivery or successful entry. If a separately authorized notification fails, report its failure separately; do not roll back or repeat the grant. Do not say “The recipient has access now” when current proof or a known entry-path failure makes that assertion false.

When the result is unknown, use **“We could not confirm whether access was granted. Check the result before trying again.”** Provide the established same-effect reconciliation/retry path, not a fresh-permission action disguised as Retry. If reconciliation finds the earlier act already ended, show that historical result and current ended state, not a new success toast with a fresh thirty days. A definite no-commit failure may permit a new deliberate attempt under current terms; an unknown result does not prove no commit.

### 3. Recipient arrives late or never arrives

After current proof and source checks, present the existing completed context with **“Read-only access ends [actual date, time and zone].”** Show only the actions and content currently allowed. A person arriving five elapsed days after an unclipped issuance has at most twenty-five days left; do not tell them “You have thirty days” or “Your thirty days start now.” Nothing changes if nobody visits.

Do not add a forced “Start access” or “I understand” step. Date/time and next action belong in ordinary content, not only a transient toast, tooltip or ticking urgency banner. The present end remains subject to genuine later source changes; no promise of uninterrupted access through that date follows. A calm contextual explanation is enough; no alarmist repeated disclaimer is required.

### 4. Status-only, partial or unavailable content

A legitimately status-only ceiling can show its permitted status/history context and end without a file list, download control or invented empty-folder implication. For partial permitted content, preserve usable items and show an item-level failure only where revealing that item's existence is authorized. **“Preview unavailable”** does not mean permission expired; a permitted independent download may remain available but must not start automatically.

If history alone fails, do not replace it with “No submissions.” If required whole-view authority is unknown, do not continue protected access on a cached end; use a qualified unavailable/checking state. A member-specific earlier end must be understandable at that member, without hiding a later valid whole-view end or cutting off unrelated permitted status. The UI need not show every technical expiration timestamp.

### 5. Proof/session failure, wrong identity or shared device

An expired proof/session can request the existing qualified verification again while the source period remains valid. The entry instruction should describe verification, not a new source end. A failed code, network error or wrong current identity is not proof that the request expired. Preserve the existing privacy-safe alternate-identity/help route without auto-retargeting the recipient or exposing private scope to the wrong person.

Allow supported paste/autofill and clear keyboard focus. Avoid forcing a full account solely for this feature. On shared devices, existing session/privacy controls still matter; thirty days of business permission is not thirty days of browser login. Reauthentication does not reset the source clock or automatically retry a download.

### 6. Mobile, low bandwidth, DST and travel

Render one actual end with a clear zone/offset appropriate to the supported locale and sufficient precision for the admitted boundary. A localized presentation may change during travel, but its instant must not. Ambiguous abbreviations or date-only “until Friday” cannot be the sole precise boundary. If relative time is used, do not round it into an extra promised day or let it replace the actual end.

On a slow connection, preserve independently valid context and announce meaningful outcomes without moving focus on every refresh. Retry the same effect or authorized read; do not force repeat proof without cause, duplicate issuance, reupload or automatic file download. A stale open page before expiry does not authorize a later protected action. Browser downloads/streams still follow the previously qualified source and serving contract; do not claim a click saved the file or that expiry recalls bytes already delivered.

### 7. Genuine earlier restriction or actual expiry

When current permitted disclosure supports it, ordinary expiration can say **“Read access has ended.”** Offer the existing source-owned help route when available, without **“Renew for thirty days”**, a promised response time or a promise of approval. A still-completed request should not become “incomplete,” “overdue,” or an upload form merely because its read period ended.

Do not attribute a revocation, purpose restriction or unproven current state to the thirty-day timer. Use the appropriate safe current-state wording. Before qualified disclosure, keep the response neutral so an expiry badge does not confirm a private request/recipient exists. Ending this grant does not erase retained source records or deny unrelated independently authorized access. No completed-to-completed transition gives another thirty days.

### 8. Staff, reviewer and support resolve the actual problem

Authorized staff/support distinguish known source result, current end, current restricted scope, proof failure, representation failure and any independently selected delivery outcome. They do not infer human reading from a click or delivery acceptance. A repair of a still-valid qualified representation should use the existing authority and immutable-original ceiling; it should not require a new grant or add a different original version.

If normal expiry caused the problem, explain the existing help path without assuming an additional grant is allowed. If current repeated/cumulative policy is not adopted, do not activate a feature that depends on it. If a genuine source restriction prevents access, support cannot fix it by changing email, cloning the request or attaching the file elsewhere. Useful source-owned review remains possible without inventing a new queue, essay form or routine second approver.
