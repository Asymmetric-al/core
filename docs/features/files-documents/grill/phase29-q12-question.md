## Historical question and alternatives

**How long should the standard ordinary unfinished-access period be, measured
in elapsed time from the original source issuance?**

| Option                                      | Complete proposed ordinary profile                                                                                                           | Practical tradeoff                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Up to 60 elapsed days (recommended)** | Resolve the ordinary limit at original source issuance plus **1,440 hours**, preserving genuine earlier limits and all current restrictions. | More room than 30 days for delivery, recipient work and staff review, while keeping the unfinished opportunity shorter than 90 days. Allows a longer unfinished authorization window than B. |
| **B — Up to 30 elapsed days**               | Same rule, with **720 hours**.                                                                                                               | Tighter possible unfinished exposure and a shorter dormant lifecycle. Delivery or review delays consume a larger share; usefulness must not depend on unselected extensions.                 |
| **C — Up to 90 elapsed days**               | Same rule, with **2,160 hours**.                                                                                                             | Most room of these choices for slower collection/review. Keeps unfinished authority available longer, without evidence that ordinary Asym requests need that additional time.                |

All three are complete calculation proposals for the new ordinary profile:
one fixed elapsed interval resolved from the original qualified issuance,
subject to genuinely applicable earlier limits. They do not change Q11's
source occurrence/commit/recovery contract.

“Standard ordinary profile” is deliberate. This is not a tenant-editable
default, sender date picker, minimum guaranteed access, universal maximum for
every specialized domain, or permission to override a real cap. No option
creates those capabilities.

## Recommendation and honest uncertainty

Recommend **A: up to 60 elapsed days** for the ordinary unfinished stage.

Q11 starts this period at issuance, so it includes delivery/discovery time,
recipient work, permissible corrections and source review. Q10 requires
eligible normal fulfillment before the applicable unfinished end to admit
ordinary completed reading. There is no assumed extension power to repair
routine insufficiency.

Sixty days gives more initial room for those already-selected costs than
thirty, without choosing the longest ninety-day opportunity. That is a
founder/product judgment under uncertainty, not a measured ministry need,
privacy risk rate, service-level promise or industry standard.

The strongest objection is B's shorter permitted exposure: there is no measured
evidence that ordinary recipients need the extra thirty days. That is a real
reason to choose B. Conversely, an unsupported claim that thirty days is enough
would be just as weak as claiming sixty is proven sufficient. The independent
runtime review prefers the tighter thirty-day starting profile on that tradeoff;
the synthesis favors sixty because the fixed period must also accommodate the
organization's review. The underlying source/technical facts do not resolve
that preference.

C is reasonable if the ordinary cohort actually includes slower work that
needs ninety days. No such representative timing evidence has been gathered.
Longer intervals do not by themselves make a request a general drive; purpose,
exact recipient/file scope, finite stages and permitted actions remain binding.
They do lengthen the opportunity for unfinished use and must be justified.

## Exact meaning if A is selected

For explanation, `I` is the original qualified source access-issuance occurrence
and the candidate ordinary end is `I + 1,440 hours`. Resolve the actual original
unfinished end once under the source's genuine applicable earlier limits.
Persist that result and its calculation/limit provenance under Q11.

Elapsed sixty days means exactly **60 × 24 hours**, not two calendar months,
sixty business days, local end-of-day or the requested-by date plus a buffer.
Later display-timezone or DST changes do not alter the instant. The same rule
applies with the other option's hours if B or C is selected.

This proposes the initial numerical profile and elapsed-time interpretation
together, as one coherent product choice. It adds no server clock API, table
layout, due-date policy, staff override or scheduling system.

At actual issuance:

- Missing/invalid/nonfinite policy or no valid remaining opportunity cannot
  be replaced by an invented default or clamped to a later end.
- An independent earlier limit may produce less than the nominal interval;
  current authority can end sooner still.
- The requested-by target remains separate and non-authorizing under Q09.
  An absent or already-past target does not silently change this period.
- Retry resolves the original effect before any fresh calculation; a new
  credential, message, default or HTTP key cannot create another period.

## How Q10's completed stage still works

This is unfinished access, not an overall limit on all original access.
Eligible source-confirmed fulfillment ends ordinary submission/correction and
starts Q07's separately bounded read-only stage from that fulfillment.
True overall caps still bind; the old unfinished-only end does not.

Illustrative elapsed-time examples, with no earlier cap and assuming all
source/recipient eligibility predicates are met:

| Situation under A                                                                                                                | Consequence                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Normal fulfillment exactly 10 elapsed days after issuance                                                                        | Completed reading may run for at most the next 30 elapsed days: at most day 40 from issuance. It does not wait until day 60.                                |
| Normal fulfillment exactly 59 elapsed days after issuance                                                                        | Completed reading may run for at most the next 30 elapsed days: at most day 89 from issuance. The unfinished-only day-60 end does not clip it.              |
| The recipient submits on day 59, but source fulfillment is only at or after the applicable exclusive day-60 end                  | Receipt remains true and authorized source work may finish, but ordinary completed recipient admission is not automatically created from expired authority. |
| A genuine overall cap is earlier than either nominal end                                                                         | The cap keeps its declared scope; no option promises the full period.                                                                                       |
| A durably committed fulfillment whose qualified occurrence and eligibility were before the unfinished end is learned about later | Recover its original eligible completed outcome and end under Q10; observation time is not a new anchor.                                                    |

As a mathematical consequence, the latest possible original completed end
under the sixty-day profile is **strictly before 90 elapsed days from issuance**,
because eligible fulfillment must occur before the actual unfinished end.
This is not a new universal ninety-day cap, a retention rule, or a limit chosen
for Q08's separately authorized fresh access.

## User journeys and readiness

Before issuance, staff see the ordinary timing rule and known controlling
limits in the existing source review. Show the actual resolved end after
issuance. A changing estimate under unchanged approved terms does not create
another confirmation step.

Recipients see the real date/time/timezone, not merely “you have sixty days.”
That phrase would be misleading after late delivery or an earlier restriction.
Use the Q11 label in the main content:

> Access while this request is unfinished ends [localized date, time and zone].

Where present, **Requested by** remains separately labeled. Receipt, checking,
source review and completed status remain distinct. Do not count staff review
as recipient lateness, encourage early false completion, or require downloading
files to finish the task.

A requested-by target after the actual access end exposes a possible timing
mismatch that the authorized source must surface before issuance. The target
must never be represented as permission to submit after access ends. This
question does not impose a universal rejection solely because the target is
later, or resolve the mismatch by silently moving the target, lengthening
permission, cloning the request or introducing a new date editor. The owning
source must have compatible terms and a useful cohort before activation.
Genuine specialized source contracts keep their existing authority; a new
exception/profile requires its explicit owner decision.

Weak connectivity, travel, mail filtering and review delays are realistic
conditions to test, not measured ministry prevalence or a numerical reason
that proves sixty days. Preserve known received work and reconcile unknown
outcomes. Sign-in/code trouble does not imply source expiry, and repairing it
does not replenish time.

All options retain the same Base UI/base-maia flow and current permission
checks. No date-policy dashboard, extra enrollment, timer-start button,
countdown-only information or reminder schedule follows. Assess proof/session/
upload timers separately; the WCAG longer-than-20-hours exception is not a
recommendation for this product interval.

## Current primary evidence

- [Box File Request](https://support.box.com/hc/en-us/articles/360045304813-Using-File-Request-to-get-Content-from-Anyone)
  supports setting an expiration date before sharing. It does not establish
  sixty days for this request model.
- [Box enterprise sharing settings](https://support.box.com/hc/en-us/articles/4404822772755-Enterprise-Settings-Content-Sharing-Tab)
  documents values for other link/invitation scopes. A matching number there
  is not evidence that Core should reuse it.
- [Content Snare's forms integration](https://contentsnare.com/help/knowledge-base/content-snare-with-forms-2/)
  describes a fourteen-day **due-date** default. That is not proof of access
  expiry, especially under Q09's separate requested-by semantics.
- [Foundant follow-up logic](https://support.foundant.com/hc/en-us/articles/4411213334935-Follow-Up-Submission-Logic)
  distinguishes submission and administrator completion. This supports
  considering review time but supplies no duration recommendation.
- [Goodstack application expiry](https://help.goodstack.org/will-my-application-expire)
  uses thirty days for specified outstanding nonprofit-verification requirements.
  That business application rule is not proof of a thirty-day attachment-access
  grant, Asym's source review period or an expiry-as-rejection rule.
- [FileInvite's request guide](https://help.fileinvite.com/creating-a-new-blank-invite)
  distinguishes its requested due date from nonexpiring portal links.
  The distinction supports Q09, while its unlimited access differs from the
  finite Asym policy already chosen. Neither behavior selects sixty days.
- [Content Snare completed exports](https://contentsnare.com/help/knowledge-base/can-clients-export-a-request/)
  has a separately configurable completed request-PDF availability period.
  It is not original-file access, initial unfinished time or Core's Q07 policy.
- [WCAG timing guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/timing-adjustable.html)
  constrains actual interaction timers; it selects none of these three values.
- [PostgreSQL time arithmetic](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/functions-datetime.html)
  distinguishes elapsed-hour and civil-time arithmetic. Choosing hours makes
  the proposed interval precise; it does not prove source ordering or usability.

The [comparative research](phase29-q12-comparative-research.md) records current
source scopes, dates and limitations. Current public documentation is not a
representative user study or a live test of competitor behavior. Superficially
matching numbers for retention, tracking URLs, authentication or invitations
cannot establish this permission policy.
