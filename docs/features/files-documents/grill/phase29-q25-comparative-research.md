# Phase 29 Q25 — Review responsibility before initial collection

**Research date:** 2026-10-22, Asia/Bangkok. Q01–Q24 and their complete amendments are ratified according to the parent's current instruction. Q25 has no selected answer. This is next-question research, not the full adversarial review of a selected policy, canonical publication or native implementation proof.

## Recommended one decision

**Before an ordinary private document request first gives its recipient access to submit, must its source already resolve a qualified person-or-team review responsibility, or may otherwise-authorized collection begin while that review responsibility is unresolved?**

Recommend **A — Resolve a qualified review responsibility before first source issuance.** Reuse the valid source-qualified person or team permitted by Q24. Do not require a named individual, a person to be online, a Claim, an extra reviewer approval or successful email delivery. The substantive new prerequisite is a usable accountable review path at the moment initial recipient authority is admitted, not a repeated setup ceremony.

**B — Allow collection before review assignment is resolved** is a genuine alternative. It allows a known, otherwise-authorized request to become available under a real source-owned intake/recovery route while the ordinary review responsibility is explicitly unresolved. Q21/Q23 review still cannot proceed until current responsibility and the human action are qualified. B does not mean unknown issuer authority, ownerless custody or “some administrator will eventually notice.”

## Why this is a useful next question

The Q24 question and ratified package explicitly left collection-before-resolution to source policy; it settled what may carry responsibility when review actions occur. Q11 distinguishes source issuance from draft creation, task creation, email delivery, recipient confirmation and first use. Q12 starts the finite initial unfinished period at that issuance. The owner reviewer confirmed this is a genuinely open ordinary-profile choice: source-level capability/activation qualification is distinct from whether the particular request's review responsibility resolves at issuance.

The fork changes what authorized staff can accomplish when a real assignment gap exists, and what commitment is made to a recipient. It is therefore more meaningful now than choosing reminder cadence, per-item routing overrides or an assignment picker layout. It is not another question about a nominal person versus a team, and it does not re-ask Q21 human acceptance or Q23 recoverable feedback.

If the actual source contract for a specialist cohort already imposes reviewer readiness at issuance or intentionally separates a qualified intake stage, preserve it. This question concerns the new ordinary native profile. A universal configuration screen or a third “every tenant chooses a workflow mode” option would add policy machinery not justified by this fork. Existing typed specialist policies are not erased by choosing one ordinary rule.

## Exact scope: source issuance, not an email send

The meaningful boundary is the source's first durable admission of recipient authority to use this particular request. An interface may combine that with a message action, but the effects and their truth remain separate.

- Saving a draft or internal task does not itself start the recipient's period or prove the request is usable.
- A sent or delivered email does not prove a valid source issuance; a valid issuance does not prove email delivery, reading or successful recipient confirmation.
- An unchanged result recovered after a lost response is the original issuance, not a new attempt that restarts the clock.
- Under A, the new review-responsibility prerequisite belongs at the authoritative initial-admission boundary, not merely on a client-side Send button. A cached green label cannot satisfy it.
- Under B, only the review-assignment issue is allowed to remain unresolved. Unknown required issuer, recipient, purpose, entry, privacy, custody or applicable permission evidence does not become safe because collection is convenient. The actual source must establish which dependency is unresolved and still qualify the intake/recovery path.
- Neither choice makes someone accept a staff assignment, be online, read a notification or inspect a nonexistent submission before issuance. Neither promises the route will remain staffed forever.
- This initial-issuance fork does not silently change Q13–Q20 fresh-access grants, recipient changes, later handoffs, revocation or existing supported recovery. Those retain their exact source prerequisites and would require explicit treatment if a material amendment is necessary.

The word “send” should be explained with this boundary in the final founder question. Familiar product wording can remain; policy must not depend on email-provider success or a stale client display.

## Recommendation reasoning and strongest challenge

Q24 has already removed a major unnecessary cost of A: staff do not need to name a particular person when a qualified shared team is responsible. Existing valid source resolution can satisfy A without a new interaction. The ordinary request is deliberately asking a known recipient to supply private evidence; establishing where that review work belongs before admitting access is a proportionate commitment. It also avoids intentionally spending part of the recipient's finite access period on unresolved review assignment.

That reasoning does not make A free. A can block useful, otherwise lawful collection during a real gap or a required eligibility-check outage. A request preparer with no power to repair the source assignment needs a capable internal response, not a vague disabled control. If the product later shows that most ordinary intake intentionally precedes reviewer selection, the founder could prefer B; no prevalence evidence was obtained here.

B's best case is deliberate staged work: a legitimate source owner can safely receive and support the recipient now, but the exact person/team that will judge the documents will be established later. The recipient may have a practical opportunity to submit before staff scheduling is settled. That is an illustrative opportunity, not a claim about ministries, mobile users or average delays. B can avoid an unnecessary upstream dependency when custody, purpose and current access are already qualified.

B's unresolved review status must remain a real staff obligation with a capable owner response. It cannot depend on the recipient discovering the internal gap or on an eventual administrator casually finding it. Received work does not imply safety clearance, inspection, human acceptance or fulfillment. The existing 60-elapsed-day initial unfinished maximum begins at actual issuance under either option, subject to earlier limits; B does not pause it while the organization chooses a reviewer. A establishes readiness only at the specified boundary and does not promise that later staff delays disappear.

An optional per-request “bypass review setup” checkbox would quietly add an exception policy rather than simply implement A. A mandatory additional approval to use B would likewise create a different workflow. Neither is recommended in this question. Choose one ordinary rule and preserve already-qualified specialist rules.

## Small illustrative journey

Ana is preparing a private request for a form and a supporting document. This is a hypothetical example for reasoning, not observed tenant behavior.

- **When a qualified source review team already resolves:** A can reuse it and let Ana complete the existing issuance interaction. B can do the same. Neither needs a named person to claim the request or a second setup step.
- **When review responsibility does not resolve, but Ana has valid source issuance authority:** A keeps the work prepared and explains the internal review-responsibility requirement with an authorized way to resolve it. No recipient authority has yet been admitted merely by saving the draft. B can admit initial access only if all its other source conditions and the genuine intake/recovery route hold; appropriate staff see that review responsibility still needs assignment.
- **When the recipient submits under B:** record the exact received act and retain the ordinary safe handling/current access rules. The participant receives truthful receipt/status/help. Do not say a reviewer is working, expose internal rosters or let a placeholder accept the item. Staff resolve the responsibility before a current human performs Q21/Q23.
- **When responsibility is resolved later:** preserve earlier receipt and timing. A first issuance occurs only at the actual subsequent qualified admission. Under B, assignment later is not a new issuance, a free new access period, acceptance or another notification by default.
- **When a once-valid team later loses eligibility:** both options use Q24's current qualification and capable recovery. A does not retrospectively make the original issuance nonexistent; neither option automatically revokes otherwise independent recipient access, resets the clock or reopens completed work solely because staffing changed.
- **When a response is lost:** recover the same durable issuance/assignment result. A failed email or task projection does not prove that recipient authority was never admitted; an unknown required source result is reconciled. The recipient must not receive a second request or be asked to reupload solely to repair uncertainty.

Lean copy could describe **Review responsibility needs to be resolved before this request can open for submissions** under A, and an internal **Open for submissions · Review needs assignment** under B. These are examples, not fixed enum names or screen designs. A valid inherited team should not prompt either warning. Unknown lookup must say it cannot currently be checked, not falsely assert an empty team or missing authority.

The source and audience still determine which details are safe. The participant should not need to understand staffing mechanics to submit or obtain truthful help. No new composer, reminder, roster, intake dashboard or settings wizard is necessary merely to explain the chosen behavior.

## Important boundaries for the final question

1. **Keep A current and bounded.** Qualified responsibility is a real source path, not a reviewer promise to respond now, a calendar availability test or a permanent staffing guarantee. The source—not a client-side check—determines admission.
2. **Make B accountable.** It defers ordinary review responsibility, not the requesting source's purpose, issuer authority, recipient assurance, safe custody or capable intake/recovery ownership. An unidentified “somebody later” is not the strong alternative.
3. **Preserve the distinction from Q24.** Both options allow persons or qualified teams and record actual human review decisions. B still requires qualification at review time; A adds the earlier initial-issuance prerequisite.
4. **Preserve clocks and facts.** Neither changes source issuance time, the finite initial period, late-target semantics, receipt/admission, correction, human acceptance, fulfillment, source retention or fresh-access policy. A blocked draft is not an issued request; B's later assignment is not a new grant.
5. **Do not turn external copy into a Core contract.** FileInvite optional fields and Content Snare defaults do not prove effective qualified ownership. Submittable staging and HubSpot incoming-ticket triage provide fair alternatives at other business levels.
6. **Keep the ordinary question decisive.** No answer has been selected. The full chosen-answer adversarial review should test race, outage, authority-loss, task recovery and accessibility consequences, rather than inventing all those mechanisms in the question. Existing source qualification remains necessary whichever answer is chosen.
